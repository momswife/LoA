**Recommendation:** adopt as a small roadside settlement whose inn is its center, not its entire population. New proprietor: Mara Pell. Keep precise road placement and census unstated.

> Bristle Inn began as a lodging house and grew into the cluster of homes, sheds, and working yards around it. Travelers still use the same name for the inn and the settlement. The distinction matters to residents: Mara Pell owns the public house, but she does not own the smithy, the neighboring gardens, or the families who keep the road usable.
>
> Most work comes from feeding travelers, mending harness, storing goods overnight, and putting damaged carts back on their wheels. The common room serves as a hiring place before breakfast and a meeting room after supper. >
> The settlement keeps one dry room for sick or stranded travelers. Its cost is shared unevenly and argued over often. This season's disagreement concerns a carrier who repeatedly leaves exhausted animals at the inn and sends someone else to collect them. Pell wants a deposit; the farrier wants the carrier barred. Residents would welcome a reliable escort or witness more than another stranger promising to solve everything with a sword.

**Adoption effects:** replace the preliminary assessment; link the regional hospitality record; retain one settlement article. No new organization page is needed for an inn business.

### L02 — Granfield: grain storage is a public responsibility

Coopers, roofers, carters, and rat-catchers are as familiar a sight here as field workers.

Household shares are recorded separately from the reserve kept for spoiled crops, sickness, and delayed deliveries. > An older storehouse has begun taking water through its roof. Repairing it before autumn deepens would cost less than replacing a spoiled reserve, yet the families who use the newer building disagree about their share of the bill. Pristana](content/Aerathon%20-%20Eternal%20Labyrinths/II.%20The%20Living%20Atlas/01.%20Regions%2C%20Realms%20%26%20Settlements/I.%20Aerathon/5.%20Vinyot/1.%20Subregions/1.%20Greater%20Vinyot/1.%20Settlements/04.%20Pristana.md). **Recommendation:** make it a modest maritime service town with a working harbor. This is new local geography, so leave its exact map position and shipping distances unchanged until checked.

> Pristana's inhabited waterfront shares its name with the island around it. Fishing crews, repair yards, and boats carrying ordinary supplies provide most of the harbor's work. Visitors see timber drying beside patched sails and hear arguments about delivery dates more often than talk of recovered relics.
>
> A harbor clerk records berth use and repair agreements. The clerk can settle a quay fee, but cannot decide an island household's land dispute or bind every resident to a shipowner's contract. Public repair tallies distinguish work promised, work inspected, and work paid for. The practice allows a small yard to trade on reliability without pretending every customer is a friend.
>
> The town's present dispute concerns a profitable outside carrier seeking priority access to the best repair berth. Yard owners welcome dependable orders; fishing families fear being unable to repair their own boats during the busy season. A compromise would reserve part of each working cycle for local vessels. Both sides need someone to check the proposed schedule against the work the yard can actually perform.

**Adoption effects:** one town profile and local culture note; no claim of independence, a new island nation, or compulsory Vinyoti currency.

**Established anchors:** an unpublished Bestiary draft with this name, located under Beasts & Fauna. Source: [05. Sandglass Vulture](content/Aerathon%20-%20Eternal%20Labyrinths/II.%20The%20Living%20Atlas/08.%20Bestiary%20%26%20Field%20Phenomena/I.%20Beasts%20%26%20Fauna/05.%20Sandglass%20Vulture.md). **Recommendation:** adopt the following ecological concept. This draft adds no combat statistics and no named geographic range that would require changing regional records.

> The sandglass vulture is a broad-winged scavenger of dry, open country. Pale throat feathers darken with dust when the bird feeds, producing the narrowing patch from which its common name is taken. Nothing in ordinary field observation requires an ability to measure or alter time.
>
> The birds search from rising air and gather where several smaller scavengers have already found a carcass. Their arrival can help a traveler locate a dead pack animal or an unattended camp, but a circling flock is not proof that anyone below is dead. Nesting pairs defend ledges noisily and may strike at people who climb too close.
>
> Caravan workers value the birds' cleanup of carrion away from wells, while resenting them when unsecured offal draws a flock into camp. Keep waste downwind, secure food, and give nesting ledges room. A bird that remains on the ground with dragged wings should be treated as injured or sick rather than read automatically as an omen.

**Adoption effects:** complete the draft, then remove `draft: true`, update the Bestiary register, and add habitat links only when a regional distribution is actually chosen.
