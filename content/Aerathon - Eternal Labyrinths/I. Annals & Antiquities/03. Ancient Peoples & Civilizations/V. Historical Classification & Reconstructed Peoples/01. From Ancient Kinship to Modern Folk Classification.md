# From Ancient Kinship to Modern Folk Classification

### _How to Read Beastfolk, Brethren, and the Later Living Atlas Registries_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities\
**Responsible Office:** Historical Reconstruction Bureau, Registry Reconciliation Project\
**Primary Compiler:** Archivist Yelna Stonevoice\
**Contributing Authorities:** Researcher Falyn Onarra, Keeper Aeska Mourn-Wind, Magistrate Talar Shellmark\
**Current Revision:** Late Redfall, 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle\
**Document Class:** _Historical Classification Guide / Grade-I Interpretive Authority_\
**Scope:** Reader guidance for comparing ancient peoples records with current Living Atlas classifications

---

## I. Purpose

Ancient Aerathon did not use the modern seven-part Folk registry.

Many surviving records speak as though the world contained two great mortal families: **Brethren** and **Beastfolk**. Other sources use a local kinship, kingdom, covenant, profession, species resemblance, religious status, or political rank in place of any broad identity. Still others describe people through hostile categories such as monster, spirit, asset, familiar, or curiosity.

The modern Living Atlas instead recognizes **Brethren, Beastfolk, Avianfolk, Aquaticfolk, Scalefolk, Verdantfolk,** and **Mythicfolk** as distinct administrative classifications.

These systems do not align neatly.

This guide explains how a people recorded under an ancient label may appear in a different modern filing without declaring either record meaningless. It also establishes an important limit:

> Historical participation in a Beastfolk or Brethren society does not require a modern person to accept Beastfolk or Brethren as their present identity.

The archive preserves the old term when it describes an actual historical institution, treaty, law, or community. Modern reference records use the current classification chosen through community consultation, body-access needs, shared history, and the Living Atlas’s **Dominant Domain Standard**.

---

## II. Three Different Questions

Confusion usually begins when three questions are treated as one.

### Who Were These People?

This is a question of personhood, community, descent, chosen relation, and self-understanding. The answer may be a household, lineage, city, people, migration, or name that never appeared in a Crown registry.

### How Did Their Society Organize Belonging?

Ancient kinships and civic traditions joined people through law, adoption, oath, custodianship, marriage, craft, territory, faith, and shared survival. A Sky-Reeve household could belong to the Beastfolk political world without resembling every other household later placed beneath that umbrella.

### How Did an Authority File Them?

Registries were built for taxation, military service, worship law, travel, census, treaty, labor, medicine, and later civil recognition. They could protect rights, but they could also simplify people into categories convenient to rulers.

The same community may therefore have:

- a self-chosen name;
- an ancient political or kinship affiliation;
- and a modern Living Atlas classification.

All three can be historically relevant without being interchangeable.

---

## III. Why the Old Archive Looks Binary

The apparent Brethren–Beastfolk division is partly theological, partly political, and partly an accident of survival.

### Creation Traditions

The dominant Dawn Court and Old Court traditions describe different divine relationships in the making of mortal peoples. Later theologians turned complex stories into broad families of creation. People associated with durable office, craft, written continuity, or the Dawn Court were commonly described as Brethren. People associated with animal kinships, living relationship, and the Old Court were commonly described as Beastfolk.

Neither body of tradition proves that every person fitted cleanly into one family.

### Crown and Imperial Law

Large states found broad categories useful. A binary register made it easier to assign taxes, legal standing, military obligations, temple access, labor restrictions, and rules of inheritance. The categories became sharper in government documents precisely because lived identity was more complicated.

The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/04. Conflicts, Catastrophes, & Treaties/III. Treaties, Concords & Political Settlements/04. The Treaty of Breath & Beast|Treaty of Breath & Beast]] made some distinctions legible to law while also preserving unequal ideas about recognition. Later institutions inherited both effects.

### Archival Survival

Court registers, tax rolls, guild charters, and treaty copies were more likely to be recopied than household speech or local identity. As a result, the categories favored by states appear more universal than they were.

Modern reconstruction gives greater weight to community testimony, material practice, local names, and records preserved outside imperial archives.

## IV. Historical-to-Modern Crosswalk

The following table is a reading aid, not a rule of descent.

| Historical term or filing | What it commonly meant in its own record | Possible modern relationship | Reader caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brethren** | A broad family of peoples connected through creation traditions, civic inheritance, craft, oath, and public office | Many modern Brethren lineages remain under this name | Do not assume shared blood, one culture, or superior civilization |
| **Beastfolk** | Animal-kinship peoples and communities participating in Beastfolk law, territory, ritual, or political life | Modern Beastfolk, and some peoples now filed as Avianfolk, Aquaticfolk, or Scalefolk | Do not replace the term when it names a real historical coalition |
| **Sky-Reeve / winged kinship** | Aerial households within the ancient Beastfolk world | Commonly read through modern Avianfolk classification | Their current filing does not remove their Beastfolk-era political history |
| **Shell-Ledger / river or shore kinship** | Shelled and waterside peoples joined through oath, custody, and trade | May correspond to modern Chelonian Scalefolk or Testudinan Aquaticfolk, depending on community history and dominant domain | Shell resemblance alone cannot decide classification |
| **Water spirit, sea beast, maritime asset** | Inconsistent labels used by outsiders for marine people | Often modern Aquaticfolk | Many such terms denied personhood and should appear only in historical context |
| **Scaled beast, lesser dragon, monster register** | Conflicting filings for terrestrial and littoral reptilian people | Often modern Scalefolk; some draconic peoples are Mythicfolk | Scale, venom, or resemblance to dragons does not establish identity |
| **Garden spirit, awakened plant, fungal colony** | Outsider descriptions of plant-, fungal-, or arboreal-bodied people | May describe modern Verdantfolk | These labels often erased individual personhood and community names |
| **Prodigy, omen-born, living relic, singular creature** | Anomalous or politically inconvenient person outside an ordinary registry | Some are now filed as Mythicfolk; others belong elsewhere | Mythicfolk is a modern protective category, not an ancient unified civilization |

No modern classification should be assigned from an old word alone. The surrounding settlement, kinship, body, route, law, and self-description matter.

## V. The Modern Classifications

### Brethren

**Brethren** is the most continuous name across the two systems, but its contents and legal meaning have changed.

Modern Brethren filing recognizes multiple peoples connected by long histories of civic institution, durable craft, chosen oath, public office, and record. It does not claim one ancestry. Modern lineages include communities whose ancestors entered Brethren society through adoption, covenant, migration, intermarriage, political recognition, and shared work.

Ancient Brethren records should therefore be read as histories of peoples and institutions, not as evidence of a single biological family.

### Beastfolk

Modern **Beastfolk** is narrower than many ancient uses of the word. It generally concerns terrestrial animal-kinship lineages whose ordinary embodied and communal lives are shaped primarily on land.

Ancient Beastfolk was often a wider political and sacred world. It could include winged, river-associated, shelled, or otherwise distinct peoples who shared councils, law, ritual obligations, and territorial relationships.

For that reason, an old Beastfolk coalition should remain Beastfolk in the historical record even where some participating peoples now appear elsewhere in the Living Atlas.

### Avianfolk

Modern **Avianfolk** includes sapient peoples fundamentally shaped by flight, gliding, aerial migration, or sustained vertical habitation. It is not limited to birdlike bodies.

Older records often placed aerial peoples under Beastfolk kinships or recorded only a specific clan or roost. The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/03. Ancient Peoples & Civilizations/III. Beastfolk Kinships/08. The Sky-Reeve Clans|Sky-Reeve Clans]] are the clearest surviving example: their history belongs within the ancient Beastfolk archive, while their descendants’ modern classification may be Avianfolk.

### Aquaticfolk

The comprehensive **Aquaticfolk** filing is new in its current form. Earlier authorities scattered marine, littoral, deepwater, and amphibious peoples across Beastfolk rolls, spirit catalogues, monster registries, naval records, and local curiosities.

Modern filing begins from water’s bodily and historical consequence. A person’s resemblance to a terrestrial mammal, reptile, or amphibian does not override a life for which water is indispensable.

### Scalefolk

Modern **Scalefolk** addresses an old administrative absence. Reptilian people were formerly divided among Beastfolk, Aquaticfolk, Mythicfolk, draconic appendices, and monster registries.

The present classification centers self-identification, collective history, body form, and dominant domain. It explicitly rejects the assumption that reptilian peoples are unfinished dragons or naturally aligned with monsters.

### Verdantfolk

**Verdantfolk** is a modern collective filing for sapient peoples whose living bodies arise primarily through plant, fungal, or arboreal processes.

No evidence establishes one ancient Verdantfolk nation or common origin. Many older sources failed to distinguish a person from the landscape, grove, network, or magical event around them. Modern filing recognizes personhood first and does not infer communal consciousness, ownership, ecological duty, or consent to harvesting.

### Mythicfolk

**Mythicfolk** is the least suitable category for projection into the past.

It is a modern, deliberately revisable administrative home for rare, anomalous, transformed, created, or campaign-sensitive sapient lineages that do not fit safely elsewhere. It does not describe a shared ancient people, culture, body plan, faith, or origin.

When an ancient source calls someone an omen, relic, monster, prodigy, or singular wonder, historians should preserve the source’s wording only as evidence of how that society reacted. The word does not establish modern Mythicfolk identity.

## VI. Rules for Retrospective Naming

Annals & Antiquities uses the following practice.

### Preserve a Historical Proper Name

Keep **Beastfolk Confederation**, **Brethren court**, **Sky-Reeve Clan**, or another period name when it identifies a real institution, coalition, law, archive, or self-described community.

### Add Modern Context Without Rewriting the Past

Where a reader could mistake an old umbrella for a current lineage, add a brief clarification or link to the modern overview.

Preferred:

> The Shell-Ledger Houses belonged to the wider Beastfolk political world of their era. Related modern peoples may be filed as Chelonian Scalefolk or Testudinan Aquaticfolk.

Avoid:

> The Shell-Ledger Houses were actually Scalefolk.

The second statement erases the historical relationship rather than explaining it.

### Do Not Infer Identity from Appearance

Wings do not automatically establish Avianfolk identity. Scales do not automatically establish Scalefolk identity. Plantlike tissue does not automatically establish Verdantfolk identity. A rare or magical body does not automatically establish Mythicfolk identity.

Use the dedicated subject page, community testimony, dominant domain, and continuous history.

### Mark Hostile or Obsolete Terms

Words such as monster, asset, specimen, lesser dragon, conditional person, or awakened animal may be necessary in a quotation or legal history. They should be identified as the language of that source, not repeated as neutral description.

### Preserve Uncertainty

If the surviving evidence cannot establish which modern community is connected to an ancient name, leave the relationship unresolved. A visible uncertainty is more accurate than a confident reclassification.

## VII. What Classification Can and Cannot Do

A sound classification can support:

- legal recognition and protection;
- accessible architecture, equipment, and medicine;
- appropriate bodily privacy;
- community consultation;
- and the discovery of records hidden under an older label.

It cannot prove:

- personality, culture, faith, profession, or moral character;
- a single biological origin;
- political loyalty;
- a universal homeland;
- or consent to be described primarily through a Ministry category.

Modern classifications are tools of recognition. They are not replacements for names, communities, or histories.

---

## VIII. Reader’s Method

When moving between the Annals and the Living Atlas, ask:

1. Is this word the people’s name, a coalition’s name, or an official filing?
2. Does the passage describe a body, a culture, a legal status, or all three?
3. Is the author writing in the period being described, or applying a later category?
4. What does the dedicated subject record say?
5. Would replacing the old term clarify the person, or erase the institution they actually lived within?

The correct reading may preserve both statements:

- the Sky-Reeve Clans were part of the ancient Beastfolk world;
- many related modern communities are Avianfolk.

History becomes more accurate when it can hold both.
