# **Elder Civilizations**

### _The Kingdoms, Courts, and Ordered Peoples of the Pre-Labyrinth World_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Historical Reconstruction Bureau  
**Document Class:** _Civilizational Index / Pre-Labyrinth Historical Record_  
**Scope:** Organized societies existing before the emergence of the Eternal Labyrinths

---

## **I. Purpose and Scope**

The designation **Elder Civilizations** applies to the kingdoms, city-states, confederated realms, sacred polities, and other organized societies that arose before the emergence of the Eternal Labyrinths.

These civilizations did not inhabit a single continuous ancient world.

They rose upon the remains of older peoples, inherited machines they did not always understand, revised the histories of those they conquered, and built new systems of law around fragments of divine and arcane authority. Some endured for centuries. Others vanished so completely that their existence is supported only by a ruined road, a repeated name, or a language preserved inside another people’s prayers.

The Ministry distinguishes an elder civilization from a broader ancestry or culture by the presence of several recognized features:

- an enduring political or civic identity;
- a recognized territory or network of settlements;
- systems of law, leadership, or collective authority;
- shared institutions or public traditions;
- and evidence of sustained cultural continuity across multiple generations.

A people need not have possessed a crown, permanent capital, or written language to meet this classification.

---

## **II. The Problem of Ancient Identity**

Modern maps cannot be placed cleanly over the ancient world.

The borders now called Louvain, Vinyot, Allemance, Arneria, and Al’ar did not exist in their present forms. Rivers followed different courses. Coastlines have been broken, raised, or swallowed. Entire settlements now lie beneath modern cities, mountain shelves, and Labyrinth exclusion zones.

Names present an equal difficulty.

Many ancient civilizations are known only through titles assigned by later historians. Others are remembered by the names of ruling courts, surviving capitals, religious offices, or the peoples who eventually replaced them. In several cases, what was once described as a single empire is now understood to have been a network of distinct societies joined by trade, ritual, or shared magical practice.

The records preserved here therefore distinguish between:

- names used by the civilization itself;
- names given by contemporary rivals;
- names preserved through descendant traditions;
- and names assigned during modern reconstruction.

Where uncertainty remains, it is retained.

---

## **III. The Earliest Civilizations**

The oldest recoverable societies are collectively described as the **Orichalcum Civilizations**.

Their structures predate the recognized historical calendar. Their surviving works suggest cultures in which craftsmanship, language, magic, and civic authority were deeply intertwined. They left behind metals that retained intention, structures responsive to sound and light, and mechanisms that continued operating long after their makers vanished.

Whether these societies formed a united civilization, a network of related cultures, or several unrelated peoples using the same inherited material tradition remains disputed.

Their disappearance created the first great rupture in mortal history.

---

## **IV. The Convergence Courts**

The **Convergence Courts** arose during the closing centuries of the Orichalcum world.

Unlike their predecessors, the Courts attempted to classify power rather than merely wield it. Their philosopher-magistrates divided existence into measurable jurisdictions and constructed city-states devoted to the regulation of magic, divinity, law, blood, thought, breath, dream, and flame.

Their experiments produced some of Aerathon’s earliest surviving legal codes, standardized spell structures, and theories of civic magical authority.

Their failure was equally influential.

The philosophical conflicts that destroyed the Courts scattered their scholars among the emerging Crowned Realms, where knowledge once justified through study was recast as inheritance, divine favor, and sacred blood.

---

## **V. The Crowned Realms**

The fall of the Convergence Courts gave rise to the civilizations of the **Age of Crowns**.

These kingdoms built their legitimacy around access to surviving wonders, divine recognition, inherited magical resonance, and the ability of rulers to maintain the ancient systems upon which their subjects depended.

Three eventually became dominant:

- the **Bell-Crown of Louvain**;
- the **Stone Throne of Dhalraen**;
- and the **Sun Court of Vaelcour**.

Together they became known as the **Three High Thrones**.

Around them stood dozens of lesser kingdoms, sacred principalities, temple domains, itinerant courts, and crowned city-states. Some became vassals. Some disappeared through conquest or marriage. Others survive only as names preserved in ruins and outlawed genealogies.

---

## **VI. Civilizations and Their Peoples**

A civilization is not synonymous with an ancestry.

Brethren and Beastfolk peoples frequently lived across several kingdoms, migrated between territories, or maintained cultural identities independent of the rulers who claimed authority over them. Some civilizations were deliberately mixed. Others enforced rigid divisions of ancestry, vocation, faith, or magical inheritance.

For this reason:

- broad Brethren cultural histories are recorded under **Brethren Peoples & Traditions**;
- Beastfolk clans and confederated kinships are recorded under **Beastfolk Kinships**;
- and practices shared across several societies are recorded under **Shared Customs & Ways**.

The present index concerns the civilizations themselves: the structures people built together, the powers that governed them, and the worlds those structures created.

---

## **VII. The End of the Elder World**

The elder civilizations did not fall in a single catastrophe.

Some collapsed centuries before the first Labyrinth appeared. Others weakened through religious conflict, dynastic failure, magical exhaustion, famine, or war. Several endured in altered forms until the wider collapse of the pre-Labyrinth order.

The emergence of the Eternal Labyrinths marks the final boundary of this archive.

Civilizations founded after that emergence—including early delver settlements, guild sovereignties, post-collapse states, and modern political entities—are documented within **II. The Living Atlas**.

Their histories may descend from the elder world.

They do not belong to it.
