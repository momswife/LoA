# The Thesis Wars

### _The Conflict That Made Reality Jurisdictional and Ended the Convergence Courts_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Conflict Antiquities Review  
**Supporting Offices:** Historical Reconstruction Bureau, Convergence Studies Commission, Hazardous Texts Review  
**Primary Compiler:** Archivist Emeritus Venn Tarras, Jurisdictional History and Linguistic Reconstruction  
**Contributing Scholars:** Archivist Yelna Stonevoice, Professor Arav Dhal-Senn, Magistrate Talar Shellmark  
**Current Revision:** 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle  
**Document Class:** _Civilizational Conflict Reconstruction / Grade-III Fragmentary Record_  
**Primary Evidence:** damaged Concordances, Court evacuation layers, altered genealogies, sealed Prism fragments, refugee copies, hostile Crown summaries, and residual jurisdictional anomalies  
**Historical Range:** Final generation of the [[The Convergence Courts|Convergence Courts]], before continuous Crown-era reckoning

---

## I. Purpose and Name

The **Thesis Wars** destroyed the political system of the Convergence Courts.

The name is misleading if it suggests scholars merely argued until their cities collapsed. The conflict included siege, assassination, famine, forced migration, archive destruction, magical coercion, and conventional battle.

Ideas mattered because Court authority could make an accepted interpretation physically operative.

The Courts had been built upon a demanding principle: no form of power could validate itself. Divine claim, inherited blood, spellcraft, memory, civic law, and material evidence had to converge across several jurisdictions before receiving full authority.

That principle restrained arbitrary power while the Courts continued recognizing one another.

The Thesis Wars began when rival Courts no longer agreed upon what counted as a valid judge.

Once each jurisdiction could declare the others conceptually unlawful, theory became a weapon system.

---

## II. The Twelve Theses of Unity

Near the height of the Convergence system, the Twelve Harmonists attempted to formalize the principles shared by every Court.

Their work became the [[The Twelve Theses of Unity|**Twelve Theses of Unity**]].

Fragments indicate that the Theses addressed:

- divine authority;
- the relation of soul and body;
- the limits of mortal consent;
- the origin of magical law;
- inheritance and identity;
- observation and truth;
- and the conditions under which one jurisdiction could bind another.

Eleven Theses survive through title, citation, or partial copy.

The Twelfth survives only through reaction.

Some texts call it the completion of mortal reason. Others call it an instruction by which creation could be made subject to proof. The [[The Prism Vault|central Prism Vault]] was sealed shortly after its presentation, and the Courts began withdrawing recognition from one another within a generation.

No surviving evidence proves that the Twelfth Thesis caused the war.

It may have exposed a contradiction the Courts had postponed: a system requiring convergence possessed no agreed authority for deciding what happened when convergence itself failed.

---

## III. Causes

The strongest reconstruction identifies five connected causes.

### Jurisdictional Expansion

Several Courts extended specialist authority beyond its earlier limits. Blood magistrates attempted to decide citizenship. Dream adjudicators claimed that intention outweighed public action. Flame jurisdictions treated destruction of unlawful matter as a legitimate form of correction.

### Control of the Concordances

Court rulings were preserved in documents called **Concordances**. Whoever controlled the accepted copy could define which earlier judgments remained binding. Rival copies began diverging before open war.

### The Authority of the Harmonists

The Harmonists could coordinate the Courts but possessed uncertain power to command them. Their attempt to formalize unity appeared to some as necessary preservation and to others as the creation of a concealed supreme court.

### Material Scarcity and Civic Rivalry

Court cities competed for Orichalcum mechanisms, trained witnesses, food, trade, and the specialists required to maintain increasingly complex systems. Philosophy did not replace material interest. It gave material interest a universal vocabulary.

### The Twelfth Thesis

Whatever the missing text claimed, several factions believed possession of it would decide which jurisdiction could lawfully interpret all others.

That belief was enough to make the sealed Vault a strategic target.

## IV. From Dispute to War

No reliable chronology divides the conflict into exact years. Surviving layers suggest an escalation through three broad stages.

### The Withdrawal of Recognition

Courts stopped accepting one another’s licenses, marriages, appeals, citizenship, and magical judgments. Travelers could cross a boundary and discover that their name, profession, household, or bodily status no longer possessed legal force.

### The War of Precedent

Factions seized archives, witnesses, and interpretive instruments. Concordances were stolen, altered, or destroyed. Courts issued retroactive judgments declaring rival acts invalid from their beginning.

### The Jurisdictional Sieges

Cities attempted to make their governing thesis physically supreme within contested territory. Conventional armies entered behind laws designed to decide in advance who counted as soldier, citizen, owner, witness, or living target.

The stages overlapped.

Several Courts remained formally at peace while conducting archival and conceptual attacks that left neighboring governments unable to function.

---

## V. Weapons of the Thesis Wars

Recorded weapons included:

- recursive summons that called altered versions of themselves;
- laws that changed who qualified as a citizen;
- thought-plagues that removed the ability to understand an opposing argument;
- prophetic loops that punished actions before they occurred;
- blood judgments that rewrote inheritance retroactively;
- flame formulae that destroyed objects classified as unlawful;
- dream incursions that replaced waking memory with a politically useful past;
- and silences within which no recognized testimony could be produced.

These descriptions combine technical record, refugee testimony, and later metaphor. Not every account should be interpreted literally.

Physical evidence confirms the underlying danger. Some ruins still recognize contradictory populations in the same room. Certain documents change legal subject when moved across an old Court boundary. Several family lines possess mutually exclusive but magically responsive genealogies.

The war did not merely destroy evidence.

It trained systems to enforce incompatible histories.

---

## VI. Archives as Battlefields

Archives became primary targets because Court law depended upon precedent.

A city deprived of records could not prove its jurisdiction. A household whose descent was erased could lose property, office, citizenship, and magical recognition. A witness removed from every accepted index might remain physically alive while becoming procedurally impossible.

Some Courts destroyed their own libraries rather than allow capture. Others created false archives so complete that later historians cannot identify which layer was intended to deceive an ancient enemy and which was later accepted as truth.

Ordinary clerks, translators, couriers, and household memory-keepers therefore became strategic actors. They hid copies, carried names across borders, preserved dissenting judgments, and sometimes decided which past a refugee community could still prove.

The modern archive inherits a warning from their choices:

Redundancy protects history.

It can also preserve several weaponized versions of it.

---

## VII. Civilian Experience

The conflict’s most common violence was loss of standing.

People found that:

- marriages ceased to exist at a border;
- children became unlawful descendants;
- craft licenses turned into evidence of sabotage;
- old debts became retroactively unpaid;
- burial rights vanished when the dead changed jurisdiction;
- food stores could not be distributed because no claimant qualified as owner;
- and refugees were rejected because their city had been declared conceptually false.

Famine followed the collapse of shared measures and transit law. Cities held food they could not lawfully exchange. Specialists were kidnapped or prevented from practicing outside the Court that licensed them.

Mixed Brethren–Beastfolk settlements were especially vulnerable where different systems of testimony had once required negotiated recognition. Several survived by abandoning Court adjudication and returning authority to household, route, and local witness.

The war’s survivors did not reject law.

They rejected law that required reality to become simpler than the people living within it.

---

## VIII. No Final Battle

The Thesis Wars ended without one accepted surrender.

The Convergence system ceased to function because no surviving Court recognized the legitimacy of any other. Long-distance appeals failed. Harmonists disappeared, died, fled, or lost the ability to assemble. Local Courts either dissolved, became isolated city governments, or placed themselves beneath rising monarchs.

Later Crown histories describe a final council in which the Harmonists voluntarily ended their own order. No reliable evidence supports the scene.

The story made the collapse legible to societies that believed every authority required a final sovereign decision.

The more likely ending was administrative exhaustion, material failure, and local survival.

Convergence did not lose one debate.

It lost the ability to convene it.

---

## IX. The Crowned Inheritance

Surviving adjudicators, spellwrights, genealogists, witnesses, and temple specialists entered the emerging [[The Crowned Realms|Crowned Realms]].

The new monarchies preserved Court methods while simplifying their restraints.

- licensed spellwrights became court mages;
- blood jurisdictions became royal genealogy offices;
- divine examination became coronation proof;
- witness systems became temple administration;
- and convergent authority became sovereign authority concentrated in one crown.

The early crowns promised an answer to jurisdictional war: where several truths conflicted, the ruler would decide which one governed.

That solution produced stability.

It also created the political conditions that later made the [[The War of Seven Crowns|War of Seven Crowns]] possible.

---

## X. Ministry Conclusions

1. The Thesis Wars were a material and military conflict as well as a philosophical one.
2. The missing Twelfth Thesis may have accelerated division, but no evidence proves it alone caused the war.
3. Withdrawal of reciprocal recognition preceded conventional battle.
4. Archives, witnesses, identities, and precedent were primary strategic targets.
5. Jurisdictional weapons created anomalies that remain dangerous to interpret.
6. Civilian harm most often appeared as loss of standing, movement, household recognition, and access to necessary systems.
7. No authenticated final council or decisive victory ended the conflict.
8. Crowned sovereignty emerged partly as a simplified answer to the failure of convergence.
