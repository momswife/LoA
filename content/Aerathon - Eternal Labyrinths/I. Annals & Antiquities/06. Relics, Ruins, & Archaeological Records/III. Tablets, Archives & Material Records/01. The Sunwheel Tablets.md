## **I. What the Name Describes**

The **Sunwheel Tablets** are several circular or radial arrangements of inscribed pieces recovered from buried terraces at Sunwound.

They are not one bound text.

Each assemblage contains stone, ceramic, metal, crystal, or mixed-material tablets fitted around an open center. Piece count varies. Several wheels were amended by inserting new wedges, reversing older pieces, or leaving deliberate gaps.

Early excavators treated them as calendars or solar prophecy.

Their astronomical content is real.

Their principal subject appears to be obligation: what work, warning, access, repair, migration, water release, burial protection, and resource sharing followed a seasonal condition.

The tablets record time as responsibility coming due.

---

## **II. Excavation Context**

Sunwheel assemblages occur in civic and infrastructural settings rather than royal tombs.

Known contexts include:

- a water-distribution hall;
- a terrace aligned to dawn and planting light;
- a workshop court;
- a mixed burial and route enclosure;
- and a damaged chamber connected to heat storage.

Tools, food remains, household marks, animal-route symbols, and repair materials occur beside them.

No evidence supports exclusive use by one priesthood.

Wear indicates that people stood, knelt, moved pieces, placed objects, and traced paths across the arrangements. A Sunwheel was read through action in space as well as inscription.

Removing a tablet preserves the piece while damaging the sentence made by its position.

---

## **III. Auric Line Behavior**

The tablets use [[The Orichalcum Civilizations#X. Auric Line and the Recording of Intent|Auric Line]], a script that records intent and relationship rather than speech alone.

Marks alter with:

- angle and sunlight;
- the reader’s identity;
- presence of related tools or water;
- active obligations in nearby structures;
- and the arrangement of the other tablets.

A single wedge can appear to concern heat when held alone, water when returned to one wheel, and witness when touched by a person recognized by the site.

Translation detached from context produces fluent error.

Several pieces famously shimmer when a lie is spoken nearby. The response also occurs when an accurate statement omits the person expected to bear its consequence.

The script reacts to contradiction in relationship, not merely false words.

---

## **IV. The Open Center**

Every sufficiently complete Sunwheel retains an open center.

Offerings, tools, seeds, water, ash, and name tokens were placed there. The center does not appear to represent the sun itself. It may represent the work or relationship currently under review.

In one agricultural wheel, placing grain in the center illuminates obligations for water release, pest control, storage, and route protection. Placing a burial token instead changes several marks toward access, memory, and seasonal flooding.

The same landscape supported food and the dead.

The wheel did not decide which mattered more.

It required both claims to become visible before alteration.

---

## **V. Seasonal and Astronomical Content**

The arrangements track solstice, equinox, sunrise angle, water height, animal movement, heat accumulation, and certain Weave changes.

They do not predict one fixed future.

Most signs are conditional:

- if light reaches this channel, open that gate;
- if water fails before this shadow, release reserve;
- if migration arrives during planting, preserve this route;
- if heat remains after the expected turn, inspect the buried line;
- if the dead are uncovered by flood, these households answer.

Later solar and Crown cultures extracted astronomical knowledge while often discarding reciprocal obligation.

They inherited the schedule and forgot who could challenge it.

---

## **VI. Copies and Reconstruction**

Convergence scholars made line-by-line and geometric copies of several wheels.

Most copies are less active because they lack original material, site relationship, or social recognition. Some compensate through Harmonium annotations that force one translation to remain stable.

Those copies are easier to read.

They are also less faithful to the source’s capacity for revision.

Modern diagrams preserve position without moving original pieces. Interactive replicas allow scholars and communities to test readings without activating the ancient assemblage.

No reconstruction is described as complete where a gap may have been intentional.

Absence can be part of the record.

---

## **VII. Activation Incidents**

Past accidents followed apparently correct translation.

In one case, opening a marked water channel activated an associated obligation to preserve downstream burial. The excavation team had removed the relevant mortuary tokens, so the structure treated its workers as the nearest responsible household.

In another, a copied heating instruction continued beyond the intended season because no authorized Hand of Renewal remained to close it.

These incidents demonstrate the danger of reading command without governance.

The tablets assume a social world capable of answering, revising, and ending their instructions.

Modern activation must supply an exit relationship as well as a technical procedure.

---

## **VIII. Ministry Conclusions**

1. The Sunwheel Tablets are several radial civic assemblages rather than one prophetic text.
2. Their original position and relation to tools, light, water, and participants form part of the inscription.
3. Auric Line records relational intent and can alter meaning when removed from context.
4. The open center allowed different works and obligations to be placed under review.
5. Astronomical observations were expressed as conditional civic duties rather than fixed prediction.
6. Later cultures often preserved schedules while narrowing reciprocal claims.
7. Convergence copies stabilize meaning at the cost of some original flexibility.
8. Missing pieces may represent deliberate revision or open obligation rather than loss.
9. Activation is unsafe without procedures for closing the social as well as magical instruction.
