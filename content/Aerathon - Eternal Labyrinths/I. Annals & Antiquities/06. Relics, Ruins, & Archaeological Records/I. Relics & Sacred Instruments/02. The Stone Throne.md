# **The Stone Throne**

### _The Immovable Dhalraeni Mechanism That Made Burden Feel Like Divine Judgment_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Antiquities Restoration Bureau  
**Supporting Offices:** Stone Throne Survey, Dwarven Compact Archive, Structural Breach Commission  
**Primary Compiler:** Professor Arav Dhal-Senn, Archaeometallurgy and Ancient Engineering  
**Contributing Scholars:** Curator Rook-of-Rings, Archivist Yelna Stonevoice, Magistrate Talar Shellmark  
**Current Revision:** 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle  
**Material Class:** _In-Situ Sacred Mechanism / Lithic-Metallic Civic Instrument_  
**Evidence Grade:** _Grade-I Existence and Crown-Era Use; Grade-II Origin and Function_  
**Primary Evidence:** in-situ stone, metallic conduits, thermal residues, Bearer impressions, chamber rebuilding phases, funerary armor, and Burden-Roll references  
**Historical Range:** Predates the traditional enthronement of [[Othran the Unbroken]] c. 118 A.D.; active and altered through Crown and Imperial rule

---

## **I. Object and Site**

The **Stone Throne** is an immovable seat of dark mountain stone threaded with metallic veins and joined to conduits descending beneath the oldest royal citadel of [[The Stone Throne of Dhalraen|Dhalraen]].

It is both object and architecture.

The visible seat cannot be separated from:

- the floor beneath it;
- the columns surrounding it;
- the thermal channels of [[The Stone Throne of Dhalraen#VI. The Trial of the Molten Seat|the Molten Seat Chamber]];
- resonance lines extending into the citadel;
- and deeper mechanisms not fully excavated.

Removing the throne would destroy much of the thing being removed.

For archival purposes, this record concerns the seat and its active structural connections. The chamber’s excavation history is recorded separately.

---

## **II. Material Composition**

The seat consists principally of dense black stone not quarried from any currently accessible seam in the surrounding mountain.

Its metallic veins include iron, nickel, Orichalcum traces, and phases that remain liquid or semi-liquid within sealed channels at temperatures that should not sustain them. The stone displays no ordinary mortar joints. It appears to have grown, fused, or been compressed around an earlier metallic frame.

Tool marks from several periods are present.

The deepest surfaces show shaping inconsistent with Crown-Era craft. Later Dwarven and Brethren interventions widened the seat, repaired cracks, rerouted channels, and fitted armor contact points.

The throne is not one untouched elder object.

It is an ancient mechanism made politically usable through generations of difficult maintenance.

---

## **III. The Othran Imprint**

Tradition holds that Othran sat for six days while the realm’s names, debts, failures, and burdens were recited. At the end of the trial, the metal cooled and the seat retained the shape of his body.

The oldest deep impression is real.

It does not match the full dimensions of later ceremonial armor and shows several modifications made to accommodate subsequent Bearers. Thermal alteration in the surrounding metal is consistent with prolonged heating. Othran’s attributed funerary armor carries burns compatible with contact against a patterned hot surface.

These findings support some form of ordeal.

They do not establish its duration, every participant, or the claim that the mountain selected him.

The impression became evidence because later rulers built succession around it.

Its existence does not explain its cause.

---

## **IV. The Weighting Effect**

When the chamber is activated, observers report:

- floor compression;
- groaning columns;
- increased pressure around the seated subject;
- illumination of metallic veins;
- and low vibration through the citadel fabric.

Court doctrine called this **the Weighting**: the realm placing its burdens upon the Bearer.

Modern survey indicates that the apparent increase in weight is distributed through the chamber. Columns, floor plates, and deeper anchors alter load in sequence. Some force acts directly upon the seat; some acts upon the surrounding structure and the bodies within it.

The effect varies with recitation, number of witnesses, condition of the conduits, and the subject’s resonance.

This may explain why the Burden Rolls were not merely ceremonial reading. Spoken obligations formed part of the mechanism’s input.

The throne did not weigh a ruler alone.

It weighted a relationship among ruler, witnesses, structure, and named responsibility.

---

## **V. The Hearing of Stone**

Bearer accounts claim that the seated ruler could feel cracks, strained bridges, damaged foundations, unstable tunnels, and failures within the mountain.

Evidence supports a narrower ability.

The throne is connected to a network of tuned stone, metal lines, and signal chambers throughout the oldest citadel. Controlled input at known points produces distinguishable vibration at the seat. A trained subject could plausibly interpret portions of the network.

Later royal histories extended this sensitivity across the entire kingdom.

No surviving mechanism supports universal range.

Engineers, road drums, clerks, and signal keepers likely supplied much of the information attributed to sacred perception. The throne gathered their work into one embodied display.

The miracle was partly real and socially miscredited.

---

## **VI. Imprints of Later Bearers**

The seat preserves physical depressions, changed mineral bands, heat shadows, and localized resonance associated with later reigns.

Not all recognized Bearers left a distinct mark.

Some rejected claimants did.

Court scholars explained inconsistency through hidden sin, sacred exception, or sabotage. Material analysis identifies more ordinary variables: armor composition, chamber setting, duration, maintenance, body shape, and prior damage.

At least two “ancestral voices” attributed to dead Bearers repeat language found in later court proclamations. These may represent induced resonance, institutional ventriloquism, genuine memory impressions, or a mixture.

No voice from the throne is accepted as independent legal testimony.

---

## **VII. Rebuilding and Control**

The throne complex was altered after nearly every major succession crisis.

Known interventions include:

- sealed side channels;
- replaced seating stone;
- new armor locks;
- rerouted heat;
- hidden relief valves;
- witness galleries narrowed to control access;
- and imperial Harmonium regulators added after 1100 A.D.

Royal histories described the rite as unchanged.

Its reliability increased as the ruling household gained control over the chamber’s engineering.

This does not mean every result was staged. It means divine interpretation cannot be separated from material administration.

A throne operated by technicians is not thereby fraudulent.

Erasing the technicians is.

---

## **VIII. Current Stability and Access**

The Stone Throne remains in situ.

Several lower channels are unstable, and uncontrolled activation could redirect heat or structural load into occupied levels. Seating trials are prohibited. Limited resonance surveys use remote plates and distributed sensors rather than a human subject.

The prohibition also protects against political theater.

No claimant may present survival of the throne as proof of office. No descendant may demand activation as an inherited right. No public authority may use the mechanism to test loyalty, endurance, or civic worth.

The object preserves the history of the Bearers.

It does not appoint their successors.

---

## **IX. Ministry Conclusions**

1. The Stone Throne is an in-situ mechanism inseparable from its chamber and deeper structural network.
2. Its deepest fabric predates Dhalraen, while Crown and imperial engineers altered it substantially.
3. Material evidence supports a severe early enthronement ordeal associated with Othran.
4. The Weighting is a real distributed physical and resonant effect influenced by spoken burden records.
5. The Hearing of Stone likely combined genuine network sensitivity with human reporting systems.
6. Bearer imprints are materially real but inconsistent and politically interpreted.
7. Repeated rebuilding made the rite more controllable than royal doctrine admitted.
8. The throne is unsafe for seating trials and possesses no standing authority to recognize modern sovereignty.
