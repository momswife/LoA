# **The Warbell of Korr**

### _The Forgefief Bell Cast from Both Armies so Emergency Power Had to Answer Its Makers_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Relics Registrar  
**Supporting Offices:** Archaeometallurgy Laboratory, Comparative Peoples Bureau, Cultural Custody Review  
**Primary Compiler:** Professor Arav Dhal-Senn, Archaeometallurgy and Ancient Engineering  
**Contributing Scholars:** Curator Rook-of-Rings, Researcher Falyn Onarra, Lieutenant Salvia Korrin  
**Current Revision:** 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle  
**Material Class:** _Civic Warning Instrument / Composite Battlefield Recast_  
**Evidence Grade:** _Grade-I Ancient Core and Inscription; Grade-III Post-Collapse Miracle Claims_  
**Primary Evidence:** bell fabric, slag signatures, Korr’s Pass battle strata, Crucible Oath copies, council records, repair bands, and later Ember March traditions  
**Historical Range:** Cast after the Battle of Korr’s Pass during the Age of Embers; repaired and ritually reused in later periods

---

## **I. Object Description**

The **Warbell of Korr** is a broad-shouldered iron-alloy bell cast from damaged weapons, armor, tools, fasteners, and siege metal recovered after the defense of Korr’s Pass.

Its surface is intentionally uneven.

Hammer scars, folded edges, rivet heads, and fragments of earlier inscriptions remain visible beneath the casting skin. Leonine charge marks appear beside Crown arsenal numbers. Hyenid pressure notches cross Dwarven repair signs. Several pieces derive from civilian carts and furnace fittings rather than weapons.

The bell does not present victory as pure metal.

It preserves the supply, labor, and destruction from which victory was made.

---

## **II. Korr’s Pass Provenance**

The [[Iron-Mane Forgefiefs]] defended Korr’s Pass against a Crown-aligned force seeking control of its furnace network.

Defenders used prepared slag channels, controlled structural failure, warning rhythms, and terrain to trap equipment while preserving retreat where possible. After the battle, material from both sides was gathered and recast.

Metallurgical comparison strongly supports the tradition.

The bell contains alloy families matching excavated defenders’ tools and attackers’ military fittings. Fire scale corresponds to the battle layer. The core casting predates later Forgefief fragmentation.

Not every piece can be assigned to one side.

That uncertainty is appropriate to the object’s purpose.

The bell was made so neither side’s metal could be remembered without the other’s cost.

---

## **III. Inscription and Purpose**

The principal inscription reads:

> _Ring before the forge becomes the reason for the war._

The sentence is both warning and procedure.

Ancient council records show that the bell could summon labor, household, bearer, neighbor, and military representatives before a captain converted emergency into unilateral command.

Its recognized rhythms distinguished:

- immediate attack;
- supply failure;
- forge accident;
- disputed mobilization;
- request for sanctuary;
- and council required before escalation.

The “warbell” did not merely call people to fight.

It called the people sustaining the fight to judge why it was being asked of them.

---

## **IV. Relationship to the Crucible Oaths**

The bell’s procedure reflects the [[The Crucible Oath Tablets|Crucible Oaths]], especially the duties to name intended use, keep cost visible, and preserve the right of the threatened to refuse a weapon.

Before major activation, a caller was expected to strike the bell once and state the proposed purpose before the full rhythm began. Witnesses could interrupt with a cross-strike if the purpose concealed seizure, private gain, or coercive supply.

Later commanders restricted access to the cross-striker’s platform.

Wear patterns confirm that the interruption plate was removed and reattached more than once.

The artifact preserved a restraint.

Political authority repeatedly tried to make the restraint ceremonial.

---

## **V. Acoustic and Magical Behavior**

The Warbell produces strong low-frequency vibration and layered overtones corresponding to several alloys within its body.

When struck after a declared purpose, nearby metal can respond sympathetically. Tools tremble. Cooling plates hum. Repaired armor sometimes reproduces the rhythm. The range increases where Forgefief road and furnace infrastructure remains intact.

Tradition says the bell refuses a false war.

Testing supports a less certain effect. Its tone becomes unstable when declared purpose conflicts with the expectations and intent of gathered witnesses. This may involve resonance, group magic, craft marks embedded in the alloy, or the bell’s retained relationship to the communities that cast it.

The bell detects division.

It does not decide which side is just.

---

## **VI. Repair and Later Use**

The ancient bell cracked along one shoulder before the first Forgefief confederacy ended.

Later repair bands contain Ember and post-Collapse metal. One band bears an inscription associated with the **Ember March**, when tradition claims the bell helped seal a Labyrinth rift.

The repair is authentic to the later period.

The miracle account remains partly unverified.

Some witnesses describe the bell stabilizing a formation through repeated purpose declarations. Others place the original far from the event and identify the instrument used as a ritual successor. Labyrinth recurrences of the bell appear in several accounts, further confusing provenance.

The Ministry distinguishes:

- the ancient core;
- later repairs to the same bell;
- successor warbells cast in its form;
- and Labyrinth copies.

---

## **VII. “Sounds When Aerathon Is in Peril”**

Popular tradition claims the Warbell sounds without a striker whenever Aerathon is in danger.

No controlled observation confirms a universal warning function.

The bell has sounded sympathetically during distant furnace failures, military mobilizations, and severe resonance events. Each involved connected metalwork, known infrastructure, or ritual imitation that could carry the response.

“Aerathon is in peril” is too broad to test.

The phrase survives because communities used the bell to define danger collectively rather than leave that definition to rulers.

Its political truth should not be confused with unlimited magical range.

---

## **VIII. Custody and Community Authority**

The bell remains under joint custodial practice rather than unrestricted Ministry possession.

Material examination requires representatives of craft, household, bearer, and neighboring traditions. This arrangement descends from the object’s purpose and prevents one institution from turning study into appropriation.

The Ministry documents fabric and risk.

It does not claim the right to ring the bell for public mobilization.

No modern military or guild may use the ancient artifact to imply that Korr’s defenders endorsed its campaign.

Historical custody is not a transferable war license.

---

## **IX. Ministry Conclusions**

1. The Warbell’s core was cast after Korr’s Pass from material belonging to attackers, defenders, and their shared supply landscape.
2. Its inscription and wear establish a civic procedure preceding military emergency authority.
3. The bell’s resonance can expose conflict between declared purpose and gathered intent.
4. It does not independently determine whether a war is just.
5. The interruption plate was historically restricted and restored, preserving conflict over accountability.
6. Later repair bands are genuine additions to the object’s continuing history.
7. The Ember March and universal-danger claims remain only partly verified.
8. Successor bells and Labyrinth recurrences must not be conflated with the ancient core.
9. Custody remains shared because the artifact’s meaning depends upon those whose labor war consumes.
