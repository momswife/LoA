## I. Purpose and Scope

This archive records the material remains through which Aerathon’s ancient history is tested.

It contains three principal classes of subject:

1. **Relics and instruments** — portable or bounded objects retaining active, symbolic, magical, legal, or technical function.
2. **Ruins and archaeological sites** — structures and landscapes whose layers preserve occupation, destruction, repair, reuse, or deliberate concealment.
3. **Archaeological records** — tablets, ledgers, inscriptions, registries, sealed testimony, and related assemblages whose physical form affects how their contents can be understood.

The section does not assume that survival establishes truth.

A crown may genuinely ring without recognizing justice. A throne may retain the imprint of a ruler without approving the ruler’s conduct. A registry may be authentic while every category within it was designed to harm.

Material evidence answers what existed, how it was made, where it was used, and what happened to it.

Interpretation remains a separate act.

## III. Relic, Artifact, Instrument, and Regalia

These terms are not interchangeable.

### Artifact

Any object shaped, altered, selected, or arranged through intentional action.

### Relic

An artifact or natural object retaining unusual sacred, magical, mnemonic, legal, or historical significance. A relic need not remain active.

### Instrument

An object whose function can be demonstrated through controlled operation or repeated contextual evidence.

### Regalia

An object used to signify or enact office. Regalia may also be a relic or instrument, but possession of it does not necessarily transmit the office.

Later owners frequently collapse these categories.

A royal claimant calls an object regalia because that makes custody resemble succession. A collector calls a dangerous mechanism an artwork because display law is less restrictive than breach law. A temple calls an engineered instrument a relic because sacred language conceals maintenance.

The archive classifies by evidence, not owner preference.

## IV. Ruins Are Layered Events

A ruin is not a civilization frozen at the moment of abandonment.

Most major sites contain several histories:

- original construction;
- repair and adaptation;
- conquest or political reuse;
- burial, sealing, or destruction;
- scavenging;
- later settlement;
- archaeological intervention;
- and, in some cases, Labyrinthal copying or recurrence.

The upper layer is not less historical because it is later.

Neither should it be allowed to overwrite everything beneath it.

The [[The Deepest Bell Tower|Deepest Bell Tower]] includes pre-Crown resonance work, Bell-Born ritual construction, imperial controls, post-Collapse sealing, and modern observation systems. Calling the whole complex “Maelis’s tower” preserves a powerful story while concealing most of the structure’s life.

Every site record therefore distinguishes original fabric, later alteration, modern intervention, and inferred reconstruction wherever evidence permits.

## V. Provenance and Custody

**Provenance** is the documented history of where an object came from, where it moved, and under whose control.

It is not the same as lawful or ethical ownership.

An imperial confiscation roll may establish that a ring entered a vault. It may also prove that the ring was stolen. A collector’s complete purchase record does not repair an earlier seizure. A Ministry accession number does not extinguish the claims of the community from which an artifact was removed.

Each record therefore distinguishes among:

- excavation context;
- chain of custody;
- legal title;
- cultural claim;
- ritual responsibility;
- physical control;
- and right of access.

These may belong to different parties.

Present custody is recorded only where it materially affects conservation, authenticity, or access. The modern institution itself remains filed in the Living Atlas.

## VI. Copies, Replicas, and Labyrinth Recurrences

Aerathon’s material record contains more than originals and forgeries.

The Ministry distinguishes:

1. **Original fabric** — material securely associated with the object’s documented ancient use.
2. **Period replacement** — repair or substitution made while the object remained historically active.
3. **Replica** — a later intentional copy.
4. **Forgery** — a copy presented as original to deceive.
5. **Ritual successor** — a new object lawfully or culturally treated as continuing a function.
6. **Labyrinth recurrence** — a materialized copy, variation, memory-body, or functional echo produced by a Labyrinth.

A recurrence may be older in apparent wear than the artifact it copies. It may remember an event no surviving source records. It may perform a function the original lost centuries ago.

None of those qualities makes it the original.

They make it a different kind of evidence.

## VII. Dating Material That Remembers

Ordinary archaeological dating assumes matter changes in one direction.

Orichalcum, active relics, preserved memory, divine residue, temporal disturbance, and Labyrinth recurrence complicate that assumption.

The Ministry compares:

- stratigraphic position;
- tool marks and manufacturing sequence;
- material decay;
- astronomical alignment;
- inscriptional form;
- associated organic remains;
- historical reference;
- resonance loss;
- and documented exposure to later magic.

No single method controls every case.

An object may contain ancient metal recast during the Crown Era, repaired under empire, buried during the Collapse, and copied by a Labyrinth in the present age. Assigning one date to the whole object would be false precision.

Records therefore date components, interventions, and contexts separately where possible.

---

## VIII. Human Remains and Bound Memory

Archaeology does not acquire unrestricted rights over the dead.

This is especially important where a site contains:

- ancestor walls;
- mortuary tablets;
- bound souls;
- conductor remains;
- preserved voices;
- names treated as living kin;
- or objects still participating in funerary obligation.

Excavation may constitute disturbance, testimony, rescue, or violation depending upon the people, material, and continuing practice involved.

No sentient memory is classified as a collectible artifact.

No person bound into a mechanism becomes equipment merely because the mechanism is ancient.

Where communities retain a documented relationship to remains or memory, consultation is part of evidence—not a courtesy performed after removal.

## IX. The Evidentiary Limits of Regalia

Ancient regalia attracts modern sovereignty claims.

The Ministry’s position is consistent:

- possession does not prove descent;
- descent does not prove institutional succession;
- activation does not prove moral fitness;
- ancient recognition does not automatically survive constitutional rupture;
- and an object cannot grant authority over people who have not consented to it.

This rule applies to the [[The First Bell-Crown|First Bell-Crown]], [[The Stone Throne]], [[The Solar Throne]], and every alleged object among [[The Seven Rings of Tharos]].

Material connection may be genuine.

Modern authority requires a modern basis.

## XII. Ministry Conclusions

1. Material survival establishes existence more readily than meaning.
2. Authentic objects can preserve false, coerced, or politically edited claims.
3. Ruins must be interpreted as layered histories rather than frozen moments.
4. Provenance, lawful title, cultural custody, and physical possession are distinct relationships.
5. Period repair, replica, forgery, ritual succession, and Labyrinth recurrence require separate classification.
6. Active or memory-bearing matter cannot always be dated as ordinary inert material.
7. Human remains and sentient memory retain ethical standing after excavation.
8. Regalia cannot independently establish modern sovereignty.
9. Present custodianship is recorded here only where it affects the material evidence.
10. Uncertainty is part of the record and must not be replaced by the interpretation most useful to a current claimant.
