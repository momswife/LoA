# Tharos Ravelmane, the Seven-Ringed King

### _The Beastfolk Sovereign Whose Kingdom Empire Attempted to Erase_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Historical Biography Desk  
**Supporting Offices:** Oral Memory Bureau, Seven-Ring Recovery Commission, Imperial Records Harm Review  
**Primary Compiler:** Researcher Falyn Onarra, Beastfolk Political Memory and Displaced Sovereignty  
**Contributing Scholars:** Historian Merrow Vey, Archivist Yelna Stonevoice, Curator Rook-of-Rings  
**Current Revision:** 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle  
**Document Class:** _Suppressed Royal Biography / Grade-II Hostile and Oral Composite_  
**Primary Evidence:** oath-songs, route memory, Crown military reports, forbidden shrine fragments, refugee genealogies, the Tharos Interdict, and Seven-Ring material traditions  
**Historical Range:** Ninth century A.D. through the Night of Open Gates in 927 A.D.; suppression continued throughout the [[The Age of Chains|Age of Chains]]

## I. Historical Position

**Tharos Ravelmane** was a Beastfolk sovereign who united multiple western kinships against Crown expansion during the ninth century A.D.

His realm is provisionally called the **Seven-Ring Kingdom** because its original common name has not been recovered with confidence.

Tharos matters beyond the territory he ruled.

His life disproved later claims that Beastfolk possessed no cities, law, diplomacy, mixed society, or political authority before Brethren recognition. Crown powers answered that contradiction by calling him a beast-lord. The Empire of Law and Light later made public memory of him a crime.

The record that survives is therefore divided between enemies required to describe the threat and communities required to conceal the reason he mattered.

---

## II. Name and Origin

No secure record establishes Tharos’s birth household, ancestry, or original personal name.

**Ravelmane** may be:

- a Leonine house name;
- an epithet meaning a mane divided into roads or vows;
- a later translation of an oath office;
- or a name reconstructed after imperial suppression.

Brethren sources assumed Leonine ancestry and frequently reduced his coalition to racial monarchy. Oral traditions name Leonine, Hyenid, Vulpine, Ursine, Avian, Chelonian, Bovine, and mixed communities among the people who raised or recognized him.

Some songs say he began as a road defender. Others make him a shrine keeper, liberated captive, exile from a Crown menagerie, or chosen speaker at a council unable to agree.

The Ministry treats every childhood narrative as unverified.

The multiplicity may preserve a political claim: no single kinship possessed the right to own the king’s beginning.

---

## III. The Western Crisis

Tharos rose while Crown-aligned settlements, hunting companies, fortified roads, and survey claims expanded into western Vinyot, the Fortunata Hills, and lower Betemere approaches.

Authorities described inhabited territory as wilderness where no recognized deed existed. Seasonal roads were blocked. Kinships were divided by new borders. Captives entered menageries, estates, military households, and labor systems.

Western communities did not lack resistance.

They lacked a shared procedure for answering an attack that crossed many forms of law.

Tharos’s central achievement was not erasing those differences. It was establishing a common defense that did not require every member to surrender its own kinship authority.

---

## IV. The Oath of Many Voices

The **Oath of Many Voices** bound participating kinships to answer a shared call while preserving local custom and ancestral duty.

Known provisions required:

- sanctuary for people fleeing capture;
- warning across route systems;
- shared defense of migration and water;
- return of rescued children and household records;
- limits upon the king’s authority inside local kinship law;
- and open council before permanent territorial change.

The Oath did not create equality automatically. Large kinships supplied more fighters and attempted to convert contribution into greater voice. Tharos’s officers sometimes overrode local refusal during war.

Even so, the coalition offered an alternative to Crown hierarchy: unity through callable obligation rather than one law declaring every member incomplete.

---

## V. The Seven Rings

Tharos bore [[The Seven Rings of Tharos|seven rings]] said to alter reality in their bearer’s favor.

Crown sources call them forbidden sorcery. Beastfolk sources describe covenants with powers of the Old Court. Later relic hunters assign each ring a neat domain—war, road, death, river, knowledge, shelter, or kingship—but the earliest songs are less consistent.

The rings may have been:

- seven separate relics;
- one office represented through several objects;
- covenants carried by different councilors;
- later symbols for the peoples or roads of the coalition;
- or a combination of material regalia and political metaphor.

No complete verified set is held by the Ministry.

The dedicated material classification belongs to Section 06. This record treats the rings as instruments of Tharos’s authority and as the image later governments tried to erase.

---

## VI. Rule and Refuge

Under Tharos, western communities raised fortified settlements, reopened ancestral roads, liberated captives, restored Old Court worship, and resisted the classification of inhabited land as empty.

The kingdom sheltered more than Beastfolk.

Its roads received Unplaced Brethren, hedge mages, dissident craftspeople, escaped servants, religious refugees, and people whose households crossed recognized categories. This mixed refuge is omitted from both Crown propaganda and some later purity-centered retellings.

Deeds attributed to Tharos include:

- destruction of the royal menageries at Caldran Vale;
- liberation along the western bell-road;
- defeat of several Crown expeditions;
- creation of protected migration corridors;
- and recognition of multiple evidence traditions in royal council.

Not every deed can be individually verified.

The pattern of Crown military loss and refugee movement supports a durable regional power rather than a short-lived bandit camp.

---

## VII. The Night of Open Gates

Tharos was not defeated in a conventional field campaign.

In **927 A.D.**, members of his alliance admitted Crown-supported enemies into the royal stronghold during an assembly remembered as the **Night of Open Gates**.

Motives included fear of endless war, promised autonomy, access to trade, resentment of royal command, and belief that accommodation might preserve individual territories.

No reliable account records Tharos’s final moments.

Traditions say the rings were removed one by one, that oath prevented him from striking his own people, or that he refused to preserve unity by slaughtering the communities whose consent had created it.

These endings make political meaning from uncertainty.

By dawn, the banners had fallen. Within a generation, the kingdom fractured into enclaves, refugee roads, aligned territories, and hidden communities.

---

## VIII. The War against His Memory

The Empire of Law and Light understood Tharos as a doctrinal threat.

In **1137 A.D.**, the **Tharos Interdict** prohibited his titles, seven-ring images, named songs, shrine worship, and claims that Beastfolk sovereignty had existed in western Vinyot.

Imperial records renamed him **Tharos the Feral**. The [[The Imperial Provincial Wars|Western Pacifications]] dismantled settlements, renamed roads, dispersed families associated with his councils, and converted royal sites into evidence of imperial rescue.

Suppression made his memory more portable.

Seven rings appeared as knots, cooking arrangements, children’s counting games, constellation names, tool marks, and pauses inside work songs.

The empire destroyed monuments.

It taught ordinary gestures to carry a king.

---

## IX. Claimants and Legacy

Later leaders claimed Tharos’s blood, rings, office, reincarnation, or unfinished mandate.

Few claims can be verified. Some preserved genuine refugee lineages. Some were political inventions. Several attempted to replace the Oath of Many Voices with personal monarchy while invoking the sovereign who had ruled through coalition.

The [[The Ravelmane Claimants and Seven-Ring Lineages|Ravelmane claimant traditions]] are therefore filed separately from this biography.

Tharos’s strongest legacy is not a proven descendant or recovered ring.

It is the historical fact that distinct peoples built common sovereignty without accepting the premise that one central culture had first to make them lawful.

---

## X. Ministry Conclusions

1. Tharos Ravelmane was a real ninth-century Beastfolk sovereign.
2. His original name, ancestry, and childhood remain uncertain.
3. The Seven-Ring Kingdom was a durable mixed political refuge, not merely a military band.
4. The Oath of Many Voices joined defense while preserving local kinship law.
5. The nature and present location of the seven rings remain unresolved.
6. The Night of Open Gates in 927 A.D. ended central rule through internal betrayal and Crown intervention.
7. Imperial suppression deliberately targeted evidence that Beastfolk sovereignty had existed.
8. Later descent and ring claims require independent evidence.
