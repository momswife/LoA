# The Registry of Souls

### _The Imperial Institution That Turned Identity into Assigned Purpose_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Historical Institutions Bureau  
**Supporting Offices:** Registry Harm Review, Imperial Records Commission, Comparative Peoples Bureau  
**Primary Compiler:** Archivist Yelna Stonevoice, Institutional Memory and Pre-Labyrinth Authority  
**Contributing Scholars:** Magistrate Talar Shellmark, Researcher Falyn Onarra, Historian Merrow Vey  
**Current Revision:** 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle  
**Document Class:** _Imperial Administrative Institution / Grade-I Abundant Harm Record_  
**Primary Evidence:** central and provincial rolls, Faith Density schedules, household files, correction orders, reassignment records, concealed duplicates, survivor testimony, and post-imperial audits  
**Historical Range:** Mandatory in core imperial provinces by 1214 A.D.; fragmented after the Fall of Sol Oranna in 2073 A.D.

---

## I. Institutional Character

The **Registry of Souls** was the Empire of Law and Light’s central system for recording every subject’s recognized identity and civic purpose.

It was not one book or building.

The Registry consisted of local clerks, temple records, provincial copies, examination stations, courier systems, central indexes, legal procedures, and later Faith Loom connections.

Its stated purpose was preservation. A registered person could inherit, marry, travel, work, worship, petition, receive rations, and be remembered through recognized law.

The same entry made identity available to assignment, surveillance, taxation, separation, and punishment.

The Registry protected people from disappearance by giving the state power to decide what version of them was allowed to remain.

---

## II. Institutional Origins

The Registry inherited methods from:

- Louvain petition and household ledgers;
- Vaelcour’s [[The Golden Leaves and Covered Mirrors|Golden Registry]];
- [[The Dhalraeni Burden Rolls|Dhalraeni Burden Rolls]];
- Faith Court miracle and lineage audits;
- the [[The Binding Concord|Binding Concord’s]] Faith Registries;
- and military and tax censuses across the High Thrones.

The Empire joined these systems beneath one classificatory purpose.

Faith Density testing began spreading beyond the capital around **1180 A.D.** By **1214 A.D.**, registration was mandatory throughout core provinces.

This chronology matters.

The Registry was not imposed upon a world without records. It absorbed local records while declaring itself the authority that made them legally real.

---

## III. What an Entry Contained

A mature entry could contain:

- recognized name and former names;
- ancestry and species classification;
- household and legal sponsor;
- Faith Density;
- occupation and assigned civic purpose;
- marriage eligibility;
- magical aptitude and license;
- devotional history;
- travel authority;
- inherited duty;
- correction history;
- and the names of people for whom the subject was responsible.

The record changed through life.

Change did not always follow the person.

When identity and entry disagreed, officials often treated the person as the error.

---

## IV. Faith Density

Faith Density classified response to calibrated relics, prayer fields, Harmonium tones, and sacred substances.

Imperial categories included **Radiant**, **Kindled**, **Steady**, **Dim**, **Unharmonious**, and **Unnumbered**.

The categories affected office, magic, work, property, marriage, movement, and exposure to supervision.

They were presented as objective spiritual measurement.

Testing varied by province, species, examiner, health, relic, and political need. Wealthy households bribed officials. Enemies were retested until they failed. Beastfolk were examined with devices calibrated to Brethren physiology and punished for producing responses the system called incorrect.

Faith Density did not merely mismeasure people.

It converted the system’s bias into a property assigned to the soul.

---

## V. Making the Record True

The Registry’s most consequential procedure was **correction**.

Where a person, household, or community did not fit the entry required by imperial order:

- names were changed;
- households were divided;
- children were reassigned;
- marriages were invalidated;
- professions were imposed;
- routes were restricted;
- and communities were relocated.

Officials described these actions as bringing life into alignment with truth.

The process reversed the proper relation between record and subject.

Instead of correcting the archive when evidence changed, the institution corrected people until the archive appeared accurate.

---

## VI. Benefits and Dependence

Registration carried real protections.

An entry could prevent a landlord from denying a tenancy, establish a pension, preserve a missing person’s name, identify a child after displacement, or allow appeal across provinces. During famine, registry lists directed food and medical aid.

These benefits made refusal costly.

The empire tied ordinary survival to the same institution that assigned labor and measured conformity. A person resisting spiritual examination could lose travel, food, work, household recognition, and legal standing at once.

Dependency was not an accidental flaw.

It was how a record became universal without universal consent.

---

## VII. Resistance inside the Registry

Resistance included:

- false and duplicate entries;
- preserved former names;
- invented households sheltering unrelated people;
- clerks lowering Faith Density risk;
- delayed death notices;
- route records copied before destruction;
- and blank spaces placed where compulsory information should have appeared.

The **Unwritten Names** refused central entry. The Quiet Clergy altered records while remaining inside imperial office. Beastfolk communities maintained Shell-Ledgers, Stone-Song, kinship witnesses, and distributed memory against seizure.

Registry workers were not politically uniform.

Some built the machinery of persecution. Some used its blind spots to keep people alive. Many did both at different times.

---

## VIII. Registry, Loom, and War

As the Faith Loom expanded, registry identity became increasingly connected to magical access and enforcement.

Local systems could recognize authorized movement, trigger curfew responses, direct prayer obligation, or support command over Collared Legions and assigned labor.

This made the Registry a target during [[The Echo-Wars]].

Rebels stole wanted lists, opened labor records, severed hereditary claims, broadcast erased names, and destroyed entries tying people to coercive purpose. Indiscriminate burning also erased pensions, family evidence, medical history, and proof needed by survivors.

Liberation required deciding what to destroy, what to copy, and who controlled the surviving copy.

---

## IX. Fragmentation after Sol Oranna

The [[The Fall of Sol Oranna and the First Withholding|Fall of Sol Oranna]] shattered central coordination in **2073 A.D.**

Provincial registries continued operating. Some successor governments preserved imperial categories. Others opened or destroyed archives. Communities reclaimed local records while confronting the fact that generations of legal life had been forced into imperial form.

No single moment ended the Registry.

Its habits survived in guild membership, civic census, magical licensing, inheritance law, and modern bureaucracy.

Population records are not therefore imperial by nature.

They become imperial when the institution treats legibility to itself as the condition of personhood.

---

## X. Ministry Conclusions

1. The Registry of Souls was a distributed institution, not one central book.
2. It absorbed older local record systems and claimed authority over their legal meaning.
3. Mandatory core-province registration was established by 1214 A.D.
4. Faith Density embedded unstable and biased measurement into civic identity.
5. Registry correction often altered people and communities rather than correcting records.
6. Registration offered genuine protection while making survival dependent upon compliance.
7. Internal workers and external communities developed multiple forms of resistance.
8. The Registry fragmented after Sol Oranna but its administrative habits survived.
