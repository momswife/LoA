# **The Living Conductor Lines**

### _The Households Made Hereditary Components of the Imperial Faith Loom_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Annals & Antiquities  
**Responsible Office:** Registry Harm Review  
**Supporting Offices:** Historical Institutions Bureau, Divine Residue Analysis Office, Echo-War Testimony Commission  
**Primary Compiler:** Magistrate Talar Shellmark, Imperial Law and Inherited Obligation  
**Contributing Scholars:** Archivist Yelna Stonevoice, Researcher Falyn Onarra, Professor Arav Dhal-Senn, Priest-Analyst Thera Daan  
**Current Revision:** 3388 A.D., Age of Spectacle  
**Document Class:** _Coerced Inherited Office / Grade-I Administrative and Material Harm Record_  
**Primary Evidence:** hereditary duty registers, Registry household files, Cathedral maintenance schedules, medical logs, replacement orders, conductor remains, concealed family testimony, and Echo-War liberation records  
**Historical Range:** From hereditary sacred duties formalized by [[The Binding Concord]] in 1091 A.D. through the fall and fragmentation of the Faith Loom after 2073 A.D.

---

## **I. Lineage Character**

The **Living Conductor Lines** were households whose members were made responsible for carrying, regulating, or stabilizing sacred energy within the Empire of Law and Light.

They are filed as lineages because imperial law joined ancestry, bodily aptitude, training, and inherited obligation across generations.

They were not one people, house, profession, or voluntary order.

Some families entered sacred maintenance through respected temple service. Some accepted dangerous work in exchange for food, protection, legal status, or release from another assignment. Some were transferred after conquest. Some children were taken because a Registry test identified useful resonance.

Over time, the empire treated continuation as consent.

A duty accepted—or imposed—upon one generation became evidence that the next had been born for it.

---

## **II. Before the Faith Loom**

Living sacred conductors existed before the imperial network.

Bell-tenders in Louvain, solar attendants in Vaelcour, deep listeners in Dhalraen, Orichalcum ritual specialists, Beastfolk singers, healers, mourners, and relic keepers all entered dangerous relationships with magical systems.

These practices differed greatly.

Some were honored offices with meaningful choice and communal review. Some were restricted by kinship or caste. Others already involved coercion. None should be treated as innocent merely because it was local.

The Binding Concord of **1091 A.D.** standardized households associated with sacred function as inheritable duty categories.

The later empire did not invent every form of bodily service.

It joined them into one administrative supply system.

---

## **III. From Household Duty to Conductor Line**

Early imperial registers list conductors beside bell-tenders, vessels, keepers, mourners, choir families, and relic attendants.

As the [[The Age of Chains|Faith Loom]] expanded after c. **1460 A.D.**, conductor became a more specialized classification. Provincial examination stations tested response to Harmonium tones, relic proximity, repeated prayer, pain, heat, sleep deprivation, and controlled magical discharge.

People showing desirable response could be:

- licensed for local service;
- attached to a temple or relay;
- reassigned to a central facility;
- incorporated into an existing conductor household;
- or entered as the founder of a new line.

The [[The Registry of Souls|Registry of Souls]] then attached the assignment to spouse, child, household sponsor, and future descendants.

The line was administrative before it was genealogical.

Once written, the category taught later officials which bodies to test.

---

## **IV. Many Peoples, Unequal Categories**

Conductor lines included Brethren, Beastfolk, mixed households, and people whose own identities imperial categories did not preserve.

They were not treated equally.

High-status Brethren families could describe the work as sacred stewardship and often secured safer ceremonial roles. Poor or politically suspect Brethren were assigned to maintenance chambers and replacement pools. Beastfolk were frequently classified as naturally resonant but spiritually unformed, a contradiction that allowed the empire to use their bodies while denying their authority over the songs and practices being used.

Dwarven deep resonance, feline and lupine chorus traditions, bovine memory rhythm, avian storm sensitivity, and many local disciplines were extracted from their social context and turned into technical properties.

Imperial doctrine called this proof that every people possessed a lawful purpose.

The purpose was assigned by the institution benefiting from it.

---

## **V. Pairing and Household Management**

Registry offices tracked resonance as though it were a stable inheritable substance.

Marriage permission could be granted, denied, or redirected according to predicted conductor yield. Children were tested repeatedly. Siblings producing different results were assigned different status. People with useful responses were transferred between households so that the Registry could preserve a line even when no recognized family relationship had existed before.

Official records describe these acts as **continuity placements**.

Survivor testimony describes:

- coerced marriage;
- separation of partners;
- seizure or reassignment of children;
- forced adoption;
- reproductive punishment;
- and threats to remove household protections if a tested person refused service.

Not every conductor marriage was coerced, and families formed real attachments within assigned households.

That reality does not legitimize the structure that constrained their choices.

Love can grow inside an imposed condition without making the condition voluntary.

---

## **VI. Work within the Loom**

Living conductors performed several functions.

They could:

- receive and stabilize incoming prayer resonance;
- translate incompatible ritual systems into Harmonium patterns;
- absorb excess discharge;
- maintain synchronization between distant relays;
- identify a failing relic or pylon by bodily response;
- carry commands when mechanical connections broke;
- and serve as living safeguards against magical inversion.

Some positions involved short supervised sessions. Others required long-term binding into frames, chambers, choirs, baths, or conductive architecture.

The most dangerous central assignments treated a person less as an operator than as a replaceable component.

Maintenance schedules recorded fatigue, scarring, memory loss, sensory alteration, involuntary prayer, infertility, resonance sickness, and death. The clinical language rarely preserved the conductor’s own account.

When a body failed, the schedule named the next body required.

---

## **VII. Honor, Privilege, and Captivity**

Imperial propaganda called conductors **Honored Vessels of Common Light**.

Some received housing, food, education, medical care, family stipends, and legal protections unavailable to ordinary imperial subjects. Senior ceremonial lines could advise temple officials and command lower maintenance staff.

These privileges were real.

They were also conditional upon usefulness and compliance.

A family that concealed an eligible child might lose rations or household recognition. A conductor whose resonance changed could be demoted from honored vessel to defective dependent. Retirement was often transfer into training, observation, reproduction management, or permanent temple residence.

Privilege divided the lines.

Families in safer roles sometimes defended the system that endangered provincial and low-status conductors. Others used their access to hide names, falsify readings, and redirect assignments.

Captivity did not produce one politics.

---

## **VIII. Family Resistance**

Conductor households developed methods of survival long before open rebellion.

They altered diets before tests, exchanged identity markers, taught children to suppress or imitate particular responses, created false infertility records, bribed examiners, arranged unregistered fostering, and preserved birth names outside Registry rolls.

The Quiet Clergy and sympathetic clerks created duplicate entries or marked dangerous aptitude as medically unstable. Beastfolk communities used mobile kinship and oral witness to move children beyond fixed household registration. Some Dwarven enclaves embedded warnings in work songs that taught a conductor how to recognize overload before an imperial instrument did.

Resistance could shift danger.

A falsified exemption for one child sometimes caused another to be selected. A disrupted relay could free a bound conductor while overloading people connected elsewhere.

The moral record must preserve both courage and consequence.

---

## **IX. The Echo-Wars**

During [[The Echo-Wars]], living conductors became participants, objectives, and unwilling terrain.

Resistance cells sought to:

- remove hereditary service claims from Registry copies;
- open conductor chambers;
- teach safe interruption of Harmonium signals;
- recover household names concealed by maintenance numbers;
- and prevent imperial authorities from drawing emergency power through provincial bodies.

Some conductors joined directly, using intimate knowledge of the Loom to route messages or free Collared Legions. Others refused rebel plans that would destroy systems keeping their families or regions alive. Some were never given a choice by either side.

Rebel factions disagreed over whether liberation required preserving the Loom, localizing it, or destroying it.

Those disagreements converged violently at Sol Oranna.

---

## **X. Sol Oranna and the Seventh Circle**

[[The Cathedral of Harmonium|The Cathedral of Harmonium]] contained living conductors bound to the Faith Loom’s central operation when the capital fell in **2073 A.D.**

Imperial authorities ordered maximum devotional draw while rebel choirs introduced destabilizing resonance. Some cells intended to destroy the Cathedral. Others entered principally to free its conductors. Maintenance and liberation routes crossed inside an overburdened system.

The number of conductors present, rescued, killed, or transformed is unknown.

Mutually hostile accounts describe a **Seventh Circle** leaving the city before the Inversion.

The phrase may describe a group of conductors, residents of the capital’s Seventh Circle, dissident clergy, divine fragments, or a rebel code for evacuation. No account presently proves that a conductor community escaped intact.

The Ministry does not classify the Seventh Circle as confirmed.

Hope is not evidence, even where institutions once used uncertainty to erase hope entirely.

---

## **XI. After the Loom**

The Fall of Sol Oranna shattered central command but did not release every line at once.

Peripheral relays continued incomplete routines. Successor governments attempted to restart systems. Communities depended upon conductor knowledge for weather, healing, walls, food, and communication even while rejecting imperial assignment.

Former conductors became engineers, witnesses, healers, saboteurs, teachers, political leaders, and people who wanted no further public relationship to the work.

Some communities preserved technical practice under voluntary and reviewable terms. Others retained hereditary expectation because abandoning it threatened survival. A few successor states simply changed the titles while keeping assigned households.

Freedom after infrastructure collapse was not a single legal declaration.

It required rebuilding the systems that had made captivity materially necessary.

---

## **XII. Descendants, Privacy, and Modern Research**

The Ministry does not publish complete reconstructed genealogies of living conductor descent.

Such records could expose modern people to political claim, relic trafficking, involuntary testing, cult recruitment, medical exploitation, or attempts to reactivate ancient mechanisms.

Known descent may carry historical, medical, magical, or cultural significance.

It does not create an inherited duty to serve as a conductor.

Researchers must distinguish among:

- descent from a person assigned to conductor work;
- continuation of voluntarily transmitted technical knowledge;
- a modern resonance trait;
- custody of conductor testimony;
- and institutional claims to own that descent.

The first four may deserve protection and study.

The fifth has no legitimacy.

---

## **XIII. Ministry Conclusions**

1. Living conductors existed before empire, but imperial law joined diverse practices into a hereditary supply system.
2. The Binding Concord made sacred household duties inheritable; the Registry later expanded and enforced those assignments.
3. Conductor lines included many peoples whose traditions were extracted and unequally classified.
4. Registry pairing, adoption, and child reassignment made administrative categories appear genealogically natural.
5. Some conductor offices carried real honor and protection, but those benefits remained conditional upon usefulness.
6. Central Loom work caused documented physical, cognitive, reproductive, and spiritual harm.
7. Conductor families and allied officials practiced resistance before and during the Echo-Wars.
8. Rebel efforts to free conductors sometimes created dangerous consequences elsewhere in the network.
9. The fate of the Cathedral’s conductors and the meaning of the Seventh Circle remain unresolved.
10. Modern descent from a conductor line creates no inherited obligation to ancient machinery or institutions.
