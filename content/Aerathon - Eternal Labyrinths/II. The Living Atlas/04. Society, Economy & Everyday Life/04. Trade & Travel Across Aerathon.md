# Trade & Travel Across Aerathon

_Every route is an agreement between distance, weather, infrastructure, and trust._

**Filed Division:** Office of Civil Life, Trade, and Infrastructure  
**Current Revision:** Late Redfall, 3388 A.D.  
**Classification:** Public Reference

---

## I. A Continent in Circulation

No Greater Region is fully self-sufficient, and none trades in only one direction. Food, timber, ore, medicine, glass, textiles, spell-paper, machinery, relic components, performers, teachers, sailors, scholars, and news all move through overlapping networks.

Aerathon's routes include ordinary roads, caravan tracks, rivers, canals, sea lanes, causeways, mountain passages, air routes, rail lines, scry relays, and temporary paths made safe by local agreement. The quickest route on a map may not be open, affordable, accessible, or lawful.

Travel is therefore a profession as well as an act. Porters, pilots, carters, drovers, route readers, mechanics, ferrymasters, guards, clerks, interpreters, innkeepers, healers, and weather watchers make movement possible.

## II. Regional Exchange

### Allemance

Allemance exports grain, ale, preserved foods, textiles, artisan goods, education, training, and certification. It imports industrial materials, reagents, cultural goods, and relic components. Its roads and settled markets make it an important provisioning region, while rivers and urban institutions connect rural production to Louvain's international reach.

### Al'Ar

Al'Ar lives by circulation across islands, ports, and performance routes. It exports windcraft, maritime expertise, volcanic glass, elemental reagents, performers, and cultural movements while importing food, timber, metals, textiles, and instruments. Passage may depend as much on season, crew reputation, and local witness as on posted fare.

### Arneria

Arneria exports sacred metals and glass, spices, alchemical components, and faith-bound relics. It imports grain, timber, labor, and administrative expertise. The great Causeway, caravan roads, pilgrimage paths, and water routes concentrate movement along corridors where tolls, sanctuary, temple law, and security matter.

### Oria

Oria exports metal, machinery, infrastructure, medical expertise, alchemical treatment, and northern escort services. It imports food, luxuries, reagents, and other supplies difficult to secure in its climate. Trade is negotiated House by House, and winter can transform a commercial delay into a survival emergency.

### Vinyot

Vinyot's coast, canals, delta, factories, and financial houses move a great share of Aerathon's liquid wealth. It exports processed relic components, textiles, glasswork, spell-paper, finance, and arbitration while importing raw materials, food, labor, and salvage. Its strength is also a vulnerability: a closed lock, strike, breach, or failed contract can disturb markets far beyond the affected canal.

## III. Ways of Traveling

### Road and Caravan

Road travel carries small settlements that no rail, canal, or scryline reaches. Caravans pool animals, vehicles, guards, food, tools, local knowledge, and witnesses. They are commercial systems and temporary communities. A caravan's pace is set by its slowest necessary member, not its most impatient passenger.

### River and Canal

Waterways move bulk goods efficiently and connect farms, workshops, ports, and dense cities. Vinyot's canal economy is the most famous, but water transport matters wherever depth, season, locks, current, and safe banks permit it. Flood, drought, contamination, or political closure can divide a city as effectively as a wall.

### Sea and Air

Sea routes bind Al'Ar and the continental coasts. Windcraft and skilled crews can shorten distance without making storms harmless. Air travel exists through flyers, specialized craft, and mapped aerial routes, but it is not a universal replacement for roads or ships. Cargo, weather, fatigue, landing space, and the needs of nonflying passengers all limit it.

### Rail and Mechanized Transit

Industrial centers in Vinyot—notably Malarthain—and Oric engineering hubs maintain rail, lifts, engines, and heavy transport. These systems can move people and material at scales older roads cannot match. They also require fuel, maintenance, coordinated schedules, safe stations, and workers who can stop a damaged machine before speed becomes disaster.

## IV. Documents, Tolls, and Borders

Travelers may encounter company tickets, House permissions, caravan rosters, guild seals, cargo declarations, relic reports, temple restrictions, local tolls, quarantine orders, and border inspections. The documents that satisfy one authority may mean little to the next.

Relics and magical materials receive the strictest scrutiny. An ordinary trade invoice does not replace containment records or proof of lawful custody. Travelers carrying unknown Labyrinth material risk confiscation, quarantine, or harm to everyone sharing the route.

Identity records are useful but imperfect. A person whose body, name, language, or Folk classification differs from an official's expectation should not be treated as fraudulent for that reason alone.

## V. Season, Hazard, and Delay

Late Redfall marks the end of summer in 3388 A.D. Routes are still active, but many communities are already measuring winter stores and deciding which journeys cannot wait.

Storms, ice, flood, wildfire, unrest, labor action, monster movement, Labyrinth breach, magical interference, and damaged infrastructure can all close a route. Scry alerts may spread warning quickly along connected systems, yet a message reaching a public display does not put food on the delayed cart or build a ramp around a collapsed stair.

Travel advice should therefore include contingencies: extra provisions, rest days, alternate crossings, accessible lodging, medical needs, communication limits, and a plan for refusing unsafe onward passage. A timetable is a promise made under expected conditions—not a law imposed upon the road.
