## I. Lineage Overview

Bovines are horned Beastfolk descended from great-bodied herd kinships, including Angus, Bison, and Yak peoples.

They are widely known for physical strength, deliberate movement, and an inherited sensitivity to plant life. Those qualities have made Bovines indispensable as builders, growers, wardens, porters, soldiers, and delvers.

They have also produced one of Aerathon’s most persistent forms of prejudice:

> If a Bovine can carry the burden, the burden must belong to them.

Bovine history is full of labor freely offered and labor quietly coerced. Crown armies treated Bovine strength as military property. Estate systems praised their “natural patience” while denying them the right to leave. Modern employers still mistake endurance for consent and composure for agreement.

Bovine cultures therefore tend to ask difficult questions about strength:

- When does protection become control?
- When does patience become permission for mistreatment?
- What does a strong person owe a community that values only their usefulness?
- Is a promise still honorable when it was made under pressure?
- Who is allowed to rest?

No answer belongs to the entire lineage.

The question does.

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

The oldest Bovine traditions place their first ancestors beside newly rooted fields in the Land Before Footprints.

In one common account, the Old Court did not ask them to till the earth. It asked them to listen before breaking it.

The first Bovine knelt, pressed one hand into the soil, and learned that every field contains more lives than the crop planted upon it. Roots crossed beneath property lines. Water moved without permission. Seed belonged partly to the one who saved it, partly to the one who planted it, and partly to those not yet born who would depend upon the harvest.

This teaching survives in the phrase **root connected**.

To be root connected does not simply mean loving plants. It means recognizing consequence beneath the visible surface.

Scattered Bovine records describe upland steadings where horned households maintained farms, road fortifications, refuge halls, and exchanges with neighboring forges. No surviving evidence supports treating these communities as one named civilization.

What the fragments do show is that strength and cultivation were not separate duties. A wall protected the field. A plow shaped soil whose water and timber also sustained nearby craft. Authority followed the ability to keep those relationships in balance.

Later Crown systems preserved the labor and discarded the balance.

During the Age of Chains, Bovine communities were disproportionately marked for agricultural service, siege work, quarry labor, and military transport. The later [[The Treaty of Breath & Beast|Treaty of Breath & Beast]] gave participating Ember-Era communities stronger grounds to challenge inherited status and assert kinship or territorial claims, although employers and local patrons still controlled enforcement in many jurisdictions.

Some Bovine families preserve an ancestor’s Mark of Breath beside a broken yoke.

The display is not subtle.

## III. Body and Horned Form

Bovines are commonly broad-framed, dense, and heavier than other humanoids of comparable height.

Recognized features include:

- Horns of varied shape and length
- Hooved or partially hooved feet
- Short coats, long coats, or dense highland fur
- Heavy shoulders and powerful necks
- Tails with expressive movement
- Broad muzzles and strong dentition
- Exceptional carrying and pushing capacity

Most Bovines are Medium by delver standards, though their weight and equipment requirements may resemble those of larger people.

### Horns

Horns are living parts of the body.

They may be polished, painted, wrapped, capped, carved after natural damage, or left unadorned. Decoration can indicate household, profession, mourning, marriage, political allegiance, or simple taste.

Touching another person’s horns without permission is invasive. Grabbing them is assault.

Some Bovines remove damaged horns for medical reasons. Others are born without horns or experience irregular growth. None of these conditions diminishes lineage standing.

### Hooves and Built Space

Loose stairs, polished floors, narrow ladders, and badly designed vehicle decks pose predictable dangers to hoofed citizens.

Bovine accessibility work has influenced public building codes throughout Aerathon, particularly requirements concerning:

- Load-bearing floors
- Ramp width
- Stair depth
- Rest platforms
- Non-slip surfaces
- Harness-free cargo handling
- Door and furniture clearance

These changes benefit many peoples. They were often dismissed as “Bovine accommodations” until other citizens began using them.

### Plant Memory

Most Bovines possess a limited magical ability to communicate with plants.

The resulting impressions are sensory and local rather than conversational in the ordinary sense. A tree may remember repeated footfall, fire, damaged roots, unusual cold, or the presence of many bodies. It rarely provides names, motives, or exact speech.

Bovine traditions treat this gift differently.

Some call it the Old Court’s first lesson. Some regard it as an inherited natural faculty without religious meaning. Urban Bovines may practice with courtyard trees, market herbs, canal moss, rooftop gardens, or weeds growing through stone.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Bovine communities may be organized as households, steadings, horn circles, work families, migratory companies, temple herds, neighborhood kitchens, or professional associations.

The word **herd** is used proudly in some communities and rejected in others.

Traditional kinship often emphasizes **load-sharing**.

At the beginning of a difficult undertaking, participants name what they can carry and what they cannot. A person may offer labor, coin, knowledge, land, shelter, childcare, political standing, or simple presence. The practice is intended to prevent one visibly strong member from receiving every unspoken burden.

When used honestly, load-sharing makes Bovine communities remarkably resilient.

When used poorly, it becomes a public performance in which people conceal exhaustion to avoid shame.

### The Unyoking

Many communities recognize an **Unyoking**, a formal declaration that an inherited obligation has become harmful, impossible, or unjust.

The speaker names:

- The promise
- The person or household who first carried it
- The good it once served
- The harm caused by continuing it
- What responsibility, if any, should replace it

Unyoking is not treated as casual abandonment. It exists because Bovine cultures understand that endurance without judgment can preserve cruelty.

Conservative households may permit Unyoking only before elders. Younger communities often perform it privately or with chosen kin.

### Affection and Conflict

Bovine affection is no more uniform than any other people’s.

Common practices include shared meals, mutual grooming, horn decoration, repair work, sitting in companionable silence, and giving tools whose weight or balance was selected for the recipient.

Arguments may develop slowly and conclude abruptly.

Outsiders sometimes interpret delayed response as lack of feeling. In reality, many Bovines prefer to decide whether a conflict matters before committing their full attention to it.

Once they do, the conversation may be exceptionally direct.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Bovine communities commonly admire:

- Reliability
- Patience
- Stewardship
- Honest effort
- Protection without spectacle
- Deliberation before commitment
- Keeping one’s word
- Knowing when a burden must be shared

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Patience | Making room for thought and change | Enduring mistreatment too long |
| Strength | Protecting and supporting others | Believing worth depends upon usefulness |
| Reliability | Becoming someone others can trust | Refusing rest or help |
| Deliberation | Acting with full commitment | Inflexibility after a decision |
| Stewardship | Caring for land and community | Controlling what others may do |
| Loyalty | Remaining through hardship | Staying where departure is necessary |

A recurring Bovine fear is being loved only as an anchor.

This can produce several responses. Some become indispensable. Some reject every request before it can become an expectation. Some cultivate gentleness so deliberately that anger frightens them. Others embrace anger because it is the only force that has ever made people step back.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Bovine communities debate:

- Whether plant-speaking establishes sacred responsibility or merely useful awareness
- Whether the Iron-Mane traditions should be restored
- Whether ceremonial yokes can be reclaimed from the Age of Chains
- Whether strength-based professions perpetuate stereotype or preserve valued craft
- Whether inherited land should remain collectively held
- Whether Unyoking protects the living or excuses failed duty
- How Bovine children should be taught anger

The line between endurance and submission remains one of the lineage’s defining political questions.

## VI. Bovine Inheritance in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Bovines are prominent in farming communities, road wards, brewing houses, local courts, kitchens, bridge crews, and volunteer watches.

The Hearthlands value the person who returns when promised. Bovine reliability is admired, but can become communal entitlement: everyone knows who will repair the roof, pull the wagon, settle the quarrel, or stay late.

Many Allemancian horn circles now maintain rotating rest seasons so duty does not accumulate around the same dependable people.

### Oria

Oric Bovines work throughout House forges, rescue crews, freight routes, cold-weather agriculture, structural testing, and heavy artificing.

Oria’s respect for correct function aligns closely with Bovine stewardship. It also produces harsh judgments when injury, age, or disability changes what a person can do.

Progressive Houses have begun recording **continuity work**—teaching, inspection, planning, and memory—as labor equal to physical production.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Bovines are found in port security, entertainment, construction, finance, agriculture, transport, private protection, and organized sport.

Their physical presence is profitable. That fact shapes contract negotiations.

Bovine labor societies have become influential in the League of Coin, particularly in disputes involving dangerous cargo, magically increased loads, fight venues, and employers who classify injury as voluntary “lineage risk.”

### Arneria

Arnerian Bovines maintain oasis routes, temple farms, caravan wards, pilgrimage roads, and highland settlements.

Ritual commitment is taken seriously. Some Bovine oath traditions require a speaker to touch living root or seed before promising long service, symbolizing that the vow must sustain more life than it consumes.

The greatest local controversy concerns vows sworn by children on behalf of a household.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Bovines are common among shipyards, storm reconstruction crews, orchard terraces, performance companies, dock unions, and island farms.

They are often lighter-framed than northern relatives, though no single Al’Ari body type exists.

Al’Ari culture prizes adaptation. Bovine builders have become famous for structures designed to bend, detach, or be rebuilt from numbered sections rather than resist every storm unchanged.

## VII. Bovines and Delving

Bovines often serve as front-line defenders, heavy-gear operators, survival specialists, environmental readers, and structural anchors.

Their advantages are considerable:

- High carrying capacity
- Stability during forced movement
- Strong melee commitment
- Endurance across long routes
- The ability to question local plant life
- Comfort with deliberate, coordinated advance

The same qualities produce common errors.

Delver companies may overload Bovine members, assume they want close combat, or treat their bodies as solutions to mechanical obstacles. Experienced Bovines are taught to ask whether a wall should be held at all before placing themselves beneath it.

**Root Connected** can be valuable within overgrown or living Labyrinths. It is not infallible. Labyrinth plants may be altered, deceptive, traumatized, sapient, or aware of time in unfamiliar ways.

A common Bovine warning states:

> A root remembers pressure. It does not always know whose foot made it.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

Your Bovine character has the following traits.

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Strength score increases by 2.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

### March On Hooves

You have advantage on saving throws you make to avoid suffering exhaustion from a forced march.

### Powerful Build

You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift.

### Head Down, Feet Forward

If you move at least 10 feet in a straight line toward a creature as part of your turn, your first melee attack against that creature is made with advantage.

If this attack scores a critical hit, you can roll one of the weapon’s damage dice one additional time and add it to the extra damage of the critical hit.

### Root Connected

As an action, you can touch a plant to communicate with it for 1 minute.

The plant can recount events in a 30-foot radius, or within its roots, from the past day, including the number and type of creatures, soil disturbances, and a rudimentary visual memory.

You can also sense if the plant is struggling to grow, and why.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

Sublineages describe broad physiological inheritances. They do not determine personality, profession, homeland, or social standing.

### Angus

Angus Bovines are broad-framed and dense, commonly standing between 6 feet 3 inches and 7 feet 3 inches tall.

Their cultural reputation for suppressed anger is partly the product of generations in which visible anger was punished while physical obedience was rewarded. Modern Angus communities increasingly teach anger as information rather than shame.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Bullheaded.** When you take damage equal to or greater than half your maximum hit points from a single source, you gain advantage on melee weapon attacks for 1 minute, or until you target a different creature.

### Bison

Bison Bovines possess extremely heavy frames and exceptional upper-body strength. Many carry dense coats around the head, shoulders, and arms.

Bison history is closely associated with communal defense, long migration, and weapons built for bodies other smiths failed to consider. The assumption that every Bison is a warrior remains common and unwelcome.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Unreal Grip.** You may wield two-handed melee weapons with one hand if holding no other weapons. Versatile weapons wielded one-handed deal their two-handed damage.

### Yak

Yak Bovines are thick-furred, broad, and adapted to altitude and severe cold. They commonly stand between 6 and 7 feet tall.

Yak communities often preserve detailed traditions of air, weather, and mutual shelter. Outsiders sometimes mistake their deliberate pace for conservatism. Many Yak reply that haste is impressive only until the trail disappears.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Highland Endurance.** You have resistance to cold damage. In addition, you ignore the effects of extreme cold, and you have advantage on Constitution saving throws made to resist exhaustion caused by environmental conditions, such as forced marches, high altitude, or severe weather.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

If the campaign uses Homeland Imprints, choose one trait representing the region that most strongly shaped your character.

A homeland represents upbringing and cultural experience. It does not need to match your birthplace or ancestry.

### Allemance — Tenacious Curiosity

Your Intelligence modifier is treated as 3 higher when learning a new language or tool proficiency.

Allemancian Bovines often learn through patient participation: one more season beside the brewer, bridgewright, neighbor, or traveler until unfamiliar work becomes shared knowledge.

### Oria — Ingenuity in Artificing

With 24 hours of work, you can attempt to repair a broken magic item.

A successful check, determined by the item’s type and rarity, restores its magical function. On a failed check, materials are retained, but the time required for the next attempt doubles.

Oric Bovines are taught that strength without repair merely produces larger ruins.

### Vinyot — The Bouncer’s Kid

You gain proficiency in Perception and one gaming set of your choice.

You have advantage on Perception checks to detect cheating when using any gaming set you’re proficient with.

Vinyoti Bovines often learn that maintaining order depends upon reading the room before anyone needs to be removed from it.

### Arneria — Reader of Maps and Moss

You gain proficiency with cartographer’s tools.

Once per long rest, by examining an underground plant for 1 minute, you can determine the nearest path to the surface and identify the most common non-Humanoid, non-Beast creature type within 300 feet.

Arnerian Bovines preserve route knowledge through living signs as well as ink.

### Al’Ar — Light-Touched Farmhand

You gain proficiency in Stealth.

When traveling overland, you make Dexterity (Stealth) checks to avoid being tracked with advantage.

Al’Ari Bovines learn to move through storm-bent orchards, crowded docks, and exposed islands without assuming that a large body must announce itself.

## XII. Playing a Bovine

When creating a Bovine character, consider:

- What burden do others assume you will carry?
- When did patience last protect you?
- When did patience become harmful?
- What promise are you proud to keep?
- What obligation would you Unyoke?
- How do you respond when someone offers help?
- Does strength feel like freedom, expectation, craft, danger, or all four?
- What living place do you know well enough to notice when it is hurting?
- Who is allowed to see your anger?
- What do you do that has nothing to do with being strong?

A Bovine may be restless, delicate, impatient, scholarly, theatrical, cautious, or physically weak.

Their lineage gives them inherited capacities.

It does not assign them a burden.
