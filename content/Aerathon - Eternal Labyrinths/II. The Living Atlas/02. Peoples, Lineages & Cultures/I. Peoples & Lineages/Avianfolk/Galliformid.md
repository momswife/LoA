## I. Lineage Overview

**Galliformids** are a feathered Avianfolk lineage distinguished by powerful legs, compact wings, explosive short-distance flight, keen ground awareness, and bodies adapted to forests, grasslands, scrub, cultivated country, and cold open terrain.

Their recognized sublineages include ptarmigan, grouse, partridges, quail, pheasants, turkeys, peafowl, junglefowl, guineafowl, and related groundbird peoples.

Galliformids spend more of their lives upon the ground than most feathered Avianfolk. Many can fly with startling speed, but fewer are suited to long periods aloft. Their settlements, travel customs, and inherited philosophies therefore concern both the freedom of sudden ascent and the practical knowledge required to live where one lands.

Outsiders have often mistaken this relationship with the ground for a lack of courage or aerial legitimacy.

Galliformid traditions answer that the earth is not what remains when flight fails.

It is where roads begin, food grows, warnings travel, and a community shelters when the weather makes pride irrelevant.

A common Galliformid saying offers the distinction plainly:

> The high wing sees the storm.  
> The ground wing knows where everyone will fit.

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Galliformids possess no single accepted ancestral homeland.

Their oldest communities appear in records from northern tundra, forest margins, mountain scrub, river grasslands, cultivated valleys, monsoon woodlands, and dry country where dependable cover mattered more than uninterrupted sky.

Several traditions claim that the first Galliformids emerged from seeds scattered by the South Zephyr. Northern accounts instead describe ancestors shaped from snow that refused to remain still beneath the North Gale.

An old Turkey account insists that the first Galliformid challenged thunder to a shouting match and survived only because the storm began laughing.

The Ministry has found no evidence capable of resolving these claims.

### The First Cover

Many Galliformid histories begin not with a first flight, but with a **First Cover**.

The phrase may refer to:

- A stand of brush where a household survived a conquering army

- A snow hollow that preserved travelers through a killing wind

- A farmstead that hid refugees regardless of lineage

- A sacred grove in which hunting was forbidden

- A cellar, ruin, or Labyrinth chamber converted into common shelter

- The person who opened a door after every safer household had barred theirs

To remember a First Cover is to remember that survival frequently depends upon something low, ordinary, and shared.

Some households carry a clipping, stone, seed, feather, or pinch of earth from that place whenever they establish a new home. Others refuse to remove anything from it, believing that a shelter ceases to be common when its pieces become trophies.

### The Carried Warning

Galliformid communities developed intricate systems of alarm suited to country where sightlines were short and danger often approached through grass, snow, or trees.

Warnings might be carried through calls, foot-stamping, wing-drumming, rattled seed vessels, colored cloth, or the sudden coordinated movement of a covey.

Traditional alarm patterns communicate more than fear. They may distinguish:

- Aerial danger from a ground approach

- Fire from flood

- A lost child from an armed stranger

- A threat requiring concealment from one requiring evacuation

- A genuine emergency from a practice alarm

The person who gives warning is not expected to defeat the danger alone.

Their duty is to make sure no one else meets it unknowingly.

## III. Body and Aerial Form

Galliformids commonly stand between **4 feet 8 inches and 6 feet 5 inches**, though individual and sublineage variation is considerable.

Their bodies tend toward powerful lower limbs, stable centers of gravity, and broad wings capable of producing sudden lift. Some are compact and lightly built. Others possess substantial frames suited to carrying weight, crossing broken ground, or weathering severe climates.

Hands and feet may end in blunt claws, digging nails, or defensive spurs. Settlements with large Galliformid populations commonly provide floor grips, low work surfaces, broad stairs, dust-bathing courts, and sheltered outdoor paths.

### Plumage

Galliformid plumage displays extraordinary variety.

Common expressions include:

- Mottled brown, cream, grey, rust, and black suited to ground cover

- Seasonal white plumage among Ptarmigan populations

- Iridescent green, blue, copper, and violet

- Banded wings, speckled chests, and barred tails

- Crests, wattles, facial skin, ornamental trains, and fan-shaped tails

- Plumage whose most vivid colors remain hidden until the wings or tail are opened

Some Galliformids molt into dramatically different seasonal appearances. Others retain a stable pattern throughout the year.

Plumage does not reliably indicate gender, occupation, temperament, or social role. Many cultures use dyes, trimming, jewelry, protective wraps, or cultivated presentation to affirm, reject, or complicate inherited appearance.

Touching a crest, train, wattle, or seasonal mantle without permission is a serious breach of bodily courtesy.

### Legs, Feet, and Spurs

Galliformid legs are commonly stronger than their compact wings suggest.

Many run comfortably across slopes, roots, loose soil, snow crust, and dense undergrowth. Some scratch or dig instinctively when searching the ground, though doing so is no more compulsory than climbing is for a person with capable hands.

Spurs vary greatly. They may be prominent, reduced, absent, deliberately capped, or incorporated into regional martial traditions.

The old claim that visible spurs indicate aggression is unsupported.

### Flight

Most Galliformids are capable of powered flight.

Their usual flight is lower, shorter, and more forceful than that of soaring Avianfolk. A Galliformid may cross a ravine, reach a canopy roost, clear a wall, or explode from concealment with remarkable speed while finding a long open crossing exhausting.

Some populations maintain true migratory routes. Others seldom fly farther than the next safe landing.

Flight limitation does not make Galliformids lesser Avianfolk. Their architecture, histories, warning systems, roosts, and defensive customs remain inseparable from the possibility of sudden ascent.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Galliformid households are frequently organized around **coveys**, **roosts**, **broods**, or **grounds**. These words overlap, but they are not interchangeable in every community.

A covey is commonly a group that travels, works, or shelters together. A roost describes a place and the people responsible for keeping it safe. Brood may refer to children raised together regardless of parentage. Ground often describes a wider neighborhood, ceremonial assembly, or shared territorial obligation.

Blood ancestry may matter greatly within one household and very little within the next.

Many Galliformid communities practice communal child supervision. Adults rotate responsibility for watching play areas, teaching alarm calls, maintaining shelter, and making sure no young person is forgotten during a sudden evacuation.

### Cover-Right

Many communities recognize **Cover-Right**: the expectation that immediate shelter will not be denied to a person facing an obvious common danger.

Cover-Right does not erase ownership. It creates a temporary duty.

A traveler admitted beneath it may be expected to:

- Follow the shelter keeper’s safety instructions

- Make space for later arrivals

- Share relevant information about the danger

- Help repair reasonable damage afterward

- Leave when the danger has genuinely passed

Disputes arise when authorities define a threat too narrowly, when wealthy compounds claim they lack room, or when someone invokes Cover-Right to evade ordinary law.

Even so, few accusations carry more shame in traditional Galliformid communities than **counting empty spaces while someone remains outside**.

### The Common Ground

Serious disputes are often heard upon a designated **Common Ground**.

Participants meet at the same physical level. Elevated seats, private balconies, concealed listeners, and flight above the assembly may be forbidden until testimony has ended.

The purpose is not to pretend differences in authority do not exist. It is to prevent height, display, or control of the exits from deciding whose account is heard.

Modern city communities reproduce the custom in courtyards, union halls, cellar kitchens, and circular chambers with no raised central platform.

### Display and Reserve

Some Galliformid cultures cultivate elaborate public display through plumage, dance, drumming, costume, hospitality, competitive craft, or formal speech.

Others prize concealment and restraint.

These practices often coexist within the same community. A person may dress brilliantly for a public rite and disappear into winter cover the following morning. Visibility may be a celebration, a profession, a courtship practice, a political statement, or simply a pleasure.

It is never public ownership of the person being seen.

## V. Inner Life and Modern Debate

Galliformid communities commonly admire:

- Readiness without panic

- Practical shelter

- Clear warning

- The ability to move together without becoming identical

- Pride that does not abandon caution

- Making beauty visible by choice

These values can produce attentive neighbors, resilient settlements, and people capable of acting decisively when a quiet situation changes without warning.

They also cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Readiness | Preparing before danger arrives | Living as though danger never leaves |
| Alertness | Noticing subtle changes | Mistaking suspicion for wisdom |
| Communal shelter | Protecting vulnerable people | Punishing anyone who needs solitude |
| Caution | Choosing survivable risks | Allowing fear to make every choice |
| Display | Claiming joy and presence | Treating attention as proof of worth |
| Adaptation | Changing with season and place | Concealing oneself until nothing honest remains |

A recurring Galliformid anxiety concerns **the false alarm**.

Failing to warn others may be catastrophic. Warning too often may cause a community to stop listening. Some Galliformids therefore rehearse every possibility until action becomes difficult. Others speak loudly and accept that occasional error is the price of preventing silence.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Galliformid communities disagree over:

- Whether separate Ptarmigan recognition preserves important adaptations or divides a wider Grouse inheritance

- Whether public display traditions have been reclaimed from spectacle or merely repackaged for sale

- Whether agricultural settlements offer safety, surveillance, or both

- Whether Cover-Right should be enforceable law or remain a moral obligation

- Whether traditional alarm systems remain useful in dense modern cities

- Whether long-flight training should be encouraged despite the lineage’s different aerial strengths

- Whether Sky-Reeve institutions ever regarded ground crews as equals

These debates vary by region and generation. No council speaks for every Galliformid people.

### The Sky-Reeve Inheritance

Galliformids participated in the ancient [[The Sky-Reeve Clans|Sky-Reeve Clans]], though surviving heroic accounts often reduced their contribution to a footnote beneath the deeds of long-distance pilots.

Galliformid households served as weather callers, launch keepers, ground navigators, roost wardens, supply coordinators, signalers, and rescuers responsible for finding those who came down beyond the planned landing.

Some were Stormriders.

Others made Stormriding possible without entering the storm themselves.

Modern Galliformid historians refer to this neglected network as **the Ground Beneath the Winds**. The phrase describes the routes, shelters, growers, repair crews, warning posts, and recovery grounds that supported the better-known aerial clans.

Traditionalist Sky-Reeve families generally accept the underlying history while disputing whether the Ground ever formed a unified institution.

The historians answer that a road need not possess a throne to remain a road.

### Galliformids Among the Four Currents

Galliformid households belong to all Four Cardinal Winds.

- **North Gale** traditions emphasize winter stores, concealment, mutual shelter, and the preservation of warnings across generations.

- **South Zephyr** traditions frequently emphasize cultivation, public beauty, abundance, courtship arts, and the duties created by hospitality.

- **East Tempest** traditions favor direct challenge, oath-drumming, sudden action, and the courage to become visible when concealment would be safer.

- **West Cyclone** traditions emphasize roadside coveys, adaptable shelter, changing routes, and the ability to scatter without losing one another.

These are cultural inheritances, not descriptions of personality.

A Ptarmigan may belong to the South Zephyr. A Peafowl household may have kept the North Gale for centuries.

Wind follows history and affiliation, not plumage.

### Stormriding

Galliformid Stormriders remain uncommon but well documented.

Their compact wings demand different harnesses and routes. Many enter a storm from towers, ridgelines, or engineered launch platforms, using violent rising currents to sustain movement their bodies would not maintain in calm air.

Supporters call the practice proof that aerial mastery cannot be measured by ordinary distance.

Critics argue that it turns a lineage’s physical limits into entertainment.

Galliformid ground crews remain far more numerous than riders. They inspect landing fields, read low weather, maintain signal lines, and coordinate recovery teams after a storm changes direction.

Among them, **the last safe landing matters more than the first spectacular launch**.

## VI. Galliformid Peoples in Modern Aerathon

Galliformids live throughout the Five Greater Regions.

Their sublineages do not determine their professions. Their ground mobility, warning traditions, plumage, and familiarity with cultivated or difficult terrain nevertheless shape the opportunities offered to them—and the stereotypes imposed upon them.

### Allemance

In [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. The Material Realms/Five Greater Regions/Allemance/∅ Allemance|Allemance]], Galliformid communities are common near hedgerows, farm roads, forest margins, winter shelters, and small settlements where news travels by repeated personal contact.

Many households maintain common storm cellars or marked refuge paths. A lantern placed low beside a road may indicate that travelers can claim Cover-Right during dangerous weather.

Galliformids work throughout Allemancian life as growers, millers, wardens, teachers, traders, craftspeople, messengers, and delvers. Their visibility in agricultural communities has produced the insulting assumption that they are naturally suited to farm labor or domestic service.

Allemancian Galliformids generally answer that familiarity is not ownership.

### Oria

In [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. The Material Realms/Five Greater Regions/Oria/∅ Oria|Oria]], Galliformids are strongly represented in emergency planning, facility safety, supply accounting, controlled agriculture, low-clearance inspection, and evacuation design.

Oric Houses value warning systems that distinguish inconvenience from true failure. Galliformid engineers have helped replace indiscriminate alarms with layered signals indicating the location, type, and urgency of a hazard.

Some Houses nevertheless treat Galliformid caution as an inexhaustible resource, assigning the same workers to permanent vigilance until they are unable to rest.

Oric coveys increasingly insist that readiness is a rotation, not a personality trait.

### Vinyot

In [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. The Material Realms/Five Greater Regions/Vinyot/∅ Vinyot|Vinyot]], Galliformid plumage and display traditions are commercially valuable.

Pheasant and Peafowl performers, designers, speakers, duelists, hosts, and public figures may earn extraordinary wealth. Their contracts often include detailed control over shed feathers, costume likenesses, magical color reproduction, and the circumstances under which a display may be recorded.

Less visible Galliformids work in market logistics, urban food systems, security, rooftop cultivation, and the maintenance of public shelters.

Vinyoti law recognizes that admiration does not create a right to touch, collect from, imitate, or reproduce someone’s body without consent.

Enforcement remains inconsistent when the offender is profitable.

### Arneria

In [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. The Material Realms/Five Greater Regions/Arneria/∅ Arneria|Arneria]], Galliformid calls, feathers, tracks, and seasonal molts appear in rites concerning warning, sacrifice, dawn, concealment, rightful challenge, and renewal.

Temple grounds may maintain open courts where oath-speakers stand without cover while witnesses remain sheltered around them. The arrangement represents a voluntary acceptance of scrutiny rather than a claim that honesty requires constant exposure.

Arnerian Galliformids serve throughout civil, military, agricultural, religious, and scholarly life. Some hereditary alarm-callers hold respected ceremonial offices. Others reject those offices as a ritualized expectation that Galliformids must always notice danger first.

### Al’Ar

In [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. The Material Realms/Five Greater Regions/Al'Ar/∅ Al'Ar|Al’Ar]], Galliformids inhabit storm scrub, dry uplands, monsoon woodland, dockside neighborhoods, traveling camps, and settlements rebuilt from whatever remained after the last disaster.

Al’Ari coveys favor portable shelter markers, loud layered alarms, and reunion calls that can be recognized through wind and debris.

Galliformid runners and low-flight rescuers are particularly effective where taller structures have collapsed and broad-winged pilots cannot safely maneuver.

Their humor often turns upon the difference between a dignified plan and the direction everyone actually ran when the roof departed.

## VII. Galliformid Delvers

Galliformid delvers commonly serve as ground scouts, route finders, warning specialists, close-range rescuers, vanguard skirmishers, survivalists, and negotiators accustomed to reading crowded rooms.

Their explosive flight is valuable in chambers where a sudden change of level matters more than sustained movement. Powerful legs and low centers of gravity help them cross rubble, roots, snow, unstable slopes, and passages where larger wings become a liability.

Their greatest danger is often the Labyrinth’s ability to manipulate alarm.

False movement, repeated distant calls, shifting brush, illusory tracks, and hazards that punish a startled retreat can exhaust a watchful delver before the true threat appears.

Experienced Galliformids are trained to identify:

- The nearest cover that is actually defensible

- Whether a warning came from a companion, creature, mechanism, or echo

- The direction a scattered party will regroup

- The clearance needed for an emergency takeoff

- Whether apparent ground can bear the weight of a landing

- Which route remains usable by companions who cannot fly

- How many alarms the group can endure before attention begins to fail

### The Last Safe Cover

Many Galliformid delvers mark the last defensible position before entering an uncertain chamber.

The mark may be chalk, thread, a turned stone, a scent, or a short call answered by the rear guard. It identifies the place to which the group can retreat without needing another discussion.

The practice is not an expectation that retreat will occur.

It prevents pride from wasting time if it must.

### Alarm Discipline

Galliformid teams often agree upon a limited set of warnings before descent.

Each call should produce a specific response. A warning without an agreed action creates noise; too many signals create hesitation.

A common instruction states:

> Name the danger if you can.  
> Name the direction if you cannot.  
> Never make panic carry both.

Galliformid naming traditions vary by region, covey, and sublineage.

Some use family or settlement names. Others adopt seasonal names, shelter names, call names, or by-names earned through public rites. A person who undergoes a dramatic molt may choose a new descriptive name without changing their household identity.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

Your Galliformid character has the following traits.

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Constitution score increases by 2.

### Size

Your size is Small or Medium, chosen when you select this lineage.

Galliformids commonly stand between 4 feet 8 inches and 6 feet 5 inches tall.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

You have a flying speed of 30 feet.

### Flight

You can fly using your wings. To do so, you cannot be wearing medium or heavy armor.

### Beak and Spurs

Your beak, claws, or defensive spurs are natural weapons that you can use to make unarmed strikes.

When you hit with one of them, the strike deals slashing or piercing damage equal to `1d4 + your Strength modifier`. You choose the damage type when you make the attack.

### Skyward Pulse

As a bonus action, you can beat your wings with explosive force.

Each creature within 5 feet of you must succeed on a Strength saving throw or be pushed 5 feet away from you.

The saving throw DC equals:

`8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier`

You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

### Grounded Stride

Nonmagical difficult terrain caused by low vegetation, loose earth, rubble, or shallow snow does not cost you extra movement while you are walking.

### Explosive Takeoff

When a creature you can see enters a space within 5 feet of you while you are standing on a solid surface, you can use your reaction to fly up to half your flying speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

Galliformid sublineages represent distinct hereditary and physiological branches within the broader lineage. They may influence appearance, anatomy, inherited adaptations, and cultural history, but do not determine personality, morality, profession, homeland, or Household Wind.

### Ptarmigan Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Wisdom score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Small or Medium, chosen when you select this sublineage.

**Winter Mantle.** You have resistance to cold damage. You can attempt to Hide when you are lightly obscured by falling snow or snowy terrain.

Ptarmigan Galliformids include Willow Ptarmigan, Rock Ptarmigan, White-Tailed Ptarmigan, and related cold-country peoples.

Their plumage commonly shifts with the seasons, ranging from mottled brown, rust, grey, and black during warmer months to partial or complete white in winter. Some retain dark tails, eye markings, or feathered feet throughout the year.

Ptarmigan communities are especially numerous beneath the North Gale, where snow roosts, willow cover, seasonal movement, and the careful reading of thaw conditions shape daily life.

The Ministry records Ptarmigans separately from the wider Grouse branch because their seasonal plumage and cold adaptations create distinct access needs. This administrative distinction does not deny their close Grouse kinship.

### Grouse Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Drumming Ground.** You gain proficiency in either the Intimidation or Performance skill. You can produce a resonant call, wingbeat, or foot-drumming pattern audible up to 300 feet away in quiet conditions.

Grouse Galliformids include ruffed, spruce, sage, sharp-tailed, prairie, capercaillie, and related forest or grassland branches not registered as Ptarmigans.

They commonly possess mottled plumage, strong legs, broad tails, neck ruffs, crests, or inflatable display structures. Many communities maintain drumming grounds used for announcement, courtship, warning, ceremony, and territorial negotiation.

A drumming ground is not necessarily restricted by gender or ancestry. Modern urban Grouse reproduce them through resonant floors, public percussion, and architecture designed to carry low sound without overwhelming nearby homes.

### Partridge and Quail Sublineages

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Dexterity score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Small or Medium, chosen when you select this sublineage.

**Quick Step.** Your walking speed increases by 5 feet. You can take the Disengage action as a bonus action a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, regaining all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

Partridge and Quail Galliformids tend toward compact frames, rounded wings, and rapid movement through dense ground cover.

Their communities frequently preserve strong covey traditions. These may involve communal child care, coordinated travel, shared warning, or winter shelter rather than any single household structure.

Small stature is often mistaken for fragility. Partridge and Quail delvers are quick to note that survival beneath brush, stone, and hungry skies rewards a different measure of strength.

### Pheasant Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Charisma score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Startling Display.** You gain proficiency in the Performance skill. When a creature fails its saving throw against your **Skyward Pulse**, it cannot take reactions until the start of your next turn.

Pheasant Galliformids include ring-necked, golden, silver, tragopan, monal, and related branches.

Their plumage may be vividly colored or carefully mottled, with long tails, crests, facial ornaments, and hidden flashes revealed only in movement. Pheasant communities have produced influential traditions of textile work, theater, fencing, diplomacy, and ceremonial hosting.

The expectation that every Pheasant must enjoy attention remains a frequent source of resentment. Display is an ability and an art, not a permanent state of consent.

### Turkey Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Powerful Build.** You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift.

Turkey Galliformids are generally large-bodied, strong-legged, and capable of forceful short flight. Their plumage often includes layered brown, black, copper, cream, bronze, or iridescent coloration, accompanied by expressive facial skin, wattles, beards, or broad tail fans.

Many Turkey traditions value direct speech, shared provision, public challenge, and humor delivered with impressive volume.

Outsider stories frequently portray them as foolish. Turkey scholars have documented this tradition with great thoroughness and considerably less patience.

### Peafowl Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Charisma score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Many-Eyed Bearing.** You gain proficiency in either the Insight or Performance skill. If you already have proficiency in the chosen skill, you gain proficiency in the other instead.

Peafowl Galliformids commonly possess crests, iridescent neck plumage, and ornamental trains or patterned tails. These features vary across individuals and are not restricted to a single gender.

Peafowl cultures have long explored the relationship between visibility and power. Some use deliberate splendor to make a household impossible to ignore. Others treat the folded train as a symbol of privacy: beauty retained without obligation to display it.

The “eyes” of Peafowl plumage are artistic and social symbols. They do not grant supernatural vision.

### Junglefowl Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Wisdom score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Small or Medium, chosen when you select this sublineage.

**Dawn Vigil.** You gain proficiency in the Perception skill. While you are conscious, you have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing.

Junglefowl Galliformids include red, grey, green, and related woodland branches, together with many Chicken peoples whose ancestries have diverged through long settlement beside other Folk.

Their communities are extraordinarily varied. Some preserve forest routes and dawn-call traditions. Others have lived in cities or farming regions for thousands of years and regard claims of a single “natural” Galliformid way of life as nonsense.

The term **domesticated** is not an acceptable description of a sapient Junglefowl person or community.

### Guineafowl Sublineage

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Alarm Chorus.** When you roll initiative, you can choose a number of creatures up to your proficiency bonus within 30 feet of you that can hear you. Each chosen creature adds `1d4` to its initiative roll. Once you use this trait, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

Guineafowl Galliformids commonly possess strong legs, spotted or patterned plumage, bare or lightly feathered heads, and calls capable of carrying across open country.

Their settlement traditions often emphasize perimeter awareness, collective response, and the rapid circulation of local information.

Guineafowl are frequently stereotyped as noisy or anxious. Their communities reply that a warning system judged only by those who wished to sleep through it has been reviewed by the wrong audience.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

If the campaign uses Homeland Imprints, choose one trait representing the region that most strongly shaped your character.

A homeland represents upbringing and cultural experience. It does not need to match your birthplace, ancestry, sublineage, or Household Wind.

### Allemance — Hedgerow Recall

You gain proficiency in the Insight skill.

When you take the Dash or Disengage action on your turn, you can also make a Wisdom (Perception) check as a bonus action.

Allemancian Galliformids learn which low roads lead home, which hedges hide a gate, and which familiar shelter has gone unexpectedly quiet.

### Oria — Ordered Withdrawal

While flying, you have advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened.

When you are reduced to half your hit points or fewer, you can use your reaction to fly up to half your flying speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

Once you use this reaction, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

Oric Galliformids train alarm and evacuation together. A warning without an orderly route away from danger is only half a system.

### Vinyot — Contracted Spotlight

While falling, you can reduce the fall speed of yourself or one willing creature within 5 feet of you by 60 feet per round, potentially negating falling damage.

You also have advantage on Dexterity (Acrobatics) checks made while airborne.

Vinyoti Galliformids learn that controlling an entrance also means controlling the landing that follows it.

### Arneria — Reading of Cast Feathers

You know the _gust_ cantrip.

When you reach 3rd level, you can cast _feather fall_ once with this trait. You regain the ability to do so when you finish a long rest.

When you reach 5th level, you can cast _augury_ as a ritual, using cast feathers, seed, dust, and the movement of air across the ground as your ritual focus.

Arnerian Galliformids read both the open sky and the smallest current passing beneath a door.

### Al’Ar — Thunder-Start

When you use **Skyward Pulse**, you may release a sudden call carried by the force of your wings.

Creatures of your choice within 10 feet of you must succeed on a Constitution saving throw against your Skyward Pulse saving throw DC or be deafened until the start of your next turn.

Once you use this trait, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

Among Al’Ari Galliformids, thunder is both warning and invitation.

The wise decide which before they begin running.

## XII. Playing a Galliformid

When creating a Galliformid character, consider:

- What does safe cover mean to them?

- Who taught them the warnings of their household?

- Do they trust their first alarm or question it?

- Do they feel more comfortable upon the ground, in brief flight, or at a sheltered height?

- Is their appearance subdued, brilliant, seasonal, cultivated, or difficult to categorize?

- When do they choose to be visible?

- What form does their covey, brood, roost, or household take?

- Have they ever been denied Cover-Right—or offered it to someone dangerous?

- Do they preserve a First Cover?

- How do they respond to assumptions about cowardice, agriculture, display, or domesticity?

- Are they connected to the Ground Beneath the Winds?

- Do they regard Stormriders as brave, wasteful, incomplete without their crews, or all three?

- What warning will they never ignore again?

- Where will they regroup when everyone scatters?

A Galliformid may be cautious or reckless, quiet or thunderous, brilliantly visible or almost impossible to find.

Their lineage gives them a powerful relationship with the ground.

It does not forbid them the sky.

## XIII. Registry Advisory

> [!note] MDO Registry Advisory  
> Galliformid lineage does not indicate cowardice, docility, agricultural aptitude, domestic status, reproductive role, willingness to perform, or suitability as food or game.
>
> Ministry personnel are forbidden from assigning farming, sentry, alarm, entertainment, feather-production, or sacrificial duties solely on the basis of lineage or sublineage.
>
> Shed feathers remain subject to the owner’s consent and regional property law. Ornamental plumage does not constitute permission to touch, collect, reproduce, or magically imitate it.
>
> Flight endurance must be assessed individually. Short-distance flight does not invalidate Avianfolk recognition or justify denial of aerial access accommodations.
