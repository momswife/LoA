# Culture, Lineage & Regional Life

The Living Atlas records many kinds of identity that may overlap in one person without becoming interchangeable. A lineage can influence bodily experience. A household can teach obligation. A homeland can shape food, language, work, and public memory. A faith can create ritual community across every border. None alone determines character, loyalty, morality, or way of life.

## I. The Main Record Types

| Record | What it describes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Folk** | A broad modern Ministry classification used to organize related peoples and records | One ancestry, culture, nation, or political interest |
| **Lineage** | A recognized people or inherited bodily lineage, including physiology, access needs, collective history, and playable mechanics | A required personality, profession, faith, homeland, or culture |
| **Sublineage or lineage tradition** | A recognized addition or variation within a playable lineage record | A complete culture or a fixed social role |
| **Cultural tradition** | Learned, practiced, inherited, adopted, or contested ways of belonging | Universal behavior among everyone associated with that tradition |
| **Regional life** | Practices shaped by living within a place: household life, livelihood, hospitality, language, dress, food, etiquette, and local memory | A biological trait or a uniform regional personality |
| **Citizenship or jurisdiction** | A person's legal relationship to a settlement, House, dock-state, company polity, barangay, or other authority | Their ancestry, private loyalties, or complete identity |
| **Faith** | Worship, doctrine, religious office, ritual obligation, and sacred community | Proof of lineage, nationality, or political allegiance |
| **Profession or affiliation** | Learned work, guild membership, service, military office, or organizational duty | Inherited temperament or a permanent culture |

## II. How Identities Overlap

A person may be classified as Aquaticfolk, belong to a Meran lineage, hold citizenship in an Al'Ari dock-state, serve on a mixed-lineage crew, practice a household faith, and carry an Oric Homeland Imprint acquired through long residence. Those facts describe different relationships. One should not be used as shorthand for the others.

The same principle applies within households. Adoption, partnership, apprenticeship, refuge, shared work, and chosen kinship can transmit language, custom, responsibility, and memory without changing a person's lineage. Conversely, two people of the same lineage may have no culture, homeland, faith, or political interest in common.

## III. Where Records Belong

- File physiology, inherited abilities, access needs, and playable ancestry mechanics under **Peoples & Lineages**.
- File a named learned tradition that crosses households or regions under **Cultural Systems & Traditions**.
- File practices tied to a particular region or subregion under its **Life, Customs & Identity** shelf.
- File settlement-specific habits in the settlement record unless they support a mature standalone cultural article.
- File formal doctrine, clergy, and sacred institutions under **Religion & Worship**.
- File law, offices, and political authority under **Governments & Geopolitics**.
- File ordinary continental systems such as housing, labor, provision, and travel under **Society, Economy & Everyday Life**.
- File reconstructed origins and earlier forms under **Annals & Antiquities**.

Cross-linking is expected. Separation prevents category error; it should not make connected lives appear unrelated.

## IV. Reading Playable Mechanics

A lineage trait is a game rule, not an ethnography. Ability Score Increases, movement, senses, innate abilities, recognized options, and Homeland Imprints should be read as the campaign's current character-creation package. They do not prescribe mannerisms, values, occupations, or social status.

A **Homeland Imprint** represents adaptation, training, experience, or magical relationship associated with a place. It does not mean that everyone born in that region has the same feature, nor that only one lineage can belong there. The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/02. Peoples, Lineages & Cultures/I. Peoples & Lineages/01. Playable Lineage Quick Reference|Playable Lineage Quick Reference]] summarizes what each current record actually files.

## V. Evidence and Responsible Description

Culture should be described at the narrowest scale the evidence supports. A custom observed in one harbor is not automatically Al'Ari. A House practice is not automatically Oric. A company rule is not automatically Vinyoti. A lineage tradition practiced by one community is not instinct.

Good cultural records identify who practices something, where, under what conditions, how it varies, whether participation is chosen, and who disputes its meaning. When those answers are not known, the Atlas should preserve the question rather than complete the culture from terrain or anatomy.
