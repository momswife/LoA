# Bound Intelligences and Relic Custody

## Custody is a responsibility before it is a verdict

The working distinction is between **possession**, **custody**, and **authority to use**. A delver may carry an object without owning it. A workshop may hold it for repair without permission to activate it. A lawful owner may still lack authority to expose workers or neighbors to its effects. [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/II. Field Operations & Safety/03. Relic Handling & Regulation|Relic Handling & Regulation]] remains the operational safety standard; this companion record addresses the additional problems raised by an apparent intelligence.

These distinctions guide handling and documentation. They do not create one continental law of elemental personhood or displace the jurisdiction governing a particular recovery.

## Record two histories

A useful intake record follows the physical object: where it was recovered, who transferred it, what was altered, and which activations preceded an incident. A second record follows communication: who heard it, what was requested, whether it refused, and whether answers persisted when the operator changed.

Neither history should silently replace the other. A perfect salvage receipt does not prove an occupant agreed to confinement. An occupant's account does not by itself establish ownership of the surrounding machinery. Transcribe a demand as testimony; do not turn it into a workshop instruction merely because it came from an ancient voice.

## The repairer's dilemma

Consider a mine lift whose recovered pressure vessel begins answering workers. The crew depends on the lift, the purchaser has paid for the vessel, and the workshop has an unfinished repair contract. Calling the vessel property does not make continued activation safe. Calling its voice a person does not make immediate dismantling safe for the miners below.

The useful first intervention is a replacement load path: support the cage, isolate the suspect vessel, and preserve a means of returning the workers. Only then can the competing claims be heard without making someone's survival the price of agreement. This is a handling example, not a report of a particular lift or a newly adjudicated precedent.

## Practical agreements

Repair agreements should distinguish permission to inspect from permission to alter a binding. They should name who can authorize a test, who can stop it, how injuries and damaged equipment will be addressed, and where the object goes if cooperation fails. A spoken assent given during pain, restraint, or threatened destruction deserves separate notation rather than being filed as uncomplicated consent.

An intelligence's refusal can be important evidence even where its legal standing remains disputed. It does not automatically establish that every release is safe. An independent observer and a reversible stopping point protect both the workers and the subject of the examination.

## Lessons from famous relics

[[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/IV. Delver Registry/2. Lost/01. Arthur 'Cap' Penjamin|Cap's record]] demonstrates that experience and public admiration cannot substitute for monitoring changes in conduct. A powerful keeper can also become the person least able to recognize that the terms of use have changed.

The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/08. Bestiary & Field Phenomena/II. Elementals & Weather-Born/06. Djinn, Bargains, and Bound Intelligences|djinn comparison]] shows why containment must follow identified behavior. Methods appropriate to wish-born reflective flame may endanger a workshop dealing with an air intelligence. Relic custody is therefore shared work among handlers, claimants, skilled repairers, and witnesses, with each person's authority stated rather than assumed.

## Workshop practice

The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/5. Vinyot/1. Subregions/3. Fortunata Hills/1. Settlements/01a. The Brass Burrow|Brass Burrow]] separates salvage valuation, safety inspection, and permission to alter a device. This practice makes a modest but consequential promise: paying for a repair does not quietly purchase the right to awaken everything inside the machine.
