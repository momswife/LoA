# Scrytech & Public Communication

_Aerathon can watch a delver cross a Labyrinth live and still wait weeks for a letter from beyond a damaged relay._

**Filed Division:** Office of Civil Life, Trade, and Infrastructure  
**Consulting Division:** Arcane and Anomalous Research Directorate  
**Current Revision:** Late Redfall, 3388 A.D.  
**Classification:** Public Reference

---

## I. What Scrytech Connects

**Scrytech** is the family of crafted systems that capture, stabilize, transmit, display, and store magical observation. Its most famous use is live delving, but connected networks also carry hazard alerts, breach warnings, guild notices, Ministry bulletins, public records, sponsor messages, and selected civic broadcasts.

The continent-spanning **Grand Scrying Network**, or GSN, joins mirrors, relay towers, conduits, projectors, memory stones, public viewing circles, guild halls, theaters, private crystals, and archive vaults. It is broad without being everywhere.

The public often speaks of “the network” as though it were a single object. In practice, scrytech is many maintained systems with different owners, permissions, delays, audiences, and standards of reliability.

## II. How a Transmission Travels

A scry transmission depends on several layers working together:

- An observing device or authorized source captures an image, sound, reading, or record.
- Resonance anchors and discriminator systems stabilize the intended signal and reject some interference.
- Scryline conduits and relay points carry the transmission across distance.
- Mirror arrays, projectors, crystals, or public displays translate it for an audience.
- Memory storage preserves selected material for review, evidence, education, or rebroadcast.

Every step can alter what reaches the viewer. A missing angle, damaged relay, delayed calibration, editorial selection, or false signal may produce a record that is vivid but incomplete.

For the engineered system, see [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/06. Magic, Technology & Infrastructure/II. Scrytech & Public Communication/01. Scrying Architecture|Scrying Architecture]].

## III. Oculi, GSN, and Arcanet

These names are connected but not interchangeable.

**Divine Oculi** are semi-sentient scrying constructs used to observe delves, transmit footage, record hazards, and support conduct review. Their title does not establish that they are gods, nor does their apparent intelligence make every decision transparent.

The **Grand Scrying Network** carries transmissions to public and private destinations. It is the broadcast and distribution structure through which much of the Age of Spectacle is experienced.

The **Arcanet Core** is a specialized construct beneath Oria's Hall of Ascendancy. It draws upon echo data, beacons, anchors, and runic relays to generate Unified Delver Merit Index rankings. It is not a universal public message service, and an Arcanet ranking should not be confused with a complete civic identity.

## IV. Public Access and Ordinary Communication

Many people encounter scrytech collectively. They watch a public display, gather at a viewing circle, consult a guildstream node, hear a bulletin repeated by a clerk, or read a notice copied from a transmitted record. Private crystals and dedicated connections are less evenly distributed.

Long-distance life still relies on letters, couriers, caravan messages, ships, rail deliveries, temple or guild contacts, travelers, and local noticeboards. These methods are slower but can reach beyond the maintained resonance grid. Wise institutions preserve non-scry procedures for evacuation, legal notice, navigation, and family contact.

Access requires more than a nearby display. Information must be available in forms people can perceive and understand: spoken, written, signed, tactile, translated, repeated, or privately explained as circumstances require. A spectacular image is not an accessible warning if it provides no usable direction.

## V. Privacy, Proof, and Performance

Scrying creates evidence, but it also creates an audience. The same device may serve safety, entertainment, discipline, advertising, and surveillance.

Consent and authority matter before observation begins. A public street, guild contract, or Ministry jurisdiction does not erase every claim to privacy. Clinics, homes, changing areas, private rituals, and protected testimony require stricter boundaries. Memory storage introduces further questions: who can copy a record, how long it is kept, whether it can be edited, and who may profit from its replay.

Viewers should distinguish **what was shown** from **everything that occurred**. Live commentary, sponsor framing, chosen angles, rankings, and delayed disclosure shape public understanding. A missing record may indicate failure, interference, protection, censorship, or simple absence of coverage; it is not proof of any one explanation.

## VI. Failure and Public Safety

Scrytech can fail through damaged conduits, resonance drift, environmental interference, sabotage, overload, false calibration, memory corruption, or the loss of power and trained staff. Labyrinth conditions introduce additional behaviors that ordinary engineers cannot always reproduce.

Critical systems use tamper detection, calibration schedules, memory chains, and redundant relays. Communities still need local alarms, runners, marked evacuation routes, gathering points, and people authorized to act without waiting for a distant confirmation.

Connection shortens the time between event and witness. It does not eliminate distance, uncertainty, or the human duty to decide what happens next.
