# The Sunwardens

**Filed Division:** _Living Atlas — Clergy, Orders & Sacred Vocation_  
**Primary Compiler:** Priest-Analyst Thera Daan  
**Consulting Office:** Faith-Field and Divine Anomaly Unit  
**Date of Filing:** 3388 A.D.  
**Document Class:** _Sacred Organization Record / Grade-III Composite_  
**Access Designation:** _Black Seal by association with an active containment inquiry_

## I. Institutional Overview

The **Sunwardens** were—or may still be—a sacred custodial order concerned with boundaries, thresholds, prisons, and the ethical separation of a danger from the world around it.

The surviving record does not establish a single headquarters, continuous leadership, patron deity, or modern registry. It establishes something narrower and more reliable: a recognizable discipline of **solar geometry** transmitted through at least one family line and used in the construction of magical restraints.

Sunwarden geometry does not chiefly strengthen a wall. It defines relationships across it.

Its marks identify:

- what is confined;
- what performs the confinement;
- what must remain outside;
- which persons may approach without becoming part of the restraint;
- and where a prison’s authority must end.

That final distinction separates Sunwarden practice from many containment traditions. A successful prison is not permitted to expand its definition of prisoner merely because doing so would make the seal easier to maintain.

## II. Evidence and Historical Status

No complete Sunwarden charter survives in the accessible Lorevault. The Ministry’s current reconstruction rests upon:

- fragments of solar boundary diagrams;
- repeated geometric marks in old containment architecture;
- later copies of unattributed Sunwarden maxims;
- a restricted record transmitted through the father of **Solaris**;
- and the use of that inherited knowledge during the Malarthain containment of 3377 A.D.

These sources do not prove that every bearer belonged to one centralized institution. _Sunwarden_ may have named an order, an office, a lineage of instruction, or all three at different times.

The MDO therefore recognizes the Sunwardens as a **fragmented sacred organization or inherited custodial discipline**. Claims about their founding date, original seat, numerical strength, and present leadership remain unverified.

## III. Purpose and Mandate

The recoverable Sunwarden mandate can be summarized as the defense of the boundary itself.

Where other warding traditions ask how a dangerous thing can be held, Sunwarden practice also asks:

1. What has lawful cause to be confined?
2. Who has accepted the duty of confinement?
3. Which bystanders, heirs, servants, and rescuers must remain outside that duty?
4. Can the restraint distinguish a keeper from an inmate?
5. Does the prison cease where its stated danger ceases?

The order’s surviving geometry suggests a deep suspicion of containment systems that gain strength by absorbing their operators. A warden may accept risk or obligation. The prison may not silently rewrite that acceptance into captivity.

This is both a magical principle and an ethical one.

## IV. Solar Geometry

The word _solar_ does not establish which god, if any, the Sunwardens served. In surviving diagrams, the sun functions as a model of distinction: light reaches a boundary, reveals it, and continues outward without becoming the thing it illuminates.

Sunwarden constructions use nested lines, open rays, interrupted circles, and deliberately unclosed angles. Their purpose is not decorative radiance. Each mark declares a condition of relation.

Four functions recur in authenticated fragments:

| Function | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Exterior definition** | Identifies people, places, or principles the prison may not claim |
| **Custodian distinction** | Prevents a keeper or repairer from being recognized as an inmate |
| **Threshold witness** | Records that a crossing occurred without automatically authorizing further passage |
| **Terminal boundary** | Establishes the point beyond which the prison’s laws possess no standing |

Solar geometry is therefore poorly suited to conquest. It can clarify a lawful boundary, but it resists attempts to make one authority universal.

## V. Structure and Transmission

The available evidence preserves methods more clearly than offices.

No verified titles, ranks, or central hierarchy survive. Later commentators use terms such as _warden_, _geometer_, _witness_, and _keeper_, but the Ministry cannot establish whether these were formal ranks or descriptions supplied after the order fragmented.

At least one body of Sunwarden knowledge passed through family instruction. A restricted Malarthain record identifies **Solaris’s father** as the bearer through whom a viable sequence of the geometry survived into the modern era.

That inheritance does not establish ancestry as a magical requirement. It shows only that familial transmission preserved material no public temple, archive, or academy is presently known to possess in complete form.

The absence of a visible institution may be deliberate. A distributed custodial order is harder to compel, corrupt, or destroy, but it is also harder to review. The record does not establish which explanation applies.

## VI. Doctrine and Ethical Limits

Sunwarden fragments repeatedly distinguish **containment** from **ownership**.

A sealed entity may be dangerous. A keeper may be necessary. Neither fact grants the keeper an unlimited claim over the entity, the surrounding land, or the people required to maintain the boundary.

Three reconstructed limits appear consistently:

- **No inherited captivity:** descendants and successors do not become prisoners merely because a seal outlives its first keeper.
- **No expanding definition:** a prison may not redefine nearby people or structures as components for administrative convenience.
- **No custody without an outside:** every lawful restraint must preserve a place, authority, or witness beyond its own control.

These principles explain why Sunwarden work is especially resistant to systems that attempt to turn a living person into permanent containment infrastructure.

They do not prove the historical order always obeyed its own doctrine.

## VII. The Malarthain Safeguard

The clearest modern use of Sunwarden knowledge occurred after the 3377 collapse beneath [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/5. Vinyot/1. Subregions/3. Fortunata Hills/1. Settlements/01. Malarthain Stronghold|Malarthain Stronghold]].

The disaster damaged an older prison beneath the Forge-Heart. Its repair required four bodies of knowledge no participant possessed alone:

| Tradition | Contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Balval mortal craft | Constructed the visible prosthetic cuff and articulated golden hand |
| Lady Neve’s Malarite rites | Bound endurance, protection, and the Forge-Heart’s recognition into the cuff |
| Sunwarden solar geometry | Defined what must remain outside and prevented the bearer from being mistaken for the prisoner |
| Black Writ living containment | Joined an oath-tempered bearer to the broken counterseal through responsive black-gold |

The bearer was **Yyna Balval**.

The prison had already taken part of Yyna’s body during the collapse, and her freely sworn defense of Malarthain had acquired divine force. Lady Neve used Sunwarden geometry to ensure that reconnecting Yyna to the counterseal did not classify her as the being confined below.

This safeguard did not make the arrangement consensual. Yyna was not told what the cuff had become. The geometry protected her from one form of captivity while Neve, Cardwell, and their allies denied her the knowledge required to accept or refuse another.

The distinction is essential. A technically successful safeguard does not erase the moral failure of those who installed it without the bearer’s informed consent.

## VIII. Conflict with the Black Writ

The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/05. Governments & Geopolitics/I. Continental Institutions/1. Ministry of Delving Operations (MDO)/04. Forbidden Archives/01. The Black Writ|Black Writ]] treats Sunwarden geometry as an uncontrolled competing authority.

The council’s Doctrine of Necessary Custody depends upon its ability to expand the category of asset: a relic becomes a continuing holding, a bearer becomes a living interface, and the land around a danger becomes a sovereign hazard. Sunwarden practice interrupts that expansion by defining what the custody order cannot claim.

This is why Caldris Venn could provide the theory and responsive anchor used in Yyna’s cuff without reproducing the completed design. Lady Neve added Malarite and Sunwarden safeguards after receiving his method, then concealed their arrangement from him.

The Black Writ’s modern Malarthain plan requires the Sunwarden layer to be seized, neutralized, or rewritten before the council can make the Forge-Heart accept its command brand. If that layer survives intact, the prison may recognize the difference between a lawful keeper and an authority attempting ownership.

## IX. Public Standing and Modern Survival

No current Sunwarden chapter is registered with the MDO. No recognized temple claims authority over the name. No public academy teaches a complete version of the surviving geometry.

That absence does not establish extinction.

The Malarthain case proves that operational knowledge survived into the present through private inheritance. Other fragments may remain in family records, sacred architecture, prison foundations, maker traditions, or rites whose practitioners no longer use the Sunwarden name.

The order’s modern significance therefore lies less in the recovery of an institution than in a question:

> Who still knows where a prison must end?

## X. Current Assessment

The Sunwardens cannot yet be described as an active continental order. They can be identified as the source of a coherent custodial tradition whose principles remain magically operative and politically dangerous.

Their geometry embodies the limit the Black Writ most fears: the declaration that necessity does not make every person, place, or power available for use.

Further inquiry should distinguish recovery from appropriation. The Ministry has no lawful claim to complete the Sunwarden archive merely because the knowledge could improve Ministerial containment.
