# Surviving Old Court Traditions

### _Living Traces of the Old Beastfolk Pantheon_

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — Living Atlas Division  
**Responsible Office:** Faith & Cultural Practices Bureau  
**Primary Compiler:** Clerk Senna Virel  
**Advisory Editors:** Archivist Yelna Stonevoice, Director Halin Drauve, Observer Drixa Wellfeather

## I. Scope and Current Assessment

The centralized temples, hereditary priesthoods, and public political authority once associated with the **Old Beastfolk Pantheon** no longer operate as a unified religious system.

That institutional collapse does not mean the tradition disappeared.

Old Court worship survives unevenly through household rites, funerary customs, regional observances, inherited language, sacred architecture, claimed devotional communities, and practices whose participants may not describe them as religious at all.

The historical pantheon and its principal deities are documented in [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/01. Fundamentals of Recorded Reality/III. Divinity & The Two Heavens/04. The Old Beastfolk Pantheon|The Old Beastfolk Pantheon]]. Its transformation after divine withdrawal is reconstructed in [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/01. Fundamentals of Recorded Reality/III. Divinity & The Two Heavens/06. Worship After the Silence|Worship After the Silence]]. This Living Atlas record concerns only present-day practice and inheritance.

> [!note] Revision Notice
> Earlier Ministry surveys described the Old Court religion as effectively extinct. Current filing distinguishes the loss of centralized institutions from the survival of worship, custom, memory, and community practice.

## II. Surviving Practice and Cultural Inheritance

### Funerary Customs

Some Beastfolk households continue to wrap the dead in linen, place carved feather-shapes among grave goods, invoke scales or roads during mourning, or preserve rites associated with Anubis, Osiris, Nephthys, and Wepwawet.

The meaning of these customs varies. A household may understand a rite as active devotion, ancestral obligation, family tradition, regional practice, or respectful inheritance without doctrinal commitment.

### Idiom and Ritual Gesture

Phrases such as _“May the Scales Favor You”_ and _“Sun Above, Earth Below”_ remain in circulation. Some Beastfolk touch two fingers to the brow when speaking of death, a gesture associated in surviving records with Anubite invocation.

Continued use does not prove that every speaker knows or accepts the original theology.

### Material Culture

Old Court imagery remains visible in:

- sun-barrel temples and ruined sanctuaries;
- jackal statues, lotus columns, river emblems, and funerary art;
- protective charms associated with crocodiles, cats, falcons, feathers, and solar crowns;
- relic imitations and recovered devotional objects;
- and modern art, clothing, memorials, and commercial decoration.

Sacred meaning may persist, change, or be lost when an image survives its institution.

### Study and Revival

Universities, community historians, delvers, religious specialists, and cultural-custody advocates maintain active interest in the Old Court. Their purposes differ: reconstruction, devotion, heritage preservation, relic study, political revival, artistic use, or opposition to earlier erasure.

No single scholarly or devotional movement speaks for every Beastfolk community.

## III. Reported Devotional Communities

The Ministry has received recurring reports of groups associated with Old Court practice. Evidence and self-identification vary, and none should be treated as representative of the wider Beastfolk population.

### Dust-Cloaked Courts

Reports describe desert-dwelling Jackalfolk who conduct symbolic judgment rites involving the weighing of hearts. Independent verification remains limited.

### River Mothers

Small communities are reported to leave offerings of milk and lotus at dawn in rites associated with Isis and river renewal.

### Crocodile Lodges

Some fishing communities invoke Sobek and use horned-crocodile emblems as protective marks. The relationship between household custom, occupational rite, and organized worship differs by settlement.

### Order of the Obsidian Eye

Stories attribute the protection of tombs, ruins, and old Labyrinth entrances to an unnamed order using Old Court symbols. No stable membership record has been authenticated.

### Solar Choir

Travelers have documented singers whose surviving verses resemble archived hymns of Re-Horakhty. Their language and institutional continuity remain under study.

### Jackalfolk Solitaries

Some reclusive Jackalfolk decline to discuss their ancestry or private religious practice. Refusal is not evidence of secret worship and must not be treated as such.

---

## IV. Interpretation and Cultural Responsibility

Old Court survival cannot be reduced to one hidden church waiting to reappear.

The surviving record includes devotion, custom, ancestry, art, scholarship, speculation, and deliberate revival. These forms may overlap without being identical. A funerary gesture does not establish belief. Use of a divine symbol does not establish priestly authority. Descent from an ancient community does not impose a modern faith.

Researchers must also distinguish community practice from outside fascination. Delver markets and occult collectors often prize Old Court objects because they appear mysterious or forbidden. Their enthusiasm does not grant cultural custody or interpretive authority.

## V. Ministry Assessment

| Category | Current Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Centralized Public Institutions** | No unified institution recognized |
| **Household and Regional Practice** | Uneven but continuing |
| **Cultural Inheritance** | Widespread in some communities; meaning varies |
| **Reported Devotional Groups** | Low-to-moderate confidence depending upon claim |
| **Political Influence** | Localized and generally limited |
| **Archaeological and Cultural Value** | High |
| **Delver and Collector Interest** | Growing; custody concerns apply |

The Ministry does not classify Old Court practice itself as dangerous. Investigation and restriction require evidence of harm, unlawful relic handling, coercion, or another specific offense.

Unauthorized excavation or disturbance of Old Court sites remains prohibited under heritage-preservation and relic-containment law.
