# Delving Classification Crosswalk

## I. Purpose

Modern delving uses several independent classification systems. Repeated letters and numbers do not make those systems equivalent: an A-Rank delver, an A-Class guild, a Category A relic, and a Rank A mission are four different judgments made for four different purposes.

This crosswalk identifies what each system measures, who or what receives the designation, and which record controls its use. When a subject carries several designations, each should be stated separately.

## III. Rules of Interpretation

### Rank Is Not License

UDMI rank describes recorded standing; a license describes legal permission. Promotion in the Index does not by itself waive a separate approval, suspension, site restriction, or mission requirement. The current licensing schedule names no D-Rank license: **D** is a merit standing, while a D-Rank registrant operates under whatever provisional, conditioned, or other clearance appears in their actual license.

### Rank Is Not Assignment

A delver's rank is evidence used when assigning a mission or approving labyrinth entry, but it does not classify the job or the site. A Rank B mission may involve no labyrinth at all. A Tier 3 labyrinth remains Tier 3 regardless of the rank of the team entering it.

### Guild Class Is Organizational

Guild class does not promote every member to the same delver rank. It describes the chartered body's demonstrated capacity and privileges. Likewise, a powerful unchartered faction does not become an A- or S-Class guild merely because it can rival one in the field.

### Threat Words Are Descriptors

Terms such as _low_, _moderate_, _severe_, _extreme_, and _existential_ summarize conditions within a governing system. They are not a free-standing universal ladder. The associated labyrinth tier, mission rank, breach protocol, or other controlling classification must be named.

### Integrity Tier Is Monitoring, Not Merit

An **Integrity Tier** used on an external mission describes the intensity of observation and evidence preservation. It does not measure the honesty of a delver, the authenticity of a document, or the danger of a mission.

### N Is a Designation, Not a Rank

**N-Series Activation** names a Core-observed formation event. **N-Class Team** names the resulting special operational designation. Neither replaces any member's license or UDMI rank, establishes a guild class, or creates a labyrinth tier. Shared N-Class rules are maintained in the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/10. N-Class Protocol|N-Class Protocol]].

## IV. Worked Example

An individual might be an **A-Rank delver** with an **A-Rank license**, serving in a **B-Class guild**, assigned to a **Rank B external mission**, and carrying a **Category C relic** recovered from a **Tier 3-EXL labyrinth**. The mission report might be a **Grade-II record** under **Operationally Restricted** access. None of those labels substitutes for another.

## V. Filing Standard

When a record uses a rank, class, tier, category, grade, or designation, it should identify the system on first use. Avoid bare labels such as “Class 2,” “Tier A,” or “high rank” when the controlling system is not apparent. Exact percentages, timings, and calculated scores are administrative readings produced by named instruments or procedures; they should not be presented as timeless laws of the Labyrinths.
