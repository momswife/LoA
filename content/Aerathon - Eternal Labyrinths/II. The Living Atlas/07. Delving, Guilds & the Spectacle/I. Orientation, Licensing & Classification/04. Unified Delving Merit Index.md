# The Unified Delving Merit Index (UDMI)

The UDMI distinguishes what a delver can reliably do from how the world responds to their work. The [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/03. Arcanet Core|Arcanet Core]] maintains the evidence; Ministry arbiters certify rank. Audience acclaim can win a contract. It cannot turn an unready fighter into a champion.

## I. Rank Spread

**D-Rank, C-Rank, B-Rank, A-Rank, and S-Rank** are the only standard personal ranks. Most delvers occupy D, C, or B. B-Rank is above average; A-Rank is extremely above average. S-Rank describes rare adventurers whose power appears near god-like to ordinary witnesses. It does not establish literal divinity, immortality, or exemption from law.

| Rank | Capability | Place in the profession |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-Rank | Developing field competence | Novices and early-career delvers |
| C-Rank | Independent professional capability | The established working profession |
| B-Rank | Advanced power and specialist judgment | Above-average veterans |
| A-Rank | Extraordinary mastery | Exceptionally rare champions |
| S-Rank | The summit of mortal adventuring power | Near god-like adventurers |

## II. The UDMI Score (0–10,000)

The public merit score remains a changing record of progression, conduct, team contribution, relic work, and audience response. The current model allocates 30% to Labyrinth progression, 20% to audience favor, 15% each to synergy and combat efficiency, 10% to artifact recovery, and 5% to mission integrity; penalties subtract at least 5% where applicable. These are administrative weights, not laws of magic.

Votes and donations affect visibility and opportunity. They do not satisfy a missing capability requirement. A censured S-Rank delver remains dangerously powerful even when their score collapses or their license is revoked.

## III. Rank Thresholds

Capability governs rank. Former score-only cutoffs are superseded; there is no second 1,000-point rank ladder. A score may trigger review, but is not itself promotion. The Index displays certified rank, merit score, and license status separately.

> [!info] Tabletop rank correspondence (OOC)
> These are Aerathon campaign conventions, not official D&D rank rules. Use total character level, including multiclass levels. A newly attained capability is entered on the register after assessment.

| Delver rank | D&D character levels |
| --- | --- |
| D-Rank | 1–4 |
| C-Rank | 5–10 |
| B-Rank | 11–15 |
| A-Rank | 16–19 |
| S-Rank | 20 |

A level-20 non-delver is not automatically a registered S-Rank delver. Lost and retired profiles record their last established rank, not present deployment readiness.

## IV. UDMI Point Accrual

Clears, rescue, recovered evidence, teamwork, and safe solutions contribute. The Core compares outcomes against resources, conditions, civilian harm, and objectives. Repeating low-risk performances does not establish readiness for more dangerous work.

Quiet specialists may submit sealed evidence or request private assessment. A lack of audience footage limits the public record; it does not prove a lack of merit.

## V. Rank Ascension Trials

| Transition | Minimum level (OOC) | Assessment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| D-Rank to C-Rank | 5 | Independent judgment and reliable team contribution |
| C-Rank to B-Rank | 11 | Advanced capability and specialist responsibility |
| B-Rank to A-Rank | 16 | Exceptional mastery under witnessed or sealed trial |
| A-Rank to S-Rank | 20 | Legendary capability established by trial or equivalent authenticated record |

Trials may involve rescue, adaptive hazards, relic retrieval, negotiation under magical pressure, or controlled duels. Lethality and applause are not requirements. Certification does not waive cordons, relic restrictions, suspensions, or another person's refusal of aid.

## VI. Assignments and the Monthly Ledger

Mission recommendations use the same capability bands. Ordinary paid work is **Unranked**, not a sixth rank. A mission states expected team size and roles, hazards, and uncertainty. Level alone cannot establish safety: preparation, terrain, numbers, and objectives matter.

Labyrinth sites retain independent 0–6 tiers. Letter-rated clears in old dossiers are retained as **legacy expedition ratings**. Historical totals do not convert a site to a personal rank or certify the old expedition under the current mission standard.
