# Holo-Stations & the Entrance Trial

## I. What a Holo-Station Does

A **holo-station** is an artificer-built arena that places living participants inside a controlled holographic encounter. Light, sound, responsive illusions, and bounded force effects represent creatures and terrain; operators observe the exercise and can stop or reset it. The participants remain in the physical arena. No passage into a labyrinth is opened, and no actual monster is summoned to supply the performance.

Around Monster Meadows, the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/09. Organizations, Companies & Associations/I. Civic Defense & Emergency Service/01. The Hearthward Compact|Hearthward Compact's]] Lantern Works maintains stations for watch training, paid practice, and [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/V. Broadcasts, Audiences & Sponsorship/05. The Meadow Circuit|Meadow Circuit]] events. Retired delvers return for familiar challenges without committing to another expedition; active delvers book sessions between descents to rehearse with new teammates or test a revised formation.

The same facilities host the **entrance trial**, the controlled Field Aptitude Test used in [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/01. Delving Registration|Delving Registration]]. This is the practical examination for entry into organized, licensed delving. The Compact supplies the arena and technical staff; the Ministry of Delving Operations retains responsibility for examination and certification. Joining a particular guild remains a separate matter.

## II. Inside the Artifice

| Component | Function | Practical limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Projection lattice** | Places apparent terrain, opponents, light, and sound within the arena. | A convincing landscape does not enlarge the room; operators must preserve real movement clearance. |
| **Force anchors** | Provide bounded resistance for impacts, barriers, and some contact with projected creatures. | Resistance has a rated limit. Attacks exceeding it require a different setup or an adjudicated result. |
| **Scenario matrix** | Holds the encounter's terrain, behavior patterns, objectives, and response branches. | It reproduces what its makers understand and have encoded, including their mistakes. |
| **Witness array** | Tracks position, contacts, declared effects, and objective states for review. | A record needs interpretation; a missed signal or poor calibration can distort the apparent result. |
| **Ward envelope** | Helps contain approved weapons, spell effects, and force feedback. | It must be matched to the exercise. Unapproved magic can exceed its protections. |
| **Operator console** | Allows staff to pause, terminate, or reset the exercise and isolate faulty anchors. | An attentive operator remains necessary even during a familiar scenario. |

An apparent fall can be represented by loss of footing, a brief controlled sensation, and a scored removal from play. It does not require a real chasm beneath the floor. Poison, curses, and effects that would be unsafe to reproduce are declared or marked as conditions. A simulated wound may disable an arm for the exercise without inflicting the wound itself.

Participants still move, tire, collide, and cast within a real room. Holo training can produce bruises, strains, exhaustion, or injury from mishandled equipment. Safety depends on calibration and restraint as well as wards; the word *holographic* is not a promise of invulnerability.

## III. Building an Encounter

Scenario keepers work from field accounts, available recordings, and the experience of instructors. Tiber oversees the Compact's magical training standards, while Anya tests whether a scenario teaches useful decisions under pressure. Artificers then translate the brief into movements and responses the station can sustain.

Their personal interests also inform this work: [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/10. Notable Figures/II. Military & Civic Defense/02. Tiberius Lykoi|Tiberius]] is fascinated by how labyrinths function, while [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/10. Notable Figures/II. Military & Civic Defense/01. Anya Lykoi|Anya Lykoi]] values the martial challenge of unpredictable conditions. Both bring experience of delving for the protection of threatened communities.

The married leaders' personal involvement is a major part of the stations' reputation. Delvers seek out training shaped by Anya Lykoi's battle mastery and Tiberius's command of bladesong; veteran instructors carry their methods into ordinary sessions. The couple's leadership gives the workshops a common purpose: turn hard-earned experience into instruction that another generation can use.

A good scenario is built around a problem: a road that must remain open, a frightened civilian who will not move, or a creature whose pursuit can be broken without killing it. A poor one merely produces opponents until everyone is tired.

Replays allow an instructor to return to a particular decision and ask a team to try another response. The room can be reset; the participants' fatigue cannot. Sessions therefore include real rest, equipment checks, and debriefing.

Holo-stations do not possess the authority or fidelity attributed to the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/03. Arcanet Core|Arcanet Core's Ascendancy Trials]]. They are constructed exercises with finite behavior libraries and fallible operators. Unknown creatures, divine interventions, and unstable Rift phenomena may be represented for discussion without being faithfully reproduced. A team that memorizes one projected beast has mastered that scenario, not every living example of the species.

## IV. Modes of Use

**Watch training** emphasizes interception, communication, civilian protection, relief of an exhausted teammate, and withdrawal. Its difficulty is matched to the duties being practiced. A veteran preparing for transport work does not need to pass the same exercise as a frontline patrol member.

**Open practice** lets delvers rent supervised sessions. Training partners can repeat an encounter, compare approaches, or ask an instructor to vary the conditions. Equipment and spell limits are agreed before activation. Public challenge boards record the scenario and permitted loadout alongside the result so that unlike performances are not presented as equivalent.

**Exhibition mode** supports spectators, commentary, and announced scoring. The projection is identified as a simulation. Exhibition settings are not used to certify entrants simply because a performance happens to impress an examiner.

**Examination mode** uses controlled objectives, restricted operator changes, and an examination record. Coaching stops when assessment begins. Paid rehearsal provides practice, but renting more arena time does not purchase a passing result.

## V. The Entrance Trial

### Before the Arena

The registration process still governs identity checks, capability screening, the ethical oath, resonance binding, and final issuance. The holo-station implements the practical test within that process; it does not replace the other requirements.

Candidates receive an equipment inspection and a plain explanation of the stop signal, physical boundaries, simulated injury rules, and permitted magic. Calibration allows for stature, movement, sensory needs, and approved assistive equipment while preserving the skills the test must assess. A short ungraded exercise establishes that the candidate can perceive the station's cues and that the apparatus responds correctly.

As required by the registration record, the test is overseen by **one MDO licensing official, one remote divine auditor, and one Oculi recorder**. The Lantern Works operator manages the apparatus. A Compact instructor may help prepare candidates, but does not replace the licensing official's judgment.

### The Practical Sequence

The following is the Compact's standard entrance sequence. Examiners vary the presentation so that candidates must recognize the problem rather than recite a remembered route.

| Stage | Encounter | What the examiner looks for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Read the Ground** | A route contains warning signs, uncertain movement, and a defensible alternative. | Observation, communication, and willingness to investigate before committing. |
| **Hold the Gap** | A small hostile group presses toward a protected passage. | Basic combat control, spacing, and coordination rather than maximum damage. |
| **Bring Them Home** | A projected casualty or civilian must be moved while danger remains nearby. | Rescue priorities, delegation, and attention to people who cannot keep pace. |
| **Know What to Spare** | A creature's appearance suggests danger, but its behavior permits a less harmful response. | Restraint and the ability to revise an initial judgment. |
| **Answer the Retreat** | Conditions worsen and an explicit withdrawal order ends the objective. | Recognition of limits, orderly retreat, and accounting for the whole party. |

Candidates undertake team tasks with fellow applicants or assigned support participants. Examiners record individual decisions as well as the party outcome, so a dominant teammate cannot conceal another candidate's inability to communicate or respond safely.

The trial is calibrated for entry. It is not a contest to see whether a beginner can defeat a veteran's favorite monster. A support specialist can demonstrate competence through protection, healing decisions, information, or rescue while still meeting the registration requirements relevant to their capabilities.

### Assessment and Retesting

Examiners assess **control, awareness, cooperation, restraint, and emergency response**. The witness record supports that assessment; damage totals and completion time do not decide it alone. A candidate who wins the fight while leaving the protected passage open has failed the central task.

Deliberately endangering a teammate, ignoring a clear stop command, or using prohibited magic ends the attempt. An apparatus fault pauses or invalidates the affected exercise rather than becoming evidence against the candidate. Afterward, the examiner explains which capabilities were demonstrated and which need further work.

Failure delays certification and permits a later attempt, consistent with registration policy. A pass records practical readiness for the remaining licensing decision. It does not itself award a particular rank, replace the Arcane ID Sigil, enroll the candidate in the Compact, or secure membership in a guild.

## VI. The Lesson Outside the Score

Many beginners arrive expecting the last round to contain the largest monster. Instead, the light changes, an exit appears, and the instruction is to leave. The opponent may still look beatable. That is part of the exercise: applicants must show they can hear an order over the promise of a victory.

After the room clears, an instructor can restore the scene and point to the teammate who would have been stranded or the route that was about to close. For the Compact's veterans, this is the station's greatest value. Experience that once cost a party its healer, scout, or friend can become a lesson someone else survives long enough to use.
