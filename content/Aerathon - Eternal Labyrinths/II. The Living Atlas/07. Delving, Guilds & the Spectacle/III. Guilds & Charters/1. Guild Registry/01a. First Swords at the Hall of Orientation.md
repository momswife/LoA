# First Swords at the Hall of Orientation

## I. First Swords Welcome

At the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/III. Guilds & Charters/1. Guild Registry/01. The Free Company|Free Company of Louvain]], the words **First Swords Welcome** greet applicants at gates of bluesteel and ashwood. The welcome includes people whose useful work may never involve a sword. Porters, healers, scholars, and spellcasters enter with the same uncertainty about whether they will belong.

Beyond the gates lies the **Courtyard of First Oaths**, sheltered beneath a shimmering dome. A spiral mosaic worked in silverleaf anchors a space crowded with hopefuls, clerks, and trainers. Projected maps and delver highlights compete with shouted directions. Outside, vendors and performers make a living from the intake crowds; a song about guild standards can carry a warning as readily as an advertisement.

## II. A Name Called Aloud

Ministry representatives attend intake alongside Company staff. **Overseer Atria Vael** is known for an exacting professional manner, a crystal clipboard, and a monocle that gives a faltering introduction nowhere comfortable to hide. Oculi recorders and Arcanet scribes make the ceremony part of the administrative record.

Applicants receive a medallion bound to their identifying resonance. A public presentation is one stage of intake, not proof that every licensing requirement has been completed. [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/01. Delving Registration|Delving Registration]] remains the governing process; a provisional cohort may receive its identifying tokens before practical assessment.

The medallion ceremony attracts proud speeches, awkward bows, displays of magic, and very brief acknowledgments. None determines personal rank. The current standard runs D-Rank, C-Rank, B-Rank, A-Rank, and S-Rank; the Ministry’s licensing authority remains distinct from the Company’s role as host and trainer.

## III. Finding the Right Door

**Breez Bramblegear** assists with placement and orientation. Breez’s mixture of Ministry and Company dress, theatrical introductions, and orbiting, talkative clipboard makes the administrative handover difficult to mistake for a solemn second ceremony. Sarcasm often arrives before the useful instruction, but the useful instruction does arrive.

The residential wing includes the **Ember-3 Block**, with charm-key access, lockboxes, a vending orb, and enchanted pillows. Gentle light runes cross the tiled passages; spellwax, polish, and lavender linger in the vents. These comforts make room for the ordinary business of unpacking and learning the names beyond the next door.

Breez’s warnings against nocturnal bargains and sewer rituals belong to the hall’s practical vocabulary. An applicant’s immediate task is usually less remarkable: keep the key, read the assignment, and arrive where the notice says to arrive.

## IV. Before the First Assessment

Cohorts may be directed to the **South Tower Annex** for a dawn field assessment. The reporting place and time belong to the assignment; they are not a universal schedule for every licensing examination in Aerathon. Likewise, travel restrictions attached to a provisional placement must be read as that placement’s conditions, not assumed to bind every delver.

The quiet hours before an assessment reveal a different kind of readiness. Someone checks the route, someone remembers a meal, and someone discovers that the person who seemed fearless in the courtyard also needs reassurance. Those small acts are part of learning to return together.
