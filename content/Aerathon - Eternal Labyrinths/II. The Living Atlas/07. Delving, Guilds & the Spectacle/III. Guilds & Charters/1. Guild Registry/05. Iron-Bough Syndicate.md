# The Iron-Bough Syndicate

![[13c8b5b2-ffce-4253-ad91-779645e598c8.png]]

## I. Overview

Founded in 3289 A.D., the Iron-Bough Syndicate works from Hearth-Anchor beneath the Arnerian plateau. Lord Harven Dross directs its commercial strategy, with Eyre Vaskel leading engineering and Thull Granitepaw overseeing operations. Drills, lifts, automatons, and repair crews make its influence felt wherever access can be mechanized.

The steel branch bearing gears in place of leaves on its emblem promises growth through machinery. A working lift can save lives; a badly priced maintenance agreement can leave a whole settlement dependent on the company that installed it. The Syndicate’s record contains both the practical gains and the cost of pushing production past the evidence.

## III. Founding & Philosophy

The Syndicate was founded in 3289 A.D. by **Harven Dross**, a dwarven relic-smith expelled from the Collegia for “excessive utilitarianism.”  
He and a cadre of like-minded engineers established the first **auto-delve operations**, replacing flesh labor with steel.  
Their belief: _the labyrinths are finite problems awaiting finite solutions._

### The Iron Creed

1. **Build, Break, Repeat.**

2. **Perfection is a prototype.**

3. **If it can’t be measured, it can’t be trusted.**

4. **Emotion rusts—grease it with logic.**

Dross’ mantra, _“Progress Grows from Steel,”_ became both rallying cry and warning.

## IV. Organization & Command

The Syndicate runs more like a corporation than a guild, governed by a **Board of Forges**, each chair representing a major discipline:

|**Chair**|**Director**|**Focus**|
|---|---|---|
|**Forge Chair**|Harven Dross|Policy, relic patents, Ministry negotiation.|
|**Gear Chair**|Eyre Vaskel|Automation, mechanical design, field robotics.|
|**Anvil Chair**|Thull Granitepaw|Labor, logistics, and guild discipline.|
|**Spark Chair**|Mira Jassine|Arcane infusion, containment energy, breach reactors.|
|**Root Chair**|Pel Torrin (Beastfolk Scholar)|Geological mapping and seismic delving.|
All members are salaried engineers; delvers function as field contractors paid by _yield percentage_.

## V. Operations & Technology

### 1. Auto-Delving Programs

Hearth-Anchor deploys self-navigating constructs known as **Ironroots**, semi-sentient drilling machines that chart labyrinth corridors while transmitting data to surface control towers.  
Each Ironroot carries a living pilot-core—a crystalline graft grown from a delver’s memories—creating an eerie half-conscious workforce.

### 2. Relic Processing & Fabrication

Unlike most guilds, the Syndicate **melts down relics** into component materials, repurposing them for new devices.  
This practice violates §2-B (Reliquary Safety) but continues under _Ministerial waiver #97-H_.  
Their forges produce the modern world’s rails, siege engines, and containment turbines.

### 3. The “Green Clause” Program

In reluctant cooperation with the **Verdant Collision**, the Syndicate has adopted a quota of **environmental restitution** per quarter.  
Every tenth delve must include surface restoration efforts—typically outsourced back to the Collision for profit symmetry.

---

## VI. Relationship with the Ministry

The Ministry regards the Iron-Bough with **simultaneous admiration and anxiety**.  
Their inventions enable containment, but their autonomy threatens monopoly.  
Several Arcane Breach Division devices—such as the _Prism Pressurizer_ and _Runic Excavation Frame_—originated here.

Lt. Korrin’s confidential note:

> _“If they ever stop selling to us, we’ll still be paying them for the tools they left behind.”_

## VIII. Economic Influence

The Syndicate controls nearly **60 %** of Aerathon’s industrial relic conversion market.  
They maintain export agreements with three nations and fifteen guilds.  
Their private army of mechanized guardians—the **Rustguard**—protects trade convoys and occasionally “discourages” Ministry inspection.

### Subsidiary Companies

- **Ironbough Forgeworks** (Arms Manufacture)

- **Deepline Transit Co.** (Labyrinth Transport Systems)

- **Thorn-Tine Logistics** (Inter-guild Freight)

## IX. Cultural Identity

Despite its corporate face, the Iron-Bough preserves a deep dwarven and beastfolk pride in craft.  
Work is worship. Each machine is named and blessed before activation; when one fails, it is buried with a craftsman’s token and oil-lamp vigil.

Their workers’ prayer:

> _“Let no rust outlive me.”_

Festivals such as the **Hammerwake Parade** celebrate completed constructs, where guildsmen march beside their inventions through rivers of molten light.

---

## X. Criticism & Controversy

- Accused of **excessive relic dismantling**, erasing historical artifacts.

- Linked to **mechanical accidents** in Arnerian mines (14 confirmed fatalities at an unresolved date (legacy event numbering 1422)).

- Allegedly funds lobbyists within the Ministry to weaken §2-B safety clauses.

- Internal documents suggest prototypes of **self-aware constructs** nearing sentience, in violation of Faithfield Ethics.

Despite scandals, public reliance on their infrastructure ensures continued impunity.

---

## XII. Outlook

The present projection places the Syndicate near S-Class qualification by 3398 A.D.; this remains a forecast, not an awarded charter.\
Ministry reformists urge restraint; economists predict inevitability.  
If they ascend, Aerathon may face a new paradox:  
**a world built so efficiently that even the labyrinths go unemployed.**

> _“When the hammers stop, listen—  
> that silence will be the world’s new heartbeat.”_  
> — Harven Dross

## Working With the Guild

Engineers and field delvers receive different responsibilities; neither a job title nor the guild’s A-Class charter determines personal rank. Repairs, shutdown authority, and evacuation arrangements belong in the operating agreement.

The current internal argument is whether crews may halt a profitable machine when its readings are inconclusive. A preserved incident records fourteen fatalities, but its legacy event numbering does not establish a civil date. The missing date must not be used to erase the loss or to invent a chronology that predates the guild.
