# Damas Orr

## I. The Quiet Between

Damas Orr is a dwarven S-Rank delver formerly employed tuning relay halls and theatrical resonators. He learned that a working too powerful to overpower might still depend on a repeated interval. His field career began when he kept a damaged relay from repeating an aetheric surge long enough for its operators to escape.

He remains attentive to small sounds: a loose latch, a cup set down too hard, an apprentice who has stopped asking questions. He dislikes being called the man who silences everything. Silence, he says, is often where one hears the unresolved fault.

## II. Interruption and Its Price

Damas uses paired tuning forks and a suspended ring of graduated metal tongues to identify and oppose a magical recurrence. Once he has measured its pattern, he can interrupt a working of extraordinary scale. A new pattern, a concealed source, or an intentionally irregular sequence can defeat the preparation.

The returning resonance passes through his equipment and his own senses. Prolonged work leaves him unable to judge pitch or balance reliably. He uses written signals and a spotter afterward; continuing on confidence alone risks opposing the wrong interval. His instruments cannot restore a person already killed or cancel a law simply because someone recites it aloud.

## III. Affiliations and Temperament

He takes independent containment contracts and consults for the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/III. Guilds & Charters/1. Guild Registry/07. Emberlight Union|Emberlight Union]]. The Union wants his methods to protect public venues. Damas wants repair crews trained to recognize when a show must end. Neither position is especially popular with a promoter facing a full house.

He is generous with practical instruction and guarded about the most dangerous sequences. His refusal to publish those sequences has become a dispute with former students who believe he is preserving his own importance. He cannot dismiss every critic as careless; some have spent longer repairing his equipment than he has explaining his decisions.

## IV. Present Concern

Several inexpensive relay replacements reproduce a harmless-looking timing fault. Damas is assembling a comparison of the installed units before a major public performance. He needs intact samples and honest maintenance logs. A stolen replacement proves little without knowing how it was fitted.

His work gives a crew a window in which to shut down or evacuate a failing system. It does not make him an unlimited source of antimagic.
