# The Meadow Circuit

## I. A Grandstand Beside the Watch

The **Meadow Circuit** is the tournament and exhibition program operated by the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/09. Organizations, Companies & Associations/I. Civic Defense & Emergency Service/01. The Hearthward Compact|Hearthward Compact]] outside the quarantine perimeter of [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/2. Allemance/3. Geography & Landmarks/10. Monster Meadows|Monster Meadows]]. Its regular cards combine contests against intercepted monsters with holographic challenges, veteran bouts, and team defense trials.

Across Allemance, it is one of the largest broadcast attractions outside live delving. Audiences come for familiar champions, emerging teams, and the unusual pleasure of watching expertise ripen rather than disappear into retirement. A former delver can become famous again for reading a charge correctly or bringing every teammate through a difficult retreat.

**Anya Lykoi and Tiberius**, the Compact's celebrated married leaders, are central to that appeal. Their appearances draw viewers who know Anya's battle mastery or Tiber's bladesinging by reputation, while their stewardship gives competitors confidence in the event behind the spectacle. Together they have made the Circuit a place where veterans can earn recognition and a livelihood while sustaining the defense of the surrounding communities.

That public recognition coexists with their dislike of televised delving's superficial pursuit of fame and glory. [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/10. Notable Figures/II. Military & Civic Defense/01. Anya Lykoi|Anya]] and [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/10. Notable Figures/II. Military & Civic Defense/02. Tiberius Lykoi|Tiber]] have themselves delved to protect threatened communities. They support the Circuit for the monsters it helps manage, the defenders it trains, and the watch it sustains; its celebrity culture remains a source of tension for both.

The crowd has its own tastes. It loves a spectacular finishing stroke, but an old shield bearer holding a gate long enough for the last cart to pass can bring the stands to their feet. Teams acquire followings through recognizable habits: impeccable rescues, stubborn formations, or a veteran's habit of scolding opponents made entirely of light.

## II. Live Monsters and the Outer Rings

The Rift has remained open since the 3377 Pulse Ascension, with active openings recurring at regular intervals. The Circuit's live-monster tournaments help manage the resulting flow of creatures, making competition part of the Compact's ongoing defense work. Live-monster cards use hostile creatures intercepted during these surges or after escaping containment elsewhere in the Compact's local response area. Only creatures that can be assessed and held within the outer arenas are eligible. Unknown hazards, unstable manifestations, and threats beyond the wards' capacity remain emergency or specialist work.

The ring is a controlled engagement ground where qualified teams can confront an already contained threat. A creature is never released into the countryside to begin a round. The field captain and ward-tenders must both clear an encounter, and the duty watch remains staffed independently of the competitors.

A bout may require a team to stop a charge, defend a marked refuge, secure a capture, or defeat a creature whose safe containment cannot be maintained indefinitely. Its objective is announced before entry. Marshals judge control, coordination, and protection of the defended space as well as completion. A quick kill does not erase a broken defensive line.

The tournament schedule follows the Rift's recurring openings, with the wardens arranging controlled engagements to manage the monsters that emerge. The composition of each surge still determines which live bouts can proceed; unsuitable or empty slots become holo challenges or veteran exhibitions. The Compact does not provoke the Rift, disturb peaceful herds, or delay an urgent response to fill a bill. A surge beyond the arenas' capacity or a major awakening cancels the Circuit and turns its grounds toward evacuation and relief.

## III. The Program

| Event | Format | What draws the audience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fence Trials** | Recurring team contests against eligible intercepted monsters, with an announced defensive objective. | Real creatures, disciplined teamwork, and the possibility that an unfashionable formation beats a celebrity lineup. |
| **Last Lantern** | Holo-station endurance rounds ending in a compulsory extraction. Teams choose how much ground to surrender before the final withdrawal. | The decision to leave can be more dramatic than the decision to fight. |
| **Old Blades Open** | Veteran and off-duty delver competition using matched holo scenarios; entrants declare equipment and exercise limits. | Familiar names adapting to new partners and younger challengers. |
| **The Homeward Cup** | A larger team tournament combining a hold, a rescue, and a retreat across successive rounds. | Balanced parties have room to challenge teams built around a single star. |
| **Lanternwrights' Showing** | Artificer demonstrations of new training environments, followed by supervised public trials. | Visitors watch a bare floor become a bridge, a stormbound road, or a corridor full of moving shadows. |
| **The Empty Chair Muster** | A gathering for absent companions, shared meals, equipment repair, and relief fundraising. | Former party members reunite without needing to enter a competitive ring. |

Live and simulated results have separate standings. The Homeward Cup's published card identifies which rounds use holographic opponents; no projected monster is advertised as a captured one. If a live round is withdrawn, organizers announce the replacement and its scoring basis before teams compete.

## IV. A Tournament Day

The day's first public spectacle is usually a ward inspection. To an impatient visitor, it looks like people walking around an empty field. Regulars watch the artificers instead of the grass: a second inspection means a late start, and a rolled-up signal banner means the card is changing.

Competitors enter through the same service yard used by patrols. There are polished guild colors among the old cloaks, but the Compact's marshals make everyone demonstrate the withdrawal signal. Famous delvers occasionally resent being tested on so simple a thing. The crowd enjoys seeing them tested anyway.

Between rounds, healers work, teams argue over replays, and the holo-stations sell sessions to spectators convinced they have identified an easy mistake. Many emerge with new respect for the person they were heckling. Veteran instructors do a brisk trade in explaining why standing three feet farther left mattered more than the sword.

When the Rift Watch sounds the Retreat Call, competition stops. The broadcast gives evacuation instructions, marshals open the homeward routes, and cleared competitors help move the crowd. The signal is never reused as an entertainment cue.

## V. Broadcasts, Purses, and Public Trust

Scrying coverage carries the Circuit to public viewing halls and distant followers through the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/06. Magic, Technology & Infrastructure/II. Scrytech & Public Communication/03. Grand Scrying Network (GSN)|Grand Scrying Network]]. Revenue pays both competitors and the organization maintaining the outer defense. Training bookings, arena concessions, and sponsorship make the grounds commercially valuable even when no live card can proceed.

Audience recognition can revive a retired delver's livelihood. It can also turn instructors into performers who spend more time preparing a marketable maneuver than teaching a dependable one. The Circuit's attraction rests on visible danger, while its continued existence rests on containing that danger. Arguments about purse sizes, closure decisions, and sponsor influence are therefore part of its ordinary public life.

Circuit standings are competition records, not automatic changes to a delver's license or [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/04. Unified Delving Merit Index|UDMI]] rank. A famous exhibition does not authorize a deeper descent. Broadcasting and betting remain subject to the existing [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/V. Broadcasts, Audiences & Sponsorship/04. Broadcast Manipulation Laws|broadcast rules]].

The entrance examinations held in the nearby holo-stations are kept separate from the entertainment program. Applicants are not required to win an audience before they can begin a profession.
