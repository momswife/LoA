# Tiberius Lykoi

**Filed Division:** _Living Atlas — Notable Figures_

## I. Profile

**Tiberius Lykoi**, commonly called **Tiber**, is a [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/02. Peoples, Lineages & Cultures/I. Peoples & Lineages/4. Brethren/12. Tiefling|tiefling]] bladesinging wizard and one of Allemance's most notable defenders. As **Ward Chair** of the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/09. Organizations, Companies & Associations/I. Civic Defense & Emergency Service/01. The Hearthward Compact|Hearthward Compact]], he leads its magical defense and works with the artificers responsible for its training systems. He runs the organization jointly with his wife, the battle master [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/10. Notable Figures/II. Military & Civic Defense/01. Anya Lykoi|Anya Lykoi]], whose surname he deliberately chose to take.

Tiber has entered labyrinths to address the danger they posed to nearby communities. He shares Anya's conviction that the people threatened by a labyrinth matter more than the glory available within it. He also finds the Labyrinths fascinating. Their inner workings give him questions that cannot always be answered from the safe side of an entrance.

That curiosity is a personal source of enjoyment. A labyrinth can be a threat requiring intervention and an extraordinary thing worth understanding at the same time. Tiber's interest in how it works does not lessen his commitment to those it might harm.

## II. Curiosity in Service of Defense

Tiber's approach begins with attention to the conditions of an encounter: what is changing, what appears to cause the change, and what the defenders can actually rely upon. Around [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/2. Allemance/3. Geography & Landmarks/10. Monster Meadows|Monster Meadows]], where an open Rift produces recurring surges, that attention has a practical purpose. Understanding a danger can improve the next response even when it cannot remove the danger altogether.

Theories about the Meadows range from resonant scars to sleeping entities. Tiber's fascination belongs in that difficult space between observable behavior and uncertain explanation. His work as Ward Chair requires systems that can be inspected and tested, regardless of which grand theory eventually proves persuasive.

He brings the same distinction to instruction. An exercise can teach a useful response without proving that its makers understand every creature or phenomenon it represents. A successful simulation gives a team something to practice; it does not make the Labyrinths predictable.

### The Pleasure of a Difficult Question

Tiber becomes more animated when a familiar explanation stops working. A recruit who notices an inconsistency can hold his attention more readily than a visitor reciting his accomplishments. He enjoys following an observation through its possible causes, and his explanations can grow considerably longer than the question that prompted them. When he catches himself doing it, he tries to return to the part his listener actually needs.

That enthusiasm is also a weakness he must manage. He can remain with a problem after others have accepted that they have enough information to act. A second reading, another comparison, or one more controlled test is always tempting. His responsibility as Ward Chair requires him to decide when a question can wait. Anya is particularly good at asking him what the answer would change about the people waiting outside.

His patience with honest uncertainty makes him approachable to students, though he has less patience for certainty performed to impress an audience. He would rather hear a nervous artificer admit that they cannot explain a fault than listen to a polished assurance that conceals it. The distinction matters personally to him: being trusted should make it easier to speak plainly.

## III. Bladesong and the Lantern Works

Tiber's reputation as a bladesinger draws both experienced practitioners and aspiring students. His instruction connects movement with awareness of the whole encounter. A beautiful evasion is incomplete work if it leaves a companion exposed. The value of precision lies in what it allows the team to do next.

Within the Compact, he oversees magical readiness and works with the **Lantern Works**, whose artificers maintain arena protections and the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/I. Orientation, Licensing & Classification/01a. Holo-Stations & the Entrance Trial|holo-stations]]. His contribution is one of magical judgment and training direction within a collective effort. The stations depend on their operators, scenario keepers, and craftspeople as well as on their celebrated overseer.

Tiber and Anya approach a proposed exercise from complementary directions. He examines what the environment and its magical effects represent; she tests whether the encounter demands useful decisions from its participants. Together they help turn the experiences of veteran delvers into lessons that can be repeated without sending every beginner into the original danger.

As Ward Chair, Tiber decides whether the Compact's magical systems are fit to operate. The right to halt an event is part of that responsibility. A full grandstand does not change the capacity of an anchor or repair a failing ward.

## IV. Fame and the Work It Supports

Tiber strongly dislikes the superficiality of televised delving. He has little interest in making a descent into a performance of fame and glory. The workings of a labyrinth deserve more attention than its value as a dramatic backdrop, and the people threatened by it deserve more consideration than a distant audience's appetite for spectacle.

His leadership of the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/07. Delving, Guilds & the Spectacle/V. Broadcasts, Audiences & Sponsorship/05. The Meadow Circuit|Meadow Circuit]] places that conviction alongside a practical dependence on broadcasts. The tournaments help manage the emerging monsters, support training, and fund the wardens. Tiber accepts the usefulness of that system while remaining critical of the habits it encourages.

He and Anya are widely recognized because their work has been successful. Their names attract attention, but the achievement behind them is durable: a dependable defense force, respected instruction, and an organization able to sustain its people. The MDO trusts their leadership and leaves local defensive discretion and recruitment in the Compact's hands.

## V. Marriage and Family

### Choosing Lykoi

**Lykoi was Anya's surname before their marriage. Tiber chose to take it.** People who assume that a wife has taken her husband's name sometimes reverse that history. For Tiber, the decision was intentional and personal, shaped by hardship within his own family in the past.

The particulars of that hardship are not part of this public account. Its significance to his choice is clear: he wanted the name he carried forward to belong to the family he was choosing to build with Anya. Lykoi connects his public accomplishments to that commitment. He carries it as his own name, with full acknowledgment of where it came from.

He is willing to correct the assumption that Anya acquired it from him. That correction does not oblige him to explain his earlier family life. His public reputation offers strangers many things to discuss; it does not give them ownership of the reasons behind every personal decision.

### A Partnership With Room to Disagree

Tiber and Anya share a protective purpose without having identical reasons for enjoying a delve. He is drawn to the Labyrinths' inner workings; she appreciates the opportunity to sharpen her abilities against unpredictability. Their marriage allows room for those different pleasures. Anya can enjoy watching him become absorbed in a question even when she has no wish to spend the evening answering it.

Their disagreements often concern pace. Tiber wants to understand enough to avoid acting on a false premise; Anya wants to know when further deliberation starts costing someone else time. Either tendency can become excessive. Their confidence in one another lets them name that excess without treating disagreement as disloyalty.

Tiber also knows that Anya's willingness to take responsibility can outlast her strength. He can take a practical task off her hands, insist that another capable warden finish a review, or remain beside her without asking her to turn an old loss into a lesson. Their marriage includes the ordinary work of allowing someone formidable to be tired.

The division between Field Chair and Ward Chair gives their professional partnership a clear shape. The marriage beneath it rests on a more personal trust: each can be corrected, helped, and cared for without surrendering the qualities the other first valued.

They have a son, **Corvo Lykoi**, a Chikitu who has recently become a delver.
