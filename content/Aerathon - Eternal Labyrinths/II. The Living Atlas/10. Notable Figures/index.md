# Notable Figures

This section is the home for individual profiles of important people whose principal public role lies outside professional delving. It includes civic leaders, wardens, scholars, religious figures, inventors, merchants, performers, and people whose influence operates beyond lawful institutions. Notability records influence and significance, not moral approval or official rank.

## Categories of Public Life

The following categories define the filing scheme. Category folders are opened when individual profiles are ready; this index provides orientation while the collection grows.

| Category | People filed here |
| --- | --- |

| **II. Military & Civic Defense** | Commanders, wardens, protectors, emergency-service leaders, and veterans notable for defense outside professional delving. |
| **III. Faith & Religious Leadership** | Clergy, spiritual teachers, living religious leaders, and people influential in contemporary religious movements. |

| **VII. Underworld & Clandestine Figures** | Outlaws, criminal leaders, spies, and covert actors whose established public record supports a profile. |

## Filing Boundaries

Keep one canonical personal profile, categorized by the person's principal established role. Cross-link secondary roles from the relevant institutions, settlements, and subject indexes. A ruler who practices magic belongs with political figures when their importance rests on governing; an inventor who serves on a council may still belong with scholarship and invention.

A person's biography and the record of their office, house, faith, or organization have different subjects. Institutional records retain mandates, succession rules, structures, and collective history; personal profiles contain individual character, relationships, career, and accomplishments.

Historical figures remain in the Annals. Living Atlas profiles concern the present-day setting and may retain the earlier history needed to explain a person's current role. Public profiles of clandestine figures must preserve the distinction between established information, allegations, and restricted knowledge.
