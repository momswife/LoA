# College of Many Hearths

## I. Institutional Overview

The **College of Many Hearths** is a university based in [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/5. Vinyot/1. Subregions/10. Calorwood/1. Settlements/03. Bellariva|Bellariva]]. It is the principal teaching and examination institution of [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/02. Peoples, Lineages & Cultures/II. Cultural Systems & Traditions/02. Hearth Witchcraft and the Returning Roads|hearth witchcraft and the Returning Roads]]. That standing is recognized within its associated hearths; it does not establish authority over all Aerathonian witches or other magical academies.

The college's public promise is to leave behind someone who can carry the work forward. Its graduates are expected to understand, adapt, maintain, and teach useful magic. Powerful casting alone is insufficient evidence of readiness.

## II. Hearth, Coven & Common Court

A **hearth** is a continuing teaching household or workshop. It may be led by one teacher or several practitioners with different specialties. A **coven** is the small group of witches who undertake shared work and responsibility; an apprentice coven normally remains together through changes of host.

The Common Court provides libraries, specialist equipment, an infirmary, and places for collective instruction. Daily teaching remains distributed among hearths. A student's regular mentor knows their habits and difficulties, while visiting teachers challenge the limits of that mentor's experience.

Hearths differ greatly in resources and magical strength. A modest kitchen practitioner may supervise a lesson that a renowned ward-maker is unqualified to teach. Students can seek a change of hearth through the college without first obtaining their current teacher's permission.

## III. Leadership & Support

The **Assembly of Hearths** brings together affiliated teachers and delegates from hosting communities. It appoints the rector, reviews teaching standards, and hears disputes about exchange obligations. Student representatives participate in matters concerning lodging, labor, access, and complaints.

**Rector Elira Venn**, a teacher of wards and domestic enchantment, favors broad admission while insisting that new placements have adequate supervision. **Roadkeeper Sava Dren** arranges host invitations and can suspend a journey when welcome, transport, or safe accommodation falls through. **Keeper of Works Teren Ossa**, a ceramicist and enchantment repairer, oversees shared tools and argues that maintenance should count equally with original invention in examinations.

Fees, paid work, donations, and contributions from affiliated hearths sustain the college. Bursaries cover instruction and some travel. Service placements have stated hours and tasks; they are not an unlimited claim on a student's labor. Funding remains uneven, and poorer hearths cannot host visitors as often as their expertise warrants.

## IV. Admission & Study

Admission considers attention, persistence, practical judgment, and willingness to learn. Applicants need not possess a particular lineage, faith, gender, or inherited magical gift. Access to foci and collaborative practice can support learning, but admission does not promise equal aptitude, effortless casting, or exemption from magical limits.

Initial study joins ordinary craft to the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/01. Fundamentals of Recorded Reality/II. Laws of a Changed World/02. Mortal Magic and the Three Weaves|Three Weaves]]. Students identify a target, form an effect, account for its power, and release it safely. A kettle, a seam, or a shutter can support serious instruction because its ordinary purpose supplies constraints against which a spell can be tested.

Early examinations ask students to solve a bounded household problem, explain the cost, provide a shutoff, and return after the work has been used. Advanced examinations require teaching another person and adapting a method to unfamiliar conditions.

## V. The Returning Roads

The **Returning Roads** are reciprocal teaching journeys, not a fixed road system or a network of portals. A term alternates between work at the home hearth, residence with invited hosts, and review upon return.

Hosts choose what they teach and which practices may be recorded or shared. Local teachers retain credit and authority over participation in their own rites. College approval does not replace permission to enter a community, use a sacred place, or perform regulated magic.

## VI. Teaching Traditions & Collective Work

The college recognizes overlapping teaching traditions rather than assigning students to permanent houses by temperament.

- **Menders** study repair, reinforcement, and continuity.
- **Lanternkeepers** study guidance, thresholds, and safe passage.
- **Rainbinders** study water handling, storage, drainage, and bounded weather work.
- **Stillroom practitioners** study cultivation, preservation, and patient transformations.
- **Marginalists** specialize in adapting established methods to unusual circumstances.

Each tradition uses several Weaves. Students learn across them, and none grants a fixed rank or exclusive claim to a kind of magic.

During a **Gathering of Hearths**, visitors share meals, demonstrations, and unresolved practical questions. Younger witches often connect methods encountered on different journeys. Experienced teachers remain responsible for containment and supervision while unfamiliar combinations are tested.

## VII. Artifacts, Delving & Public Service

The college accepts recovered objects as possible sources of knowledge and useful power. Its position is that recovery begins a responsibility: origin, custody, conditions of use, maintenance, and consequences must be examined.

Members may participate in properly authorized delving, but enrollment is not a delver license. Expedition work supports the college's educational and civic mandate rather than defining it. Most placements involve inhabited places.

Inspection and research follow the distinction between [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/06. Magic, Technology & Infrastructure/I. Everyday Magic & Artifice/02. Relics, Resonance, & Modern Artifice|relics and modern artifice]]. A promising recovered effect may never become safe, reproducible, or appropriate for public use. Conversely, a manufactured device that reduces pain or labor is not rejected merely because it replaces a traditional service.

## VIII. Disagreement & Current Priorities

Teachers debate whether the spread of purchased enchantments is weakening local magical education. Some hearths have lost apprentices and commissions as devices have become easier to obtain. These experiences establish pressure on particular practices, not a measured worldwide decline in magical power.

Critics within the college also blame long apprenticeships, guarded family knowledge, inaccessible tools, and dependence on unpaid work. Students increasingly ask whether a tradition's disappearance reflects public indifference or a failure to welcome successors.

Current work includes reconnecting inactive exchanges, supporting hosts who cannot afford visitors, and revising assessments that reward impressive invention more readily than dependable repair. The college's standing depends on whether communities continue to find its presence useful.
