# Order of the Reclaimed

> _“Guard one another’s nights.”_
> — Reclaimed maxim, adapted from a common Selûnite prayer

**Filed Division:** _Living Atlas — Organizations, Companies & Associations_

**Document Class:** _Reconstructed Organization Record_

**Research Status:** _Credible but incomplete_

**Access Designation:** _Public summary; operational locations and contacts withheld_

**Revision State:** _Current assessment_

## I. Institutional Overview

The **Order of the Reclaimed**, called the **Pale Vigil** in several intercepted references, is a small and deliberately concealed fellowship of people living with dangerous supernatural conditions. Available accounts associate the Order with lycanthropes, vampires, dhampirs, and others whose transformations, appetites, or compulsions may place nearby communities at risk.

The Order does not promise a cure. It offers discipline, mutual accountability, refuge, and a path toward responsible service. Its members distinguish the presence of a violent impulse from the decision to obey it, while refusing to treat affliction as an excuse for harm.

The organization also conducts covert field work. Credible reports attribute supernatural hunts, extractions, escorts, and violent interventions to its operatives, usually where ordinary authorities are unable, unwilling, or considered too compromised to act. The Order holds no recognized charter or public jurisdiction. Even when an intervention prevents harm, its trespass, deception, theft, and use of lethal force remain subject to the laws of the territory in which they occur.

> [!note] Filing distinction
> The Order is filed here because concealed membership and clandestine operations define its practical organization. Its Selûnite traditions do not grant it recognized religious authority, and its mutual-aid work does not make its field actions lawful.

## II. Founding and Historical Record

Most accounts identify **Ser Santiago Valcázar**, a Brethren lycanthrope and former paladin of Selûne, as the Order’s founder. The title used for him within the fellowship is **First Reclaimed**.

Surviving versions of his history agree on a narrow sequence. Valcázar belonged to a martial Selûnite tradition responsible for protecting travelers after dark. He broke an oath while granting sanctuary to people afflicted by a predatory curse, lost the sacred power carried through that oath, and later contracted lycanthropy himself. The names of the sanctuary, the afflicted community, and the other participants vary or have been deliberately removed from accessible copies.

The distinction Valcázar drew from the event became foundational to the Order: his oath had failed to protect people whom he believed deserved sanctuary, but the breaking of that oath did not prove that Selûne had personally rejected him. This view accords with current scholarship on [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/01. Fundamentals of Recorded Reality/II. Laws of a Changed World/03. Divine Covenants and Sacred Oaths|Divine Covenants and Sacred Oaths]], which warns that the function or failure of sacred magic does not by itself establish a god’s judgment.

No reliable date for the Order’s founding has entered the public record. Its small size and fragmented documentation make institutional continuity difficult to trace, though the consistency of its doctrine across separated reports suggests more than an improvised local cell.

## III. Doctrine of Reclamation

The Order’s central teaching is commonly rendered as:

> **A condition may influence desire. It does not determine character.**

The Reclaimed reject both the claim that dangerous supernatural impulses can be overcome by good intentions alone and the claim that experiencing such impulses makes a person irredeemable. Their discipline concentrates upon what they call **the Stillness**: the interval between impulse and action.

Training attempts to lengthen that interval. Members study the bodily and mental signs that precede a loss of control, including altered breathing, fixation upon scent or territory, sensory narrowing, involuntary movement, predatory thought, and the invention of moral excuses for an immediate appetite. Controlled exposure may occur under supervision, sometimes while a member is hungry, exhausted, frightened, partially transformed, or otherwise under strain.

The most frequently reported warning within the Order concerns rationalization. To acknowledge _“I want blood”_ is considered dangerous but honest. To decide that a desired victim deserves death may indicate that appetite has begun manufacturing permission.

The Order does not equate successful meditation with harmlessness. Its methods are intended to make dangerous people more capable of choosing and accepting restraint; they do not remove the danger or absolve past acts.

## IV. The Three Anchors

Instruction within the Order is organized around three linked disciplines.

### The Anchor of Flesh

Members learn the physical course of their condition: triggers, transformation stages, hunger cycles, pain responses, sleep disruption, sensory overload, and other early signs. Ignorance of one’s own condition is treated as a preventable hazard.

### The Anchor of Mind

Members practice identifying an impulse without denying it or building a justification around it. This work includes meditation, truthful self-reporting, and rehearsed responses to known patterns of fixation or compulsion.

### The Anchor of Another

Every full member names two peers with authority to intervene when that member’s judgment becomes compromised. These anchors may remove the member from an assignment, confiscate weapons or catalysts, restrict access to blood or magical implements, order isolation, or call for containment. Accounts agree that the authority extends to lethal intervention when no safer protection remains.

This third discipline is central to the Order’s meaning. The Reclaimed do not regard self-command as a solitary achievement. A member who refuses accountable restraint is not considered ready for independent service.

## V. Structure and Internal Limits

The Order’s known offices divide sanctuary care, field operations, secrecy, and religious study so that no single authority controls them all.

| Office or standing | Principal responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **First Reclaimed** | Sanctuary policy, recruitment, diplomacy, and emergency direction |
| **Warden of Stillness** | Control training, transformation practice, observation, restraint, and fitness for field work |
| **Warden of the Road** | Contracts, weapons, transport, field assignments, extraction, and combat readiness |
| **Warden of the Veil** | Intelligence, safehouses, false identities, covert travel, and protection of contacts |
| **Moonkeeper** | Selûnite rites, supernatural study, relic review, dream records, and care of the Pale Basin |
| **The Reclaimed** | Full members approved for field work, sponsorship, instruction, and internal deliberation |
| **Those Under Watch** | Candidates receiving shelter, care, training, and supervised containment |

Two Wardens acting together may overrule the First Reclaimed. The First Reclaimed reportedly cannot approve an execution, major political intervention, or exceptionally dangerous contract alone. These limitations are attributed to Valcázar himself and appear designed to prevent the Order from becoming the personal weapon of its founder.

The Moonkeeper does not possess automatic field command. This separation permits the Order to preserve religious practice without allowing a theological office to govern contracts or operations.

## VI. Membership, Care, and Accountability

Estimates place the Order at no more than twenty-five to thirty active members. Most are Brethren, with a small number of Beastfolk and members of other peoples or uncommon supernatural lineages. No reliable evidence indicates that ancestry determines eligibility; the observed distribution is consistent with Aerathon’s wider demographics and with the fact that dangerous afflictions are not unique to one people.

New arrivals are placed **Under Watch**, sometimes for months or years. Reports describe shelter, food, medical aid, study of the individual’s condition, supervised training, protection, and companionship. Candidates are watched more closely during known periods of danger but are described as residents rather than prisoners.

The Order does not require flawless control before accepting a person. It requires disclosure and cooperation. A member who loses control and reports the failure may remain under stricter supervision. Concealing such a failure is treated as a graver breach because it disables the accountability upon which the sanctuary depends.

Full members may train or sponsor others, accept assignments, participate in internal deliberations, and operate independently. Rank does not release any member from the Three Anchors.

## VII. Stillwater House and the Pale Basin

The principal sanctuary is consistently named **Stillwater House**, though its location is withheld from this filing. Descriptions agree that it occupies a ruined Selûnite waystation or observatory along an abandoned route, with most habitable spaces concealed below the surviving structure.

At its center lies a shallow circular pool called the **Pale Basin**. Testimony claims that supernatural compulsions become quieter when a willing subject enters or meditates beside the basin under moonlight. Hunger, rage, and other impulses remain present; the reported effect is clarity rather than purification. Coerced exposure is said to produce a weaker response.

The cause has not been established. Proposed explanations include a surviving covenant, a divine remnant, an altered relic, or a sacred site through which Selûne exerts limited influence. Each possibility is compatible with some known post-Edict phenomena, but no available evidence distinguishes among them. The Order’s own Moonkeeper reportedly refuses to declare one explanation certain.

## VIII. Selûnite Tradition and the Quiet Edict

The Order’s devotion centers upon Selûne as a guide through change, uncertainty, and the long hours in which formal protection is absent. This emphasis is consistent with established accounts of [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/01. Fundamentals of Recorded Reality/III. Divinity & The Two Heavens/02. The Dawn Pantheon#Selûne — The Moonmaiden|Selûne]], particularly her associations with wanderers, refugees, moonlit roads, dreams, and lives lived between settled institutions.

The fellowship adapts the common prayer _“Guard my nights”_ into _“Guard one another’s nights.”_ The change turns a request for protection into a mutual obligation.

Accounts of dreams, unexpected moonlight, unusual reflections, and altered behavior near Selûnite relics circulate within the Order. None occurs reliably enough to establish instruction from the goddess. Under the [[The Quiet Edict]], dreams, omens, relic responses, and sacred sites remain possible channels of limited divine influence, but they do not provide automatic certainty about authorship or intent. The Reclaimed therefore treat such events as possible guidance rather than command.

This restraint has not prevented religious criticism. Some Selûnite institutions reportedly regard the Order as heretical for offering sanctuary to predatory afflictions or for separating sacred purpose from inherited oath law. The existence of that dispute does not establish which interpretation, if either, most closely reflects Selûne’s intentions.

## IX. Operations and the Weighing

Credible attributions connect the Order to the hunting of predatory supernatural creatures, the destruction of dangerous cult cells, the extraction of afflicted people threatened with summary execution, the recovery of abducted or trafficked victims, and the escort of dangerous but non-hostile travelers to sanctuary.

Proposed contracts undergo an internal review called **the Weighing**. Reconstructed criteria include the reality and immediacy of the threat, evidence of actual harm, the capacity and integrity of ordinary authorities, the likely consequences of intervention, the necessity of lethal force, the client’s interest, and facts the client may be withholding.

The Order is reported to reject ordinary debt collection, private revenge, inheritance disputes, witness suppression, commercial killings, convenient political assassination, and violence justified only by a target’s supernatural status.

Payment may be accepted, but inability to pay does not necessarily bar aid. Reported funding includes legitimate high-value contracts, old investments, sympathetic benefactors, recovered valuables, and assets confiscated from criminal operations. The legal standing of the latter categories remains doubtful.

## X. Known Figures

The following names recur across otherwise independent accounts. Their exact present duties remain difficult to verify.

- **Ser Santiago Valcázar**, First Reclaimed: founder, former paladin of Selûne, and lycanthrope.
- **Elias Draeven**, Warden of the Road: vampire, former monster hunter, gunslinger, and field mentor. Records attributed to the Order describe him as a former killer whose recovery remains inseparable from honest remembrance of his victims.
- **Orsava Keld**, Warden of Stillness: lycanthrope whose ursine transformation is associated with territorial and possessive impulses; principal instructor in recognizing when protection becomes control.
- **Sister Vaelisse Corven**, Moonkeeper: dhampir scholar of Selûnite theology, supernatural conditions, relics, and the limits of divine interpretation.
- **Taviri Sedge**, Warden of the Veil: covert-travel and intelligence specialist. The nature of her reported affliction has not been authenticated and is withheld from general summaries.

The Order’s full roster is neither verified nor reproduced. This omission protects possible residents and prevents rumor from placing uninvolved afflicted people under suspicion.

## XI. Symbol, Reputation, and Present Assessment

The Order’s reported sign is a broken silver crescent holding a dark circle within its curve. The darkness is contained but not crossed out. Marks may be concealed beneath a weapon grip, inside a coat, upon an ammunition stamp, on a small coin, or under a doorway. No standard uniform is known.

Most residents of Aerathon have never heard the organization’s name. Monster hunters and occult investigators tell less certain stories of cursed prisoners disappearing before execution, afflicted hunters turning upon more predatory members of their own kind, and moon-marked safehouses empty by daylight.

Those who believe the accounts disagree over whether the Reclaimed are a sanctuary network, a heretical order, or monsters policing monsters. Each description captures only part of the evidence. The organization shelters people whom other institutions may condemn without distinction, yet it also arms those people and permits extralegal violence under an internal process answerable to no public court.

The Order’s small size limits its reach and may also be one of its principal safeguards. A saying attributed to Valcázar explains the choice:

> _Thirty dangerous people who know one another can remain a family. Three hundred become a weapon._

Current evidence supports the Order’s existence, name, doctrine, senior offices, and broad operating pattern. Its location, complete roster, founding date, secondary sanctuaries, government contacts, and any direct favor from Selûne remain unverified.
