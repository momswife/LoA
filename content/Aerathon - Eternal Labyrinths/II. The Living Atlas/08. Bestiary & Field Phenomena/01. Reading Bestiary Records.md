# Reading Bestiary Records

Bestiary filings combine practical field knowledge with testimony, scholarly interpretation, religious explanation, and local memory. Their formal tone does not make every sentence equally certain. Readers should distinguish what was encountered from what the encounter is believed to mean.

## I. Evidence Classes

| Evidence class | What belongs here | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Confirmed observation** | A directly witnessed form, trace, injury, response, specimen, or repeatable instrument reading | Reliable within the stated conditions; not automatically universal |
| **Known range and habitat** | Authenticated sightings and environmental associations | A record of where the subject has been found, not proof that it occurs nowhere else |
| **Reported behavior** | Credible witness accounts not yet reproduced under controlled conditions | Operationally useful, but dependent upon witness quality and circumstances |
| **Scholarly interpretation** | Proposed origin, ecology, mechanism, or classification | A working explanation subject to revision |
| **Religious or cultural interpretation** | Theological identity, omen, symbolic meaning, customary name, or community account | Evidence about belief and relationship, not automatic proof of mechanism |
| **Folklore or disputed account** | Stories, warnings, exceptional claims, and incompatible explanations | Preserve attribution and uncertainty; do not convert repetition into confirmation |
| **Field guidance** | Avoidance, evacuation, containment, treatment, or encounter procedure | Follow the current operational instruction even when its underlying theory remains disputed |
| **Game mechanics** | Statistics or rules explicitly filed for play | A table-facing model, not a complete biological or metaphysical claim |

## II. Limits of a Bestiary Classification

A Bestiary category describes how a subject is encountered and studied. It does not settle personhood, intelligence, moral standing, origin, or allegiance. Terms such as _beast_, _construct_, _aberration_, _divine_, _infernal_, and _undead_ are filing tools; individual records must establish what is actually known.

Classification also does not establish danger by itself. Threat depends upon location, behavior, scale, exposure, and the actions of observers. A normally non-hostile creature may still require protected habitat, while a dangerous manifestation may be avoidable without destruction.

## III. Reading Numbers and Universal Claims

Exact distances, timings, percentages, recurrence intervals, and biological absolutes require an identified measurement or documented observation. When an older filing provides precision without a method, treat it as an archival estimate pending renewed study.

Likewise, statements that a subject _always_, _never_, or _only_ behaves in one way should be read narrowly unless the record identifies the sample and conditions. A poetic summary may preserve the archive’s voice, but it is not a substitute for evidence.

## IV. Relationship to the Wider Atlas

A mature Bestiary record should connect to the places where the subject has been observed and to the people or systems that encounter it. Useful relationships include habitat, affected settlements, faiths, historical events, delving procedures, relic regulation, expeditions, and active Ledger notices.

These links are contextual rather than taxonomic. Appearing near a settlement does not make a creature part of that settlement, and appearing in a religious account does not authenticate the account’s theology.
