# Crocodile

**Filed Division:** Ministry of Delving Operations — _The Living Atlas_ (Beasts & Fauna)\
**Document Class:** _Standard Bestiary Record / Current Field Reference_\
**Access Designation:** _Public_

---

## I. Identification & Classification

Crocodiles are armored aquatic ambush predators filed as natural fauna. A still animal at the waterline may expose little more than eyes, nostrils, and the upper ridge of its back, making distant identification unreliable in poor light.

## II. Field Signs, Habitat & Behavior

Broad belly trails, clawed prints, bank slides, and drag marks near water indicate occupation. Crocodiles favor shorelines and confined crossings where prey must enter their reach. Flooded Labyrinth chambers, subterranean rivers, reservoirs, and water-filled ruins can all support them when prey and basking space are available.

## III. Threat Assessment & Delver Guidance

Survey both banks before entering water and avoid kneeling at an obscured edge. Use a pole or thrown line to test crossings; do not send a single swimmer ahead. A crocodile's bite can hold and restrain a target, so extraction plans should prioritize breaking the hold and leaving the water.
