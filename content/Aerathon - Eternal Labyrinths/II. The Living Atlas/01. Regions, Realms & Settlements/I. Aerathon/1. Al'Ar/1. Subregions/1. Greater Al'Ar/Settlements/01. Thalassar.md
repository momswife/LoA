# Thalassar

## I. Civic Overview

**Thalassar** is Aerathon's only authenticated **wholly submerged large city**. It spreads across a reef-ringed basin within the maritime extent of the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/5. Vinyot/1. Subregions/2. Tresoli Isles/∅ Tresoli Isles|Tresoli Isles]], where shelf currents descend into the **Tiger Sea** and divide into navigable channels through the city.

Its inhabited extent is immense, though its unverified population and lower density keep it below the Ministry's metropolitan classification. Thalassar is large because its markets, pressureworks, neighborhoods, memorial reefs, and current-lanes occupy a vast three-dimensional civic space—not because every old foundation remains inhabited.

The city is the principal exchange point between surface shipping and Aerathon's pelagic communities. Vinyotian grain, spice, ceramics, tools, and credit descend through its transfer harbors; salt, dyes, sea-silk, coralwork, medicinal brines, pressure-grown crystal, deep metals, preserved food, and current charts rise in return. Trade is not an accommodation at Thalassar's edge. It is part of the city's daily rhythm.

> [!note] Geographic and political filing
> Thalassar is geographically located within the **Tresoli Isles**, but it remains politically and archivally **Al'Ari**. Its relocation established a home within Vinyotian geography without transferring its civic inheritance or the Tide Concord's regional allegiance to Vinyot.

**Primary Renown:** Underwater resources, mixed pelagic culture, music, food, and interregional trade<br>
**Civic Maxim:** _“Make room at the table. Mind the gates.”_

## II. Place & Built Form

Thalassar occupies the **Tresoli Shelf Basin**, a broad depression where warm shelf water meets colder Tiger Sea currents. Those currents behave like a submerged river delta: they carry silt, nutrients, messages, travelers, and freight into several branching approaches before rejoining beyond the lower reefs.

The Tresoli Shelf shelters the basin, and Thalassar lies well outside the usual tracks of Al'Ar's famous hurricanes. Most severe Al'Ari systems exhaust themselves or turn before reaching the city. An exceptional storm can still drive a disruptive surge through the shelf currents, but this is an uncommon infrastructure emergency rather than a defining part of Thalassari life.

The city has no single enclosing dome. Most Thalassari districts are open to the sea and built for water-breathing residents. Air-dependent visitors move through sealed salons, inns, counting rooms, and embassy courts linked by pressure locks.

### The Highwater Exchange

Surface platforms, buoy towers, cargo bells, and descending lifts connect ships above to the customs halls below. The Exchange is noisy at every depth. Brokers translate between Vinyotian contract law, Al'Ari harbor compacts, and Thalassari ward custom while crews transfer goods between vessels designed for entirely different environments.

Foreign traffic is welcome here, but unsponsored visitors do not pass into residential wards merely by paying a fee.

### The Gallery Wards

The city's best-known neighborhoods rise in layered terraces around the basin walls. Grown-stone arcades, coral galleries, screened balconies, interior courtyards, and narrow current-lanes make each ward feel both open and intimate. Homes and workshops accumulate ornament over generations: colored shell, worked metal, family mosaics, lantern growths, carved lintels, and memorial tiles.

Many buildings retain submerged remnants of the old offshore anchorage, but the city does not present itself as a museum of surface loss. New construction is expected to converse with the old foundations rather than imitate them.

### The Backreef

Behind the public markets lies a dense belt of kitchens, repair yards, net lofts, pressurewright shops, neighborhood halls, and modest homes. The Backreef keeps Thalassar functioning. When a gate fails, a household loses income, or the surface lifts are interrupted, aid is usually organized here before the Tide Concord completes a formal response.

### The Low Ward & Labyrinth Scar

The deepest civic works control pressure, wastewater, current flow, and access to the sealed **Labyrinth Scar**. The Scar is warded and excluded from ordinary traffic. Its keepers treat it as dangerous infrastructure, not a sacred mystery and not an invitation to delve.

## III. People, Work & Daily Life

Thalassari culture formed through repeated arrival. Sea-bearing beastfolk constitute most permanent residents, but no single lineage, island, guild, or migration owns the city's identity. Al'Ari sailors, Vinyotian factors, mixed surface-pelagic households, reef cultivators, salvagers, pressurewrights, cooks, musicians, and visiting crews have all left durable practices behind.

Locals describe this as **confluence culture**: a current remains itself when it enters the city, but it does not leave unchanged.

### Food & Hospitality

Public kitchens and neighborhood tables are central civic spaces. The characteristic meal is a heavily seasoned shared pot built from whatever several waters can provide: shellfish or firm reef fish, sea greens, Vinyotian marsh grain, peppers and aromatics, preserved surface meats, and a dark stock kept alive across repeated servings. Recipes are argued as fiercely as harbor law.

Hospitality is generous but structured. A newcomer is commonly asked who received them, where they can breathe, and which table has claimed responsibility for feeding them. These questions are practical protections, not ornamental etiquette.

### Music, Procession & Night Life

Music changes with the room. Open-water ensembles use shell horns, resonant metal, pressure drums, chimes, and voices carried through the current. Air salons favor brass, reeds, hand drums, and imported instruments. Musicians regularly answer one another across locks, so that a melody begun underwater may return from a dry balcony in a different form.

The city is famous for neighborhood processions. Memorial observances begin with a measured passage to a family reef or name-wall. The returning procession is brighter and louder, carrying the remembered person's songs back into ordinary life. Grief is not rushed, but neither is it permitted to remove the dead from the city's rhythm.

During the annual **Turning Tide**, wards wear masks, trail luminous ribbons, and guide buoyant sculptures through the current-lanes. Neighborhood societies compete through music, dress, food, and wit; the Tide Concord intervenes only when a procession blocks emergency flow or threatens a pressure gate.

## IV. Authority & Local Institutions

Thalassar is governed by the **Tide Concord**, a civic council whose membership is drawn from:

- Residential ward delegates
- Harbor and cargo unions
- Reefkeepers and pressurewrights
- Neighborhood mutual-aid societies
- Wardens of the Labyrinth Scar
- Delegates responsible for surface and foreign compacts

Most civic questions are settled through negotiation, public hearing, and reciprocal obligation. Pressure integrity, emergency current control, and Scar security are exceptions: authorized wardens may act immediately and answer to the Concord afterward.

Thalassari law distinguishes **welcome** from **access**. Foreign merchants can trade without becoming citizens; Al'Ari captains can invoke rescue compacts without claiming a dock; Vinyotian firms can finance infrastructure without acquiring the ward beneath it. This distinction is the foundation of the city's independence.

Local defense is handled by current pilots, gate crews, harbor wardens, and neighborhood response societies rather than a conventional surface army. The city can redirect approaches, close pressure routes, darken navigation markers, and turn familiar currents into barriers. Its greatest defense is that outsiders cannot occupy what they cannot safely breathe in, navigate, or maintain.

## V. Trade & Regional Belonging

Thalassar is culturally distinct from both regions contesting its classification, but it is not isolated from either.

| Relationship | Present character |
| --- | --- |
| **Tresoli Isles** | Thalassar's present geographic home, surface supply route, and principal local partner |
| **Vinyot** | Territorial host and principal source of commercial credit, grain, manufactured goods, and surface shipping; location does not make Thalassar a Vinyotian polity |
| **Al'Ar** | Thalassar's political region and principal source of pelagic kinship, pilots, rescue obligations, music, and harbor custom |
| **Thalassar** | The Tide Concord signs its own compacts and maintains Al'Ari affiliation without surrendering local civic authority |
| **MDO** | Records the geographic-political distinction, certifies safe transfer infrastructure, and monitors the sealed Scar |

The most useful current description is therefore **geographically Tresoli, commercially Vinyot-facing, politically Al'Ari, and civically Thalassari**.

## VI. History & Public Memory

Thalassar descends from **Tresoli City**, whose original site lay outside the present Tresoli Isles. Surviving ledgers do not preserve that first location securely enough for a modern map, but they establish an Al'Ari port community with extensive pelagic wards and longstanding ties to island merchants.

In **1189 A.D.**, the **Abyssal Breach** devastated Tresoli City and made permanent habitation at its original site impossible. Over the following seven years, the city's surviving population, civic records, harbor compacts, and recoverable infrastructure were transferred to a reef-ringed basin beneath the Tresoli Isles. The relocation was an evacuation and reconstruction, not a Vinyotian annexation.

The rebuilt city adopted the name **Thalassar** while retaining Tresoli City's Al'Ari civic continuity. Its old arches and drowned foundations are real, but the city rejects the outside habit of treating catastrophe as its only history.

**Key Historical Events:**

- **732 A.D.** — First surviving charter of Tresoli City documents organized exchange between its surface harbor and pelagic wards.
- **1189 A.D.** — The Abyssal Breach devastates Tresoli City and begins the evacuation toward the Tresoli Isles.
- **1196 A.D.** — The relocation is completed; the surviving city is re-established beneath the isles as Thalassar.
- **1364 A.D.** — The Tide Concord unites the surviving wards, harbor interests, and pressurekeepers under a common civic compact.
- **3011 A.D.** — The Labyrinth Scar is formally sealed under Concord and proto-MDO accords; Thalassar is recognized as a living city rather than a salvage claim.

Older foreign filings call Thalassar **the Drowned Crown**. The name survives in memorial speech, but many residents dislike it in ordinary use: it makes the city sound like something that ended rather than somewhere people still fall in love, quarrel over dinner, raise children, repair gates, and stay out too late listening to music.

## VII. Current Conditions & Delver Relevance

Thalassar's current priorities are commercial growth, pressurework renewal, protection of residential access, and preserving Al'Ari civic continuity within Vinyotian geography. Expansion of trade with Vinyot has increased wealth and surface dependence at the same time. Al'Ari councils remain essential partners in navigation, rescue, and pelagic movement, while the Tide Concord manages local affairs.

The **Labyrinth Scar remains sealed**. Delving within city bounds is forbidden, and no license to trade, visit, or conduct ordinary salvage grants access to it. Delvers are otherwise common in the Highwater Exchange as escorts, buyers, cargo specialists, and travelers seeking pelagic expertise.

For surface visitors, the principal hazards are unsponsored movement, incompatible breathing arrangements, pressure transition, and failure to respect emergency current signals. Thalassar is not broadly hostile to outsiders. It is intolerant of outsiders who mistake invitation for entitlement.
