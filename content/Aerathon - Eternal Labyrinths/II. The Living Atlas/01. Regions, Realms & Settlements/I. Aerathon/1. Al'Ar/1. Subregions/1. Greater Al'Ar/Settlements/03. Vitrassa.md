# Vitrassa

## I. Civic Overview

**Vitrassa** is a wholly submerged city built across the volcanic shelf between **Dakshin** and **Jarik**. Hydrothermal vents provide heat and mineral-rich water; colder cross-currents provide cooling; cultivated reefs stabilize the shelf and turn what would otherwise be an extraction camp into a permanent settlement.

The city is known as the **Living Foundry** because its industry and ecology cannot be separated. Vitrassan workshops pressure-form volcanic glass, deep ceramics, metal fittings, and coral composites. Its nurseries cultivate food, medicine, dyes, sea-silk, and structural reef growth used to repair breakwaters throughout Al'Ar.

Vitrassa does not merely supply aquatic residents. Dry forges, air galleries, pressure chambers, and rotating work crews allow surface artisans to participate in industries that can only be sustained at the shelf itself.

**Primary Renown:** Pressure craft, volcanic materials, marine cultivation, and harbor restoration<br>
**Civic Maxim:** _“Take heat. Return shelter.”_

## II. Place & Built Form

Vitrassa spans a series of basalt terraces cut by hot vents and colder cooling channels. The city is brightest near the industrial center, where forge light, mineral plumes, and cultivated bioluminescence make a false horizon across the dark shelf.

### The Ventworks

The Ventworks capture heat without sealing the vents completely. Heat exchangers feed kilns, glass chambers, metal shops, and dry-air forges. Pressurewrights adjust the system constantly as vent temperature and mineral content change.

No workshop owns a vent outright. It receives a time-bound draw under the city's Balance Charter and can lose access if its use destabilizes neighboring wards.

### The Dry Forges

Air-filled workshops allow nonaquatic smiths, ceramicists, glassworkers, artificers, and inspectors to work below the surface. Materials may pass repeatedly between flooded pressure chambers and dry workrooms during manufacture.

This alternating process produces fittings and composites difficult to reproduce in an ordinary surface forge. It also makes Vitrassa dependent on meticulous seals, cooling schedules, and coordination between workers who cannot safely share the same room.

### The Reef Nurseries

Broad cultivated gardens surround the industrial terraces. They produce food and useful organisms while binding loose shelf stone, filtering industrial runoff, and growing structural coral for export.

Nursery walls are arranged in replaceable sections. Mature growth may be transported to damaged harbors, where Vitrassan crews graft it into broken reefs and breakwaters rather than waiting generations for unassisted recovery.

### The Cooling Canals

Cold currents are directed through channels between the Ventworks and residential districts. The canals are both infrastructure and public space. Their safest stretches hold markets, schools, kitchens, and communal baths adapted to different temperature needs.

## III. People, Work & Daily Life

Vitrassa's population includes reef cultivators, miners, glassworkers, ceramicists, pressurewrights, metalworkers, healers, cooks, current engineers, merchants, and rotating surface specialists. Residency is not limited to a profession, but almost every household depends on the production and restoration cycle.

Daily schedules follow heat draw and cooling capacity rather than sunlight. Work bells announce changes in vent pressure, safe transfer windows, and nursery closures. A delayed kiln can inconvenience a workshop; a delayed cooling cycle can endanger an entire terrace.

Craft reputation matters, but maintenance carries equal status. The artisan who completes a celebrated harbor fitting is expected to name the crews who stabilized its materials, kept the seals, and restored the nursery beds disturbed in production.

Meals combine cultivated shellfish, reef fish, sea greens, imported grain, and preserved island produce. Surface workers often arrive expecting an austere industrial camp and discover a city intensely concerned with gardens, color, and public meals.

## IV. Authority & Local Institutions

Vitrassa is governed by a **Balance Council** composed equally of **Foundry Circles** and **Reefkeepers**.

Foundry Circles represent workshops, material specialists, infrastructure crews, and export interests. Reefkeepers represent nurseries, food producers, water quality, shelf stability, and long-term restoration obligations. Neither side can authorize a major expansion alone.

The **Balance Charter** requires every extraction license to identify:

- What will be removed or altered
- Which ward receives the economic benefit
- How cooling, waste, and shelf stability will be managed
- What habitat or structural growth will be restored
- Who remains responsible if the original workshop closes

Emergency authority belongs to ventwardens and gate engineers. They may shut down an entire Foundry Circle when heat migration or seal failure threatens the city.

Vitrassa remains in Greater Al'Ar because the productive shelf lies between Dakshin and Jarik. Both islands rely on it, but neither has an uncontested territorial claim over the full vent field.

## V. Work, Exchange & Regional Use

Vitrassa supplies materials and services that Al'Ar would otherwise import at great expense:

- Pressure-worked metal and ceramic fittings
- Volcanic glass, heat-resistant tile, and sealed instrument housings
- Structural coral and reef-bound breakwater sections
- Sea-silk, dyes, oils, medicines, and preserved marine food
- Diving bells, pressure locks, cargo chambers, and pelagic vessel components
- Harbor surveys, reef grafting, and post-disaster restoration crews

The city imports grain, timber, worked components, fine tools, air-system materials, and goods that cannot be cultivated or manufactured on the shelf.

Vitrassa's most valuable export may be institutional rather than material: it teaches Al'Ari ports to treat reef health as infrastructure. A harbor that destroys its protective ecology eventually pays for the loss in repairs, navigation, and lives.

## VI. History & Public Memory

The shelf first supported seasonal vent camps and reef farms operated by separate crews. The vent workers regarded the gardens as useful barriers; the cultivators regarded the workshops as temporary intrusions. Neither group expected a city to form.

Permanent settlement became unavoidable as pressureworks grew too complex to abandon between seasons. Early expansion damaged cooling channels and killed several nursery beds, proving that industrial success could destroy the conditions that made further industry possible.

The first Balance Charter joined the workshops and reef wards under shared obligations. Vitrassa's public memory treats that agreement as the city's true founding: not the discovery of the vents, but the decision that extraction without restoration could not support a civic future.

## VII. Current Conditions & Delver Relevance

Demand for Vitrassan fittings and restoration crews is increasing faster than the city's nurseries can mature. Foundry Circles want additional vent access; Reefkeepers argue that existing obligations must be completed first. The dispute remains procedural but affects contracts throughout Al'Ar.

Minor shifts in the volcanic shelf have also changed several cooling routes. No Labyrinth manifestation is authenticated beneath Vitrassa, and the city resists attempts to describe every geological irregularity as delver business.

Delvers nevertheless purchase pressure equipment, sealed containers, resistant armor fittings, medicines, and deep-route supplies here. Salvaged materials may enter the Foundries only after inspection; unstable relics are prohibited from the Ventworks and nursery system.
