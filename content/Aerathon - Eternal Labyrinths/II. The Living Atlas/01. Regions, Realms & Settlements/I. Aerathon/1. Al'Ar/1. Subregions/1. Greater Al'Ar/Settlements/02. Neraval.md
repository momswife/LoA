# Neraval

## I. Civic Overview

**Neraval** is a wholly submerged city beneath the shared inner passage between **Kandela** and **Aurica**. It lies below the depth where ordinary surface waves can break a harbor, but close enough to the island circuit to receive crews, cargo, and distress signals from several major Al'Ari routes.

The city exists because a surface refuge can fail at the same moment it is most needed. Neraval maintains descent stations, pressure-safe shelters, rescue crews, current charts, submerged navigation beacons, and emergency stores that remain usable when hurricanes close the docks above.

Neraval is not immune to those storms. Exceptional systems can drive violent currents through the passage and strain its gates. Its advantage is continuity: the city is engineered to remain navigable, supplied, and coordinated after surface signals disappear.

Most Al'Ari settlements serve their own harbor first. Neraval's founding obligation is different. It exists to receive people who do not belong to it.

**Primary Renown:** Rescue, refuge, deep navigation, emergency logistics, and current engineering<br>
**Civic Maxim:** _“Shelter first. Reckoning after.”_

## II. Place & Built Form

Neraval occupies a series of natural hollows along the passage wall. Its chambers descend in tiers, allowing damaged arrivals to be stabilized before they attempt deeper pressure transitions. Open-water civic wards surround sealed air galleries used by surface crews and dryland specialists.

### The Descent Harbors

Weighted cargo bells, diving chambers, guide cables, and marked current shafts connect Neraval with several surface rendezvous points. Surface ships do not enter the city. They lower people and essential cargo or transfer them to vessels designed for submerged travel.

Every descent harbor contains triage space, breathing stores, signaling equipment, and a station empowered to suspend ordinary customs rules during a declared emergency.

### The Quiet Vaults

The Quiet Vaults are pressure-safe refuge galleries maintained above Neraval's ordinary residential capacity. Some hold air; others are flooded and divided for pelagic arrivals, livestock, medicine, or protected cargo. Their emptiness in calm seasons is deliberate.

Using a vault for profitable storage without preserving its emergency capacity is among the city's most serious civic offenses.

### The Beacon Spine

A chain of resonant towers runs along the passage floor and up both island shelves. The towers carry coded vibrations, light signals, and guide tones through conditions that blind surface lanterns. Pilots can follow the Spine into Neraval even when the upper passage has become unnavigable.

### The Working Wards

Homes, kitchens, schools, repair chambers, current observatories, and neighborhood halls occupy the calmer hollows behind the harbor tiers. Neraval is an inhabited city rather than an emergency installation, but ordinary construction is expected to preserve evacuation lanes and access to shared equipment.

## III. People, Work & Daily Life

Neraval's permanent population includes pelagic households, current pilots, pressurewrights, healers, beacon keepers, cargo specialists, and the families that support them. Surface crews, Al'Ari harbor delegates, and rotating engineers live in the sealed galleries for weeks or seasons at a time.

Readiness shapes public life without making it joyless. Every ward conducts drills, maintains shared stores, and knows which gates it must clear. Children learn refuge markings alongside neighborhood routes. Musicians and callers participate in signal practice because a familiar rhythm can guide frightened arrivals more effectively than a written notice.

The city distinguishes between **rescue debt** and **rescue price**. A person may owe labor, replacement supplies, or future aid after receiving shelter. No lawful station may demand payment before admitting someone in immediate danger.

Meals during a passage closure are communal and deliberately simple. The first return of fresh food, private cooking, and ordinary market noise is treated as the true end of an emergency.

## IV. Authority & Local Institutions

Neraval is governed by the **Deep Harbor Council**, whose authority derives from the **Refuge Compact** rather than territorial ownership of the waters above. Its seats are held by:

- Resident ward delegates
- Rescue and medical crews
- Current pilots and beacon keepers
- Pressurewrights responsible for the Quiet Vaults
- Rotating representatives from participating Al'Ari harbors

The Compact requires emergency access regardless of origin, dock affiliation, debt, or current political dispute. Weapons may be secured, cargo may be quarantined, and movement may be restricted, but immediate life-preserving shelter cannot be withheld.

During calm conditions, the Council behaves like an ordinary civic government. During a declared passage emergency, operational authority passes temporarily to rescue captains, medical leads, and gate engineers. Their decisions are reviewed when the passage reopens.

Neraval maintains no claim over Kandela or Aurica. Both islands support the city because neither wants the other to control the refuge route alone.

## V. Work, Exchange & Regional Use

Neraval is sustained through harbor contributions, pilotage fees, equipment contracts, repair work, protected storage, and training services. Refuge is not supposed to generate profit, but maintaining refuge capacity is expensive.

The city provides:

- Current charts and deep-route certification
- Rescue training and pressure-transition medicine
- Beacon construction and maintenance
- Emergency food, air, medicine, and repair stores
- Submerged inspection of anchors, foundations, and ship damage
- Protected transfer of people and high-priority cargo during passage closures

Its air galleries allow nonaquatic crews to work below for extended periods. Neraval therefore serves all Al'Ari harbors, not only settlements with large pelagic populations.

The city's neutrality is practical rather than passive. Any dock-state attempting to monopolize its beacons or refuge capacity risks losing the cooperation that keeps the entire inner circuit open.

## VI. History & Public Memory

Neraval began as a collection of weighted moorings, natural caves, and supply caches maintained by independent rescue crews. Each installation was useful, but their signals, pressure fittings, and access rules were incompatible. People survived the descent only to discover that the next station could not receive them.

Repeated passage closures forced the participating harbors to standardize equipment and recognize one another's distress marks. The Refuge Compact transformed those linked stations into a permanent civic system. Families followed the crews, workshops followed the families, and the deep harbor became a city.

Neraval's oldest memorials record failed rescues alongside successful ones. The purpose is not public shame. Each name preserves a condition the next crew is expected to recognize sooner.

## VII. Current Conditions & Delver Relevance

Neraval is currently expanding its air-capable refuge galleries and replacing older sections of the Beacon Spine. Increased traffic through Tempestat and the inner dock circuit has placed pressure on emergency capacity even during relatively calm seasons.

Delvers commonly pass through Neraval as escorts, medics, salvage specialists, or travelers waiting for a safe surface route. Labyrinth artifacts are isolated from the refuge galleries until inspected; no emergency claim permits an unstable object to endanger the people already sheltered there.

The city's principal political risk is slow capture by the harbors that contribute the most money. The Deep Harbor Council treats financial dependence as a greater long-term threat than open annexation.
