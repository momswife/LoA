# Crew, Harbor & Safe Return

Al'Ari life is joined by movement rather than administered through one nation. Crew, dock, neighborhood, household, and remembered voyage can each establish belonging. Their importance comes from the work of keeping people alive across stormbound islands, not from an inherited Al'Ari temperament.

## I. Belonging in Motion

Residence is only one form of attachment. A household may remain tied to one port while its members spend much of the sailing year elsewhere. Crews can become working households; docks can remember service; a voyage can create obligations recognized long after its members disperse.

This mobility does not erase local citizenship. Each dock-state retains its own laws and terms of entry. It does mean that reputation often travels through witnesses: who repaired a vessel, shared water, returned for the missing, or abandoned an agreement when weather changed.

## II. Harbor Obligation

Fresh water, shelter, repair labor, protected anchorage, and reliable weather knowledge are scarce enough to carry moral and civic weight. Offering them may create an obligation, but the form of repayment varies by harbor and should never be assumed from a regional label alone.

Emergency refuge is narrower and more serious than ordinary hospitality. Participating communities recognize that refusing survivable shelter during a storm can endanger an entire crew. Neraval's Refuge Compact formalizes that principle along the inner passage; it does not grant unrestricted access to every home, store, or residential district.

## III. Return, Reputation & Public Memory

Music, movement, body ornament, performance, and public craft frequently carry memory and reputation. A person may commemorate a voyage through song, visible work, a Witness Mark, or the testimony of those who returned. None of these forms is mandatory, and quiet or private lives are no less Al'Ari.

The recurring ideals of expression as survival, movement as authority, and beauty as responsibility describe public expectations about communication and care. They do not assign artistry, extroversion, or wanderlust to every resident.

## IV. Local Variation

Tempestat's coordinating role, Muraya's cliffbound port, submerged refuge communities, volcanic foundries, outer-island routes, and small dock-states do not produce identical customs. The authority of a captain, council, household, or rescue crew expands and contracts with the situation. An agreement honored in one harbor may require new witnesses in the next.

This record therefore establishes a regional pattern of negotiated maritime obligation, not one Al'Ari culture. Island- and settlement-specific practices remain controlling where they are documented.
