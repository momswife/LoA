# Granfield

## Civic Overview

Granfield is a farming village built around shared grain stores, a drying yard, and the cart sheds that serve surrounding households. Its prosperity depends less on a remarkable harvest than on keeping an ordinary one usable through damp weather. Coopers, roofers, carters, and rat-catchers are as familiar a sight here as field workers.

## Work & Local Institutions

At the communal weighing, grain enters the stores in front of its owner and two witnesses. Household shares are recorded separately from the reserve kept for spoiled crops, sickness, and delayed deliveries. Steward Eda Marr maintains the tally, but disputed measures are checked in public rather than settled by her word alone.

## Current Conditions

An older storehouse has begun taking water through its roof. Repairing it before autumn deepens would cost less than replacing a spoiled reserve, yet the families who use the newer building disagree about their share of the bill. Visiting laborers can find paid work moving sacks, replacing tiles, and escorting small deliveries. The village needs dependable hands; it does not regard every storage problem as evidence of a curse.
