# The Cracked Cauldron

## I. A Table off the Causeway

The Cracked Cauldron stands on Turnwell Lane, off the Silver Causeway in [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/2. Allemance/1. Subregions/1. Greater Allemance/1. Settlements/01. Louvain (Global Capital)|Louvain]]. Its lanterns cast a warm light onto the street, carrying the smell of bread, roasted herbs, and slow-cooked stew beyond the door. Inside, moss borders the stone floor and a harp can be heard near the hearth even when no musician is seated there.

Alchemists, delvers, neighborhood patrons, and solitary writers share the room. Conversation is usually quiet enough to hear the person across the table. For newcomers arriving from the crowded guild district, that can be the first invitation of the day that does not require a performance.

## II. The People Who Keep It

**Mr. Thatch**, an elderly feline Beastfolk, owns the tavern. His warmth has weight behind it: he remembers people and the help that made the house possible. A greeting from him may carry the history of an earlier kindness without turning the whole room into its audience.

**Mera of the Downhill Flock**, a lamb Beastfolk, waits the tables. Attentive and gentle in manner, she gives uncertain guests time to choose and lets a table find its own conversation. Her name is part of the tavern’s welcome; the record does not presume a wider history for the Downhill Flock.

## III. Food and Quiet Magic

Delver’s Stew, prismfish, and woolcake appear among the house offerings, alongside bread and herb-scented dishes. The kitchen feeds people arriving with very different appetites. The names on the menu promise a meal rather than a combat advantage; no restorative enchantment is established for the food.

The lanterns and hearth music lend the room its character without making every dinner an exhibition. It is a place where a new company can learn who asks questions, who listens, and who finally relaxes once the bowls arrive.

## IV. The Extra Place

When a party expects one more companion, Mera leaves a place clear until the table says otherwise. The custom is practical before it is sentimental: someone may still be returning equipment, finishing a shift, or finding the lane. Staff ask before clearing it.

An empty chair can mean lateness, an invitation, or an absence no one wants to explain. The house does not require guests to tell that story in order to keep the place.
