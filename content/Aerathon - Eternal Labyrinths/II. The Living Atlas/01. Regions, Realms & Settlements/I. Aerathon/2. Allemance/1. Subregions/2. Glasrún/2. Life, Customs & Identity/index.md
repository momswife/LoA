# Life, Customs & Identity in Glasrún

To call oneself Glasrúni is to place local stewardship before national affiliation. The forest is unruled, not disordered: authority remains close to the hearth and is judged by care, memory, and demonstrated responsibility.

## Local Order

- Family heads, elders, and wardens chosen by reputation mediate disputes.
- Restitution and exile carry more weight than distant statute; remembered precedent is trusted more readily than written law.
- Harm done to the forest without communal consent is treated as a civic offense.

## Daily Life & Worldview

- Old Allemantic hearth rites, beastfolk reverence for land and hunt, and unnamed Loamlink-adjacent practices coexist without a large formal clergy.
- Villages clear only what they need, maintain narrow unmarked roads collectively, and build homes to be repaired or dismantled rather than expanded indefinitely.
- The common ethic is summarized by the maxim: _“If you did not tend it, you do not command it.”_

## Livelihood

Fallen timber, medicinal mosses and fungi, furs, cured game, and the knowledge of guides move outward. Metal tools, salt, and winter grain move inward. Exchange is commonly arranged at known clearings under customary obligation rather than elaborate contract.
