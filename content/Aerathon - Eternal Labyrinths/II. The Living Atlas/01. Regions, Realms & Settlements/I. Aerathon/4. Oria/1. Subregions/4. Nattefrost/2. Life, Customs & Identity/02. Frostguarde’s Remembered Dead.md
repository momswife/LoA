# Frostguarde’s Remembered Dead

> [!warning] Historical record
> Frostguarde and its approaches remain under the northern plague cordon. This account does not establish present services, safe access, or surviving inhabitants. See [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/01. Regions, Realms & Settlements/I. Aerathon/4. Oria/1. Subregions/4. Nattefrost/1. Settlements/01. Frostguarde City|the city’s records status]].

## I. The Hall of Remembered Authority

A mausoleum beneath Frostguarde’s oldest bastion preserved an image of civic service in stone. Wide, shallow steps were made for processions. Warding sigils and civic oaths recorded names and obligations at the descent, placing entry among the dead within the city’s language of duty.

The Hall of Remembered Authority formed a subterranean avenue between granite pillars. Alcoves held effigies of generals, wardens, winter-kings, and siege-matrons. Hands rested on weapons; armor was carved whole rather than as it might have been broken. These were public images of authority, not faithful records of every wound or failure.

## II. Offerings and Unadorned Tombs

Some tombs carried blades, coins, goblets, or pressed frostflowers. Others bore only a name. The account does not treat these differences as a scale of honor. An unadorned bier could remain part of living remembrance without accumulating objects around it.

Silver prayers against rot and unrest, stiff standards, and pale lamps joined civic emblems with funerary care. The city’s public recitation of the dead’s names supplied a related practice above ground: memory was renewed by voices as well as preserved by stone.

## III. Earlier Burials

Deeper crypts were described as simpler, with shorter names and fewer insignia. They suggest more than one period of burial, but do not establish a dated sequence of rulers or a settlement older than the city’s recorded foundation in 3068 A.D.

The titles winter-king and siege-matron require interpretation before they can support a political genealogy. A commemorative title is not enough to establish hereditary sovereignty. The mausoleum is retained here as a historical funerary place, without a claim that its chambers or wards remain intact.
