# The Low Lantern

> [!warning] Historical record
> Frostguarde and its approaches remain under the northern plague cordon. This account does not establish present services, safe access, or surviving inhabitants. ## I. An Evening Room

The Low Lantern was a warm, busy tavern in Frostguarde. At the end of the evening, dice crossed worn tabletops, mugs met, coins changed hands, and someone was usually certain the next throw would recover the last loss.

Its value to the city lay partly in that ordinariness. Travelers and residents could occupy the same room without every conversation becoming a research briefing or a warning about the north. Warmth, company, and the passing of an evening were reasons enough to enter.

## II. Rumor Across a Table

The northern-assault account places talk of sickness among the concerns carried into the city. A tavern offered room for such reports to spread, but repetition did not make their explanations reliable. Stories of cold, dimmed eyes, or failing luck could carry fear more quickly than useful distinctions.

The Northcall interrupted the evening’s rhythms. Watchmen sought people able to help elsewhere, including those whose contribution might be care rather than force. The Low Lantern thus appears at the edge between ordinary life and mobilization, not as an established headquarters or refuge.

## III. Limits of the Record

The proprietor, precise street address, regular menu, and eventual fate of the house remain unrecorded. No accommodation, supplies, surviving staff, or sanctuary are offered by this historical entry. The tavern’s name preserves a piece of Frostguarde’s daily life without promising that the room still waits behind a safe door.
