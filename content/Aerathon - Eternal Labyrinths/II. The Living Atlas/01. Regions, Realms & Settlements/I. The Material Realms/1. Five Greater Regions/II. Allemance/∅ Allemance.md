## I. Regional Snapshot

Allemance occupies Aerathon's central plains and river country. Its farms, roads, archives, schools, guild halls, and densely connected settlements make it the continent's principal administrative heartland. **Louvain** is both Allemance's coordinating center and Aerathon's global political and archival capital.

Allemance is not a unitary monarchy. Its old crown survives as an institution of memory, ceremony, charter, and occasional mediation, while practical authority is distributed among baronies, cities, guild coalitions, civic councils, and the Ministry of Delving Operations.

A torch within a ring of clasped hands remains a widely recognized regional emblem: flame held through fellowship rather than a claim that one authority owns the hearth.

## II. Land, Boundaries & Climate

The **Hearthlands** are broad temperate plains crossed by navigable rivers, managed fields, hedgerows, low wooded hills, and old roads. Much of the region's political geography grew around watersheds, harvest routes, and the distance a local authority could reliably provision.

Allemance's borders are easier to cross than to define. Old walls and crown markers remain in some districts, but trade, settlement, and modern charter law have outgrown many of them. The former military line at **Fort Kingsfang**, for example, survives more clearly as a historical institution than as an active Allemance–Oria frontier.

The region's greatest geographic exception is **Glasrún**, a forested subregion whose independent status is protected by the **Glasrún Pact**. It lies within the wider mapped region without functioning as an ordinary Allemagnian barony.

## III. Subregions, Routes & Named Features

Allemance is organized as much by connection as by terrain.

- **The central Hearthlands** support Louvain, major agrarian settlements, and the densest network of maintained roads.
- **The river baronies** combine local noble authority with guild, agricultural, and civic institutions.
- **The western Causeway approach** terminates at Sourisport and carries traffic between Allemance and Arneria.

## V. Government, Law & Authority

Allemance's practical political units are its baronies and chartered cities. Local authorities set law, taxation, guard obligations, and land use within their jurisdictions. Guild coalitions and urban councils provide a growing counterweight by controlling labor standards, trade access, and municipal services.

The crown no longer exercises general executive rule. Its inherited charters, ceremonial offices, and prestige can still support mediation or collective response, but no current regional filing establishes a reigning sovereign who commands Allemance as a whole. Surviving crown and noble institutions are catalogued under [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/05. Governments & Geopolitics/IV. Crowns, Noble Houses & Inherited Offices/1. Allemance/index|Allemance: Crowns, Houses & Inherited Offices]].

The MDO supplies cross-baronial continuity where delver licensing, relic law, or Labyrinth access exceeds local jurisdiction. Its influence is substantial, especially from Louvain, but it does not erase baronial and civic law.

## VI. Peoples & Regional Life

Allemagnian identity is often expressed through the hearth, table, neighborhood, guild, and remembered place. Hospitality, feastcraft, household memory, and mutual aid are widely valued public practices, though no resident should be treated as personally warm, traditional, or communal merely because they are Allemagnian.

Local identity varies sharply. Louvain lives under record; Goldentide organizes around harvest security; Patrie preserves the memory and fabric of a displaced world; Sourisport embraces the freedoms and pressures of a border terminus. Rural households may understand authority through a barony or parish long before they understand it through the region.

The **Table Oath**—a promise made under conditions of shared food and shelter—illustrates a recurring Allemagnian principle: provision creates responsibility. Its exact legal or sacred force depends on the community witnessing it.

Some local codes treat the refusal of emergency shelter as a civic offense. That protection is jurisdiction-specific and should not be expanded into an unlimited right to enter a private household.

## VII. Economy & Infrastructure

Allemance is a major producer of grain, ale, preserved foods, textiles, and artisan goods. Louvain's schools, archives, ministries, and training institutions add administrative and educational labor to the regional economy. Imports include industrial materials, exotic reagents, relic components, and goods that arrive through Vinyoti, Al'Ari, Oric, and Arnerian routes.

Roads, river docks, bridges, granaries, inns, courier systems, and public records are the region's defining infrastructure. Their density supports ordinary travel and allows the MDO and guild networks to operate across multiple jurisdictions.

The system's strength is also a dependency: a failed bridge, disputed charter, contaminated storehouse, or blocked river can affect settlements far beyond the authority responsible for repairing it. Broader exchange practices are maintained in [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/04. Society, Economy & Everyday Life/04. Trade & Travel Across Aerathon|Trade & Travel Across Aerathon]].

## VIII. Faith, Magic & Delving

Allemagnian worship is closely associated with hearths, fields, memory, learning, craft, hospitality, and the keeping of promises. Local shrines and household rites coexist with institutions serving gods recognized in the [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/II. The Living Atlas/03. Religion & Worship/I. Pantheons & Inherited Traditions/01. Modern Aerathon Pantheon|Modern Aerathon Pantheon]]. Some shrines leave an empty guest seat in case the divine arrives unannounced. No short regional list should be treated as exhaustive.

Delving is commonly framed as public responsibility before personal glory. Training sites, protected rest zones, published maps, and licensing systems are more developed here than in many regions, particularly around Louvain. That institutional confidence does not make every Labyrinth safe or every barony equally welcoming to delvers.

Magical practice is deeply embedded in archives, transport, preservation, lighting, and civic maintenance. Allemance's approach favors systems that can be recorded, taught, inspected, and repaired.

## IX. Travel, Hazards & Access

Allemance is generally approached by maintained road, river, coast, or the western end of the Causeway. Travelers should carry the permits required by their work—especially for delving, relic transport, restricted archives, or guild-regulated cargo—but ordinary regional movement is not governed by a single border authority.

Common hazards are infrastructural and jurisdictional as often as environmental: flooding, damaged roads, disputed tolls, local closures, harvest congestion, and inconsistent rules between neighboring baronies. Glasrún requires separate attention; the existence of a mapped road or woodland path does not override its pact or local authority.

## X. Historical Context

Allemance's modern identity rests on the institutions that followed collapse: shared law, the memory of the **First Concord**, the hearth as a civic symbol, and Louvain's emergence as a neutral center of record. Early **hearth-circles** joined food, testimony, and collective decision-making; their legacy persists in guild, archive, and civic practice. Over time, royal and noble authority became localized while cities, guilds, and the MDO assumed responsibilities that no crown could reliably exercise across the region.

Earlier dynastic claims and the breaking of old political orders belong in the Annals, particularly [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/01. Fundamentals of Recorded Reality/I. Cosmology & Origins/04. The First Concord and Its Breaking|The First Concord and Its Breaking]] and [[Aerathon - Eternal Labyrinths/I. Annals & Antiquities/05. Figures, Institutions, Houses & Lineages/III. Houses, Dynasties & Inherited Offices/01. The Bell-Born Dynasty|The Bell-Born Dynasty]]. Their relevance here is institutional: titles endure, but their former powers do not automatically endure with them.

## XI. External Relations

- **Vinyot** is a reliable trade partner whose corporate governance challenges Allemagnian inherited privilege.
- **Al'Ar** is respected for maritime autonomy and essential to overseas exchange.
- **Arneria** is connected through the Causeway; Sourisport makes that relationship immediate, commercial, and culturally visible.
- **Oria** is a northern partner in craft, defense, and overland trade, with the former Kingsfang border now largely historical.
- The **MDO** is both an Allemagnian institution and a continent-wide authority, a dual role that can complicate claims of regional neutrality.

## XII. Current Conditions

Allemance's central problem is not the absence of authority but its overlap. Baronies, cities, guilds, inherited offices, and the MDO can all possess legitimate but incomplete jurisdiction over the same movement of people or goods.

Current registry priorities are to distinguish living offices from purely historical titles, expand coverage of the lesser-documented settlement network, and keep road and border guidance synchronized with local charters. No claim of royal authority should be inferred from a surviving crown symbol alone.
