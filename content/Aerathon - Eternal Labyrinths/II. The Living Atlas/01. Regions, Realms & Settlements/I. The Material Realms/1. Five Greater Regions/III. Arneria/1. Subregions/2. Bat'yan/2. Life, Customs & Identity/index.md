# Life, Customs & Identity in Bat'yan

Bat'yani identity is village-first, family-centered, and shaped by the need to move with the forest rather than impose permanence upon it. Belonging survives absence; those who leave are generally expected to return.

## Local Order

- Barangays govern through small-scale communal authority rather than a single subregional office.
- Buildings are raised against flood and designed for repair, relocation, and seasonal change.
- Trade and Ministry access are negotiated community by community, with reputation carrying more weight than distant paperwork.

## Daily Life & Worldview

- Work begins early, pauses through the hottest part of the day, and returns households to communal evenings.
- Ancestral reverence, situational honors to nature-spirits and old gods, shared meals, seasonal songs, and communal labor are common.
- Art and music are treated as participatory obligations rather than separate professional domains.

## Livelihood

Communities prioritize subsistence before surplus. Fruits, root crops, medicines, hardwood, resin, and animal products support local life; preserved food, tonics, and crafts travel outward in exchange for metal tools, salt, and selected luxuries.
