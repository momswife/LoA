## I. Overview

**Mythicfolk** is an administrative category for sapient peoples whose rarity, origin, abilities, or relationship to setting mysteries can materially alter a campaign.

It is not one ancestry.

It is not a biological family.

It is not a polite word for monster.

The classification currently contains Dragonborn descended from draconic pacts, ageless Jackals associated with hidden cities and the Seelie Arcana, rare self-aware Kobolds known as **named kobolds**, created Constructfolk, mutable Changelings, composite-bodied Centaurs and Gryphonids, and snake-crowned Gorgonites.

Earlier editions also placed Astralborn Bats here. Those people are now recognized as [[Chiropterid]] Avianfolk; no separate Mythicfolk Bat lineage remains.

Mythicfolk require DM approval because their stories may reveal concealed histories, introduce unusual social conditions, or carry mechanics requiring campaign coordination.

Approval governs character use.

It does not govern personhood.

---

## II. Why the Category Exists

Ordinary ancestry registries assume stable populations, broadly understood origins, and enough community presence to distinguish lineage from rumor.

Mythicfolk records often lack one or more of those conditions.

A Jackal may have lived through events preserved elsewhere only as legend. A named Kobold may be the first demonstrably self-aware member of a warren. A Dragonborn’s clan compact may contain obligations to an active wyrm, a dead one, or something in a Labyrinth that merely claims the title.

These characters can change what a group knows before play begins.

The category therefore signals three needs:

- Shared decisions about secrets and campaign scope
- Review of unusual or experimental mechanics
- Careful integration of rare people into local society

It does not signal greater power, importance, virtue, or narrative entitlement.

---

## III. Recognition and Personhood

The Ministry distinguishes **recognition** from **eligibility**.

Recognition concerns whether an individual is a person entitled to safety, consent, property, movement, testimony, and civic protection.

Eligibility concerns whether a particular expedition or campaign can safely and coherently support that character’s abilities and history.

No Dragonborn loses personhood because a breath weapon complicates a guild license.

No Jackal becomes public property because their memory reaches into antiquity.

No named Kobold may be caged, studied, conscripted, or assigned a guardian merely because officials consider their consciousness anomalous.

The Ministry’s older phrase **conditional recognition** is retained in historical quotations but rejected in current personhood law.

## IV. Active Lineages

### [[Dragonborn]]

Dragonborn are mortal peoples shaped by draconic pacts, transformation, descent, and elemental inheritance. Their clans debate whether a dragon’s legacy is ancestry, contract, theft, blessing, or burden.

DM review establishes the relevant dragon, oath, clan history, and version of breath-weapon rules.

### [[Jackal]]

Jackals are rare Fey peoples associated with the Beast World, hidden cities, extraordinary longevity, and direct intimacy with the Arcana. Their ability to alter memory demands explicit table consent and clear narrative limits.

DM review establishes what the Jackal remembers, which hidden city shaped them, and what knowledge remains inaccessible.

### [[Kobold|Named Kobold]]

Named Kobolds are individuals who demonstrate self-awareness among populations historically described as instinctive draconic thralls. Their emergence raises unresolved questions about cognition, magical design, and the reliability of Ministry tests.

DM review establishes the circumstances of awakening, the surrounding warren, and whether other Kobolds may also be changing.

### [[Constructfolk]]

Constructfolk are created or awakened people whose forged, carved, woven, or arcane bodies raise campaign questions about lost technology, repair, memory continuity, and emancipation from designed purpose.

DM review establishes chassis traits, healing and repair rules, creator history, and which commands possess no authority.

### [[Changeling]]

Changelings are innate shapechanging people whose many forms are authentic expressions rather than evidence of deception. Their identity records and transformation histories require exceptional privacy.

DM review establishes transformation limits, impersonation consent, recognition procedures, and the campaign’s interest in identity themes.

### [[Centaur]]

Centaurs are composite equine-bodied people whose road histories and large quadrupedal forms require route, equipment, and access planning distinct from [[Equine]] Beastfolk.

DM review establishes climbing, squeezing, carrying, vehicle, and map-access rules.

### [[Gryphonid]]

Gryphonids—also called Gryphonfolk or Gryffinkind—are composite raptor-feline people associated with eyries, flight, broken mount contracts, and freely chosen sky-oaths.

DM review establishes flight progression, carrying, body shape, armor, and the role of ancient guardian traditions.

### [[Gorgonite]]

Gorgonites—also called Medusans or Medusa Folk—are snake-crowned people with controlled stone-affecting gazes. They are distinct from [[Ophidian]] Scalefolk.

DM review establishes gaze safety, mirrors, petrification limits, living-crown boundaries, and relevant body-horror consent.

## V. Reclassification and Disputed Boundaries

Mythicfolk is deliberately revisable.

When a people becomes better understood, establishes wider communities, or chooses another collective identity, the Ministry may transfer its record.

Astralborn Bats were once treated as planar anomalies because early registries focused upon rarity and physiology. Community consultation demonstrated that aerial anatomy, Astral flight, architecture, and shared culture placed them more coherently among Avianfolk. They are now [[Chiropterid|Chiropterids]].

Similar disputes continue around:

- Whether Dragonborn should form an independent folk classification
- Whether named Kobolds belong within Beastfolk, Mythicfolk, or a self-defined people
- Whether Jackal secrecy makes meaningful population estimates impossible
- Whether the category attracts exploitation by collectors, scholars, and broadcasters
- Whether DM approval language encourages the public to treat actual citizens as optional

The Ministry considers a classification useful only while it protects more than it distorts.

## VI. Mythicfolk in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Rare lineages become objects of court fascination, scholarship, patronage, and spectacle. Access may look like hospitality while functioning as surveillance.

### Oria

House traditions judge unusual peoples through ancestral precedent. Protection can be strong once offered, but recognition may depend upon categories that have no appropriate precedent.

### Vinyot

Mythicfolk draw broadcast contracts, research offers, brand licensing, collectors, and betting markets. Commercial visibility can finance independence or make privacy nearly impossible.

### Arneria

Story, oath, and hospitality provide ways to receive people whose history exceeds written proof. The danger is forcing a living person into the role their legend assigned them.

### Al'Ar

Plural religious and kinship traditions often allow anomalous lineages to find community without complete explanation. Outsiders sometimes romanticize this acceptance and ignore local disputes.

## VII. Delving and Campaign Impact

Mythicfolk frequently interact with the parts of a Labyrinth that imitate ancestry, bargain through blood, awaken constructed servants, distort memory, or answer forgotten names.

Before play, the group should establish:

- Which lineage mechanics are approved
- Whether they replace or supplement current edition rules
- What setting secrets the character may know
- Which authorities recognize the character
- What attention the lineage attracts
- Which themes require safety boundaries
- Whether rarity will create spotlight and how that spotlight will be shared

DM approval should produce a playable agreement.

It should not become a permanent threat that the character may be removed whenever their unusual nature matters.

---

## VIII. Secrecy, Evidence, and Exploitation

Mythicfolk records contain information attractive to governments, cults, guilds, collectors, dragons, arcanists, and illegal broadcasters.

The common abuses are predictable:

- Treating testimony as a research sample
- Demanding demonstrations of inherited magic
- Publishing a hidden settlement’s location
- Testing personhood through hostile examinations
- Claiming ancient contracts remain binding upon descendants
- Confusing rarity with ownership
- Turning a citizen into evidence for someone else’s theory

Ministry field personnel are instructed to ask what information may be recorded, who may receive it, and what harm publication could cause.

An archive can endanger a people as efficiently as a weapon.

## IX. Mechanics and Approval Standard

Each active lineage page identifies its current mechanics.

Unless a page states otherwise:

- Lineage mechanics require DM approval
- Homeland traits represent upbringing rather than blood
- A character selects only the options authorized for the campaign
- Rare abilities should be reviewed for action economy, spell access, and setting consequences
- Narrative restrictions must be discussed before character creation
- Approval may adjust mechanics, but should preserve the lineage’s central fantasy

Mechanics labeled historical are not current player options.

Current Astralborn Bat culture and mechanics are maintained exclusively in [[Chiropterid]].

---

## X. Naming and Public Address

Use the person’s stated lineage and name.

**Mythicfolk** is appropriate for registry discussion and collective policy. Many individuals do not use it socially.

Avoid terms such as _specimen_, _cryptid_, _lesser dragon_, _awakened animal_, _storybook creature_, or _living relic_ unless a person has reclaimed one for themselves.

Do not ask a rare person to prove what they are through magic, transformation, blood, scale, memory, or ancestry.

Rarity does not suspend ordinary courtesy.

---

## XI. Playing a Mythicfolk Character

Consider:

- Who knows what you are?
- Who thinks they know?
- What part of your origin is genuinely uncertain?
- Which story about your people is most damaging?
- What ability do strangers feel entitled to witness?
- What ordinary life do you protect from myth?
- Which authority wants to classify you?
- What campaign secret do you know, and what have you misunderstood?
- Why did this group earn your trust?
- What would make rarity stop defining you?

A Mythicfolk character should possess interests, flaws, routines, relationships, and ambitions unrelated to their classification.

Mystery is context.

It is not a complete personality.

---

## XII. Registry and Table Advisory

> [!warning] DM Approval Required  
> Mythicfolk player characters require explicit approval of lineage mechanics, origin details, campaign knowledge, and likely social consequences before play.
>
> Approval requirements exist for campaign coordination. They do not imply conditional personhood within the setting.
>
> Memory alteration, inherited servitude, sapience testing, bodily experimentation, and coercive ancestry claims should be used only with table consent.
