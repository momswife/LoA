## I. Lineage Overview

Goliaths are tall Humanoids whose histories are associated with mountains, storms, elemental pacts, oral memory, and trials through which a person publicly names what an experience changed.

Oria’s high passes and Arneria’s sky-facing canyons contain ancient Goliath communities, but modern Goliaths also live in courts, ports, factories, farms, arenas, ships, and lowland cities.

They are commonly reduced to strength.

Goliath cultures value capability, but capability includes navigation, memory, restraint, teaching, weather reading, carrying responsibility, and recognizing when a group must turn back.

A deed matters because it changes a relationship—not because it makes the doer superior.

The central Goliath question is not whether hardship can be survived.

It is what survival obligates the survivor to do next.

---

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Goliath traditions preserve no single written origin. Sagas describe ancestors who entered pacts with mountain, wind, thunder, and the exposed places where shelter cannot be assumed.

Some Dawn scholars classify them as specialized Brethren. Many Goliath memory-keepers describe later covenant rather than original creation. The difference matters because divine-design language has been used to assign Goliaths roles as soldiers and laborers.

### Deed Memory

History is carried through recitation, route, scar, gesture, rhythm, and repeated action. A saga may be performed while climbing the terrain where it occurred so that distance, breath, and weather remain part of the evidence.

Oral record is not casual memory. Goliath reciters train for precision, source distinction, and the public correction of disputed verses.

### Trial-Names

A Trial-Name records a defining ordeal, choice, failure, or return. It is earned by meaning rather than victory.

No legitimate tradition may impose a dangerous trial solely to manufacture a name.

---

## III. Body, Scale, and Environment

Goliaths are usually tall, powerfully built Humanoids with broad physiological variation. Skin may bear natural markings, ritual pigments, tattoos, weathering, or scar records.

Large bodies require appropriate food, equipment, beds, armor, doorways, rescue plans, and medical care. Institutions often celebrate Goliath size while refusing to accommodate it.

Strength does not eliminate pain, disability, fear, or fatigue.

Goliaths use mobility aids, prosthetics, support harnesses, and cooperative climbing systems. A person who cannot complete a traditional physical deed may adapt it, witness it, teach it, or choose another form of trial.

The body is evidence of experience.

It is not a public challenge.

---

## IV. Society, Fellowship, and Trial

Goliath households include clans, climbing fellowships, urban blocks, arena companies, weather stations, caravan circles, ships, and chosen families.

### The Witnessed Turn

Many trials include a witness authorized to call a turn. Turning back under agreed conditions is a completed judgment, not failure.

The practice distinguishes courage from submission to pressure.

### Story-Rights

The person whose deed is told holds the first right to frame its meaning. Witnesses may preserve what they observed, but may not convert another person’s trauma into public inspiration without consent.

Commercial arenas and broadcasts regularly violate this principle.

### Competition

Competition may train skill, celebrate effort, and reveal limits. It becomes dishonorable when conditions are concealed, refusal is punished, or unequal resources are treated as proof of unequal worth.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Goliath communities commonly admire courage, candor, endurance, competence, generosity, self-knowledge, and public accountability.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | Using capacity in service of need | Treating help as humiliation |
| Courage | Acting while naming danger honestly | Performing recklessness for recognition |
| Competition | Making improvement visible | Turning every relationship into rank |
| Endurance | Continuing through meaningful hardship | Ignoring injury until others must intervene |
| Candor | Speaking conditions plainly | Using honesty as permission for cruelty |
| Memory | Carrying deeds accurately | Reducing a person to one famous act |

A recurring Goliath fear is being valued only while useful under pressure.

Some continually prove themselves. Some refuse assistance. Others cultivate gentleness, scholarship, humor, or stillness as deliberate rejection of the role assigned to their bodies.

## VI. Goliaths in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemagnian Goliaths work as wardens, bodyguards, advocates, performers, architects, and crowd readers. Their physical presence is often used as a symbol by institutions that exclude them from actual authority.

### Oria

Oria is a major Goliath heartland. Cliff villages, wind bridges, mountain keeps, rescue fellowships, and Trial-Name traditions shape community life.

Current debates focus upon inherited trials, disability access, and who may tell a deed publicly.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Goliaths work in docks, industry, security, debt collection, spectacle, and professional sport. Arena traditions create fame while encouraging audiences to treat injury as authenticity.

### Arneria

Arnerian Goliaths are ruin climbers, sand-walkers, caravan defenders, astronomers, and memory-keepers. Their sagas are performed through landscapes threatened by shifting borders and Labyrinth weather.

### Al'Ar

Al'Ari Goliaths are swimmers, dock foremen, stormbreakers, volcanic guides, and open-sea rescuers. Ocean traditions emphasize yielding, timing, and lateral movement rather than simply resisting force.

## VII. Goliaths and Delving

Goliath delvers commonly serve as front-line defenders, porters, climbers, rescue specialists, navigators, storm readers, and formation anchors.

Their strengths include:

- Moving through vertical environments
- Carrying loads and injured allies
- Reading weather and exposed terrain
- Maintaining group confidence under pressure
- Testing physical routes carefully
- Acting decisively when retreat becomes necessary

The common failure is assigning every heavy, dangerous, or sacrificial task to the Goliath.

Load plans should rotate where possible. Rescue equipment must fit the rescuer. A Goliath’s willingness to attempt a route does not obligate them to become the party’s disposable safety margin.

The Labyrinth may present a perfect trial: public, measurable, and apparently meaningful.

Experienced Goliaths ask who designed it and what the test wants to own.

## X. Homeland Traits

### Allemance — Civic Sentinel

You gain proficiency in Insight. Additionally, when you take the Ready action to guard an ally, they gain temporary hit points equal to your proficiency bonus.

### Oria — Trial-Name

Once per long rest, when you would be reduced to 0 hit points, you can drop to 1 hit point instead. When you do, you gain temporary hit points equal to your level and cannot be frightened until the end of your next turn.

### Vinyot — Arena's Roar

When you reduce a creature to 0 hit points or score a critical hit, you can force all hostile creatures within 10 feet to make a Wisdom saving throw (DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier) or be frightened until the end of their next turn.

### Arneria — Sunforged Grit

You gain proficiency in Survival. Additionally, you can go twice as long without water before suffering exhaustion.

### Al'Ar — Breaker of Storms

You gain a swimming speed of 30 feet. When you are hit by lightning or thunder damage, you can use your reaction to grant yourself resistance to that damage and move up to 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks.

## XI. Playing a Goliath

Consider:

- Do you possess a Trial-Name?
- Who may tell its story?
- What kind of strength matters to you?
- When did you choose to turn back?
- What challenge do others keep assigning to you?
- How do you receive help?
- Which deed has overshadowed the rest of your life?
- What landscape carries your history?
- Is competition joyful, exhausting, or irrelevant?
- What will you do after the trial ends?

A Goliath may be physically weak, cautious, soft-spoken, urban, uncompetitive, disabled, scholarly, or uninterested in public deeds.

Worth is not a trial result.
