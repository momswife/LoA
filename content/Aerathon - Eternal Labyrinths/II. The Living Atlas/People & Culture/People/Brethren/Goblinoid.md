## I. Lineage Overview

Goblinoid is a collective Brethren classification encompassing Goblin, Hobgoblin, and Bugbear peoples joined by ancient Fey ancestry, histories of conquest, and a persistent talent for making durable life in spaces other societies discard.

The category describes kinship without erasing difference. Goblins, Hobgoblins, and Bugbears possess distinct bodies and traditions. None is a developmental stage, military caste, or moral tendency of another.

---

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Old accounts place Goblinoid ancestors in Fey territories later conquered by a war power that reorganized communities into raiders, officers, and heavy infantry. Mortal empires copied the classification because it made occupation legible.

The **Three Doors Compact** rejects those assigned roles. Each door bears a different height and shape; all open into the same council chamber. The compact is a declaration that coordination does not require hierarchy by body.

---

## III. Body and Sensory Life

Goblins are Small and quick, Hobgoblins generally Medium and broad-framed, and Bugbears tall, long-limbed, and quiet-moving. Complexion, hair, ears, teeth, and build vary widely.

None has an instinctive military rank. Bugbear reach does not create consent to lifting or intimidation work. Goblin size is not childhood. Hobgoblin composure is not obedience.

---

## IV. Society and Kinship

Goblinoid households include warrens, street councils, disciplined fellowships, extended families, night markets, salvage cooperatives, theaters, farms, and mobile camps.

The **Useful Ruin Principle** grants first consideration to people already maintaining an allegedly abandoned place. It emerged after cities repeatedly displaced Goblin neighborhoods only after residents made dangerous infrastructure livable.

---

## V. Inner Life and Modern Debate

Goblinoid communities admire improvisation, mutual aid, precision under pressure, humor, hospitality without ceremony, and loyalty that remains chosen.

Internal debate centers on discipline. Some reclaim coordinated martial traditions as collective protection. Others hear every uniform as the return of the conquered order.

---

## VI. Goblinoid Peoples in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Goblinoid districts flourish in service corridors, old walls, theaters, and guild margins where residency law remains contested.

### Oria

Winter warrens and disciplined road crews maintain warm routes through infrastructure larger peoples overlook.

### Vinyot

Night markets, salvage unions, arena companies, and contract fellowships shape visible public life.

### Arneria

Ruin settlements and caravan camps preserve the Useful Ruin Principle in local law.

### Al'Ar

Port warrens, ship crews, and island repair circles specialize in compact shared infrastructure.

---

## VII. Goblinoid Delvers

Goblinoid delvers excel in scouting, ambush prevention, logistics, salvage, formation support, and using confined terrain.

Crews must distinguish familiarity with marginal spaces from obligation to enter them first. A Goblin is not a trap tool, a Hobgoblin is not an automatic commander, and a Bugbear is not portable siege equipment.

## X. Recognized Sublineages and Traditions

### Goblin

You are Small and have a walking speed of 30 feet. **Nimble Escape:** you can take the Disengage or Hide action as a bonus action. **Fury of the Small:** proficiency bonus times per long rest, deal extra damage equal to your proficiency bonus to a creature larger than you.

### Hobgoblin

You are Medium and have a walking speed of 30 feet. **Fey Gift:** proficiency bonus times per long rest, take the Help action as a bonus action; from 3rd level, the assisted creature also gains temporary hit points equal to your proficiency bonus.

### Bugbear

You are Medium and have a walking speed of 30 feet. **Long-Limbed:** your reach is 5 feet greater when you make a melee attack on your turn. **Quiet Giant:** gain proficiency in Stealth.

## XI. Playing a Goblinoid

Consider:

- Which of the Three Doors is yours?
- What place did your community make livable?
- How do you feel about discipline?
- What assigned role do you reject?
- Who may call you by a collective name?

A character may embrace, reinterpret, or reject every tradition described above. Lineage creates context, not personality.
