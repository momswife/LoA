## I. Lineage Overview

Gnomes are small Humanoids associated with magical resilience, experimental craft, intense curiosity, and the conversion of improbable ideas into working—or instructively nonworking—things.

They are prominent in Vinyoti workshops and Allemagnian laboratories, but Gnomish communities also maintain Oric observatories, Arnerian road schools, Al'Ari tideworks, farms, theaters, archives, kitchens, and quiet domestic lives.

The public often treats every Gnome as an inventor.

Curiosity is broader than machinery.

A Gnome may study jokes, fungi, contracts, weather, memory, pedagogy, medicine, shoes, ghosts, bread, or the sound a particular stair makes before a Labyrinth rearranges itself.

Gnomish culture values questions that can survive the failure of their first answer.

Its central ethic is not that everything should be attempted.

It is that every attempt must teach someone more than the inventor alone.

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Gnomish origins before the Convergence remain unclear. Some traditions describe specialized branches of the first Brethren. Others claim separate Material worlds where magic behaved more erratically and small communities survived by learning quickly.

The most widely discussed inherited quality is **mana resilience**: an unusual capacity to endure, interpret, or recover from unstable magical environments.

Mana resilience is neither immunity nor universal spellcasting talent.

It may appear as rapid acclimation, resistance to particular flux, intuitive pattern recognition, or simply survival after exposure that should still have been prevented.

### Failure Ledgers

Many Gnomish workshops maintain public **failure ledgers** recording unsuccessful designs, injuries, false assumptions, unexpected uses, and abandoned questions.

A beautiful failure is one documented well enough that nobody must repeat it unknowingly.

Secret failure is considered especially dangerous when the original experiment could be reproduced from published success claims.

---

## III. Body, Scale, and Magical Exposure

Gnomes are Small Humanoids with wide variation in build, complexion, hair, features, and visible magical adaptation.

Some display color shifts, luminous freckles, unusually responsive hair, minor static, or other harmless traces of ambient mana. Others possess no visible markers.

Their scale shapes architecture and tool use. Gnomish spaces often use adjustable surfaces, layered storage, narrow maintenance passages, mobile steps, and controls accessible from several heights.

Mana resilience has led institutions to assign Gnomes disproportionate exposure to wild magic.

This is unsafe and discriminatory.

A Gnome’s ability to survive an accident does not create consent to become the detector, shield, or test subject for one.

---

## IV. Society, Learning, and Experiment

Gnomish households include families, laboratories, traveling schools, repair circles, performance troupes, neighborhood workshops, and correspondence networks whose members may never meet in person.

Status often follows the ability to explain a difficult idea clearly, repair another person’s work without humiliating them, or recognize when an experiment should stop.

### The Third Question

Before a risky project, many Gnomish teams ask three questions:

1. What do we expect?
2. What would prove us wrong?
3. Who pays if we are wrong in a way we did not imagine?

The third question distinguishes experimentation from gambling with another person’s safety.

### Open Benches

An **open bench** is a time when tools, unfinished designs, and instruction are shared without guild rank. Commercial workshops argue over how much knowledge can be opened without surrendering livelihoods to larger firms.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Gnomish communities commonly admire curiosity, play, ingenuity, candor, flexibility, and intellectual generosity.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | Asking questions others missed | Treating privacy as an obstacle |
| Ingenuity | Solving problems with available means | Creating a solution before understanding the need |
| Optimism | Continuing after failure | Minimizing another person’s risk |
| Candor | Publishing error honestly | Performing bluntness without care |
| Play | Discovering through experimentation | Refusing seriousness when harm occurs |
| Flexibility | Revising a theory quickly | Abandoning people with the discarded plan |

A recurring Gnomish fear is that the next question will disappear before they can ask it.

Some collect projects. Some specialize fiercely. Some reject experimental culture because their boundaries were ignored by enthusiastic teachers, employers, or relatives.

## VI. Gnomes in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemagnian Gnomes staff guild laboratories, universities, patent offices, theaters, and magical safety boards. Their debates are exacting, public, and frequently theatrical.

Access to prestigious equipment remains controlled by old guild families.

### Oria

Oric Gnomes maintain observatories, winter experiments, glacier instruments, and projects intended to gather evidence across generations. Their precision challenges the stereotype of Gnomish impatience.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Gnomes create personal devices, one-use enchantments, defensive systems, broadcast effects, and disposable conveniences. They lead movements demanding repair rights and responsibility for magical waste.

### Arneria

Arnerian Gnomes travel between outposts as teachers, alchemists, bomb-makers, philosophers, and spell-dissectors. Their road culture prizes knowledge that can be carried, repaired, and taught without a dedicated laboratory.

### Al'Ar

Al'Ari Gnomes build wind-driven machinery, tide reactors, navigation aids, and coastal defenses. Marine environments teach them that a machine’s boundary never ends at its casing.

## VII. Gnomes and Delving

Gnomish delvers commonly serve as trap analysts, artificers, shield-casters, alchemists, teleportation technicians, cartographers, and structural improvisers.

Their strengths include:

- Testing a theory quickly
- Repurposing limited supplies
- Recognizing wild-magic behavior
- Building temporary systems
- Documenting unexpected outcomes
- Maintaining morale through play

The Labyrinths reward curiosity until they weaponize it.

Some doors exist solely because someone will ask what they open. Some devices change behavior after being understood. Some puzzles punish the assumption that a solution is morally neutral.

Good crews give a Gnome time to investigate and authority to say that investigation should stop.

## X. Heritage Traits

### Allemance — Paradox-Solver

You gain proficiency in Arcana. When you fail to identify a spell or magic item, you can immediately retry the check with disadvantage.

### Oria — Frostfield Modulator

You are resistant to cold damage. Additionally, when you cast a spell that deals cold damage, you can reroll one damage die. You must use the new result.

### Vinyot — Pocket Wonders

You can cast _absorb elements_ once per long rest without expending a spell slot. Intelligence is your spellcasting ability for this spell.

### Arneria — Kaboomsmith

You gain proficiency with alchemist’s supplies. When you throw an alchemical item, such as alchemist’s fire or acid, the target has disadvantage on the saving throw if they are within 5 feet of the explosion point.

### Al'Ar — Saltforged Ingenuity

You can breathe underwater for up to 10 minutes at a time. Additionally, you ignore difficult terrain caused by sand, silt, or shallow water.

## XI. Playing a Gnome

Consider:

- What subject earns your deepest attention?
- Which failure taught you the most?
- Who paid for an experiment you regret?
- What question are you forbidden to ask?
- Do you publish your work, guard it, or teach it selectively?
- How do you respond when people expect entertainment?
- What ordinary routine keeps you grounded?
- When does curiosity become intrusion?
- What device do you refuse to build?
- Who is allowed to tell you to stop?

A Gnome may be cautious, humorless, nonmagical, technically inept, uninterested in invention, or exhausted by novelty.

Curiosity may be quiet.

## XII. Registry Advisory

> [!note] MDO Registry Advisory  
> Gnomish lineage does not indicate technical competence, magical immunity, emotional cheerfulness, willingness to experiment, or consent to handle unstable materials.
>
> Mana resilience is not protective equipment. Gnomes must receive the same hazard controls and informed-consent standards as all personnel.
>
> Experimental work affecting a community requires that community’s participation before deployment.
