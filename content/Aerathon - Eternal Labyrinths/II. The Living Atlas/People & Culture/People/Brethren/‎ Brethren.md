## I. Overview

**Brethren** is a collective historical and cultural classification for several mortal peoples whose societies placed particular emphasis upon durable craft, written record, chosen oath, public office, and institutions capable of surviving their founders.

It is not one ancestry.

It is not a synonym for human.

It is not evidence of shared blood, divine superiority, or a singular lost empire.

The term has included Humans, Elves, Dwarves, Gnomes, Halflings, Goliaths, people of Orcish and mixed descent, Tieflings, and numerous communities whose historical names no longer match modern registries.

The oldest surviving forms of the word are plural. They describe people joined by responsibility: those entrusted with unfinished work, those who may answer inherited law, and those who choose obligations capable of outliving one household.

Modern Brethren are far more diverse than that inheritance suggests. They live in every Greater Region, practice every major religion, participate in Beastfolk and Avianfolk kinships, and disagree vigorously over whether the category remains useful.

The Living Atlas retains it because the term still shapes law, history, education, and self-identification.

It must never be mistaken for a measure of personhood.

## II. Origins and Historical Meaning

The dominant Dawn Court tradition holds that the first Brethren were made during the [[01. The First Making|First Making]] to learn designs, repair them, teach them, and change them when mortal circumstances demanded change.

Human, Elven, and Dwarven peoples appear most consistently in the oldest accounts. Gnomish, Halfling, Goliath, Orcish, infernal-marked, and other ancestries enter the record through independent origins, later covenants, migration, adoption, intermarriage, civic recognition, and political reclassification.

No surviving evidence establishes a single Brethren progenitor.

The earliest settlements were mixed worlds. Brethren measurement and Beastfolk environmental knowledge appear in the same foundations. Permanent roads bent around migration paths. Written measures stood beside signs carried through song, touch, scent, and living memory.

The later claim that Brethren alone invented cities, law, writing, reason, or ordered magic is Crown-era propaganda.

Brethren institutions did become unusually effective at preserving decisions. That strength built archives, schools, courts, workshops, roads, and public works. It also allowed records to overrule memory, titles to overrule use, and offices to preserve themselves after their purposes had failed.

Modern Brethren scholarship therefore treats continuity as both inheritance and responsibility.

## III. Shared Inheritances

Five inheritances recur across Brethren history without appearing identically in every lineage.

### Name

A name establishes a person across household, profession, worship, office, and law. Many Brethren traditions use several names rather than one permanent legal identity.

### Record

Records allow absent people and future generations to answer earlier decisions. They preserve knowledge, but they can also classify people who had no authority over what was written about them.

### Craft

Craft joins knowledge to repeatable action. Brethren craft traditions tend to value teachability, revision, and a lineage of practice extending beyond one family.

### Oath

Chosen words can create obligations among strangers. Oaths support households, marriages, guilds, temples, expeditions, governments, and peace agreements.

### Office

Authority may belong to a role rather than the individual holding it. This permits durable institutions and creates the danger that officeholders will mistake survival for legitimacy.

No Brethren person is culturally required to value all five.

They are historical patterns, not instincts.

## IV. Peoples Recognized Within the Classification

### [[Human]]

Humans are widespread, rapidly adapting peoples whose kingdoms frequently treated their own experience as the Brethren default. Modern Human communities are distinguished less by one shared culture than by their ability to build identity through place, institution, and chosen purpose.

### [[Elves|Elf]]

Elves are long-lived peoples of echo, song, arcane resonance, and century-spanning memory. Their relationship to inherited obligation is profound and frequently contested.

### [[Dwarf]]

Dwarves preserve material knowledge through stone, metal, tools, structures, and craft lineages. Their mountain compacts complicate claims that they were shaped solely by the Dawn Court.

### [[Gnome]]

Gnomes are associated with mana resilience, experimentation, and the conversion of curiosity into devices, techniques, and occasionally spectacular administrative problems.

### [[Halfling]]

Halflings have built durable communities through hospitality, neighborhood obligation, cultivation, and the skilled work of making home possible under changing conditions.

### [[Goliath]]

Goliaths preserve deed, trial, and oral memory across highland, desert, urban, and island traditions. Their histories emphasize embodied knowledge without reducing worth to strength.

### [[Half-Orc]]

Half-Orc is a modern registry term used for people with Orcish and other ancestry. Many accept it; others prefer Orc, mixed-descent, household, regional, or self-chosen names.

### [[Tiefling]]

Tieflings are fully mortal Brethren whose bodies carry infernal, lower-planar, or pact-shaped inheritances. Ancestral bargains do not determine allegiance, morality, or continuing consent.

## V. Household and Belonging

Brethren households may be formed through birth, adoption, marriage, sworn partnership, apprenticeship, temple service, shared property, military fellowship, civic sponsorship, refuge, or common work.

Different communities give these relationships different weight.

An Elven memory household may maintain responsibility across centuries. A Dwarven craft lineage may receive an apprentice into obligations that continue through their students. A Human household may transform repeatedly through migration. Halfling neighborhoods often formalize mutual care without requiring shared property. Goliath fellowship can preserve a deed-name after the original witnesses have died.

The legal household recorded for taxation or inheritance is only one form of kinship.

Modern Brethren increasingly use **answerable belonging** to describe relationships that create duties while leaving those duties open to question, revision, and release.

An oath that cannot be reconsidered is not automatically sacred.

It may simply be coercion with excellent handwriting.

## VI. Brethren in the Greater Regions

### Allemance

Allemance emphasizes court, guild, education, performance, title, and civic reputation. Brethren institutions are highly visible and highly stratified. Reform movements focus upon inherited office, guild exclusion, and the treatment of polished speech as proof of competence.

### Oria

Oric Brethren preserve House, stronghold, mountain, observatory, herd, and winter-service traditions. Continuity is a survival tool. The danger is allowing an ancestral solution to become compulsory after the conditions that produced it have changed.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Brethren dominate trade, invention, contract, broadcast entertainment, industry, and portions of the delving economy. Their institutions can produce extraordinary mobility and reduce every relationship to a transaction.

### Arneria

Arnerian Brethren maintain caravan, archive, oath-road, ruin, temple, and oral traditions. A written record is respected, but never assumed to contain the whole truth when living witnesses, environmental change, and sacred obligation disagree.

### Al'Ar

Al'Ari Brethren live among Beastfolk-majority societies, maritime communities, monasteries, ports, and volcanic islands. Cooperation depends upon learning that participation does not require cultural ownership.

## VII. Religion, Philosophy, and Public Life

Brethren are often associated with the [[02. The Dawn Pantheon|Dawn Court]], but modern religious life includes Old Court worship, regional divinities, ancestor traditions, philosophical schools, atheism, mixed observance, and practices that reject court categories entirely.

No lineage possesses a required god.

Public life commonly revolves around schools, guilds, councils, courts, workshops, temples, neighborhood assemblies, archives, delver companies, and mutual-aid societies.

Brethren traditions often ask:

- What should survive us?
- Who is allowed to revise it?
- What makes an oath legitimate?
- When does stewardship become possession?
- Who remains absent from the record?
- Can an institution apologize, or only its current members?

These questions unite Brethren philosophy more reliably than any answer.

---

## VIII. Brethren and Delving

Brethren delvers fill every role.

Their historical strengths include institutional memory, craft specialization, logistical planning, contract formation, archival interpretation, structural analysis, and the ability to transmit lessons between expeditions.

Their recurring failures are equally institutional:

- Treating permits as proof of ethical authority
- Claiming ruins because a Brethren script survives within them
- Prioritizing salvage records over living communities
- Mistaking official maps for complete landscapes
- Enforcing inherited expedition roles by lineage
- Assuming the best-documented account is the most truthful

Responsible crews record uncertainty, name contributors, distinguish discovery from ownership, and recognize oral or embodied testimony alongside writing.

The Labyrinths preserve paperwork with the same indifference that they preserve bones.

Survival is not endorsement.

---

## IX. Modern Debates

Modern Brethren disagree over:

- Whether the category should remain in official use
- Whether it describes shared culture or preserves Dawn-era hierarchy
- How ancient records should be returned to descendant communities
- Whether hereditary office can be made accountable
- How mixed-descent identities should appear in registries
- Whether homeland mechanics describe culture too rigidly
- What reparations are owed for Crown-era classification and dispossession
- When continuity becomes refusal to change

Some people identify proudly as Brethren.

Some use the term only historically.

Some reject it because it was used to exclude Beastfolk and other peoples from civic personhood.

All three positions are part of modern Brethren life.

---

## X. Naming and Address

Brethren naming practices are lineage-, region-, household-, and profession-specific.

Common structures include:

- Personal name and household name
- Personal name and place of upbringing
- Craft or office title
- Earned deed-name
- Temple or oath name
- Several names used in different relationships
- A self-chosen name replacing an inherited registry name

Ask which name a person uses in the present context.

Do not assume a long-lived person welcomes an obsolete title, that a craft name indicates current employment, or that a registry label accurately describes ancestry.

---

## XI. Playing a Brethren Character

Consider:

- Which institution shaped you most?
- What did it preserve well?
- Whom did it fail?
- Is your lineage central to your identity, incidental, or contested?
- What inherited obligation have you accepted freely?
- Which one are you trying to end?
- What form of evidence do you trust first?
- What do you hope will outlive you?
- Who taught you to revise a promise without abandoning responsibility?
- Where do you belong when the official record says otherwise?

A Brethren character need not be lawful, literate, religious, settled, tradition-minded, or institutionally loyal.

The classification describes a historical field of inheritance.

It does not dictate personality.
