## I. Lineage Overview

Crocodilians are river, marsh, delta, and littoral Scalefolk with powerful jaws, armored scutes, amphibious bodies, and traditions shaped by patient observation and nesting-water responsibility.

They are routinely depicted as lurking violence. Crocodilian stillness is often skilled environmental attention, rest, temperature management, prayer, fishing, or privacy.

---

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Ancient Crocodilian communities maintained flood markers, nesting banks, ferry rights, and channels between inland and coastal peoples. Later kingdoms portrayed them as hazards to justify draining or militarizing their wetlands.

The **Bank Kept Open** law protects both nesting areas and public passage without assuming that one right erases the other.

---

## III. Body and Sensory Life

Crocodilians possess long jaws, strong tails, scutes, excellent swimming ability, and eyes and nostrils adapted to surface observation.

Jaws are expressive and medically complex. Asking for a bite demonstration or testing scutes is intrusive. Their apparent armor does not make restraint safe.

---

## IV. Society and Kinship

Households include nest families, river blocks, ferry companies, flood councils, temples, and chosen basking circles.

The **Surface Signal** announces approach to a shared bank so stillness is not mistaken for absence and surprise does not become policy.

---

## V. Inner Life and Modern Debate

Crocodilian cultures admire patience, decisive action, territorial clarity, parental care, water literacy, and boundaries stated before conflict.

Their shadow is delayed response. Waiting for certainty can allow harm to become entrenched; decisive action after long silence can appear without explanation.

---

## VI. Crocodilian Peoples in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Canal Crocodilians serve as flood judges and ferry leaders amid persistent fear campaigns.

### Oria

Thermal infrastructure supports lake and hot-spring communities.

### Vinyot

Delta labor, security stereotypes, and combat broadcasts make bodily rights central.

### Arneria

River and oasis Crocodilians maintain ancient bank and passage agreements.

### Al'Ar

Crocodilian settlements shape mangrove, lagoon, and storm-channel politics.

---

## VII. Crocodilian Delvers

Crocodilian delvers excel in flooded ambush detection, rescue, anchoring, swimming, and decisive close defense. Parties must not assume they should lead every underwater breach.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

> [!warning] Playtest Mechanics  
> These traits are an initial Aerathon playtest package and require DM approval until table testing is complete.

### Creature Type and Size

You are a Humanoid, Medium.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet and your swimming speed is 30 feet.

### Hold Breath

You can hold your breath for 30 minutes.

### Scuted Hide

When not wearing armor, your Armor Class equals 13 + your Dexterity modifier, to a maximum Dexterity bonus of +2.

### Powerful Jaws

Your bite is a natural weapon dealing 1d6 piercing damage. Once per turn after hitting, you may attempt to grapple the target as a bonus action. Use this proficiency bonus times per long rest.

---

## X. Recognized Sublineages

### Crocodile

Crocodile peoples are prominent in warm rivers, deltas, estuaries, and coastal channels.

### Alligator

Alligator peoples often inhabit freshwater marsh, lake, and temperate wetland systems.

### Caiman

Caiman communities include compact-bodied river and rainforest peoples with dense littoral settlement traditions.

### Gharial

Gharial peoples possess narrow jaws adapted to fish-rich rivers and maintain extensive current and sandbank knowledge.

---

## XI. Playing a Crocodilian

Consider:

- What does stillness mean to you?
- Which bank are you responsible for?
- How do you announce approach?
- When does patience become delay?
- What body stereotype follows you?

Lineage creates embodied circumstances, not assigned temperament or destiny.
