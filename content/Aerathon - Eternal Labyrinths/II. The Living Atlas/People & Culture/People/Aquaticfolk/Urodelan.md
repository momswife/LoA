## I. Lineage Overview

Urodelans are Salamander, Newt, and Axolotl Aquaticfolk distinguished by tailed amphibious bodies, sensitive skin, and varying capacities for tissue repair.

They are often romanticized as endlessly regenerative. Healing remains slow, costly, incomplete, and personal. The existence of regrowth has been used to justify injuries no person should be asked to endure.

---

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Wetland and spring records describe Urodelan communities tending waters believed to connect surface life with deep elemental channels. Some Fire Salamander families later developed heat-associated traditions, though they remain amphibian rather than elemental beings.

The **No Second Wound Law** forbids inflicting a repeat injury merely because the first one healed visibly.

---

## III. Body and Environmental Needs

Urodelans possess tails, smooth or textured permeable skin, strong swimming movement, and gills retained externally in many Axolotl families. Some regenerate limited tissue over extended rest.

Gills and tails are intimate anatomy. Skin requires moisture and chemical safety. Regrowth may produce changed color, sensation, scar, or function.

---

## IV. Society and Kinship

Households include spring circles, lake neighborhoods, laboratory-reform groups, cave pools, and seasonal land-water families.

Care traditions emphasize consent checkpoints because a healing process can outlast the circumstances under which treatment began.

---

## V. Inner Life and Modern Debate

Urodelan cultures admire renewal, patience, curiosity, medical honesty, adaptability, and the courage to name permanent change.

Their shadow is recovery performance. People may be praised for healing until they feel forbidden to grieve what was lost.

---

## VI. Urodelan Peoples in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Medical colleges and ornamental-garden communities generate both opportunity and bodily exploitation.

### Oria

Hot springs and deep lakes support protected cold-climate settlements.

### Vinyot

Regeneration research makes consent law a central political concern.

### Arneria

Sacred springs and cave-water routes support dispersed communities.

### Al'Ar

Rainforest streams and volcanic pools sustain the largest populations.

---

## VII. Urodelan Delvers

Urodelan delvers are medics, water scouts, alchemists, cave navigators, and recovery specialists. Limited regenerative traits never justify hazardous testing.

Teams record contamination carefully because permeable skin may reveal environmental danger before instruments do, while harming the person first.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

> [!warning] Playtest Mechanics  
> These traits are an initial Aerathon playtest package and require DM approval until table testing is complete.

### Creature Type and Size

You are a Humanoid, Small or Medium.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet and your swimming speed is 30 feet.

### Amphibious

You can breathe air and water.

### Restorative Tissue

When you spend Hit Dice during a short rest, reroll one die showing a 1 or 2 and use the new result. Once per long rest, when you receive magical healing, add your proficiency bonus.

### Permeable Sense

Gain proficiency in Medicine or Nature. You have advantage on checks to identify a substance after it contacts your skin, though contact can still harm you.

### Tail Balance

You have advantage on checks and saving throws made to avoid being knocked prone.

---

## X. Recognized Sublineages

### Salamander

Salamander Urodelans include woodland, cave, stream, and heat-associated families with highly varied markings.

### Newt

Newt peoples often move seasonally between land and water and preserve strong pond-return traditions.

### Axolotl

Axolotl peoples commonly retain external gills and other youthful anatomical traits throughout adulthood; these do not indicate immaturity.

### Olm

Olm communities inhabit dark cave waters and possess strong nonvisual spatial traditions.

---

## XI. Playing an Urodelan

Consider:

- What changed through healing?
- Who controls your medical story?
- Which water keeps your skin healthy?
- What loss are you allowed to grieve?
- When does care require renewed consent?

A character may embrace, reinterpret, or reject every tradition described above. Environment and body create circumstances, not personality.
