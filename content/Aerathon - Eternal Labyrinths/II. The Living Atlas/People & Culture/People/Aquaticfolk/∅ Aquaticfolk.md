## I. Classification Overview

**Aquaticfolk** is the modern Ministry classification for sapient peoples whose inherited bodies and collective histories are principally shaped by life in seas, great lakes, deep rivers, littoral waters, and submerged environments.

The category includes mammalian, cartilaginous, shelled, cephalopod, and deepwater lineages.

It does not include every person capable of swimming.

An Anatid, Pelagid, Otter Laetine, or Limnid may spend much of their life in water without being Aquaticfolk. A magically altered Brethren diver does not become Aquaticfolk through adaptation alone. Conversely, an Aquaticfolk person raised inland does not lose their lineage because they rarely enter natural water.

Current Ministry practice distinguishes:

- **Folk classification:** Aquaticfolk
- **Lineage:** a major inherited people, such as Cetacean or Selachian
- **Sublineage:** a recognized physiological inheritance, such as Dolphin, Whale, Shark, or Ray
- **Homeland:** the society and environment that shaped a person’s life
- **Water tradition:** the body of route, depth, current, breathing, and access knowledge through which a community moves

A person might therefore be recorded as:

> **Aquaticfolk → Cetacean Lineage → Dolphin Sublineage → Al’Ari Homeland**

These are registry terms.

Most Aquaticfolk use more specific identities in daily life: reef, trench, pod, current, harbor, migration, family, island, city, or profession.

The classification is new in its current form.

Earlier records divided marine peoples inconsistently among Beastfolk, “water spirits,” monsters, regional curiosities, and maritime assets. Several governments recognized Aquaticfolk communities only when negotiating fishing rights, salvage, or naval passage.

The present filing begins from a simpler fact:

They are peoples.

The water they live within is not an excuse to record them as part of the landscape.

## II. The Meaning of Aquaticfolk

Aquaticfolk are often described from land.

That description produces predictable errors.

Landbound observers imagine an ocean as a flat surface with depth beneath it. Aquaticfolk live within volume.

A road may pass above another road without crossing it. A settlement may be entered from the roof, floor, side, thermal vent, current, or darkness below. A territorial boundary may move several miles between seasons. Sound may travel farther than sight. A distant storm may alter a conversation long before anyone sees the sky.

For many Aquaticfolk, direction is relational:

- Shoreward and seaward
- Up-current and down-current
- Lightward and deepward
- Warmward and coldward
- Toward breath
- Toward shelter
- Toward the remembered route

This does not make fixed maps useless.

It means no map is complete without condition.

### Bodies of Water, Bodies in Water

Aquaticfolk bodies vary more dramatically than most folk classifications.

Some breathe air and must surface.

Some breathe water and require accommodation on land.

Some move naturally in both.

Some tolerate tremendous pressure.

Some are harmed by it.

Some possess legs, others fins, tentacles, broad tails, webbed limbs, flexible mantles, or magically supported humanoid postures.

There is no universal Aquaticfolk body and no single aquatic way of life.

### Aquatic Does Not Mean Oceanic

While the largest populations live along coasts and open seas, Aquaticfolk also inhabit:

- Great lakes
- Inland salt basins
- Deep river mouths
- Flooded caverns
- Permanent reef cities
- Ice margins
- Mangrove channels
- Submerged ruins
- Labyrinth reservoirs
- Planar waters

Communities separated by land may have less in common than neighboring Aquaticfolk and surface peoples who share the same harbor.

## III. Proposed and Recognized Lineages

The comprehensive filing recognizes twelve principal Aquaticfolk lineages.

Several classifications remain provisional while community councils complete review.

### [[Cetacean]]

Whale, Dolphin, and Porpoise peoples shaped by breath, acoustic memory, long migration, collective navigation, and the ethics of carrying voices across great distance.

### [[Selachian]]

Shark, Ray, Skate, and Sawfish peoples with strong electroreceptive traditions, current discipline, and cultural debates concerning hunger, restraint, and the misuse of predatory imagery.

### [[Pinnipedan]]

Seal, Sea Lion, and Walrus peoples associated with littoral settlements, ice margins, communal rest grounds, amphibious travel, and the defense of places that exist between sea and shore.

### [[Sirenian]]

Manatee and Dugong peoples whose communities often center grazing waters, patient stewardship, warm shallows, intergenerational teaching, and resistance to outsiders mistaking gentleness for passivity.

### [[Testudinan]]

Sea Turtle peoples shaped by long routes, shell inheritance, nesting shores, return, and the political consequences of carrying one’s shelter while depending upon distant land.

### [[Cephalopodan]]

Octopus, Squid, and Cuttlefish peoples known for flexible bodies, chromatophoric language, distributed craft traditions, intense privacy, and social systems in which color, posture, and concealment may carry legal meaning.

### [[Abyssan]]

A provisional convergent lineage including Angler, Lantern, Gulper, and other pressure-adapted deepwater peoples. Abyssans are grouped through shared life below ordinary sunlight rather than close biological relationship.

### [[Merfolk]]

Humanoid-tailed and dual-environment peoples shaped by reef cities, current law, surface exchange, and the difficulty of building civic life across water and air.

### [[Ichthyan]]

Bony-fish peoples including reef, schooling, solitary, migratory, and open-water kinships whose color, fin, and lateral-line communication resist simple surface translation.

### [[Batrachian]]

Frog and Toad peoples associated with wetland thresholds, vocal assemblies, metamorphic childhood, rain knowledge, and protection of waters dismissed as empty swamp.

### [[Crustacean]]

Crab, Lobster, Shrimp, and related peoples with armored bodies, molt traditions, fine manipulative limbs, and communities spanning seabed, shore, and estuary.

### [[Urodelan]]

Salamander, Newt, and Axolotl peoples shaped by regeneration, moisture, river-cave settlements, and difficult questions about medical access to remarkable bodies.

The Ministry expects revision.

Additional lineages may be recognized where community, physiological, and historical evidence supports independent standing.

## IV. A History Recorded at the Shore

Most surviving Aerathonic archives were built on land.

This has distorted the record.

Aquaticfolk appear suddenly when they block a harbor, rescue a fleet, demand payment, recover a relic, or are killed in sufficient numbers to enter a Crown ledger. Centuries of ordinary life disappear between those entries.

Common archival terms include:

- Leviathan
- Harbor spirit
- Speaking catch
- Deep emissary
- Naval beast
- Salvage familiar
- Tide omen
- Submerged claimant

Some entries describe monsters.

Some describe Aquaticfolk citizens whose names the recorder did not ask.

### The Surface Signature

For centuries, several maritime governments refused treaties made underwater.

Aquaticfolk representatives were required to surface, assume an approved posture, and sign documents that could not survive immersion. The practice became known as the **Surface Signature**.

Its stated purpose was verification.

Its effect was to make landbound procedure the condition of legal existence.

Modern continental charter law recognizes:

- Pressure-sealed records
- Acoustic witness
- Chromatophoric signature
- Current-knot notation
- Shell scoring
- Scent and electrical identity marks
- Repeated-route testimony
- Authorized translation through mixed-medium councils

Not all local courts comply equally.

### Salvage and the Theft of Homes

Surface law often classified submerged objects as salvage even when they remained inside inhabited Aquaticfolk settlements.

Relic hunters learned to describe homes as ruins, ceremonial objects as cargo, and resistance as hostile marine behavior.

The Ministry now distinguishes:

- Lost cargo
- Abandoned property
- Archaeological material
- Living settlement property
- Mortuary material
- Ecological commons
- Labyrinth-generated objects

The distinction exists because “salvage” was frequently a polite word for entering through someone’s roof and taking their ancestors.

## V. Current, Route, and Belonging

Aquaticfolk communities organize kinship in many ways: pods, schools, shoals, reef houses, drift households, trench circles, breeding waters, foster currents, ship-linked neighborhoods, and solitary adults bound into long-distance networks.

No one form is universal.

### Routes as Inheritance

A route may be inherited without being owned.

It can include:

- Knowledge of safe depth
- Seasonal food
- Thermal layers
- Predator movement
- Storm behavior
- Places where air is available
- Listening points
- Burial water
- Neutral crossings
- Obligations to settlements along the way

To obstruct such a route can be equivalent to closing a road, poisoning a well, and severing a family connection at once.

### The Right of Passage

Many Aquaticfolk legal traditions begin from movement rather than fixed boundary.

A community may defend a reef while recognizing passage above it. A migrating pod may possess a right to cross waters controlled for most of the year by another settlement. A surface fleet may receive a seasonal corridor in exchange for noise limits and waste controls.

These systems are not inherently peaceful.

They are highly negotiated.

Conflict often begins when a permanent map is used to erase a moving obligation.

### Distance and Family

Aquaticfolk households may spend months or years apart while remaining socially close.

Sound, messenger routes, remembered meeting waters, shared migration, and periodic convergence allow relationships to function across distances that landbound officials mistake for abandonment.

Custody, inheritance, and residency law have repeatedly failed to account for this.

## VI. Communication

Aquaticfolk languages make use of several channels.

Depending upon lineage and environment, communication may include:

- Spoken voice
- Song
- Click and echo
- Low-frequency resonance
- Gesture
- Fin and body posture
- Chromatophoric color
- Bioluminescent sequence
- Electrical signaling
- Touch
- Current manipulation
- Written pressure-script

Translation across water and air is difficult.

Some sounds distort at the surface. Some cannot be produced by landbound throats. Some color patterns require a field of skin larger than an ordinary page. Some deepwater light-signals are painful or invisible to surface-adapted eyes.

The Ministry therefore rejects the assumption that speech is more reliable than other forms.

### Silence

Silence carries different meanings across communities.

For a migrating Cetacean, silence may protect a route from distant hunters.

For a Selachian, the absence of expected electrical movement may signal danger.

For a Cephalopodan, a neutral skin pattern may represent privacy rather than calm.

For an Abyssan, extinguishing light may be intimacy, mourning, concealment, or threat.

Officials are instructed not to interpret unfamiliar silence as consent.

## VII. Architecture and Public Access

Aquaticfolk settlements challenge landbound assumptions about walls, doors, streets, and gravity.

Common forms include:

- Open-water marker fields
- Reef chambers
- Kelp structures
- Pressure domes
- Ice-edge halls
- Submerged towers entered vertically
- Wreck communities
- Thermal vent settlements
- Floating and semi-submerged platforms
- Amphibious harbor districts

A home without a sealed wall is still a home.

A city without dry ground is still a city.

### Dry Access

Aquaticfolk visiting land may require:

- Water-rest chambers
- Gill irrigation
- Skin protection
- Temperature control
- Broad wet ramps
- Reinforced basins
- Salt-balanced water
- Rest cycles for air-breathing lineages
- Translation support

These are access requirements, not luxuries.

### Wet Access

Surface visitors to Aquaticfolk settlements may require pressure wards, breathing magic, orientation lines, thermal clothing, light discipline, and instruction concerning three-dimensional traffic.

The visitor’s difficulty does not make the settlement primitive.

It makes the visitor poorly adapted to it.

## VIII. Aquaticfolk in the Five Greater Regions

### Allemance

Allemancian Aquaticfolk live along rivers, lakes, western coasts, estuaries, and trading ports.

Their communities often connect water routes to Hearthland traditions of hospitality and return. Ferry houses, flood kitchens, bridge watches, and mixed wet-dry markets are common.

Inland law remains inconsistent concerning submerged homes and moving river boundaries.

### Oria

Oric Aquaticfolk inhabit cold coasts, ice margins, deep lakes, industrial harbors, and House-maintained waterworks.

Oria values functional design and has produced some of Aerathon’s best pressure equipment. It also tends to classify water through infrastructure: reservoir, shipping lane, intake, drainage, hazard.

Aquaticfolk councils regularly remind the Houses that a functioning water system may also be a neighborhood.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Aquaticfolk are central to port trade, salvage law, shipping, performance, fisheries, insurance, relic recovery, and undersea transit.

The League of Coin recognizes an impressive range of aquatic contracts.

It also monetizes access aggressively.

Disputes over current tolls, sound rights, wreck ownership, and corporate reef leases are common.

### Arneria

Arnerian Aquaticfolk live along warm coasts, volcanic lakes, monsoon rivers, Bat’yan wetlands, and sacred springs.

Oath traditions may be witnessed through immersion, shared breath, or the release of marked water into a larger current.

Conflicts arise when temples claim sacred authority over waters already inhabited by Aquaticfolk communities.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ar contains Aerathon’s densest concentration of surface-integrated Aquaticfolk settlements.

Island channels, storm coasts, reef cities, fleets, performance routes, and rebuilding traditions create constant contact among aquatic and landbound peoples.

Al’Ari law recognizes many amphibious districts in which the water column, surface platform, and shore are governed together.

The system is imperfect.

It is nevertheless the model most often studied elsewhere.

## IX. Aquaticfolk and the Shattersea

The [[The Shattersea|Shattersea]] occupies a complicated place in Aquaticfolk memory.

Some communities understand it as a wounded sea.

Others regard it as an origin, frontier, prison, divine scar, or expanding danger. Deep routes connected to the plane have altered pressure, migration, magic, and species across Aerathon.

Shattersea-born creatures are not automatically Aquaticfolk.

Aquaticfolk touched by the Shattersea are not automatically corrupted.

The distinction matters because surface institutions have often treated unfamiliar marine bodies as evidence of monstrosity.

Ministry expeditions entering Shattersea-linked waters require Aquaticfolk consultation when local communities exist. Consultation does not mean hiring one guide and ignoring every warning they provide.

## X. Aquaticfolk and Delving

Aquaticfolk delvers are essential to flooded Labyrinths, reservoir complexes, submerged ruins, Shattersea breaches, and chambers where gravity or pressure behaves as water.

Common roles include:

- Three-dimensional navigation
- Flood rescue
- Acoustic survey
- Pressure assessment
- Salvage identification
- Current reading
- Underwater combat
- Environmental mediation
- Deep-route communication

Their abilities do not eliminate aquatic risk.

A marine lineage adapted to warm shallows may be as vulnerable to abyssal pressure as a landbound person. An air-breathing Cetacean can drown if trapped without access to breath. A water-breathing Selachian may suffocate in stagnant or magically emptied water.

Responsible expeditions document:

- Breathing medium
- Pressure tolerance
- Temperature range
- Required surface interval
- Skin or gill care
- Communication method
- Light and sound sensitivity
- Emergency extraction route
- Whether a chamber’s “water” is chemically or magically alive

A common Aquaticfolk warning states:

> Do not ask whether there is water. Ask what the water is doing.

## XI. Common Misconceptions

### “All Aquaticfolk can breathe underwater.”

Many cannot. Several lineages breathe air and must surface.

### “All Aquaticfolk can survive any depth.”

Pressure tolerance varies by lineage and individual.

### “Aquaticfolk communities are nomadic because they cannot build.”

Some migrate. Others maintain cities older than the ports claiming jurisdiction over them.

### “The sea belongs to whoever controls the shore.”

Aquaticfolk law rejects this assumption, as does current Ministry charter practice.

### “Aquaticfolk are culturally isolated.”

Harbor, river, island, and fleet communities have shaped continental trade and politics for millennia.

### “Predatory aquatic lineages are dangerous by nature.”

Diet, anatomy, and inherited senses do not determine moral character.

### “A wreck is abandoned.”

It may be a home, grave, shrine, boundary marker, nursery, or active public structure.

### “Silence means agreement.”

It does not.

## XII. Creating an Aquaticfolk Character

When creating an Aquaticfolk character, consider:

### Lineage

What inherited body, senses, movement, and breathing needs do they possess?

### Sublineage

Which physiological inheritance do they carry? Do they use the Ministry term?

### Homeland

Which shore, reef, river, lake, trench, island, fleet, city, or inland community shaped them?

### Water Tradition

How were they taught to understand route, depth, current, breath, and shelter?

### Relationship to Land

Is land familiar, difficult, liberating, threatening, boring, sacred, or simply another place?

### Individual Life

What do they want that has nothing to do with the water?

An Aquaticfolk character may love the sea, fear it, leave it, return to it, or know it only through inherited stories.

Their lineage shapes how they move through a world of water and land.

It does not decide which one is home.
