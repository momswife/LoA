## I. Lineage Overview

Selachians are Aquaticfolk including Shark, Ray, Skate, and Sawfish peoples.

The Ministry groups them through shared cartilaginous inheritance, electroreceptive senses, and a long political history surrounding predatory classification.

No Aquaticfolk lineage has been more persistently described as appetite wearing a person’s shape.

The stereotype appears in naval propaganda, dockside entertainment, delver recruitment, criminal law, religious allegory, and children’s stories. Selachian anger becomes frenzy. Selachian hunger becomes threat. Selachian silence becomes calculation. Selachian confidence becomes evidence that an attack was always intended.

Selachian cultures are not built around uncontrollable hunger.

They are deeply concerned with **attention**:

- What can be sensed before it becomes visible?
- When does pursuit become commitment?
- How should a powerful body announce itself?
- What obligations arise when one detects vulnerability?
- When is restraint protection, and when is it submission demanded by frightened observers?

Many Selachians practice deliberate movement and direct boundary language because uncertainty about their intentions is rarely granted the benefit of innocence.

This should not be mistaken for lack of passion.

It is difficult to live while every ordinary appetite is treated as a prophecy.

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

In the **Account of the Unclouded Water**, the Old Court creates early sea life with ordinary senses.

Predators conceal themselves. Wounded creatures disappear into murk. Storms bury movement beneath silt. Every creature learns only what reaches the eye.

The first Selachian asks for a sense that cannot be fooled by appearance.

The Old Court grants awareness of the living current: the faint electrical presence created by muscle, nerve, and motion.

The Selachian immediately discovers a frightened animal hidden beneath the sand.

The gods ask what they will do with this knowledge.

Every surviving version pauses here.

Some end with the Selachian hunting.

Some end with them guarding the hidden creature from something larger.

Some say the first Selachian leaves without acting because knowledge does not create ownership.

The absence of one ending is the point.

The gift provides awareness.

Choice creates character.

### The Salt Wards

Ancient Selachian communities maintained **salt wards** around breeding grounds, migration corridors, wreck graves, and vulnerable shallows.

A ward was not always a closed border.

It might establish:

- A speed limit
- A prohibition on blood disposal
- A seasonal no-entry depth
- A corridor for surface vessels
- A ban on disruptive magic
- A duty to render aid
- A warning that hunting was occurring nearby
- A neutral water in which pursuit had to end

Salt wards were marked through stone, current, scent, electrical pattern, and repeated patrol.

Surface maps recorded many as dangerous waters without recording why they were defended.

### The Red-Water Laws

During the Age of Chains, several ports adopted **Red-Water Laws** allowing officials to detain Selachians whenever blood entered a harbor.

The laws claimed to prevent frenzy.

In practice, they criminalized Selachian presence after industrial accidents, slaughterhouse dumping, battles, medical emergencies, and violence committed against Selachians themselves.

A wounded Selachian could be arrested for the blood leaving their own body.

Most Red-Water Laws have been repealed.

Their logic survives in public fear and emergency policing.

## III. Body and Electroreceptive Form

Selachian bodies vary widely.

Common features include:

- Cartilaginous skeletal structure
- Gills or branchial breathing structures
- Fins, fin crests, or wing-like pectoral limbs
- Tails adapted to thrust or steering
- Dermal denticles or smooth ray-like skin
- Multiple rows of replaceable teeth in some sublineages
- Electroreceptive organs concentrated around the head
- Acute scent and pressure awareness

Player-character Selachians possess upright, semi-upright, or magically supported forms capable of moving on land and using ordinary tools.

### Amphibious Respiration

Modern Selachian lineages can breathe both water and air.

Their land breathing depends upon moisture-retaining branchial passages and inherited magic. Dry, hot, or polluted conditions may still cause discomfort or injury even when suffocation is not immediate.

Many Selachians use gill scarves, skin oils, filtered collars, or water-rest facilities during extended land travel.

### Electroreception

Selachians perceive faint electrical fields produced by living nervous systems and muscle.

The sense is strongest in water and at close range.

It may reveal location, movement, stress, or unusual stillness. It does not read thought, morality, identity, or intention.

Constructs, many undead, magical projections, heavy insulation, and Labyrinth interference may produce little useful signal or misleading signal.

Electroreceptive attention can be intimate.

Directing it toward another person at close range without need or permission may be treated like deliberate staring, listening at a door, or touching a pulse.

### Teeth and Fear

Many Shark Selachians replace teeth throughout life.

Shed teeth may be used in jewelry, household records, tools, memorials, or private disposal. Buying and selling them is lawful when voluntary.

The display of Selachian teeth has been regulated repeatedly in surface jurisdictions that impose no comparable restriction upon claws, horns, tusks, or weapons.

Most current laws treat ordinary dentition as part of the body rather than an implied threat.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Selachian communities include schools, shoals, salt wards, current houses, reef courts, patrol lines, solitary wardens, craft families, and mixed harbor neighborhoods.

Some sublineages prefer close collective life.

Others value long periods of solitude.

No arrangement establishes greater legitimacy.

### Announced Approach

Many Selachian traditions require a person approaching from behind, below, or obscuring water to announce their presence through gesture, current, sound, or electrical signal.

The custom developed partly from sensory courtesy and partly from politics. A people presumed to be ambushing others learned to make ordinary movement unmistakable.

Younger Selachians disagree over whether the practice protects community or requires them to perform harmlessness continually.

### The Table of Empty Teeth

Before certain negotiations, participants eat.

The meal may be elaborate or extremely plain. Its purpose is to prevent hunger—literal or metaphorical—from remaining an unnamed force in the room.

Each participant then states what they want.

This is the **Table of Empty Teeth**.

The custom rejects the idea that desire becomes dangerous only when admitted. Concealed appetite is considered more likely to shape a dishonest agreement.

The phrase **empty teeth** does not mean satisfied forever.

It means no one may pretend need is absent while deciding who must sacrifice.

### Boundaries

Selachian boundary language is often direct.

Common distinctions include:

- You may pass but not stop.
- You may stop but not feed.
- You may feed but not pursue.
- You may pursue but must end at the marker.
- You may enter with a witness.
- You may return after the water changes.

Outsiders sometimes interpret such precision as hostility.

Selachians often find vague welcome more dangerous because its hidden limits appear only after someone crosses them.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Selachian communities commonly admire:

- Attentive restraint
- Direct intention
- Respect for boundaries
- Decisive movement
- Awareness of unseen life
- Control without shame
- Protection of vulnerable waters
- The courage to admit desire

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Restraint | Choosing action deliberately | Treating every feeling as dangerous |
| Directness | Making boundaries clear | Leaving no room for uncertainty |
| Attention | Detecting need and threat early | Constant vigilance |
| Pursuit | Sustaining meaningful commitment | Inability to release a goal |
| Territorial care | Protecting habitat and community | Exclusion and possessiveness |
| Admission of appetite | Honest negotiation | Treating desire as entitlement |

A recurring Selachian fear is that any loss of control will be used as proof that control was never real.

Some become exceptionally formal. Some reject reassurance as an endless demand. Some display appetite, teeth, anger, and joy openly because concealment never earned safety. Others devote themselves to mediation, healing, guardianship, or law.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Selachians debate:

- Whether announced approach should remain customary
- Whether Red-Water memorials preserve history or preserve fear
- Whether salt wards should receive authority under surface law
- Whether predatory terminology can be reclaimed
- Whether electroreceptive evidence is admissible in court
- Whether naval and industrial electrical pollution constitutes trespass
- Whether community patrols protect vulnerable waters or reproduce surveillance
- Whether the Table of Empty Teeth works in institutions with unequal power

The deepest disagreement concerns reassurance.

How much must a person alter ordinary life to make prejudice feel safe?

## VI. Selachians in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Selachians live along river mouths, lakes, western coasts, and harbor towns.

They commonly work as ferry wardens, flood rescuers, fishery mediators, mill inspectors, river guides, and local judges.

Hearthland familiarity can soften old fear. It can also make acceptance conditional upon being “one of ours,” leaving travelers and unfamiliar Selachians under suspicion.

### Oria

Oric Selachians inhabit cold ports, industrial waters, deep lakes, and House reservoirs.

Their electroreception is valuable in inspecting submerged machinery, electrical systems, damaged cables, and living workers trapped within collapsed structures.

Oric industry can overwhelm the same senses. Selachian engineers have pushed for current shielding, quiet grids, and mandatory notice before large discharges.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Selachians are prominent in trade security, finance, salvage, shipping, contract enforcement, sport, medicine, and risk assessment.

The League markets their image relentlessly.

Selachian advocates have become influential in advertising law, particularly against campaigns that use involuntary blood response, hidden-camera feeding, or aggressive imagery to sell unrelated products.

### Arneria

Arnerian Selachians live along warm coasts, river mouths, sacred lakes, and monsoon waters.

Salt wards often carry ritual as well as civic authority. Entering a protected water may require vow, purification, or a named purpose.

Conflict arises when temple authority and resident Selachian law claim the same water through different sacred histories.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Selachians inhabit reef cities, storm channels, fleet communities, dock districts, and deep island passages.

They serve as navigators, guards, performers, storm responders, hunters, advocates, and current judges.

Al’Ar’s shifting waters favor precise temporary boundaries. A channel may be public in calm weather and restricted during migration, spawning, storm recovery, or Labyrinth instability.

## VII. Selachians and Delving

Selachians are skilled underwater scouts, close-range detectors, pursuit specialists, rescue workers, and guardians.

Their advantages include:

- Amphibious breathing
- Strong swimming
- Electroreception
- Awareness in obscured water
- Decisive movement
- Physiological diversity suited to several aquatic environments

Common hazards include:

- Constructs with no living current
- Undead producing false or absent signals
- Electrical machinery overwhelming perception
- Illusions wrapped around real living bait
- Blood used to provoke or misdirect
- Chambers with currents that repeat movement
- Dry heat and chemical desiccation

Selachian delvers are often assigned to follow wounded creatures.

Experienced crews recognize that pursuit is the moment when a Labyrinth most easily determines the party’s route.

A common warning states:

> The trail you can sense may be the trail you were meant to take.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

The following mechanics are an initial playtest version.

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Wisdom score increases by 2.

### Size

Your size is Medium.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

You have a swimming speed of 40 feet.

### Amphibious

You can breathe air and water.

### Living Current

While underwater, you know the location of any creature within 10 feet of you that is not behind total cover.

This sense cannot detect Constructs or Undead, and it does not reveal a creature’s identity.

### Mark the Current

As a bonus action, choose one creature you can see or detect within 60 feet.

Until the end of your next turn:

- You have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) and Wisdom (Survival) checks made to locate that creature.
- The creature gains no benefit from being invisible against you while it remains within 30 feet and is not behind total cover.
- The first time you hit it, you deal an additional `1d6` damage.

You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

Sublineages describe broad physiological inheritances. They do not determine personality, profession, diet, homeland, or temperament.

### Shark

Shark Selachians possess streamlined bodies, pronounced dorsal features, replaceable teeth, and acute scent and current awareness.

They include many local forms adapted to reef, open sea, cold water, estuary, and deep routes.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Bite.** You have teeth that you can use to make unarmed strikes.

When you hit with them, the strike deals piercing damage equal to `1d6 + your Strength modifier`.

**Unbroken Motion.** Difficult terrain caused by water, currents, or aquatic vegetation does not cost you extra movement when you take the Dash action.

### Ray and Skate

Ray and Skate Selachians possess broad pectoral fins, flattened or diamond-shaped profiles, and exceptional control near seabeds, reefs, and open water columns.

Some possess defensive barbs. Others do not.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Dexterity score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Siltcloak.** You can attempt to Hide while lightly obscured by silt, sand, fog, aquatic vegetation, or moving water.

While underwater, you may take the Hide action as a bonus action a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

### Sawfish

Sawfish Selachians possess an elongated rostrum lined with tooth-like structures capable of sensing and redirecting nearby movement.

The rostrum is part of the body, not a public tool or weapon to be handled by others.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Rostral Sweep.** Your rostrum is a natural weapon that deals `1d6 + your Strength modifier` slashing damage on a hit.

When you hit a Large or smaller creature with it after moving at least 10 feet in a straight line, you can force the target to make a Strength saving throw.

The DC equals `8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier`.

On a failure, you push the target 5 feet.

You can force this saving throw a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

If the campaign uses Homeland Imprints, choose one trait representing the region that most strongly shaped your character.

A homeland represents upbringing and cultural experience. It does not need to match your birthplace or ancestry.

### Allemance — River Boundary

You gain proficiency in Insight.

When a creature crosses into your reach, you can use your reaction to learn whether it is charmed or frightened, provided it is not immune to your Living Current.

You can use this reaction once per long rest.

### Oria — Static Audit

You gain proficiency with tinker’s tools.

While touching water that is in contact with a machine or electrical system, you have advantage on Intelligence checks made to determine whether that system is active, damaged, or dangerously overloaded.

### Vinyot — Terms of Pursuit

You gain proficiency in Persuasion or Investigation, your choice.

When you use Mark the Current, you may also learn whether the target is above or below half its hit point maximum. You do not learn its exact hit points.

### Arneria — Fast of Clear Water

You have advantage on saving throws against being charmed.

Once per long rest, when you succeed on a saving throw against being charmed or frightened, you may gain temporary hit points equal to your proficiency bonus.

### Al’Ar — Storm Current

You learn the `shape water` cantrip.

When you reach 3rd level, you can cast `thunderwave` once with this trait and regain the ability to do so when you finish a long rest.

Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for these spells.

## XII. Playing a Selachian

When creating a Selachian character, consider:

- What appetite are you comfortable naming?
- Which boundary do you state clearly?
- When has restraint protected you?
- When was restraint demanded for someone else’s comfort?
- What can you sense that others cannot?
- Do you trust first impressions less or more because of it?
- What pursuit have you refused to end?
- What water would you defend?
- How do you react when someone fears your body?
- What do you do when attention becomes exhaustion?

A Selachian may be timid, indirect, herbivorous, inattentive, physically weak, socially exuberant, or uninterested in territory.

Their lineage provides awareness and aquatic form.

It does not provide an appetite for harm.
