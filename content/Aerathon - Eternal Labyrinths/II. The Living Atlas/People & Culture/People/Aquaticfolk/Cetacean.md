# Cetacean

**Folk Classification:** [[‎ Aquaticfolk|Aquaticfolk]]  
**Lineage Classification:** Cetacean  
**Registry Scope:** Sapient Lineage and Homeland Variance  
**Recognition Status:** Full Sapient Recognition  
**Delver Eligibility:** Unrestricted with Environmental Provision  
**Common Names:** Songkin, Longbreaths, Wake-Keepers  
**Playtest Status:** Initial Lineage Mechanics  
**Date of Initial Filing:** 7th Cycle, 3388 A.D.

---

## I. Lineage Overview

Cetaceans are air-breathing Aquaticfolk including Whale, Dolphin, and Porpoise peoples.

They are shaped by three conditions that landbound accounts frequently separate but Cetacean traditions hold together:

- Breath must be sought.
- Sound continues beyond sight.
- A route may be remembered by people who never travel it at the same time.

Cetaceans are famous for song.

The fame is accurate and misleading.

Cetacean acoustic traditions include music, but also law, navigation, genealogy, weather warning, grief, private address, civic announcement, mathematical measurement, and the maintenance of relationships across distance.

A low call carried through deep water may be part of a conversation whose speakers are separated by days of travel. A sequence of clicks may describe the shape of a chamber no one can see. A harbor melody repeated once each season may constitute a claim of continuing residence.

Surface audiences often hear beauty.

Cetaceans hear information, obligation, identity, and sometimes an argument that began before the audience’s grandparents were born.

The central Cetacean question is:

> How far can a voice travel before the speaker loses responsibility for what it carries?

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Cetacean creation traditions often begin with a world that had water but no distance.

In the **Song of the First Breath**, the Old Court places life throughout the sea. Each creature knows only the water immediately around it. Storms arrive without warning. Migrants vanish beyond sight. Grief ends at the horizon because no one farther away knows a death occurred.

The first Cetacean rises for air and hears thunder travel through the water.

They return below carrying the sound.

The Old Court asks what they learned.

The Cetacean answers:

> The sky struck one place, and the sea knew elsewhere.

They are then given a voice capable of making distance part of community.

Not all Cetaceans understand this account literally. Its cultural principle is more important than its cosmology: communication creates responsibility between people who cannot see one another.

### The Long Routes

Early Cetacean societies maintained migration routes across waters later divided by kingdoms, ports, naval claims, and Labyrinth storms.

Routes were carried through layered song.

One voice held depth.

One held season.

One held hazards.

One held the names of communities with rights along the way.

No singer possessed the entire route. The journey remained accurate because several memories corrected one another.

This practice became known as a **many-mouth map**.

Fragments of ancient many-mouth maps survive. They are difficult to archive because removing one part can make another appear to mean something it never meant alone.

### The Silenced Miles

Several major routes were lost during the Age of Collapse.

Labyrinth anomalies produced false coastlines, inverted current, acoustic dead zones, and calls that returned in voices belonging to the missing. The longest affected region is remembered as the **Silenced Miles**.

Cetacean expeditions still enter portions of it to recover route phrases.

Some returned songs contain accurate information about places no modern expedition has reached.

Others teach a route directly into stone.

## III. Body, Breath, and Acoustic Form

Cetaceans possess mammalian bodies adapted to water and air.

Common features include:

- Smooth or lightly textured skin
- Fins, broad tails, or fin-like limbs
- A dorsal line or ridge
- Blowholes or specialized upper breathing passages
- Dense musculature and insulating tissue
- Strong lungs
- Acute underwater hearing
- Vocal structures capable of clicks, whistles, song, and low resonance

Player-character Cetaceans usually possess upright or semi-upright forms capable of moving on land, using tools, and wearing equipment.

They remain more capable in water.

### Breath

Cetaceans breathe air.

They do not possess gills.

They can hold their breath far longer than most landbound peoples, but they can drown.

Cetacean architecture and expedition planning treat breath access as basic infrastructure. A sealed underwater room without a reliable air source is no more accessible than a land building filled entirely with water.

### Sleep

Many Cetaceans practice divided or shallow sleep, particularly while traveling in water.

This does not mean they never require true rest.

Communities establish protected rest water where another person listens for danger and reminds sleepers to breathe. Trusting someone with a breath-watch carries considerable intimacy.

### Echolocation

Cetacean echolocation produces an acoustic impression of nearby form, distance, movement, and density.

It does not reveal color, writing, motive, or every hidden property. Soft material, magical silence, acoustic distortion, and irregular Labyrinth geometry can produce incomplete readings.

Using echolocation without restraint inside another person’s body space or private dwelling may be considered intrusive.

### Voice in Air

Most Cetaceans can speak ordinary surface languages.

Their full vocal range may not carry properly in air. Some experience surface speech as physically small or emotionally imprecise.

Mixed-medium councils often use flooded resonating basins so Cetacean testimony can be given with its intended tone intact.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Cetacean communities may be called pods, routes, choirs, wakes, houses, sounding circles, or breathholds.

These words are not interchangeable.

A **pod** usually describes close social kin.

A **route** connects people through migration or passage rights.

A **choir** carries a shared body of acoustic knowledge.

A **wake** is a temporary group traveling together.

A person may belong to all four with different companions.

### Many-Mouth Decisions

Many Cetacean councils do not expect one speaker to present an entire position.

One person states the proposal. Others carry history, risk, dissent, or the needs of absent communities. A decision is considered stronger when no single voice can remove an inconvenient part without everyone hearing the change.

To outsiders, the process may sound repetitive.

Cetaceans often find landbound debate dangerously dependent upon one persuasive mouth.

### Naming the Absent

Important gatherings commonly begin by naming people affected by the decision who cannot hear it.

The absent may include:

- Migrating relatives
- Future travelers
- Surface neighbors
- A damaged reef
- The dead
- A community beyond acoustic reach

Naming does not grant the absent a vote that can be counted easily.

It prevents convenience from becoming invisibility.

### Breath-Watch

To stand breath-watch is to accept temporary responsibility for another person’s safe return to air.

The practice appears in:

- Childhood
- Illness
- Grief
- Long travel
- Intimate partnership
- Military service
- Delving

Abandoning a breath-watch without transferring it is among the most serious ordinary breaches of trust in many Cetacean communities.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Cetacean communities commonly admire:

- Attentive memory
- Communication across distance
- Emotional range
- Reliability in shared travel
- The ability to listen for what is not visible
- Breath discipline
- Responsibility for carried information
- The inclusion of absent voices

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Memory | Preserving routes and relationships | Refusing to let grief change |
| Communication | Preventing isolation | Treating privacy as abandonment |
| Collective navigation | Correcting individual error | Pressuring everyone to remain with the pod |
| Emotional expression | Making feeling audible | Expecting public performance of pain |
| Long perspective | Considering future consequence | Delaying urgent action |
| Attentive listening | Hearing distant danger | Hearing threat in every silence |

A recurring Cetacean fear is the unanswered call.

For some, silence suggests loss. For others, it is relief from communities in which every absence demands explanation. Cetaceans raised on land may feel judged for lacking songs they were never taught. Those who leave migration routes may be treated as though distance itself were rejection.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Cetaceans debate:

- Whether ancient route songs should be public, licensed, or protected
- Whether commercial broadcasting changes a song’s legal meaning
- Whether migration obligations remain binding after a route becomes dangerous
- Whether surface-born children must learn full acoustic tradition
- Whether many-mouth governance dilutes individual responsibility
- Whether the Silenced Miles should continue to be explored
- Who owns a call recorded without the speaker’s knowledge
- Whether naval sonar constitutes trespass, pollution, or assault

The loudest political movement of the present decade concerns **acoustic sovereignty**: the right of a community to control invasive sound within inhabited water.

## VI. Cetaceans in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Cetaceans live along coasts, great rivers, lake systems, and mixed harbor towns.

They commonly serve as ferrymasters, flood wardens, singers, teachers, couriers, bridge inspectors, and keepers of return routes.

Hearthland traditions align with Cetacean practices of naming absent travelers. Tension arises when close communities treat continual communication as proof of belonging and regard privacy as withdrawal.

### Oria

Oric Cetaceans inhabit cold coasts, deep lakes, industrial harbors, and House reservoirs.

They work in acoustic engineering, ice navigation, rescue, structural inspection, and long-range warning.

Oria’s machinery creates severe underwater noise. Cetacean engineers have led the development of quiet drives, dampened forge outlets, and legally protected listening waters.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Cetaceans are prominent in shipping, performance, insurance, communications, navigation, salvage, and public broadcasting.

Their songs can be immensely valuable.

Contracts concerning reproduction rights, route knowledge, emotional likeness, and magical resonance are among the League’s most complex.

Several famous Cetacean performers have discovered that a company owned not only a recording, but every navigational phrase embedded within it.

### Arneria

Arnerian Cetaceans inhabit warm coasts, pilgrimage waters, volcanic lakes, and monsoon river mouths.

Breath is often incorporated into oath ritual. A speaker may submerge, carry a phrase through held breath, and release it only after surfacing before witnesses.

Some temples preserve sacred route songs. Cetacean communities dispute whether preservation grants the temple authority to restrict the descendants of the people who composed them.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ar contains large Cetacean populations within reef cities, island channels, harbor districts, fleet communities, and storm routes.

Cetaceans are central to weather warning and inter-island communication.

Al’Ari performance culture has produced call-and-response forms shared by aquatic and surface audiences. The most famous stages place part of the audience above water and part below, allowing each to hear a different but complete composition.

## VII. Cetaceans and Delving

Cetaceans are valued as acoustic surveyors, rescue swimmers, navigators, communicators, and interpreters of flooded space.

Their advantages include:

- Long breath
- Strong swimming
- Echolocation
- Coordinated movement
- Memory for route and sound
- The ability to detect form without relying upon light

Common hazards include:

- Magical silence
- Echoes that arrive before their source
- Chambers repeating a party member’s voice
- False air pockets
- Sudden pressure changes
- Sound that compels movement
- Geometry producing several acoustic locations for one object

Cetacean delvers often establish a **return call** before entering uncertain water.

Every party member learns the call.

No one follows a variation unless the change was agreed in advance.

A common warning states:

> The Labyrinth knows your voice as soon as you use it.

Cetacean names may possess an acoustic form, a surface-language form, and a route designation.

Written examples approximate only the surface form:

Imitating a Cetacean call without understanding its purpose may be harmless, embarrassing, or a serious offense depending upon the call.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

The following mechanics are an initial playtest version.

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Wisdom score increases by 2.

### Size

Your size is Medium.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

You have a swimming speed of 40 feet.

### Long Breath

You can hold your breath for up to 1 hour.

### Acoustic Bearing

You gain proficiency in the Perception skill.

While underwater, dim light, darkness, fog, silt, and similar visual obscurity do not impose disadvantage on your Wisdom (Perception) checks if the target can be heard.

### Sound the Depths

As a bonus action, you release a focused series of clicks.

Until the start of your next turn, you know the location of creatures and objects within 30 feet of you that are not behind total cover, even if you cannot see them.

This trait does not reveal color, writing, illusions without physical substance, or a creature’s identity.

You cannot use this trait while deafened or within magical silence.

You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

Sublineages describe broad physiological inheritances. They do not determine personality, profession, homeland, or social role.

### Whale

Whale Cetaceans are broad-bodied and powerful, often possessing low vocal ranges capable of traveling enormous distances through water.

Player-character Whale Cetaceans are Medium, though their weight and carrying requirements may greatly exceed those of other people of similar height.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Powerful Build.** You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift.

**Deep Resonance.** As an action, choose one creature within 30 feet that can hear you. It must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or be unable to take reactions until the start of your next turn.

The DC equals `8 + your proficiency bonus + your Wisdom modifier`.

Once you use Deep Resonance, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Dolphin

Dolphin Cetaceans commonly possess narrow bodies, flexible fins, pronounced acoustic range, and exceptional social awareness.

Their reputation for cheerfulness is burdensome to individuals whose communication, grief, or temperament does not match surface expectations.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Charisma score increases by 1.

**Guiding Click.** You can take the Help action as a bonus action when aiding a creature within 30 feet that can hear you.

You can do so a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

### Porpoise

Porpoise Cetaceans are typically compact, fast, and adapted to coastal, river-mouth, or cold-water movement.

Many Porpoise communities value concise signaling and rapid correction over elaborate long-distance song.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Dexterity score increases by 1.

**Wake-Step.** When a creature you can see misses you with an attack, you can use your reaction to move up to 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks.

You can use this trait a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus. You regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

If the campaign uses Homeland Imprints, choose one trait representing the region that most strongly shaped your character.

A homeland represents upbringing and cultural experience. It does not need to match your birthplace or ancestry.

### Allemance — Call Them Home

You gain proficiency in Survival.

When you make a Wisdom (Survival) check to locate a known person, settlement, or return route, you may add `1d4` to the roll.

Once you add this die, you cannot do so again until you finish a short or long rest.

### Oria — Quiet Machinery

You gain proficiency with tinker’s tools.

You have advantage on Intelligence checks made to identify a machine, vessel, or structure through the sound it produces.

### Vinyot — Licensed Voice

You gain proficiency in Performance or Persuasion, your choice.

When a creature attempts to imitate your voice or reproduce a performance you created, you have advantage on checks made to identify the imitation or prove the original source.

### Arneria — Oath of Held Breath

You know the `guidance` cantrip.

Once per long rest, you may cast `compelled duel` without a spell slot, requiring no material component.

Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for these spells.

### Al’Ar — Storm Answer

When you take thunder or lightning damage, you can use your reaction to gain resistance to that instance of damage.

After doing so, you may move up to 10 feet if you are swimming.

Once you use this trait, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

## XII. Playing a Cetacean

When creating a Cetacean character, consider:

- Whose call will you always answer?
- What silence do you need others to respect?
- Which route were you taught?
- Do you preserve memory accurately, emotionally, or selectively?
- What does breath-watch mean to you?
- Have you ever carried a message farther than its speaker intended?
- What do you hear in a crowded harbor that others miss?
- Does your voice feel complete in air?
- Which absent person shapes your decisions?
- What would make you stop singing a route?

A Cetacean may be solitary, forgetful, quiet, inland-born, poor at music, uncomfortable in groups, or afraid of deep water.

Their lineage gives them breath and acoustic inheritance.

It does not require them to become anyone’s song.
