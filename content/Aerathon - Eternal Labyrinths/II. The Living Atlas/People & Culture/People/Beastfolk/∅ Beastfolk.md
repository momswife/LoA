## I. Classification Overview

**Beastfolk** is the modern Ministry classification for a wide family of sapient peoples whose inherited forms preserve terrestrial animal kinships.

The category includes horned, hoofed, furred, burrowing, predatory, grazing, scavenging, and nocturnal lineages. It does not include every person who resembles an animal, nor does resemblance alone establish Beastfolk identity.

Placement follows the **Dominant Domain Standard**. Beastfolk lineages conduct the greater part of their ordinary embodied and communal lives upon land. Swimming, climbing, burrowing, or brief gliding does not alter that classification unless water or aerial movement becomes indispensable to the lineage’s physiology and historical way of life.

Primarily aquatic peoples are filed as [[∅ Aquaticfolk|Aquaticfolk]], while peoples fundamentally shaped by flight or sustained aerial movement are filed as [[∅ Avianfolk|Avianfolk]]. Terrestrial reptilian peoples remain [[∅ Scalefolk|Scalefolk]]. A lineage may not be registered simultaneously beneath multiple Folk classifications.

Current Ministry practice distinguishes four related terms:

- **Folk classification** identifies the broad registry family: Beastfolk.
- **Lineage** identifies a major inherited people, such as Bovine or Tenebrine.
- **Sublineage** identifies a recognized physiological inheritance within that lineage, such as Angus, Bison, Raccoon, or Possum.
- **Homeland** identifies the society that most strongly shaped a person. It is learned and lived rather than inherited through the body.

A person might therefore be recorded as:

> **Beastfolk → Tenebrine Lineage → Raccoon Sublineage → Vinyoti Homeland**

These are registry terms. They are not required forms of everyday self-description.

Many Beastfolk identify first by family, settlement, profession, faith, historical kinship, or homeland. Some use the Ministry lineage readily. Others regard it as useful only when dealing with officials. A smaller number reject the Beastfolk category because of its association with old Crown registries and the conditional personhood laws that followed the [[01. The Treaty of Breath & Beast|Treaty of Breath & Beast]].

The Ministry retains the classification because it supports legal recognition, medical practice, accessible equipment, and lineage-specific rights. It also acknowledges that a useful category may still carry an unjust history.

## II. The Meaning of Beastfolk

Beastfolk are not animals made humanoid.

They are not incomplete Brethren.

They are not a single culture.

The dominant historical reconstruction in [[03. The Creation of the Beastfolk|The Creation of the Beastfolk]] holds that the Old Court shaped the first Beastfolk through animal kinships so mortal civilization would retain many ways of understanding its obligations to the living world.

In that account, different kinships preserved different relationships:

- Herd peoples carried cooperation, vigilance, migration, and shared survival.
- Predatory peoples carried territory, restraint, and necessary violence.
- Burrowing peoples carried shelter, hidden labor, and patience.
- River peoples carried flood, fertility, danger, and return.
- Scavenging peoples carried cleansing, reuse, death, and survival after disaster.
- Nocturnal peoples carried dream, secrecy, and navigation through unseen danger.
- Great-beast peoples carried guardianship, endurance, and long memory.

These were not commands written into personality.

A Vulpine is not born deceitful. An Ursine is not born violent. A Celerine is not condemned to fear. A Bovine does not owe the world labor merely because they are strong.

Kinship provides an inherited body and a field of cultural memory. History, household, homeland, disability, faith, profession, and individual choice determine what any person makes of it.

### A Living Relationship

Many Beastfolk traditions understand identity through relationship rather than isolation.

A person may carry obligations to:

- Living relatives
- Adopted kin
- Ancestors
- A route or seasonal gathering
- A water source, grove, pasture, ruin, or neighborhood
- A craft taught through generations
- Creatures sharing an ancestral kinship
- A promise whose first speaker is long dead

This does not mean all Beastfolk live in rural clans or worship the Old Court.

An urban Murine barrister may understand case law as a maze maintained for everyone who must pass through it. A Vinyoti Feline broker may treat a contract as a territorial boundary. An Oric Ligonine engineer may express ancestral obligation through the safe maintenance of tunnels. A Tenebrine performer may preserve a neighborhood’s history through jokes that outsiders mistake for disposable entertainment.

The relationship changes.

The expectation that life is connected to other life remains.

## III. Classification and Its Discontents

Modern Beastfolk taxonomy is cultural, physiological, historical, and administrative at once.

It is not a perfect biological tree.

Tenebrines group raccoons and possums because both developed within nocturnal settlement ecologies and share a long political history. Ferines group hyenas, wolverines, honey badgers, and Tasmanian devils through traditions formed under scarcity, aftermath, and contested authority. Ligonines include peoples whose shared relationship to enclosed, defended, or structurally unusual habitats matters more than close natural resemblance.

The Ministry therefore recognizes **convergent lineages**: peoples who became one lineage through shared life, law, intermarriage, adaptation, and memory.

This approach prevents registry practice from reducing sapient peoples to zoology. It also creates disputes.

Common objections include:

- A broad lineage may conceal meaningful differences between sublineages.
- Old MDO surveys sometimes treated cultural practices as biological facts.
- Crown-era labels survive in modern paperwork after the communities rejected them.
- Mixed households may be forced to select one lineage for systems built around a single answer.
- A person’s apparent form may not match their legal or cultural lineage.
- Smaller kinships may be absorbed into a larger category to simplify administration.

Current policy requires disputed classifications to be recorded as disputed. No Ministry office may reassign an adult’s folk identity solely through visual inspection.

### The Avian Reclassification

Older files placed Aarakocra and several other winged peoples beneath Beastfolk.

That classification is obsolete.

Following the Aerial Peoples Review and sustained community petition, those lineages are now recorded under [[∅ Avianfolk|Avianfolk]]. The obsolete Aarakocra-era mechanics have been fully retired; current aerial lineage rules are maintained exclusively in the Avianfolk dossiers.

The reclassification is an important precedent: Ministry taxonomy is a filing system, not an eternal truth.

## IV. Recognized Beastfolk Lineages

The Living Atlas presently recognizes seventeen principal Beastfolk lineages.

### [[Bovine]]

Horned and great-bodied kinships including Angus, Bison, and Yak peoples. Bovine traditions frequently examine patience, stewardship, strength, and the danger of being valued only for what one can carry.

### [[Canine]]

Wolf, Dog, and Hound peoples associated with scent memory, coordinated action, chosen loyalty, and disagreement over when belonging becomes obedience.

### [[Celerine]]

Rabbit, Squirrel, and Hare peoples whose cultures often prize alertness, momentum, social grace, and the transformation of fear into timely action.

### [[Cervine]]

Deer, Elk, and Moose peoples carrying traditions of seasonal memory, antlered inheritance, guarded stillness, and responsibility toward paths used by more than one generation.

### [[Equine]]

Horse, Donkey, and Mule peoples shaped by roads, endurance, horizon-reading, and the contested difference between service freely offered and burden merely expected.

### [[Feline]]

Small-cat and great-cat peoples with strong traditions of territory, poise, private affection, sudden motion, and resistance to outsiders treating independence as emotional absence.

### [[Ferine]]

Hyena, Wolverine, Honey Badger, and Tasmanian Devil peoples whose convergent lineage formed in environments of pressure, aftermath, and unstable authority. Ferine cultures often distinguish conflict honestly named from violence disguised as order.

### [[Laetine]]

Ferret, Otter, and Mink peoples known for flexible bodies, applied curiosity, close work, watercraft, and social practices built around entering spaces others overlook.

### [[Ligonine]]

Mole, Armadillo, and Sloth peoples joined by long traditions of unusual shelter, structural adaptation, patient problem-solving, and life lived outside conventional settlement plans.

### [[Murine]]

Mouse and Rat peoples whose histories emphasize route memory, collective survival, quiet communication, salvage, and the ability to build community inside systems designed for larger bodies.

### [[Porcine]]

Pig, Boar, and Peccary peoples associated with soil knowledge, dense kinship, direct communication, cultivated abundance, and resistance to degrading ideas about appetite or cleanliness.

### [[Primatine]]

Monkey, Ape, and Lemur peoples with varied climbing bodies, tactile social traditions, tool cultures, expressive gesture, and persistent struggles against false hierarchies of civilization.

### [[Proboscine]]

Elephant, Mammoth, and Mastodon peoples shaped by trunks, low-frequency sound, route memory, shared weight, and the right to release memories others expect them to carry.

### [[Ovine]]

Sheep, Goat, and Ram peoples carrying pastoral, educational, climbing, and guardianship traditions. Ovine communities frequently debate the difference between guidance and control.

### [[Tenebrine]]

Raccoon and Possum peoples shaped by nocturnal cities, hidden labor, improvisation, endurance, and the politics of who is allowed to move unseen.

### [[Ursine]]

Brown, Polar, and Black Bear peoples with powerful mnemonic traditions. Ursine culture often treats memory as a public duty while recognizing the harm caused when remembrance becomes possession.

### [[Vulpine]]

Tradewind and Desert Fox peoples associated with deduction, vigilance, patient inquiry, and the social cost of noticing what others prefer to leave unspoken.

Recognition remains open to revision. Smaller kinships may seek independent standing, merge through community decision, or reject Ministry lineage entirely.

## V. Shared History and the Mark of Breath

Beastfolk peoples possessed cities, laws, faiths, and treaties long before Brethren courts debated whether to recognize them as persons.

The [[01. The Treaty of Breath & Beast|Treaty of Breath & Beast]] did not grant Beastfolk personhood. It granted limited recognition within several Crown systems that had already presumed the authority to deny it.

The treaty’s **Mark of Breath** became both passport and shackle. It permitted selected Beastfolk to own property, enter guilds, and receive legal standing under Brethren law. It also made recognition conditional upon patronage, registration, and obedience.

The later Vel-Orann Betrayal transformed the mark into a tool of seizure and collective punishment.

Modern Beastfolk responses to this history vary.

Some families preserve an ancestor’s mark as evidence of survival. Some cover inherited marks with new designs. Some refuse any tattoo placed by an institution. Others have reclaimed the paired-lung symbol as a declaration that no government owns the breath it records.

Ministry registry work is shaped by that memory.

As a result:

- Medical samples require informed consent and limited retention.
- Lineage cannot be inferred for criminal profiling.
- Ancestral materials cannot be collected as public property.
- Sacred routes and seasonal land use must be documented alongside formal deeds.
- Community consultation must include people without Crown-recognized titles.
- Registry refusal does not suspend personhood or ordinary legal protection.

These safeguards are imperfect and frequently contested.

They exist because earlier records were used as weapons.

## VI. Kinship, Household, and Inheritance

There is no universal Beastfolk household.

Recognized arrangements include:

- Multigenerational homes
- Herd or pack households
- Burrow neighborhoods
- Seasonal migration groups
- Solitary adults bound to a wider kinship
- Craft families
- Delver households
- Adoptive circles
- Mixed-lineage and mixed-folk families
- Ancestor houses maintaining obligations rather than common residence

Adoption is widespread but not uniform. In many communities, kinship is established through a witnessed obligation: feeding someone through winter, teaching a protected craft, sharing responsibility for a child, maintaining a grave, or agreeing to answer a particular call for aid.

Inheritance may include property, but often includes duties that cannot be sold easily.

A family may inherit:

- The upkeep of a bridge or trail
- Responsibility for an orchard, canal, or communal oven
- A place in a seasonal procession
- The care of a story or name
- A debt to another household
- A warning that must be repeated
- A right of refuge paired with an obligation to offer refuge

Younger Beastfolk frequently question obligations whose purpose has been lost. Elders may fear that releasing one inherited duty will weaken all the others.

One ancient question remains current:

> If a promise has become harmful, is the ancestor honored by keeping it—or by admitting they could not foresee what followed?

No lineage has produced one answer.

## VII. Beastfolk in the Five Greater Regions

Beastfolk live throughout Aerathon. No Greater Region belongs to a single folk classification.

### Allemance

Allemancian Beastfolk commonly interpret kinship through belonging, hospitality, local memory, and mutual aid. Roadside shelters, shared meals, village watches, repair gatherings, and the reliable return of travelers often matter more than formal clan hierarchy.

The danger within this tradition is exclusion disguised as familiarity. A community that defines family warmly may still decide too quickly who does not belong.

### Oria

Oric Beastfolk often express inherited duty through maintenance, function, and competent stewardship. A promise is respected because someone can depend upon it under bad conditions. Lineage crafts frequently intersect with House workshops, rescue teams, winter stores, structural inspection, and long-distance supply.

Oric culture can turn usefulness into a measure of worth. Beastfolk who cannot or will not perform the work expected of their bodies may face quiet but persistent prejudice.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Beastfolk have shaped the League’s markets, ports, entertainment districts, farms, security companies, and contract law. Kinship obligations may be translated into shares, mutual-aid funds, family firms, neighborhood associations, or carefully negotiated favors.

This translation creates opportunity and risk. A duty written clearly can be defended. A relationship assigned a price can also be purchased by someone with more coin.

### Arneria

Arnerian Beastfolk traditions often place weight upon oath, survival, ritual discipline, and sacred ecology. In Bat’yan especially, Beastfolk communities maintain dense relationships with canopy routes, rain cycles, ancestor groves, and living settlements.

Arnerian institutions sometimes formalize tradition so strongly that refusal appears as moral failure. Young people regularly contest which inherited rites remain protective and which have become instruments of authority.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Beastfolk live among islands, storm coasts, rebuilt cities, fleets, performance cultures, and rapidly changing alliances. Adaptation is praised. A custom that cannot travel, improvise, or survive catastrophe is likely to be changed.

The shadow of this flexibility is exhaustion. Communities celebrated for rebuilding may receive little permission to mourn what was lost.

## VIII. The Loamlink and the Old Court

Many Beastfolk traditions call [[The Loamlink]] **the Listening Earth**.

Communion groves, root wells, stone-tapping routes, and ancestral gardens are maintained by communities across several lineages. Some Beastfolk interpret sensations carried through the Loamlink as ancestral speech. Others understand them as ecological information given ritual form. The Ministry has verified long-distance biomagical effects but has not established a single explanation.

The Loamlink is sacred to many Beastfolk.

It is not sacred to all Beastfolk, and it is not exclusively theirs.

The same is true of the Old Court and the [[03. The Far Heaven|Far Heaven]]. Beastfolk identity does not require Old Court worship. Old Court worship does not belong only to Beastfolk.

Modern Beastfolk may follow Dawn faiths, local gods, secular philosophies, ancestor traditions, the Old Court, several systems at once, or none.

The Ministry does not record faith as a presumed consequence of lineage.

## IX. Beastfolk and Delving

Beastfolk delvers are often recruited according to crude assumptions about their bodies.

Bovines are sent to carry. Canines are sent to track. Felines are sent to scout. Murines are sent into small passages. Ursines are expected to remember. Ferines are expected to endure violence. Tenebrines are expected to steal.

These assignments sometimes overlap with genuine capability.

That does not make them neutral.

Responsible delver companies assess training, consent, equipment, and individual preference. Lineage may inform safety planning, but it cannot replace it.

Important considerations include:

- Horn, antler, tail, claw, and muzzle clearance
- Armor fitted to nonstandard frames
- Sensory overload caused by scent, vibration, or magical noise
- Small-body and great-body route access
- Dietary and temperature requirements
- The effect of polymorphic chambers upon inherited traits
- Cultural restrictions surrounding remains, blood, fur, bone, or ancestral material
- The right to refuse tasks assigned through stereotype

Beastfolk delvers have been central to the modern understanding of living Labyrinth ecology. Many recognize behavioral or environmental changes before instruments register them.

The Labyrinth also exploits inherited expectations.

A false ancestral voice, a perfect migration route, a familiar scent, or a plant that appears to remember can be more dangerous than an obvious monster.

## X. Common Misconceptions

### “Beastfolk are one culture.”

They are hundreds of cultures connected through several broad historical and legal experiences.

### “A Beastfolk person behaves like the animal they resemble.”

Body and sensory experience shape life. They do not dictate morality, profession, temperament, or desire.

### “Sublineage determines culture.”

Sublineage is physiological inheritance. Homeland, household, faith, class, and personal history shape culture.

### “Beastfolk are naturally closer to nature.”

Some traditions emphasize ecological relationship. Beastfolk also live in towers, ports, courts, factories, universities, estates, and dense urban neighborhoods.

### “Every Beastfolk worships the Old Court.”

They do not.

### “Predatory lineages are more violent.”

No evidence supports this claim. It survives because institutions repeatedly confuse heraldry, diet, anatomy, and moral character.

### “Herd lineages are naturally obedient.”

Coordination is not obedience. Communal cultures may be deeply suspicious of solitary authority.

### “The Ministry created the current lineages.”

The peoples existed first. The Ministry records, revises, and sometimes misunderstands them.

## XI. Creating a Beastfolk Character

When creating a Beastfolk character, consider five distinct layers.

### Lineage

What inherited body, senses, and signature traits do they carry?

### Sublineage

Which recognized physiological inheritance best describes them? Do they use the Ministry term themselves?

### Homeland

Which place taught them how a household, promise, meal, argument, or public duty should work?

### Obligation

What relationship do they believe they must maintain? Is that obligation chosen, inherited, resented, cherished, or misunderstood?

### Individual Life

What do they want that their community did not choose for them?

A Beastfolk character may embrace ancestral tradition, revise it, leave it, return to it, or build something without precedent.

Their lineage provides a history.

It does not provide a destiny.
