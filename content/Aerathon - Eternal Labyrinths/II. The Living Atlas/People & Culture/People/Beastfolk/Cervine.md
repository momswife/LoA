# Cervine

**Folk Classification:** [[‎ Beastfolk|Beastfolk]]  
**Lineage Classification:** Cervine  
**Registry Scope:** Sapient Lineage and Homeland Variance  
**Recognition Status:** Full Sapient Recognition  
**Delver Eligibility:** Unrestricted  
**Common Names:** Crownfolk, Longsteps, Grove-Wardens  
**Date of Revised Filing:** 7th Cycle, 3388 A.D.

---

## I. Lineage Overview

Cervines are Deer, Elk, and Moose Beastfolk known for antlered forms, powerful frames, seasonal awareness, and cultural traditions concerned with guardianship.

They are frequently described as solemn keepers of memory.

Some are.

Others are impatient, comic, forgetful, restless, or deeply suspicious of anyone who makes dignity a requirement of their lineage.

Cervine presence has been romanticized in art and weaponized in politics. Rulers place antlers upon banners to signify continuity. Temples use Cervine silhouettes to represent quiet wilderness. Military orders recruit them as living bulwarks. Courts expect them to remember disputes no archive preserved.

These images praise the lineage while assigning it a narrow life:

Stand still.

Look ancient.

Carry what everyone else forgot.

Cervine cultures ask whether guardianship is still honorable when the guarded object has ceased to serve the living.

Their central question is:

> What deserves continuity, and who bears the cost of preserving it?

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

In the **Account of the First Crossing**, the Old Court asks several peoples to walk through a new forest.

Each chooses the shortest route.

The first Cervine chooses a longer path because the shortest crosses a place where unseen creatures are giving birth. The gods ask how they knew.

The Cervine answers that the path itself had become quiet.

The Old Court grants them crown and hoof—not as symbols of rule, but as reminders that height creates responsibility for what others cannot yet see.

Many Cervines interpret this account as a teaching about attention rather than divine status.

### Path Memory

Early Cervine communities maintained routes through seasonal movement, repeated clearing, marker trees, antler scoring, and spoken account.

A path was not preserved merely because ancestors used it.

Its continued legitimacy depended upon whether it still connected shelter, water, migration, trade, burial, or kin.

When a route became harmful, some communities performed a **Closing Walk**. Travelers crossed it one final time, named what the path had provided, removed dangerous markers, and taught the replacement route publicly.

This practice complicates the outsider belief that Cervines never release tradition.

### The Northern Estrangements

Cervine history in Oria contains periods of authority, displacement, and conflict with several other Beastfolk lineages.

Later House records often present Cervines as the natural custodians of northern land. Canine, Ursine, and smaller communities preserve accounts in which that custodianship became territorial control.

Modern Cervines disagree over how this history should be taught.

Some fear that public criticism will weaken already threatened traditions. Others argue that continuity without honest account is merely an old claim repeated until it resembles truth.

## III. Body and Crowned Form

Cervines commonly possess:

- Hooved feet
- Long limbs
- Strong necks and shoulders
- Wide peripheral vision
- Sensitive hearing
- Antlers in many individuals
- Seasonal changes in coat, antler, or magical affinity
- Bodies capable of sustained travel and forceful advance

Most are Medium, though Moose Cervines may stand near eight feet tall.

### Antlers

Antler growth varies by sublineage, sex, age, health, magic, and individual inheritance.

Some Cervines shed antlers seasonally. Others retain them. Some grow irregular crowns, small prongs, or none.

Antlers may be decorated with cloth, metal, flowers, bells, writing, spell foci, mourning ties, or household markers.

Shed antlers remain culturally and magically significant. Customs governing them range from free gifting to mortuary restriction.

Harvesting, purchasing, or enchanting an antler without clear provenance is prohibited.

### Space

Architecture designed without antler clearance can turn ordinary rooms into hazards.

Cervine advocates have influenced doorway, vehicle, bunk, helmet, and emergency-exit standards across the continent.

The recurring suggestion that a Cervine simply trim their antlers to fit a building is not considered an accessibility solution.

### Seasonal Change

Many Deer Cervines experience shifts in temperature tolerance linked to rest, environment, or ritual.

Seasonal awareness does not imply mystical certainty. Climate distortion and Labyrinth weather can create disorientation, disrupted growth, and profound anxiety among communities whose ordinary environmental signals no longer align.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Cervine communities include grove houses, road families, northern holds, urban councils, craft lineages, ritual circles, and mixed households.

### The Standing Place

Many communities maintain a place where anyone may stand to ask whether a tradition still serves its stated purpose.

The challenger must name:

- What is being preserved
- Whom it protects
- Whom it harms
- What might be lost if it changes
- What could carry its meaning forward

The challenge is not inherently disrespectful.

Refusing to permit it is often treated as evidence that authority has confused continuity with ownership.

### Personal Space

Cervine etiquette places considerable importance upon approach.

Crowding an antlered person can physically trap them. Approaching silently from behind can provoke reflexive defense. Traditional greetings therefore allow room and remain within peripheral view.

### Commitment

Cervines are stereotyped as making lifelong commitments.

Many traditions do favor deliberation before vow. They also preserve formal means of release, including Closing Walks, broken-crown ceremonies, and witnessed transfers of responsibility.

A commitment without a lawful ending becomes captivity.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Cervine communities commonly admire:

- Patience
- Continuity
- Environmental attention
- Deliberate commitment
- Guardianship
- Personal dignity
- Memory with purpose
- The ability to remain present

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Patience | Allowing understanding to develop | Failing to act in time |
| Continuity | Carrying knowledge across generations | Preserving harmful authority |
| Presence | Creating calm and stability | Intimidating without noticing |
| Guardianship | Protecting routes and people | Deciding others cannot choose for themselves |
| Dignity | Refusing degradation | Treating vulnerability as shame |
| Memory | Preventing erasure | Inheriting every grievance |

A recurring Cervine fear is that releasing one duty will cause an entire history to disappear.

Some accumulate obligations. Some become fierce archivists. Some reject inheritance completely. Others cultivate humor, travel, or ordinary craft as a life outside ceremonial expectation.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Cervines debate:

- Whether antler materials may be sold
- Whether northern custodial claims remain legitimate
- How Cervine and Canine communities should address old Oric conflicts
- Whether seasonal rituals must adapt to altered climate
- Whether four-item attunement creates special delver obligations
- When guardianship becomes territorial authority
- Whether the Ministry overstates Cervine memory

## VI. Cervines in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Cervines serve as local historians, wardens, brewers, growers, bridge keepers, magistrates, and protectors of old roads.

Hearthland respect for memory aligns with Cervine traditions. It can also turn the person who remembers into a public archive who is never allowed to forget privately.

### Oria

Oria remains a major center of Cervine political, spiritual, and craft life.

Cervines are found across the Houses as forge patrons, structural wardens, ritual leaders, artificers, rescue workers, and keepers of inter-House routes.

Their historical authority remains contested. Modern reform movements seek continuity without inherited supremacy.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Cervines work in construction, vehicle design, insurance, finance, security, archival trade, and civic planning.

Their reputation for stability is commercially valuable. It can produce contracts that make a Cervine personally responsible for structural or institutional failures far beyond their actual control.

### Arneria

Arnerian Cervines include old exiled communities, desert route keepers, temple critics, performers, and oath witnesses.

Some developed sharp verbal traditions after centuries in which ceremonial dignity provided little protection. Their mockery is not a loss of Cervine seriousness, but one method of refusing imposed reverence.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Cervines work in shipyards, music, storm construction, island agriculture, and mobile craft.

They combine long-term memory with architectures built to change. An Al’Ari Cervine may preserve the numbered sequence of a structure precisely so it can be disassembled before the storm.

## VII. Cervines and Delving

Cervines serve as front-line wardens, route keepers, spellcasters, structural anchors, and relic bearers.

Their advantages include:

- Natural weapons
- Powerful build
- Forced-march endurance
- Resistance to forced movement
- Environmental awareness
- Unusual magical attunement among Elk

Their size and antlers create route hazards in confined Labyrinths. Proper helmets, release fittings, and extraction plans are essential.

The Labyrinth exploits continuity through familiar paths, ancestral voices, repeated rooms, and relics that reward the refusal to let go.

A common warning states:

> A path repeating itself is not proof that it remembers you kindly.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Strength score increases by 2.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

### Armaments of the First

You possess natural weapons: a hoof stomp that deals bludgeoning damage and, if you have antlers, a gore attack that deals piercing damage.

These attacks deal `1d6 + your Strength modifier` damage.

### March on Hooves

You have advantage on saving throws made to avoid exhaustion from forced marches.

### Powerful Build

You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

### Deer

Deer Cervines are diverse in build, coat, crown, and seasonal adaptation.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Wisdom score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Still as the Grove.** You can attempt to hide when lightly obscured by natural terrain. Additionally, you have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing.

**Season-Touched.** When you finish a long rest, choose resistance to cold damage or fire damage. This resistance lasts until you finish your next long rest.

### Elk

Elk Cervines are tall, broad-crowned, and strongly associated with magical craft and guardianship.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Constitution score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Nature’s Conduit.** Your antlers or eyes can be used as a spellcasting focus. In addition, you can be attuned to up to four magic items instead of three.

**Unshakable Guard.** When an effect would knock you prone, you can use your reaction to remain standing. Once you use this feature, you can’t do so again until you finish a short or long rest.

### Moose

Moose Cervines possess immense frames, wide crowns, and powerful close-range movement.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Immovable Presence.** You have advantage on Strength saving throws and Strength (Athletics) checks made to resist being pushed, pulled, knocked prone, or forcibly moved.

**Crashing Advance.** If you move at least 15 feet straight toward a creature and hit it with a natural weapon on the same turn, the target must succeed on a Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.

The DC equals `8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier`.

If the target fails by 5 or more, it is also restrained until the start of its next turn.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

### Allemance — Holders of History

You gain proficiency in History.

When making History checks related to Oria, Allemance, or Cervine lineages, you roll with advantage.

### Oria — Forge Bequeathed

When casting spells with expensive material components, the gold cost is reduced by 20 percent.

You also have advantage on checks made to create magic items.

### Vinyot — Structural Sense

When a vehicle or structure you oversee would take collision damage, reduce that damage by `3d6`.

The struck object instead takes that damage.

### Arneria — Sand Whisper

You know the `vicious mockery` cantrip.

When you take the Attack action, you can cast `vicious mockery` as a bonus action a number of times equal to your Charisma modifier per long rest.

### Al’Ar — Mountain to the Isles

You gain proficiency with any two artisan’s tools or musical instruments of your choice.

## XII. Playing a Cervine

Consider:

- What deserves continuity?
- Which inherited grievance do you refuse?
- How do you feel about your antlers?
- What duty have you formally ended?
- Does dignity protect you or isolate you?
- What environmental change do you notice first?
- Who is permitted inside your personal space?
- Which path should be closed?
- What ordinary joy exists outside guardianship?
- What would you choose to forget?

A Cervine may be hurried, unserious, forgetful, physically small, non-antlered, change-seeking, or uninterested in guardianship.

Their lineage offers crown, hoof, and inherited perspective.

It does not require them to become a monument.
