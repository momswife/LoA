## I. Lineage Overview

Porcines are Pig, Boar, and Warthog Beastfolk known for powerful scent, resilient bodies, tusks in many sublineages, and cultures shaped by soil, food, salvage, and the politics of being treated as consumable.

Other peoples have projected filth, greed, appetite, and brutality onto Porcine bodies. Porcine communities answer that appetite is a relationship with abundance and scarcity—not a moral defect.

---

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Old Porcine kinships maintained truffle grounds, seed banks, forest clearings, refuse cycles, and soil renewal. Their work was later dismissed as rooting after settled powers claimed the land it improved.

The **Table Without Carving** commemorates resistance to regimes that classified Porcine people beside livestock. At the meal, every dish is named by maker and source; no body at the table may be spoken of as food.

---

## III. Body and Sensory Life

Porcines are sturdy Humanoids with snouts, keen smell, hooved feet, varied hair or bristles, and tusks ranging from absent to prominent.

They regulate heat carefully and may require water, shade, or mud as practical skin care. Calling those needs dirty has repeatedly denied safe working conditions.

---

## IV. Society and Kinship

Porcine households include farms, forest circles, urban kitchens, salvage cooperatives, breweries, trade families, and defense companies.

Food is often a language of consent and reciprocity. Declining a meal is permitted; pressuring someone to eat or commenting on appetite is not hospitality.

---

## V. Inner Life and Modern Debate

Porcine cultures admire resourcefulness, sensual attention, perseverance, generosity, ecological repair, and refusal to waste.

Their shadow is overprovision: expressing care through constant supply until rest, grief, or uncertainty is buried beneath work.

---

## VI. Porcine Peoples in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Porcine farmers, chefs, brewers, and advocates contest land taken after generations of soil repair.

### Oria

Cold-hardy Boar communities maintain root cellars, forest routes, and winter kitchens.

### Vinyot

Markets and waste-recovery guilds depend heavily upon Porcine labor while stigmatizing the same work.

### Arneria

Warthog and desert Pig communities manage oasis soils and caravan provisioning.

### Al'Ar

Island households specialize in volcanic agriculture, smoke curing, and storm stores.

---

## VII. Porcine Delvers

Porcine delvers excel at scent analysis, foraging, breaking obstructed routes, ration management, and identifying biological hazards.

Their senses do not make them sanitation workers by default. Crews must not assign spoiled supplies, corpse search, or waste handling by lineage.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

> [!warning] Playtest Mechanics  
> These traits are an initial Aerathon playtest package and require DM approval until table testing is complete.

### Creature Type, Size, and Speed

You are a Humanoid, Medium. Your walking speed is 30 feet.

### Keen Smell

You have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) and Wisdom (Survival) checks relying on smell.

### Stout Constitution

You have advantage on saving throws against poison and resistance to poison damage.

### Root Out

After searching a 10-foot area for 1 minute, you can determine whether it contains edible material, recently disturbed soil, or a concealed organic creature.

### Refuse to Fall

Once per long rest when damage would reduce you to 0 hit points, you can drop to 1 hit point instead.

---

## X. Recognized Sublineages and Traditions

### Pig

Pig Porcines display enormous physical and cultural variety and are prominent in agricultural and urban food traditions.

### Boar

Boar Porcines often possess dense bristles, pronounced tusks, and strong forest or highland traditions.

### Warthog

Warthog Porcines are adapted to heat, dry grassland, and exposed routes where shade and water law are central.

---

## XI. Playing a Porcine

Consider:

- What does food communicate in your household?
- Which stereotype makes you angriest?
- What buried thing are you seeking?
- How do you care without overprovisioning?
- Who is welcome at your table?

A character may embrace, reinterpret, or reject every tradition described above. Lineage creates context, not personality.
