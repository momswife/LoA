# Tenebrine

**Folk Classification:** [[‎ Beastfolk|Beastfolk]]  
**Lineage Classification:** Tenebrine  
**Registry Scope:** Sapient Lineage and Homeland Variance  
**Recognition Status:** Full Sapient Recognition  
**Delver Eligibility:** Unrestricted  
**Common Names:** Gloamfolk, Night-Hands, Lamplighters  
**Date of Revised Filing:** 7th Cycle, 3388 A.D.

---

## I. Lineage Overview

Tenebrines are a convergent Beastfolk lineage composed principally of Raccoon and Possum peoples.

They are joined less by close bodily resemblance than by a shared history in the hours and spaces other societies neglected.

Tenebrines built lives around:

- Night markets
- Refuse routes
- Canal edges
- Theater alleys
- Mortuary streets
- Rooftop passages
- Service corridors
- Border camps
- Temporary settlements
- The work performed after public life had gone to sleep

Where lamplight fades, a city does not become empty.

It becomes honest.

Tenebrines learned which institutions still functioned after their doors closed, which promises survived without witnesses, and how much of respectable life depended upon people forbidden from entering through the front.

Raccoon communities are often associated with quick hands, humor, recovery, and improvised opportunity. Possum communities are associated with endurance, stillness, and decisive defense.

Both descriptions contain truth.

Neither describes a personality.

The central Tenebrine question is not whether one survives the dark.

It is what one is required to become in order to survive being treated as part of it.

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Old Court fragments classify the ancestors of modern Tenebrines among the **Nocturnal Kinships** and **Scavenger Kinships**.

These categories described sacred responsibilities rather than species.

Nocturnal kin were expected to notice danger concealed by darkness, dream, secrecy, or public inattention. Scavenger kin were expected to cleanse, recover, reuse, and continue after systems failed.

Later societies made both duties shameful.

Waste work became evidence of dirtiness. Night work became evidence of criminality. Reuse became proof of poverty. Familiarity with locks, hidden routes, or discarded material became presumed intent to steal.

Tenebrine oral histories remember the first cities differently.

In the **Litany of the Second Door**, a settlement locks its public gate during a plague. The officials declare the city secure. A Tenebrine worker points toward the kitchens, drains, ash chutes, animal runs, and servants’ passages and asks:

> Which city did you secure—the one on the map, or the one that keeps the map alive?

The worker is not named in surviving versions. Raccoon tellings insist they escaped through a chimney. Possum tellings insist they sat outside the council chamber until everyone became too uncomfortable to ignore them.

Both endings are probably later additions.

Both are culturally precise.

### The Lantern Below

Many Tenebrine communities preserve a household lamp below ordinary eye level.

It may be placed near the floor, beneath a stair, beside a drain, or within a low window. The lamp honors people whose labor occurs outside public notice and reminds the household to look where respectable attention does not.

The lamp is lit during:

- Night births
- Neighborhood searches
- Epidemics
- Funerals for unidentified people
- The return of someone believed lost
- Collective action by service workers
- The first night in a new home

Some urban movements now carry small floor-lamps during protests against exploitative night labor and forced blood collection.

## III. Body and Nocturnal Form

Tenebrines possess strong low-light vision and an unusual resistance to disease.

Common features include:

- Dark-adapted eyes
- Sensitive hands
- Fur suited to varied urban and woodland climates
- Long, dexterous fingers
- Strong scent recognition
- Raccoon masks, ringed tails, and mobile ears
- Possum muzzles, pale facial markings, grasping tails, and robust jaws

Tenebrines are usually Medium, though height, build, and posture vary substantially.

### Pristine Blood

Tenebrine resistance to disease is one of the most medically significant inherited traits recorded by the Ministry.

It is also the source of generations of abuse.

Tenebrine blood can temporarily strengthen another creature’s resistance to disease. During epidemics, Tenebrine donors have saved thousands of lives.

Crown physicians responded by treating the lineage as a renewable medical resource.

Historical abuses include:

- Compulsory blood tithes
- Debt contracts repaid through donation
- Prison extraction
- Employers claiming ownership of blood shed at work
- Hospitals retaining samples beyond consent
- False rumors that Tenebrine blood cures poison, age, curses, or undeath
- Kidnapping during outbreaks

Modern Ministry law classifies Tenebrine blood as protected bodily material.

Donation must be voluntary. Payment contracts require independent review. No person may be compelled to donate as a condition of treatment, employment, release, debt relief, or civic recognition.

Many Tenebrines still carry donor tokens. Some display them proudly. Others consider public praise for donation another way of making refusal socially impossible.

### Night Is Not Silence

Tenebrine senses are adapted to darkness, not sensory absence.

Night cities contain machinery, distant conversation, animal movement, pipes, insects, footfall, and the constant negotiation of unseen bodies.

Many Tenebrines sleep comfortably amid low, familiar sound and poorly amid perfect silence. Others require quiet precisely because their attention rarely stops collecting information.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Tenebrine communities may be organized as households, stoop circles, night markets, lantern blocks, recovery crews, performance companies, canal wards, donor cooperatives, or informal networks of doors and favors.

Kinship often develops through **access**.

To give someone a key, a route, a hiding place, a true name, or permission to enter without knocking may carry as much weight as a public oath.

This does not mean Tenebrines are naturally secretive.

Privacy became culturally important because other institutions treated access to Tenebrine bodies, homes, labor, and movements as something they could claim.

### The Borrowed Key

In many Raccoon and mixed communities, a trusted person receives a useless key during a household ceremony.

The key may fit a lock that no longer exists, a childhood home, a demolished workplace, or nothing at all.

Its meaning is:

> You are not trusted because you can enter. You are trusted because you know when not to.

Returning the key can mark estrangement. Adding it to a household ring can mark adoption, reconciliation, or marriage.

### Keeping Watch by Doing Something Else

Possum communities often practice a form of quiet companionship outsiders misread as indifference.

A distressed person is not forced to explain themselves. Someone remains nearby, repairing clothing, preparing food, sorting supplies, or sitting without direct attention until conversation becomes possible.

The practice protects dignity and reduces the pressure to perform pain.

It can also become avoidance. Families sometimes remain present for years without naming the harm everyone recognizes.

### Humor

Tenebrine humor is not one thing.

Raccoon performers are famous for jokes that expose class performance, hypocrisy, institutional vanity, and the difference between property law and actual use. Possum humor is often sparse, dry, and delivered after others assumed the conversation had ended.

Humor may:

- Defuse danger
- Test whether a stranger understands local rules
- Turn humiliation back upon authority
- Preserve stories officials refused to record
- Conceal fear
- Prevent a sincere conversation from beginning

Tenebrine communities usually recognize the difference.

They do not always agree when the difference matters.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Tenebrine communities commonly admire:

- Adaptability
- Discretion
- Recovery
- Humor under pressure
- Practical generosity
- Respect for privacy
- The ability to notice overlooked labor
- Survival without surrendering curiosity

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptability | Finding a way through changing conditions | Never believing safety will last |
| Humor | Restoring courage and exposing power | Refusing vulnerability |
| Discretion | Protecting privacy | Withholding necessary truth |
| Resourcefulness | Reusing what others abandon | Taking what was not offered |
| Endurance | Remaining present through hardship | Treating pain as ordinary |
| Opportunity | Recognizing hidden possibility | Exploiting people as quickly as systems do |

A recurring Tenebrine fear is that security is only the quiet moment before removal.

Some respond by never owning more than they can carry. Some collect obsessively. Some learn every exit. Some build homes full of guests and light. Others become experts in laws, contracts, locks, medicine, or institutional procedure because they refuse to be surprised again.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Tenebrines debate:

- Whether nocturnal identity should be preserved or allowed to fade as work becomes safer
- Whether paid blood donation is mutual aid, necessary labor, or exploitation
- Whether illegal recovery from abandoned property is theft
- Whether the Ministry’s convergent lineage obscures Raccoon and Possum difference
- Whether humor should remain a shield in public politics
- Whether respectability offers protection or demands cultural erasure
- Whether neighborhood secrecy protects vulnerable people or shields abuse

The sharpest dispute concerns visibility.

Some activists insist that safety requires public recognition.

Others remember every registry that became a list of doors to raid.

## VI. Tenebrines in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Tenebrines are common in market towns, breweries, mills, theaters, inns, archives, canal settlements, and roadside communities.

Many become keepers of local rumor—not because they know every secret, but because people speak differently around those assumed to be cleaning, repairing, serving, or passing through.

The best-known Allemancian night houses provide food, spare beds, forged emergency papers, and mediation without requiring a guest to explain everything at the door.

### Oria

Oric Tenebrines work in lodge houses, ventilation systems, winter stores, night inspection, fine repair, medicine, and the crowded interior networks of the great Houses.

Oria values function. Tenebrines often know which unofficial workaround keeps a supposedly perfect system operating.

House authorities respect that competence and periodically attempt to formalize it. The result is a recurring struggle between safe recognition and the loss of informal autonomy.

### Vinyot

Vinyot’s nightlife would stop without Tenebrine labor.

They are prominent among performers, gaming houses, dock crews, printers, broadcasters, security work, costume shops, courier routes, medical cooperatives, and the informal economies surrounding legal markets.

Tenebrine celebrities are fashionable. Tenebrine neighborhoods remain heavily policed.

The contradiction is widely discussed and rarely resolved.

### Arneria

Arnerian Tenebrines serve as informants, shrine attendants, tomb workers, route keepers, apothecaries, performers, and caretakers of spaces used after daylight rites conclude.

Their resistance to disease has given them an honored role in several healing traditions.

It has also produced religious arguments that donation is an inherited sacred duty. Tenebrine reformers reject any theology in which the divine gift belongs more to the patient than to the person whose body carries it.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Tenebrines thrive in docks, storm shelters, estates, ship holds, salvage crews, performance circuits, and cities rebuilt around whatever structures remained.

They are skilled in the performance of status because island ports require constant movement across class, crew, and jurisdiction.

Many Al’Ari households maintain three versions of the same room: respectable by daylight, commercial by evening, and communal after midnight.

## VII. Tenebrines and Delving

Tenebrines are frequently recruited for infiltration, recovery, urban Labyrinth work, trap handling, scouting, and morale.

Their strengths include:

- Darkvision
- Disease immunity
- Precise manual work
- Comfort in service passages and overlooked routes
- Improvisation under uncertain conditions
- Reading how inhabited spaces actually function

Their greatest occupational danger is being treated as expendable because they are believed capable of surviving filth, infection, or social risk.

Disease immunity does not grant immunity to poison, parasites, curses, exhaustion, corrosive material, or magical contamination.

The Ministry has repeatedly issued this clarification.

Delver companies have repeatedly required it again.

Tenebrines are also vulnerable to Labyrinth spaces that imitate cities. A false alley, familiar lock, discarded object, or distant party may suggest opportunity precisely because it has been designed to be noticed.

A common warning states:

> If the Labyrinth leaves a key in plain sight, ask what it wants unlocked.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

Your Tenebrine character has the following traits.

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Dexterity score increases by 2.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

### Darkvision

You can see in dim light within 60 feet as if it were bright light, and in darkness as if it were dim light.

You can’t discern color in darkness.

### Pristine-Blooded

You are immune to all diseases.

Additionally, a creature injected with at least 1 pint of your blood gains advantage on its next Constitution saving throw against disease within 24 hours.

Donating this blood inflicts 1 level of exhaustion.

### Urbanite’s Fortune

While in a city of 10,000 or more people, you can reroll one attack roll, ability check, or saving throw.

You must use the second result.

Once you use this trait, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

Sublineages describe broad physiological inheritances. They do not determine personality, profession, homeland, or moral character.

### Raccoon

Raccoon Tenebrines commonly possess masked facial markings, ringed tails, highly dexterous hands, and an intense tactile awareness of small mechanisms.

Their reputation for theft grew from histories of repair, salvage, domestic service, and exclusion from property systems. Skill with a lock does not establish criminal intent.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Charisma score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Think with Your Hands.** By handling a key for 1 action, you gain advantage on Dexterity checks to unlock its associated lock for 7 days.

You automatically succeed on any Dexterity check to open a lock you’ve previously unlocked using this feature.

### Possum

Possum Tenebrines are often heavier in build and deliberate in movement, with pale facial markings, grasping tails, strong jaws, and remarkable tolerance for physical stress.

Their cultural reputation for silence obscures communities rich in argument, song, and abrasive humor. Stillness is a method, not a lack of feeling.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Below the Belt.** When hidden and within 5 feet of a creature no more than one size larger than you, you may use a bonus action to force a Strength saving throw.

The DC equals the result of your Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check.

On a failure, the target is knocked prone.

You deal an additional `1d4` damage with melee weapon attacks against prone targets.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

If the campaign uses Homeland Imprints, choose one trait representing the region that most strongly shaped your character.

A homeland represents upbringing and cultural experience. It does not need to match your birthplace or ancestry.

### Allemance — Dittoed Rumors

You gain proficiency with a forgery kit.

When making a Charisma (Deception) check to impersonate someone, you are treated as proficient and double your proficiency bonus.

Allemancian Tenebrines learn that a familiar voice, proper greeting, and remembered local detail can open doors no official paper recognizes.

### Oria — Mosquito in the Crowd

You are treated as proficient and double your proficiency bonus on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made to hide in crowds.

Once per long rest, you may delay the effects of a dagger or shortsword strike made from hiding until the start of your next turn. Until then, the target remains unaware.

Oric Tenebrines understand how motion, heavy clothing, work noise, and crowded House interiors conceal a small deliberate act.

### Vinyot — Peer Misdirection

You can throw your voice up to 60 feet.

Once per long rest, if you fail a Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check, you may add a bonus equal to the number of visible onlookers, to a maximum of +5, to the result.

Vinyoti nightlife teaches that an audience is not an obstacle. It is material.

### Arneria — Stash Finder

You have advantage on Intelligence (Investigation) checks to locate hidden compartments in masonry.

You are treated as proficient and double your proficiency bonus when hiding objects on your person using Sleight of Hand.

Arnerian Tenebrines learn where pilgrims, priests, soldiers, and frightened households place the things they cannot openly carry.

### Al’Ar — The Training of Privilege

Choose one skill or tool proficiency you gained through early instruction.

Once per creature, when convincing someone of your wealth, you may declare a roll result of 15 before rolling. This applies only to first impressions.

Al’Ari Tenebrines often learn the posture, vocabulary, and small assumptions through which wealth recognizes itself.

## XII. Playing a Tenebrine

When creating a Tenebrine character, consider:

- What do you notice that others consistently overlook?
- Is humor a gift, a weapon, a shield, or a habit?
- What would make you surrender a key?
- Do you believe security can last?
- What object have you kept long after it stopped being useful?
- How do you respond when someone praises your resilience?
- What kind of help can you accept without suspicion?
- What does privacy mean in your household?
- Have you ever donated blood? Was it truly a choice?
- Where do you go when you want to be seen?

A Tenebrine may be solemn, conspicuous, trusting, wealthy, orderly, physically clumsy, or uninterested in city life.

Their lineage teaches ways of surviving overlooked spaces.

It does not require them to remain there.
