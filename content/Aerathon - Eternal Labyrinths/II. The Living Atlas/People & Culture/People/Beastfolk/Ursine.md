## I. Lineage Overview

Ursines are Brown, Polar, and Black Bear Beastfolk who possess extraordinary recall.

Their memory is not merely cultural reputation.

Most Ursines can reproduce what they have seen and heard with exceptional accuracy. They learn from repeated abilities, preserve witnessed spellwork, and carry personal history with a clarity other peoples often associate only with magical archives.

This capacity creates opportunity and burden.

Ursines serve as historians, judges, teachers, spell researchers, witnesses, negotiators, artists, and delvers. Institutions trust them to remember.

Those same institutions may expect them never to forget.

An Ursine can recall an insult without still feeling injured. They can reproduce an oath without believing it should remain binding. They can remember a dead person’s voice without wanting to perform it for strangers.

Memory is information.

Meaning remains alive and changeable.

Ursine cultures ask:

> What does a person owe the past when the past never becomes indistinct?

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

In the **Account of the Second Telling**, the Old Court speaks a law to the first Ursine.

The Ursine repeats it perfectly.

Years later, the gods ask for the law again. The Ursine repeats the same words.

The world has changed. The law now causes harm.

The Old Court asks whether memory preserved truth.

The Ursine answers:

> It preserved what you said. Truth still requires judgment.

### The Stone-Song Confederacy

Ursine clans formed the principal voices of the ancient [[The Stone-Song Confederacy|Stone-Song Confederacy]], where law, history, and verdict were sung into resonant caverns.

The Confederacy treated law as concord rather than command.

Its Oath-Voices used harmony to record public agreement. Its Silent Guard communicated through rhythmic stamping. Allied Laetine and other mustelid communities shaped the chambers so different voices could be heard.

Modern Ursine traditions retain:

- Spoken or sung oath verification
- Competitions of memory and interpretation
- The belief that testimony must be heard in context
- Deep suspicion of records detached from their living witnesses
- Rhythmic silence used as deliberate communication

The Confederacy also erased people through unsinging. Modern Ursine scholars cite this as proof that perfect records can still be governed unjustly.

### Memory Courts

Ursine testimony became highly valued after the Great Drift.

Some courts treated an Ursine witness as a recording device rather than a person. Witnesses were detained, compelled to repeat traumatic events, and prevented from discussing interpretation because only exact recall was considered legitimate.

Current law protects both memory and refusal.

## III. Body and Mnemonic Form

Ursines commonly possess:

- Great height and dense frames
- Heavy fur
- Strong claws and jaws
- Broad hands
- Powerful voices
- Bodies adapted to cold or temperate environments
- Exceptional episodic recall
- Strong tolerance for physical strain

Most are Medium by game classification, though many require great-body equipment.

### Eidetic Memory

Ursine recall is accurate but not omniscient.

It preserves what the person perceived.

It does not supply:

- Events outside attention
- Hidden motive
- An unobstructed view
- Meanings the witness lacked context to understand
- Protection from illusion
- Perfect interpretation

An accurate memory of misleading evidence remains misleading.

### Mnemonic Spellwork

Some Ursines can hold one witnessed spell pattern long enough to reconstruct it through study.

This is not instant spell theft. The spell must appear on the Ursine’s class list, be studied in an arcane library, and survive a demanding research process.

Spellcasters frequently place confidentiality terms upon Ursine observers. Such terms remain contracts, not assumptions created by the witness’s lineage.

### Forgetting and Health

Ursine memory does not prevent ordinary distraction, emotional reinterpretation, sleep disorder, trauma, or cognitive illness.

The ability to recall an event accurately can intensify harm when the event returns with full sensory clarity.

Treatment focuses on changing the memory’s relationship to the present rather than attempting crude erasure.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Ursine communities include family houses, memory circles, debate halls, craft guilds, competitions, archives, and resonant societies.

### Affectionate Rivalry

Many Ursines use competition as intimacy.

Wrestling, storytelling, debate, endurance, craft, cooking, and memory contests allow participants to test growth in the presence of someone trusted.

A proper rival wants the other person to become more capable.

Competition that depends upon humiliation, unequal stakes, or an inability to refuse is not considered affectionate.

### The Right Not to Repeat

Modern Ursine ethics recognize the **right not to repeat**.

A person may verify that they remember an event without reproducing every detail publicly. Courts may require lawful testimony, but families, employers, researchers, and audiences possess no automatic claim.

### Living Archives

Some households appoint memory keepers.

Responsible practice distributes the role, maintains written or resonant copies, and permits retirement.

An archive dependent upon one person’s continual availability is considered structurally negligent.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Ursine communities commonly admire:

- Accurate memory
- Interpretive judgment
- Intellectual and physical competition
- Reliability
- Strong testimony
- Reflection
- Oath awareness
- The ability to learn from witnessed action

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Memory | Preventing erasure | Refusing release |
| Reliability | Becoming a trusted witness | Losing the right to be unavailable |
| Competition | Encouraging growth | Turning affection into constant testing |
| Reflection | Learning from experience | Living inside prior events |
| Oath awareness | Preserving accountability | Treating old words as permanent chains |
| Precision | Distinguishing record from rumor | Dismissing emotional truth |

A recurring Ursine fear is that forgetting even one thing—through illness, magic, age, or choice—will make every memory less trustworthy.

Some test themselves compulsively. Some record everything twice. Some refuse witness roles. Others embrace art, fiction, or improvisation because not every truthful thing must be exact.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Ursines debate:

- Whether eidetic testimony deserves special evidentiary status
- Whether spell memory constitutes intellectual property
- How Stone-Song traditions should be revived
- Whether competitive culture excludes people who do not enjoy challenge
- Whether magical memory alteration can ever be consensual care
- How to archive memories without owning the rememberer
- Whether exact recall has been overvalued relative to interpretation

## VI. Ursines in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Ursines are debaters, teachers, brewers, local historians, performers, mediators, and keepers of public stories.

They often use Spirited Discourse to make rigorous argument feel communal rather than hostile.

### Oria

Oric Ursines work as smiths, House chroniclers, spell engineers, rescue leaders, judges, and makers of obsidian baubles.

The Stone-Song legacy remains strong in northern caverns. Protected sites require careful resonance practice.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Ursines are lawyers, contract specialists, spell brokers, auditors, performers, and public commentators.

Their memory can dominate negotiation. Ethical rules increasingly require written shared records rather than reliance upon one Ursine participant.

### Arneria

Arnerian Ursines preserve war-pick traditions, Beylik histories, temple disputes, and memories of exile and settlement.

First-round martial techniques reflect a culture in which the opening action establishes the shape of an encounter.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Ursines are teachers, athletes, ship workers, performers, and demonstrators of embodied craft.

Their Instructive Muscle traditions treat physical knowledge as teachable rather than mysterious talent.

## VII. Ursines and Delving

Ursines serve as tactical learners, route rememberers, spell researchers, front-line defenders, witnesses, and historians.

Their advantages include:

- Exact recall
- Adaptation to repeated nonmagical abilities
- Temporary spell-pattern retention
- Strong sublineage durability or knowledge
- Regional magical and instructional techniques

Labyrinths exploit memory through false repetitions, altered details, retroactive rooms, copied party voices, and events that are remembered differently by each witness.

An Ursine’s certainty can become the mechanism of the trap.

A common warning states:

> Remember exactly. Interpret cautiously.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Constitution score increases by 2.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

### Eidetic Memory

You can accurately recall anything you have seen or heard.

Additionally, when you make a Strength or Dexterity saving throw against a creature’s nonmagical ability, you have advantage on future saving throws against that same ability for the next 24 hours.

### Mnemonic Mind

When a creature you can see within 60 feet casts a spell that is on your class’s spell list, you can use your reaction to commit it to memory.

Within 7 days, you may study the spell in an arcane library.

After 8 hours of research per spell level and a successful Arcana check with a DC of 10 + the spell level, you may add it to your spells known or copy it into a spellbook as if copying from a scroll.

You may only retain one spell this way at a time.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

### Brown Bear

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Toughen Up.** When you roll initiative, you may immediately spend a number of Hit Dice up to half your level, minimum 1, to gain that many temporary hit points. These last for 10 minutes.

### Polar Bear

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Wisdom score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Cold Memory.** You are resistant to cold damage.

When you succeed on a Wisdom saving throw, you gain advantage on your next attack roll or ability check before the end of your next turn.

### Black Bear

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Intelligence score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Quiet Recall.** You gain proficiency in History.

When you make an Intelligence (History or Arcana) check related to events you personally witnessed, treat a d20 roll of 9 or lower as a 10.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

### Allemance — Spirited Discourse

You may use Intelligence instead of Charisma for Performance and Persuasion checks.

Once per long rest, when a creature within 30 feet fails a saving throw against being charmed, you may grant it a reroll as a reaction.

### Oria — Obsidian Bauble

You gain proficiency with smith’s tools.

With 8 hours of work and 30 gp, you can forge an obsidian bauble that stores a 1st-level spell cast into it.

Any creature holding the bauble can release the spell once, destroying the bauble.

### Vinyot — Rules Lawyer

Choose two 1st-level spells.

They count as being on all class spell lists for you.

### Arneria — Born of the Bey’s Head

You gain proficiency with the war pick.

If you hit with a war pick on the first round of combat, you deal an additional `2d6` piercing damage.

### Al’Ar — Instructive Muscle

You gain proficiency in Athletics.

When you succeed on a Strength (Athletics) check, creatures that share a language with you and saw the attempt are treated as proficient in Athletics when attempting the same task.

## XII. Playing an Ursine

Consider:

- Which memory do others ask you to perform?
- What have you remembered but released?
- Do you enjoy rivalry?
- Which oath remains meaningful?
- What fact did you perceive accurately but interpret wrongly?
- Who may ask you to repeat the past?
- What would memory loss mean to you?
- Which spell do you currently carry?
- How do you distinguish archive from person?
- What truthful thing can only be expressed through fiction?

An Ursine may be forgetful through illness, uncompetitive, physically slight, uninterested in history, dishonest, or unwilling to serve as witness.

Their lineage preserves perception.

It does not settle meaning.
