# Ferine

**Folk Classification:** [[‎ Beastfolk|Beastfolk]]  
**Lineage Classification:** Ferine  
**Registry Scope:** Sapient Lineage and Homeland Variance  
**Recognition Status:** Full Sapient Recognition  
**Delver Eligibility:** Unrestricted; Legacy Restrictions Rescinded  
**Common Names:** Afterfolk, Scar-Kin, Hardlaughers  
**Date of Revised Filing:** 7th Cycle, 3388 A.D.

---

## I. Lineage Overview

Ferines are a convergent Beastfolk lineage including Hyena, Wolverine, Honey Badger, and Tasmanian Devil peoples.

Their ancestors did not become one lineage through close physical kinship.

They became one through pressure.

Ferine communities formed repeatedly in places marked by:

- War
- Scarcity
- Collapsed states
- Abandoned settlements
- Dangerous borderlands
- Failed expeditions
- Contested authority
- Disaster recovery
- Predatory labor systems
- The violent aftermath other societies preferred not to see

Outsiders often describe Ferines as comfortable with violence.

A more accurate statement is that many Ferine cultures are comfortable naming conflict before respectable institutions have admitted it exists.

Ferines distinguish sharply between peace and quiet.

Peace describes a relationship in which conflict can be survived without erasing one side.

Quiet may describe peace.

It may also describe fear, exhaustion, censorship, an empty settlement, or the moment after everyone who objected has been removed.

Ferine humor, competition, territoriality, and defiance developed within that distinction.

They are not biologically aggressive.

They have inherited bodies suited to endurance and cultures shaped by the consequences of pretending danger was over.

## II. Origins and Collective Memory

Old Court records place the ancestors of modern Ferines among several sacred kinships: Predatory, Scavenger, Nocturnal, and Great-Beast.

No single origin account explains their later unity.

The most common Ferine story begins after a battle.

The kings, priests, and chroniclers leave. The victorious army takes its banners. The defeated army takes its dead of rank. Smoke remains above the road.

Four strangers arrive separately:

- One laughs so the living will answer.
- One drives scavengers from the wounded.
- One breaks open a sealed supply cart.
- One refuses to leave when the night creatures gather.

By dawn, they have argued over everything except whether the survivors should live.

The story is called **The First Bad Camp**.

No community claims it was pleasant. No version turns the strangers into friends immediately. The lesson is that solidarity does not require comfort, agreement, similarity, or affection.

It requires a decision about what happens next.

### The After-Camp Tradition

Ferine settlements frequently began as **after-camps** established beside someone else’s disaster.

Residents cleared dangerous remains, recovered tools, buried or burned bodies, marked unstable structures, defended abandoned stores, and decided which laws had died with the authority that issued them.

Some camps dissolved after recovery.

Others became permanent towns.

The people living there were often accused of profiting from tragedy by governments that had provided no aid.

Ferine oral histories remember the accusation with contempt:

> They abandoned the bread. We ate it. They abandoned the walls. We repaired them. They abandoned the people. We became related.

The phrase **made related** remains important in Ferine kinship.

## III. Body and Survival Form

Ferine bodies vary widely.

Common inherited features include:

- Dense muscle
- Powerful jaws or claws
- Thick hides or heavy fur
- Strong resistance to poison
- Compact balance
- Highly expressive vocalization
- Scavenger-adapted digestion
- Rapid threat response

Ferines are usually Medium.

### Built for Aftermath

Ferine poison resistance is often misunderstood as proof that they can safely consume any substance.

They cannot.

Resistance developed across many forms: scavenger ancestry, harsh food systems, alchemical exposure, corrupted environments, and magical adaptation. It reduces certain risks without eliminating them.

Ferines experience illness, chronic poisoning, addiction, contamination, and environmental harm.

The stereotype of the indestructible Ferine has been used to assign them dangerous cleanup work without adequate equipment.

### Voice and Laughter

Hyena and Tasmanian Devil sublineages in particular may possess loud or unusual vocal ranges.

Ferine laughter does not always indicate amusement.

It may communicate:

- Stress
- Warning
- Group position
- Pain
- Disbelief
- Challenge
- Relief
- Genuine delight

Treating laughter during danger as evidence of cruelty has caused repeated conflict in mixed delver crews.

### Scars

Ferine relationships to scars vary.

Some communities display repaired injuries openly as evidence that the event was survived and witnessed. Others cover scars associated with captivity, coercion, or unwanted public storytelling.

Asking how a scar was obtained is not a neutral greeting.

## IV. Society and Kinship

Ferine societies include permanent towns, mobile crews, neighborhood defenses, salvage cooperatives, unions, extended households, competitive circles, veteran communities, and temporary camps that become permanent by accident.

Kinship is frequently established through **witnessed continuance**.

Someone who remains through the bad part may become family even when affection develops slowly.

This does not mean Ferines distrust easy warmth. It means many communities distinguish between welcome and proven reliance.

### The Terms of the Camp

When strangers establish shelter under dangerous conditions, many Ferines state the **Terms of the Camp**.

The exact terms vary, but commonly address:

- Who receives food first
- Whether weapons remain visible
- How sleep watches are divided
- What property remains personal
- What can be taken from the dead
- What happens if someone leaves
- Which conflicts must wait until morning
- Who may speak for an injured person

The terms are temporary and practical.

They are also treated seriously. A person who breaks them and later argues they were never “real law” has identified precisely why the tradition exists.

### Competition

Competition is common in Ferine communities, but dominance is not a universal government.

Contests may determine speaking order, work assignments, training rank, or who must perform an unpleasant task. Many are designed to expose capability without creating permanent hierarchy.

A person who wins repeatedly and never accepts risk for the group may be regarded as skilled but unworthy of authority.

Ferine cultures often respect open ambition more than concealed control.

This can make them blunt in institutions where power is expected to disguise itself as procedure.

### Conflict

Ferine arguments can be loud, physical in posture, and direct in language.

Violence remains violence.

No cultural custom permits assault without consequence. Communities differ in mediation practice, but most draw a strong line between confrontation and coercion.

The common Ferine challenge **“Say the shape of it”** asks a speaker to identify the actual conflict:

- Is this about resources?
- Rank?
- Fear?
- Territory?
- Humiliation?
- A broken promise?
- Someone needing to be blamed?

Naming the shape does not solve the problem.

It prevents the problem from pretending to be something nobler.

## V. Inner Life, Virtue, and Shadow

Ferine communities commonly admire:

- Candor under pressure
- Refusal to panic
- Courage without ceremony
- Loyalty proven through action
- Humor in terrible conditions
- The ability to recover useful things
- Resistance to intimidation
- Willingness to challenge false peace

These values cast long shadows.

| Admired Quality | Constructive Expression | Potential Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| Defiance | Resisting coercion | Rejecting necessary caution |
| Candor | Naming the actual conflict | Using honesty as permission for cruelty |
| Endurance | Functioning through hardship | Normalizing preventable suffering |
| Competition | Testing and improving capability | Turning every relationship into rank |
| Territoriality | Defending people and refuge | Possessiveness and exclusion |
| Gallows humor | Restoring courage | Making another person’s pain into material |

A recurring Ferine fear is that safety will require them to become harmless to people who are not harmless to them.

Some respond by displaying danger openly. Some become meticulous negotiators. Some refuse stable institutions. Others pursue law, medicine, engineering, or public office because they want systems that function after speeches end.

### Internal Divisions

Modern Ferines debate:

- Whether the convergent lineage remains politically useful
- Whether after-camps should accept government incorporation
- Whether salvage rights belong to recoverers, prior owners, survivors, or the public
- Whether dominance contests preserve honesty or reproduce abuse
- Whether Ferine humor should be moderated in mixed communities
- Whether permanent peace makes the lineage’s pressure traditions obsolete
- How much authority may be granted during emergencies
- Whether former military and mercenary communities should lead disaster response

The deepest divide concerns permanence.

Some Ferines believe every camp must eventually become a home.

Others believe a home that cannot be abandoned will eventually become a trap.

## VI. Ferines in Modern Aerathon

### Allemance

Allemancian Ferines live in old border settlements, crowded ports, rural watch communities, ruined fort towns, and neighborhoods formed around displaced families.

They are often called when ordinary authority fails and dismissed when it returns.

Modern Ferine organizers have forced several towns to convert emergency watches into accountable civic services instead of disbanding the workers who kept those towns alive.

### Oria

Oric Ferines work in remote Houses, salvage teams, winter rescue, mine recovery, monster disposal, structural clearing, and high-risk repair.

Oria respects competence under bad conditions.

Ferines often receive that respect while doing the work and suspicion once they return. House debates increasingly address whether emergency specialists can hold ordinary authority or must remain permanent outsiders.

### Vinyot

Vinyoti Ferines are prominent in security, organized sport, dock unions, recovery firms, debt enforcement, entertainment, demolition, and high-risk delving.

Their image sells easily.

Public audiences celebrate Ferine aggression in the arena and condemn it in labor disputes. Vinyoti Ferines are among the League’s most sophisticated critics of reputational contracts and manufactured danger.

### Arneria

Arnerian Ferines have long served along war roads, pilgrimage routes, volcanic recovery zones, temple militias, and regions where oath authority changes quickly.

Many insist that a vow made during disaster expires with the disaster unless renewed freely.

This principle places them in recurring conflict with institutions that treat emergency obedience as permanent sacred duty.

### Al’Ar

Al’Ari Ferines work in storm salvage, ship defense, rebuilding crews, harbor security, monster hunting, and mobile performance.

Al’Ar understands catastrophe as recurring rather than exceptional. Ferine after-camp traditions have therefore entered ordinary civic planning.

Several islands formally recognize the Terms of the Camp as temporary emergency law, though disputes continue over who may declare them.

## VII. Ferines and Delving

Ferines are exceptionally capable in unstable expeditions.

They are recruited for:

- Front-line combat
- Hazard recovery
- Poisoned environments
- Monster disposal
- Salvage
- Emergency command
- Morale under pressure
- Operations after party separation or plan failure

Their core strengths reward momentum during crisis.

That does not make crisis good for them.

Ferine delvers are vulnerable to companies that manufacture danger because they expect the lineage to perform best within it. A party that relies upon one member’s refusal to break may simply be refusing to build a safer plan.

Experienced Ferines are trained to distinguish:

- Necessary risk from entertaining risk
- Salvage from bait
- A failed structure from a structure designed to appear failed
- A wounded creature from a creature imitating vulnerability
- Temporary emergency authority from permanent command
- Competitive instinct from Labyrinth-induced compulsion

A common delver maxim states:

> Surviving the bad plan does not make it a good plan.

## VIII. Names, Titles, and Address

Ferine names may derive from family, camp, scars, profession, humor, terrain, or deliberately ordinary reinvention.

Examples include:

- Asha Red-Camp
- Berrik Knucklebell
- Cera Flintlaugh
- Dov Afterstorm
- Enna Three-Terms
- Harl Made-Related
- Jorra Bonewhite
- Kesh of the Bad Bridge
- Marn Quiet-Teeth
- Rava Opensupply
- Tovin Scarwright
- Yara Last-Watch

Living titles include:

- **Camp-Speaker**
- **After-Warden**
- **Termskeeper**
- **Salvage Judge**
- **Last Watch**
- **Ruin Medic**
- **Scarwright**

Terms such as **rabid**, **savage**, **carrion**, or **attack breed** are demeaning when used to characterize Ferine people.

## IX. D&D Lineage Traits

Your Ferine character has the following traits.

### Creature Type

You are a Humanoid.

### Ability Score Increase

Your Constitution score increases by 2.

### Speed

Your walking speed is 30 feet.

### Built for Aftermath

You have advantage on saving throws against the poisoned condition, and you have resistance to poison damage.

### Competitive Instincts

When a creature you can see within 30 feet drops to 0 hit points, you gain temporary hit points equal to your proficiency bonus.

You can benefit from this feature once per turn.

### No Easy Prey

When you are frightened, charmed, or poisoned, you can use your reaction to gain advantage on your next attack roll or ability check made before the end of your next turn.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest.

## X. Recognized Sublineages

Sublineages describe broad physiological inheritances. They do not determine personality, profession, homeland, or capacity for violence.

### Hyena

Hyena Ferines commonly possess sloped posture, powerful jaws, rounded ears, and highly expressive vocal musculature.

Their social awareness is often reduced to “dominance instinct” by outsiders. Hyena communities tend to understand rank as contextual: the best hunt leader may not be the proper judge, medic, parent, or camp speaker.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Charisma score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Mocking Resilience.** When you take damage, you can use your reaction to impose disadvantage on the next attack made against you before the end of the attacker’s next turn.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest.

### Wolverine

Wolverine Ferines possess compact, heavily muscled frames and a low center of gravity.

Many distinguish territory from ownership. Territory is the space one is prepared to defend and maintain; ownership may be nothing more than a distant claim.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Territorial Fury.** When a creature enters your reach for the first time on its turn, you can use your reaction to make a melee weapon attack against it.

You can use this feature once per turn, a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest.

### Honey Badger

Honey Badger Ferines possess thick hides, broad shoulders, and a strong tolerance for pain.

Their reputation for fearlessness creates intense social pressure. Many communities now teach that acknowledging fear demonstrates awareness, while ignoring it merely gives danger the first move.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Dexterity score increases by 1.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Reckless Defiance.** When you fail a saving throw, you can choose to succeed instead.

When you do, you take force damage equal to twice your proficiency bonus.

Once you use this feature, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Tasmanian Devil

Tasmanian Devil Ferines possess compact frames, sharp features, powerful voices, and rapid shifts of motion.

They are stereotyped as impatient or chaotic. Their communities often describe the trait more precisely as sensitivity to stalled momentum: when danger is already moving, waiting can feel like surrendering the choice of direction.

**Ability Score Increase.** Your Strength or Dexterity score increases by 1, your choice.

**Size.** Your size is Medium.

**Feeding Frenzy.** Once per turn, when you hit a creature that has already taken damage since the end of your last turn, you deal extra damage equal to your proficiency bonus.

## XI. Homeland Imprints

If the campaign uses Homeland Imprints, choose one trait representing the region that most strongly shaped your character.

A homeland represents upbringing and cultural experience. It does not need to match your birthplace or ancestry.

### Allemance — Crowd-Breaker

When you are adjacent to two or more hostile creatures, you have advantage on Strength (Athletics) checks and saving throws made to avoid being grappled, restrained, or knocked prone.

Allemancian Ferines learn to create room when a familiar street, public meeting, or local celebration becomes dangerous.

### Oria — Thin-Air Grit

You have advantage on Constitution saving throws made to resist exhaustion caused by environmental effects, including altitude, cold, or forced marches.

Oric Ferines are trained to preserve function when isolation and weather remove easy options.

### Vinyot — No Easy Mark

When you roll initiative, you gain temporary hit points equal to your proficiency bonus.

These temporary hit points last for 1 minute.

Vinyoti Ferines learn that public violence often begins with one party assuming the other is profitable to harm.

### Arneria — Relentless Advance

Once on your turn, when you reduce a creature to 0 hit points or score a critical hit, you can move up to half your speed without provoking opportunity attacks.

Arnerian Ferines learn to move through the opening created by decisive action before heat, oath, or enemy closes it.

### Al’Ar — Unsettling Presence

As a bonus action, you can force one creature within 10 feet that can see you to make a Wisdom saving throw.

The DC equals `8 + your proficiency bonus + your Charisma modifier`.

On a failure, the creature has disadvantage on its next attack roll against you before the end of its next turn.

Once you use this feature, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

Al’Ari Ferines learn to interrupt violence through posture, performance, and the suggestion that escalation will not proceed as planned.

## XII. Playing a Ferine

When creating a Ferine character, consider:

- What is the difference between peace and quiet to you?
- Which conflict are others refusing to name?
- Who became family by remaining through the bad part?
- Do you trust stable authority?
- What do you salvage that other people discard?
- When is competition joyful for you?
- What kind of weakness do you find hardest to admit?
- Has your endurance ever enabled someone else’s negligence?
- What place are you prepared to defend?
- What would convince you to leave it?

A Ferine may be gentle, conflict-averse, ceremonious, sheltered, soft-spoken, cautious, or uninterested in competition.

Their lineage carries survival traditions.

It does not require a life built around surviving.
