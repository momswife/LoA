## I. Identification & Classification

Identify the subject in plain language, state why it belongs on this Bestiary shelf, and distinguish confirmed observation from interpretation. Record useful common or local names without treating a filing category as a moral judgment.

## II. Physical Description & Field Signs

Describe the features, tracks, residue, sounds, or other signs that allow a delver to identify the subject. Do not invent exact measurements unless a source or observation supports them.

## III. Habitat & Distribution

Record authenticated habitats and encounter conditions. For Labyrinth sightings, distinguish recurring environments from claims that the subject occurs on every floor or route.

## IV. Observed & Reported Behavior

Separate repeatable observations from credible reports, folklore, and proposed explanations. Address signs of sapience cautiously and refer uncertain cases for review.

## V. Ecology & Anomalous Traits

Explain the subject's role in its environment and any magical, undead, planar, or artificial traits. Remove this section when it adds nothing useful.

## VI. Threat Assessment

Describe what creates danger: terrain, numbers, provocation, disease, venom, ambush, magical effects, or another specific condition. Threat is not morality.

## VII. Delver Guidance

Give concise identification, avoidance, retreat, containment, and first-response guidance. Prefer actionable instructions over dramatic summary.

## VIII. Cultural & Disputed Accounts

Include only attested local names, beliefs, customs, or scholarly disputes. Attribute them and remove this section when no such material is established.
